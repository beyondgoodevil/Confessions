## 1. Sarah (Wife of Abraham)

- **Context:** Sarah was initially barren and advanced in age when she bore Isaac.
- **Passage:** Genesis 21:1-3  
  "The LORD visited Sarah as He had said, and the LORD did to Sarah as He had promised. And Sarah conceived and bore Abraham a son in his old age at the time of which God had spoken to him. Abraham called the name of his son who was born to him, whom Sarah bore him, Isaac."

## 2. Hannah (Mother of Samuel)

- **Context:** Hannah was also barren and prayed earnestly for a child, promising to dedicate him to the Lord if her prayer was answered.
- **Passage:** 1 Samuel 1:19-20  
  "They rose early in the morning and worshiped before the LORD. Then they went back to their house at Ramah. And Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, and the LORD remembered her. And in due time Hannah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Samuel, for she said, 'I have asked for him from the LORD.'"

## Significance

- **Divine Intervention:** In both accounts, the births of Isaac and Samuel are significant as they represent God's intervention in response to prayer and faith, demonstrating His power to overcome barrenness and fulfill His promises.
- **Covenantal Fulfillment:** Isaac's birth is crucial for the fulfillment of God's covenant with Abraham, while Samuel's birth marks the transition to a new phase in Israel's history as he becomes a prophet and judge.

These miraculous births underscore themes of faith, divine promise, and God's faithfulness in the biblical narrative.
