title: Distinction in the Two Natures of Christ and Salvation
tags: [theology, christology, salvation]
summary: Five reasons a "Tertium Quid" Christ could not save.
---

If the idea of a "Tertium Quid" (a third, confused nature) were accepted, it would undermine the very foundation of salvation in Christianity because it would disrupt the proper relationship between Christ's divinity and humanity, which is essential for His role as the Savior.

1. **The Importance of Full Humanity and Full Divinity**: For salvation to be effective, the Savior must be both fully God and fully man. Christ, as fully divine, is able to save humanity because, as God, He has the power to redeem and reconcile us to the Father. However, Christ must also be fully human to represent humanity and experience human suffering and death, allowing Him to atone for the sins of mankind. If Christ’s natures were confused or blended into a "third nature" (a "Tertium Quid"), then He would not be truly human or truly divine. This would mean He could not properly act as both the divine Son of God and the true representative of humanity.
2. **The Doctrine of Recapitulation**: According to the doctrine of recapitulation, Christ "recapitulated" or summed up the entirety of the human experience. He took on the fullness of human nature and sanctified it, making salvation possible for all. If Christ’s humanity was not fully intact or was confused with His divinity, He could not truly take on the fullness of human nature. This would mean He could not restore it, thus failing to save humanity. A "Tertium Quid" would imply a hybrid nature that is neither fully human nor fully divine, and therefore unable to bridge the gap between God and man.
3. **Theological Implication of Losing the Distinction of Natures**: Without the distinction of the divine and human natures, there would be no perfect mediator between God and man. St. Paul in 1 Timothy 2:5 says, "For there is one God and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus." If Christ is not fully human, He cannot be the true representative of humanity. If He is not fully divine, He cannot be the true Savior. A "Tertium Quid" would distort this mediation and render Christ incapable of reconciling God and humanity.
4. **The Necessity of Christ's Passion**: The very act of Christ’s suffering and death on the cross requires that He be fully human, capable of experiencing true suffering and death. If His humanity were compromised or muddled with His divinity (in the form of a "Tertium Quid"), then He would not have truly died for our sins. The sacrifice would be meaningless, as there would be no true death or resurrection. Without a proper, undisturbed human nature capable of death, salvation through the cross would be impossible.
5. **The Failure of the Atonement**: If Christ is neither fully divine nor fully human because of a "Tertium Quid," then the atonement would be incomplete. The act of redemption—where the divine Son of God dies and rises again for the salvation of mankind—requires both natures in their fullness. Christ’s death, being an expression of divine love and human sacrifice, would be nullified if His nature were confused, leading to no true salvation.

In summary, if Christ were reduced to a "Tertium Quid," His ability to save humanity would be severely compromised. The bridge between God and man would be broken, and the work of salvation through the incarnation, death, and resurrection would be void. Only by maintaining both the fullness of His divinity and humanity can Christ save humanity, and any confusion or blending of the two natures would result in the failure of the salvation of mankind.
