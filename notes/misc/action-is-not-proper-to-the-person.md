---
title: Action is not proper to the person
tags: [theology, christology]
summary: Christ's actions belong to the nature through which they are performed, while attributed to the one Person of the Son.
---

The statement "action is not proper to the person" reflects a distinction crucial for understanding the nature of Christ's actions in His two natures. In this view, actions are not attributed directly to the person but to the nature through which they are performed. This ensures that the divine and human natures of Christ remain distinct while being united in the one divine Person of the Word.

1. **Person vs. Nature**: The person, as the subject, is the one who acts, but the actions are specific to the nature. Christ’s divine nature performs divine actions (like creating and sustaining the universe), while His human nature performs human actions (like suffering, eating, and being born). The actions of the two natures are fully and completely attributed to the one Person of the Son, but they remain distinct in their origin—divine actions come from His divine nature, and human actions come from His human nature.
2. **Distinct Yet United**: The key point is that Christ’s two natures are not mixed or confused in their actions. While the person of the Son performs these actions, it is the divine or human nature that provides the proper basis for them. The divine nature does not act in a human way, nor does the human nature act in a divine way. Each nature retains its own integrity while working in perfect harmony in the one Person.
3. **Christ's Two Natures**: This distinction safeguards the truth of Christ’s divinity and humanity. It prevents any blurring of the two natures, preserving the fullness of both His divinity and humanity. The divine Person, the Word, is fully and truly God, and the human nature is fully and truly human, but their actions are not confused.
4. **Patristic Foundation**: This view is supported by the teachings of the Church Fathers who emphasized the necessity of maintaining the integrity of both natures in Christ. By attributing actions to the nature rather than the person, the Fathers maintained a clear distinction between the divine and human natures, while recognizing that Christ’s actions were always performed by the Person of the Word.

In this way, the distinction between person and nature ensures that the divine and human realities in Christ remain intact and unconfused, each performing its own actions, but all attributed to the one Person of the Son.
