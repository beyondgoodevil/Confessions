1. **Solipsism Relies on Universal Truths While Denying Them**  
   To claim "only my mind exists," the solipsist assumes universal truths, such as the law of non-contradiction and the existence of truth itself. However, solipsism denies the external reality of any universals or transcendent truths beyond the solipsist's mind. If truth is merely a construct of the solipsist's mind, there is no basis to claim it applies universally. The very act of denying the external world presupposes the existence of a stable logical order outside the solipsist's subjective experience.
2. **The Solipsist Cannot Justify Language**  
   Solipsism depends on language to articulate its position. Language, however, presupposes a shared framework of meaning—grammar, syntax, and semantics—that transcends the individual mind. Where did the solipsist learn language? It requires interaction with an external reality, including other minds. By denying the external world, the solipsist undermines the coherence of the very tools they use to communicate their position.
3. **Self-Identity Is Meaningless Without Contrast**  
   Solipsism claims "my mind exists," but what does "my mind" mean without the concept of "not-my mind" or "other"? The solipsist cannot define their own existence without reference to something external. For example, how can one distinguish "thoughts" from "illusions of thought" if there is no external reality to validate this distinction? By rejecting other minds and the external world, solipsism collapses into incoherence because it cannot define the "self" it seeks to defend.
4. **Solipsism Relies on a Coherent Perceptual World While Denying It Exists**  
   The solipsist experiences a structured, consistent external world (e.g., the laws of physics, time, and causality) but denies its reality. Why does the solipsist's "mind" generate such a coherent and lawful illusion? This implies a rational structure external to the solipsist’s individual will, contradicting pure solipsism. If the solipsist argues the external world is "just their imagination," this imagination itself operates according to intelligible patterns that require an external grounding (i.e., God).
5. **Solipsism Destroys Knowledge**  
   Solipsism implies that no knowledge beyond the solipsist's own thoughts can be valid, but even this claim is self-defeating. How does the solipsist know their thoughts are true? Truth presupposes a standard outside of oneself. If there is no objective truth, the solipsist's assertion ("only my mind exists") is meaningless because it cannot be validated or even coherently evaluated.
6. **Transcendental Argument for the External World**  
   Solipsism fails to account for the preconditions of experience. For experience to be intelligible at all, certain things must necessarily exist: logic, which transcends individual minds; a stable metaphysical reality, which allows for consistent experiences; and other minds, which provide the necessary relational framework for communication, learning, and self-awareness. By denying these preconditions, solipsism undermines itself. The external world and other minds are not arbitrary beliefs but necessary for the solipsist to even form their argument.

Solipsism is self-refuting because it denies the external world while relying on external realities like logic, language, and coherence. It cannot justify its own claims, as the tools needed to argue for solipsism presuppose the existence of an intelligible, shared reality. The solipsist inadvertently borrows from the very metaphysical truths they reject—pointing to the necessity of God and an objective, external world as the basis for knowledge and existence. Without these, solipsism collapses into absurdity.
