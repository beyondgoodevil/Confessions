---

Alongside **truth** and **goodness**, the classical and Christian traditions treat **beauty** as one of the ways the soul is drawn to God. Beauty is not just a matter of taste; it is a real reflection of God's glory in creation, and it **calls** the soul beyond itself. Dostoevsky put it in a single line in *The Idiot*: **"beauty will save the world."** Here's why:

### 1. The Transcendentals

- The medieval tradition spoke of **transcendentals**: properties that belong to everything that exists, insofar as it exists, such as **one**, **true** and **good**. Many add **beautiful**.
- **Significance:** if beauty is a transcendental, then beauty is not something we project onto things, but a real aspect of being itself. And since God is Being, Truth and Goodness in the highest degree, He is also **Beauty itself**.

### 2. Plato's Ladder of Beauty

- In the *Symposium*, Diotima teaches Socrates that love begins with **one beautiful body**, rises to the beauty of **all bodies**, then of **souls**, of laws and knowledge, and finally to **Beauty itself**, eternal and unchanging.
- **Significance:** every beautiful thing points beyond itself to a source of beauty that does not fade (compare [[The Good in Plato]]).

### 3. The Fathers

1. **Dionysius the Areopagite**: in the *Divine Names* (ch. 4), he connects the Greek word for beauty, *kallos*, with the verb "to call", *kalein*. God is called Beauty because He **calls all things to Himself**.
2. **St. Augustine**: "Late have I loved You, O Beauty ever ancient, ever new, late have I loved You!" (*Confessions*, book 10). He had looked for beauty in created things, while the Beauty that made them was within him all along.
3. **The Philokalia**: the title of the great Orthodox collection of ascetic writings means "**love of the beautiful**". The ascetic life is presented as a love of divine beauty, not just discipline.

### 4. Beauty in Orthodox Worship

- **The Envoys of Prince Vladimir**: according to the Russian Primary Chronicle, when Vladimir's envoys attended the Liturgy in Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, they reported: "We knew not whether we were in heaven or on earth." Russia's conversion is remembered as a conversion through **beauty**.
- **Icons**: icons are sometimes called "**theology in colour**". They do not depict nature as it is, but transfigured, filled with the uncreated light (see [[Icons Are Biblical]] and [[The Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor]]).
- **The Liturgy** is meant to be beautiful because it is a participation in the heavenly worship (see [[Old Testament Foreshadowings of Heavenly Worship]]).
- "One thing I have desired of the LORD, That will I seek: That I may dwell in the house of the LORD All the days of my life, To behold the beauty of the LORD, And to inquire in His temple." (Psalm 27:4)

### 5. The Beauty of the Cross

"He has no form or comeliness; And when we see Him, There is no beauty that we should desire Him." (Isaiah 53:2)

- **Significance:** Christian beauty is not mere attractiveness. The highest beauty is revealed in the self-giving love of the crucified Christ, who had "no beauty that we should desire Him". This is why the saints, even when outwardly plain or suffering, are radiant.

### 6. Modern Challenges

1. **Beauty Made Subjective**: Kant described the judgement of beauty as a feeling of disinterested pleasure in the one who perceives it. After him, beauty came to be seen more and more as being "in the eye of the beholder" rather than in things.
2. **Beauty Without God**: beauty can become an idol. Of the fallen cherub the prophet says: "Your heart was lifted up because of your beauty; You corrupted your wisdom for the sake of your splendor" (Ezekiel 28:17). Beauty loved for itself, cut off from its source, leads away from God.
3. **A Way, Not the Destination**: the Fathers treat created beauty as a ladder. One climbs it to God, rather than stopping on a rung.

### Conclusion

Beauty is a real reflection of the glory of God and one of the ways the soul is drawn to Him. Plato saw that every beautiful thing points to Beauty itself; the Fathers identified that Beauty with God, who calls all things to Himself. Orthodox worship and iconography make beauty a path of prayer, while the Cross shows that the deepest beauty is self-giving love. Cut off from God, beauty becomes an idol; received as a gift and followed to its source, it is one of the surest ways to Him.
