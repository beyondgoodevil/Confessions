title: Aristotle and God as Pure Act

It would be wrong to claim that God as "pure act" requires something external to actualize Him because such a claim misunderstands the concept of "pure act" in theological metaphysics and misapplies Aristotle's framework to God. Here's why:

1. **Pure Act Means Lacking Potentiality**:  
   The term "pure act" (actus purus) in theological metaphysics means that God has no unrealized potential. Unlike creatures, who possess potentiality (the ability to change or become something they are not yet), God is fully actualized being. This entails that God's existence is complete and perfect in Himself, without dependence on anything else to "actualize" Him.
2. **Aristotle’s Unmoved Mover**:  
   Aristotle described the "Unmoved Mover" as pure actuality, meaning a being that is not moved or caused by anything else. For Aristotle, this being is the ultimate cause of all motion in the universe, not because it is moved or actualized by something external, but because it is the final, self-sufficient cause of all change and existence. The Unmoved Mover is pure act because it is already fully actual and does not need anything to bring it into being.
3. **God’s Self-Existence (Aseity)**:  
   In Christian theology, God is described as self-existent (*aseity*), meaning He is the source of His own being. This is reflected in Scripture, such as in Exodus 3:14, where God reveals Himself as "I AM WHO I AM," emphasizing His self-sufficiency and independence. God’s existence is not contingent on anything outside Himself. To claim that God as pure act requires external actualization would deny this fundamental attribute of God.
4. **Misapplication of Aristotelian Categories**:  
   While Aristotle’s metaphysics provides a helpful framework for discussing potentiality and actuality, it must be understood that Aristotle’s concept of God (the Unmoved Mover) differs from the Christian God. Aristotle’s God is not personal and does not interact with creation. Christian theology adopts and adapts Aristotle’s idea of pure actuality, applying it to a personal, relational, and self-sufficient God. Thus, applying the concept of external actualization to God as pure act is to misread both Aristotle and Christian theology.
5. **Logical Contradiction in External Actualization**:  
   If God needed something external to actualize Him, He would cease to be "pure act" and instead possess potentiality (the capacity to be actualized). Such a being would not be God because it would not be self-existent or independent. A being that requires actualization is a creature, not the Creator. By definition, God as pure act excludes any potentiality or dependence.
6. **God as the Source of All Actuality**:  
   In both Aristotelian and Christian thought, God as pure act is the source of all actuality. He is the ultimate ground of all being and change, meaning all that exists derives its existence from Him. To suggest that He requires external actualization would invert the metaphysical hierarchy, making God dependent on what He Himself sustains.

## Summary

To claim that God as pure act requires external actualization misunderstands both the concept of pure act and the nature of God as self-sufficient and self-existent. Pure act specifically denotes the absence of potentiality or need for external causation. Instead, God is the source of all existence and the ground of all actuality, fully actual and independent in Himself. This understanding aligns with both Aristotle's notion of the Unmoved Mover and the Christian doctrine of God’s aseity and perfection.
