**Epistemic questions** are questions related to **epistemology**, the branch of philosophy that studies knowledge, belief, and justification. These questions explore how we come to know things, what justifies our beliefs, and what distinguishes knowledge from mere opinion.

### Types of Epistemic Questions

1. **Questions About the Nature of Knowledge**
    - *What is knowledge?*
    - *Is knowledge justified true belief (JTB), or does it require something more?* (e.g., Gettier problem)
    - *Can we have knowledge of anything with absolute certainty?*
2. **Questions About Justification**
    - *What does it mean for a belief to be justified?*
    - *Are there objective criteria for justification, or is it relative?*
    - *What is the difference between internalism and externalism in justification?*
3. **Questions About Sources of Knowledge**
    - *What are the primary sources of knowledge?* (e.g., reason, perception, testimony, memory)
    - *Can we trust our senses to give us reliable knowledge?*
    - *Does divine revelation count as a legitimate source of knowledge?*
4. **Questions About Skepticism**
    - *Is true knowledge possible, or must we always remain skeptical?*
    - *Can we prove the external world exists beyond our perceptions?* (e.g., Descartes' skepticism)
    - *How do we respond to radical skepticism (e.g., the brain-in-a-vat scenario)?*
5. **Questions About Truth and Belief**
    - *What does it mean for a belief to be true?*
    - *Is truth objective or subjective?*
    - *Are some truths self-evident, or must everything be justified through reasoning?*
6. **Questions About Epistemic Systems**
    - *Which epistemological theory is most reliable?* (e.g., foundationalism, coherentism, reliabilism)
    - *How does epistemology relate to metaphysics and ethics?*
    - *Is empirical science the only valid way to obtain knowledge?*
