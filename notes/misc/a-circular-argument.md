A **circular argument** (also known as **begging the question**, or *petitio principii*) is a logical fallacy in which the conclusion is assumed within the premises, rather than being independently supported. In other words, the argument relies on its own conclusion to prove itself, making it **logically invalid** because it does not provide external justification.

## Structure of a Circular Argument

1. Premise: X is true because of Y.
2. Conclusion: Y is true because of X.

Since the premise assumes what it is supposed to prove, the argument does not provide **genuine justification** but merely **restates the conclusion in different words**.

## Examples of Circular Arguments

### Simple Example

- *"The Bible is true because it is the Word of God. We know it's the Word of God because the Bible says so."*
    - This argument **assumes** the Bible's truth rather than **demonstrating** it independently.

### Philosophical Example

- *"Reason is the best way to discover truth because reasoning tells us so."*
    - This argument assumes the **validity of reason** without offering justification beyond reason itself.

### Theological Example

- *"The Eastern Orthodox Church is the true Church because it has the correct teachings. We know its teachings are correct because it is the true Church."*
    - Here, the truth of the Church is assumed rather than argued independently.

## Why Circular Arguments Are Problematic

1. **They fail to provide external support.** A good argument should appeal to **independent** evidence or reasoning.
2. **They do not persuade an opponent.** Someone who does not already accept the conclusion will not be convinced.
3. **They can be disguised as valid reasoning.** Many circular arguments are **subtle**, using slightly different wording to appear justified.

## Are All Circular Arguments Always Fallacious?

Not necessarily. Some **transcendental** or **foundational** arguments (e.g., arguments about logic, reason, or metaphysics) must assume **some** basic principles. However, these must be **properly justified** rather than arbitrarily assumed.
