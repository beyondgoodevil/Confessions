## Why Eastern Orthodoxy Is Not Donatist

1. **Christ as the Source of Sacramental Efficacy**:
    - The Eastern Orthodox Church teaches that **Christ** is the true minister of every sacrament. The clergy serve as instruments, but the grace imparted through the sacraments comes from Christ, not from the personal holiness of the priest or bishop.
    - As St. John Chrysostom said:  
      *"It is not the priest who makes the offering, but Christ who consecrates."*
2. **Holiness of the Church Despite Sinful Members**:
    - The Church is a divine institution, but it operates within the human world, where sin exists. Orthodoxy holds that the Church remains **holy** because of the presence of the Holy Spirit, even if some of its members, including clergy, are sinful.
    - This is supported by Christ's parable of the **wheat and the tares** (Matthew 13:24–30), which teaches that the Church contains both the righteous and sinners until the final judgment.
3. **Sacramental Theology**:
    - The sacraments are **objective means of grace**, valid when performed according to the proper form, matter, and intent, within the canonical boundaries of the Church. The spiritual state of the minister does not affect the efficacy of the sacrament.
    - This is why the Church accepts the principle articulated by **St. Augustine**:  
      *"The unworthiness of the minister does not nullify the sacrament" (Ex opere operato).* Though this phrase is more associated with Western theology, the underlying principle aligns with Orthodox sacramental understanding.
4. **Repentance and Forgiveness**:
    - Orthodoxy emphasizes the importance of **repentance** and recognizes that even clergy who have fallen into sin can be restored through **penance**. Their previous administration of sacraments remains valid because the sacraments' power comes from God, not the individual.
5. **Condemnation of Schism**:
    - Donatism created a **schismatic church** that separated itself from the broader Christian community, insisting that the "true" Church consisted only of morally pure members. The Orthodox Church rejects this idea, viewing schism as a serious sin against the unity of the Body of Christ.
    - St. Cyprian of Carthage, although often cited by Donatists, is also upheld in Orthodoxy for his insistence on the Church's unity:  
      *"He who does not have the Church as his mother cannot have God as his Father."*

## Eastern Orthodox Response to Donatism

1. **Council of Carthage (411 AD)**:
    - Although this council was a Western gathering, it condemned Donatism, and its decisions were consistent with the broader patristic consensus, which Orthodoxy shares.
2. **Eastern Fathers on Unity and Sacraments**:
    - Fathers like **St. Basil the Great** and **St. John Chrysostom** taught that sacraments administered within the Church retain their power, irrespective of the personal failings of the clergy. Their focus was on maintaining the Church's unity and sacramental integrity.

## Summary

The **Eastern Orthodox Church** rejects Donatism because it wrongly ties the efficacy of sacraments to the moral state of the clergy. Orthodoxy teaches that sacraments derive their grace from **Christ**, and the Church remains holy and united despite the presence of sin within its members. This ensures that the Church is not reduced to a community of only the morally pure, which would undermine its mission of offering salvation to all.
