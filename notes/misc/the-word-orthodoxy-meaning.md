The term **Orthodoxy** is derived from two Greek words: **"ortho,"** meaning "correct" or "straight," and **"doxa,"** meaning "opinion" or "belief." Therefore, Orthodoxy literally translates to **"correct belief"** or **"right opinion."**

## Context of Use

1. **Religious Significance**: In a religious context, Orthodoxy refers to the adherence to accepted norms, doctrines, and practices of faith, particularly within Christianity. It signifies a commitment to the traditional teachings and dogmas established by the early Church and maintained through the ecumenical councils.
2. **Eastern Orthodox Church**: Specifically, the term is commonly associated with the **Eastern Orthodox Church**, which emphasizes continuity with the early Church and adherence to its teachings. The Eastern Orthodox Church considers itself the guardian of the true faith and the original traditions of Christianity.
3. **Contrast with Heresy**: Orthodoxy is often defined in contrast to heresy, which refers to beliefs or opinions that deviate from established doctrines. This delineation highlights the importance of maintaining theological purity and unity within the faith community.

## Broader Usage

The term can also apply to other contexts, such as political, philosophical, or cultural beliefs, where it denotes adherence to traditional or established norms.

## Conclusion

In summary, the name **Orthodoxy** means "correct belief" and encapsulates the commitment to uphold established doctrines and traditions, particularly in a Christian context.
