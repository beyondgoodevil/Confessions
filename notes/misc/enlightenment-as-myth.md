> "Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools, And changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man." (Rom 1:22–23)

> "The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork." (Ps 19:1)

Two German Jewish Marxists, exiled in California while Europe burned, asked how the century of science, reason and progress had produced Auschwitz. They did not answer "reason failed". They answered that **reason itself**, the reason of the Enlightenment, which treats everything as material to be mastered, had turned into a new myth. A generation later Baudrillard looked at the shopping centre and found a religion.

## 1. The Thesis (Preface, 1944/47)

- The aim was "nothing less than to explain why humanity, instead of entering a truly human state, is sinking into a new kind of barbarism."
- The two theses: "**Myth is already enlightenment, and enlightenment reverts to mythology.**"
- "The cause of enlightenment's relapse into mythology is to be sought not so much in the nationalist, pagan, or other modern mythologies… as in the fear of truth which petrifies enlightenment itself." "False clarity is only another name for myth."
- "The flood of precise information and brand-new amusements make people smarter and more stupid at once."
- They are not reactionaries. "We have no doubt… that freedom in society is inseparable from enlightenment thinking." But enlightenment must "reflect on this regressive moment" or "seal its own fate". Their aim is "a positive concept of enlightenment which liberates it from its entanglement in blind domination."

## 2. "The Concept of Enlightenment"

**Disenchantment as domination**
- Opening: "Enlightenment… has always aimed at liberating human beings from fear and installing them as masters. Yet the wholly enlightened earth is radiant with triumphant calamity."
- **Bacon** is the prophet: "the sovereignty of man lieth hid in knowledge"; we "should command [nature] by action". Knowledge's end is "operation", not "satisfaction, which men call truth". Their verdict: "**Power and knowledge are synonymous.**" "What human beings seek to learn from nature is how to use it to dominate wholly both it and human beings. Nothing else counts."
- "On their way toward modern science human beings have discarded meaning. The concept is replaced by the formula, the cause by rules and probability."
- "For the Enlightenment, anything which cannot be resolved into numbers, and ultimately into one, is illusion." "Bourgeois society is ruled by equivalence. It makes dissimilar things comparable by reducing them to abstract quantities."
- "Enlightenment stands in the same relationship to things as the dictator to human beings. He knows them to the extent that he can manipulate them."
- They cite Genesis ("let them have dominion…") beside a hymn to Zeus. "Man's likeness to God consists in sovereignty over existence, in the lordly gaze, in the command."

**Magic, sacrifice and science**
- Magic was "bloody untruth", but it still knew **specific representation**: the hind offered for the daughter, the lamb for the firstborn. Science ends this: "An atom is smashed not as a representative but as a specimen of matter… Representation gives way to universal fungibility."

**Why enlightenment becomes myth**
- Myth was already an attempt to explain and control, "to report, to name, to tell of origins". Enlightenment's "principle of immanence, the explanation of every event as repetition… is that of myth itself". "Whatever might be different is made the same."
- "**Enlightenment is mythical fear radicalized.** The pure immanence of positivism… is nothing other than a form of universal taboo. Nothing is allowed to remain outside, since the mere idea of the 'outside' is the real source of fear."
- "Animism had endowed things with souls; **industrialism makes souls into things.**"
- "Spinoza's proposition: 'the endeavor of preserving oneself is the first and only basis of virtue,' contains the true maxim of all Western civilization."
- On religion: Judaism keeps the ban on images and the unspeakable Name: "The right of the image is rescued in the faithful observance of its prohibition." That is "determinate negation". Hegel broke the ban by making the system "the absolute". Christianity, in the anti-Semitism chapter, is accused of the opposite fault: "Christ, the incarnated spirit, is the deified sorcerer… the humanization of God through Christ, is the *proton pseudos*", the first lie.

## 3. Excursus I: Odysseus, the First Bourgeois

- Homer's hero "turns out to be the prototype of the bourgeois individual". The epic is myth being organised by reason. Odysseus "controls from a distance a numerous, finely graded personnel" of servants.
- **Cunning originates in the cult.** Sacrifice was already a bargain with the gods, and a deceit of them. "Odysseus himself acts as both victim and priest." He survives by "throwing itself away in order to preserve itself".
- **Polyphemus**: Odysseus saves himself by calling himself *Outis*, "Nobody". "He declares allegiance to himself by disowning himself as Nobody." The self survives by denying itself.
- **The history of civilization**: "The history of civilization is the history of the introversion of sacrifice – in other words, the history of renunciation. All who renounce give away more of their life than is given back to them."
- **The Sirens**, "a prescient allegory of the dialectic of enlightenment". The Sirens know "all that has ever happened" and promise pleasure and the past. Odysseus stops his rowers' ears with wax, "Workers must look ahead with alert concentration", while he, the owner, listens bound to the mast. "The fettered man listens to a concert, as immobilized as audiences later, and his enthusiastic call for liberation goes unheard as applause." Art and work divide here, as the epic "already contains the correct theory".

## 4. Excursus II: Kant, Sade, Nietzsche

- Kant: enlightenment is "emergence from self-incurred minority", "understanding without direction from another". But reason so defined only systematises. "Any substantial objective which might be put forward as a rational insight is… delusion." Reason becomes "the agency of calculating thought, which arranges the world for the purposes of self-preservation."
- **Sade's Juliette** is the bourgeois subject "freed from all tutelage": rational, systematic, unrestrained. She mocks the Catholic "dead God": "God, which means eternal; death, which means not eternal."
- The verdict: "It is because they did not hush up the **impossibility of deriving from reason a fundamental argument against murder**, but proclaimed it from the rooftops, that Sade and Nietzsche are still vilified, above all by progressive thinkers."

## 5. "The Culture Industry: Enlightenment as Mass Deception"

- Film, radio and magazines form "a system". Their differences ("A and B films") "do not so much reflect real differences as assist in the classification, organization, and identification of consumers. Something is provided for everyone so that no one can escape."
- "Technical rationality today is the rationality of domination." The product is pre-digested: "the prepared ear can always guess the continuation after the first bars of a hit song and is gratified when it actually occurs."
- "**Entertainment is the prolongation of work under late capitalism.**" It "is sought by those who want to escape the mechanized labor process so that they can cope with it again."
- "The industry bows to the vote it has itself rigged." It "calls for Mickey Rooney rather than the tragic Garbo, Donald Duck rather than Betty Boop."

## 6. Baudrillard: The Consumer Society (1970)

- **"The Formal Liturgy of the Object"**: the opening part treats the profusion of goods in the shopping centre (the Parisian *drugstore*, Parly 2) as a ritual, a cult of abundance.
- **The cargo cult**: Melanesians, seeing the whites' goods arrive by plane, built model airstrips and planes to attract the "cargo". Likewise "the profusion of goods is felt as a blessing of nature, as a manna, a gift from heaven". "Consumption is governed by a form of **magical thinking**… a belief in the omnipotence of signs." "'Affluence' is, in effect, merely the accumulation of the signs of happiness."
- **Sign-value**: a washing machine is a tool, and also "an element of prestige". "It is strictly this latter field which is the field of consumption", where objects are not "linked in any sense to a definite function or need".
- **"The Finest Consumer Object: The Body"**: the cult of youth, fitness and beauty "bear witness to the fact that **the body has today become an object of salvation. It has literally taken over that moral and ideological function from the soul.**" Beauty is "a sign… that one is a member of the elect".
- **"Fun-morality"** (Riesman): the "imperative to enjoy oneself". The holiday-maker "conforms in every detail to the principles of duty, sacrifice and asceticism", a "salvation in leisure and pleasure which no one can now escape".
- **No Faust any more**: there is "no maleficent agency… like that of the Devil, with whom one could enter into a Faustian pact… since one is given these things by a beneficent, maternal ambience – the affluent society itself." Or society as a whole, a *société anonyme*, has made the pact.

## 8. Assessment

*Mine.*

**The diagnosis is largely true, and the Fathers would recognise it**
- "Knowledge is power" is the Fall stated as a programme. The serpent offered exactly this: knowledge as mastery, "ye shall be as gods" (Gen 3:5). Horkheimer and Adorno read Genesis 1:28 as a charter for domination. The Fathers read "dominion" as **royal priesthood**. Man stands in the middle of creation to name it, tend it and offer it back, as Maximus' mediator and Schmemann's *homo adorans*. "Industrialism makes souls into things" describes Schmemann's "opaque" world exactly: the world loved for itself, no longer transparent to God, eating become consumption. See [[For the Life of the World]] and [[Image and Likeness]].
- "On their way toward modern science human beings have discarded meaning. The concept is replaced by the formula." This is the loss of the *logoi*. Things have reasons in the Logos, not only measurable quantities. Nishitani makes the same point about the machine and nihilism. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]] and [[Nothingness East and West: Nishitani, Tanabe, Abe and the Christian Nihil]].
- "The impossibility of deriving from reason a fundamental argument against murder" is the moral argument stated by two atheists. Formal, instrumental reason cannot ground "thou shalt not kill". Dostoevsky's Ivan said the same, and Sade simply acted on it. Only reason that participates in the Logos, where goodness is real, can forbid. See [[The Moral Argument for God]], [[Is Ought]] and [[The Underground Man and the Death of God: Dostoevsky, Shestov, Girard]].

**Baudrillard has found the false liturgy**
- The shopping centre as cult, goods as manna and the body as "object of salvation" is secularism in Schmemann's sense: not the absence of worship but its **parody**. "Secularism is… the negation of worship" because it is worship turned toward the self. The cargo cult is idolatry in the precise biblical sense: "changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man" (Rom 1:23), and then into a washing machine.
- "Fun-morality" and the holiday as "duty, sacrifice and asceticism" show that asceticism never goes away. Man will fast and discipline himself for something. The Church's fasts are the freedom from that tyranny: discipline toward God, which frees, instead of discipline toward the image, which enslaves. See [[The Virtues and the Eight Passions]].
- "No Devil to make a pact with" is half right. The Fathers would say the anonymity is the Devil's best disguise. The passions work through *logismoi*, suggestions that feel like one's own wants. See [[How a Thought Becomes a Sin: The Stages of Temptation]].

**Where they go wrong: sacrifice, image and Incarnation**
- Their best insight, that sacrifice was already a bargain and cunning ("Odysseus… both victim and priest"), is Girard's: archaic sacrifice is the community's deceit. But they can't see that the Gospel **ends** this logic. Christ's sacrifice is not cunning, not a trade with the gods, not self-preservation by self-denial. It is the opposite of Odysseus' "Nobody": "I lay down my life… No man taketh it from me" (John 10:17–18). The history of civilisation may be "the introversion of sacrifice", but the Cross is its **unmasking**: the innocent victim who forgives. See [[Girard's Scapegoat Mechanism: Sacrifice, Myth and the Gospels]].
- They call the Incarnation the *proton pseudos* and Christ the "deified sorcerer", as if Christianity brought God near in order to *manipulate* Him. That is backwards. Magic is man trying to command the divine. The Incarnation is God freely giving Himself, unmanipulable, "made flesh" in order to be crucified by men who did try to control Him. The Eucharist is not *do ut des* ("I give that you may give") but thanksgiving for a gift already given.
- Their choice between the Jewish **ban on images** and Christian "idolatry" leaves out the Church's actual answer. The Seventh Council held that the Incarnation *founds* the image: the invisible God became visible, so may be depicted. "The right of the image is rescued" not by prohibition but by the Word becoming flesh. The icon is the opposite of the culture industry's image. It does not flatter the viewer or prolong his work. It looks back at him and calls him to change. See [[Theology of the Icon]] and [[Icons: Biblical Justification]].

**The dead end of "determinate negation"**
- Their hope is enlightenment that reflects on itself: reason criticising reason. But negation without a positive ground cannot stop. Every image is "script" to be cancelled, and the critic is left with the Frankfurt School's famous melancholy and no "outside". Their own sentence condemns them: "Nothing is allowed to remain outside, since the mere idea of the 'outside' is the real source of fear." The Christian claim is that the Outside has come in: the Logos is not a system to be negated but a Person to be met. Apophaticism is real, and God is beyond every image, but it ends in communion, not in permanent suspicion. See [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]].

**What I take from it**
- Read the culture industry chapter and *The Consumer Society* as two hard Lenten sermons by unbelievers. They see clearly what the Fathers called the passions and Schmemann called secularism. Then go to the Liturgy, where the world is offered back and given again as gift, and where the Sirens' song is answered not by wax or ropes but by another song: Orpheus becomes Christ, whose music outsings the Sirens. That was the Fathers' own reading of the mast as the Cross. See [[How Philosophers Saved Homer: Allegory from the Stoics to the Byzantines]].
