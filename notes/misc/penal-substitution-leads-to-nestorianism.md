---

1. **Splitting the Trinity:** Penal substitution suggests that Christ, as the Son, bears the punishment for sin from the Father. This implies a division in the relational dynamics of the Trinity, as it posits that the Father’s justice requires punishment on the Son, creating a scenario where the Father and the Son are seen as acting in opposition. Such a view can undermine the unity and co-equality of the Trinity, as it portrays the Father as the one inflicting punishment while the Son is the one suffering it.
2. **Connection to Nestorianism:** Nestorianism is the heresy that posits a division between the divine and human natures of Christ, suggesting that these natures exist as ==two separate persons== rather than a single person with two natures. In a similar manner, penal substitution can imply a split in the Trinity by suggesting that the divine nature of the Son is distinct from His human nature when it comes to experiencing punishment. This separation can lead to a misunderstanding of the unity of Christ’s person, paralleling the Nestorian view that fails to see Christ’s divine and human natures as perfectly united.
