### World Council of Churches (WCC):

- **Founded**: 1948 in Amsterdam, the Netherlands.
- **Membership**: The WCC is composed of over 350 Christian denominations, including most of the Protestant, Anglican, and Orthodox churches, but notably excluding the Roman Catholic Church (although it maintains a close working relationship with the WCC, and individual Catholics can be involved in its activities).
- **Purpose**: The WCC aims to foster Christian unity and cooperation in order to promote peace, justice, and development globally. It also focuses on a variety of social and humanitarian issues, including poverty, human rights, and environmental protection.
- **Notable Members**:
    - **Eastern Orthodox Churches** (such as the Greek Orthodox Church, Russian Orthodox Church, and others).
    - **Protestant Churches** (such as the Lutheran, Reformed, Baptist, and Methodist traditions).
    - **Anglican Communion**.
    - Some **Oriental Orthodox Churches** (such as the Ethiopian Orthodox Church and the Armenian Apostolic Church).
- However, the **Roman Catholic Church** is not a member, although it participates in certain activities and dialogues. The **Assyrian Church of the East** (Nestorian) is not a member, either.

### Other Ecumenical Organizations:

1. **The Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity (Catholic Church)**:  
   Established by the Vatican to foster dialogue and cooperation with non-Catholic Christian groups, especially after the Second Vatican Council (1962-1965). Though not an ecumenical organization in itself, it works closely with the WCC and other groups to promote unity, particularly with Orthodox and Protestant denominations.
2. **The Anglican Communion**:  
   While not strictly an ecumenical organization, the Anglican Communion is a loose association of national and regional churches with a shared Anglican heritage. Many Anglican provinces have ecumenical dialogues with other Christian communities and are members of the WCC.
3. **The National Council of Churches (NCC)**:  
   A U.S.-based organization, which represents many of the Protestant denominations, and has the goal of promoting Christian unity and addressing social justice issues. It's an ecumenical body similar in many ways to the WCC but with a focus on U.S. churches.
4. **Lutheran World Federation (LWF)**:  
   An ecumenical organization representing Lutheran churches worldwide. It is involved in dialogue with other Christian traditions and is a member of the WCC.
5. **World Methodist Council (WMC)**:  
   An international organization that promotes unity among Methodist and related Christian denominations. It also participates in ecumenical dialogue with other Christian bodies.
6. **World Alliance of Reformed Churches (WARC)**:  
   This is a global ecumenical group representing Reformed Christian churches. It advocates for the unity of the Reformed tradition and works alongside other Christian ecumenical bodies.
7. **The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople**:  
   The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople, though not an organization in itself, plays a leading role in ecumenical activities within the Eastern Orthodox Church. The Patriarch is involved in global ecumenical dialogues with both Protestant and Catholic communities and is a member of the WCC.
8. **The Fellowship of Evangelical Churches (FEC)**:  
   An evangelical Christian association that aims for cooperation and dialogue with other evangelical churches, but with a more conservative stance on certain theological issues compared to the mainline Protestant ecumenical movements.
9. **World Evangelical Alliance (WEA)**:  
   Although not traditionally associated with the more liberal or progressive ecumenical movements, the WEA represents a large number of evangelical Christian denominations. It promotes global cooperation and dialogue on social issues and unity among evangelical Christians.
