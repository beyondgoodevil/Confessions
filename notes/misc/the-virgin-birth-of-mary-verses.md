## 1. Isaiah 7:14

"Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign. Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."

- This Old Testament prophecy foretells the virgin birth and is often cited in relation to the Nativity.

## 2. Matthew 1:18-25

The image quotes the full passage in a modern copyrighted translation; in outline:

- Mary, betrothed to Joseph, is found to be with child from the Holy Spirit before they come together.
- Joseph, a just man, resolves to divorce her quietly.
- An angel of the Lord tells him in a dream not to fear taking Mary as his wife, since the child is conceived of the Holy Spirit; he is to be named Jesus, "for he will save his people from their sins."
- This fulfils the prophecy that the virgin shall conceive and bear a son called Immanuel, "God with us".
- Joseph takes Mary as his wife, and knows her not until she has given birth to a son, whom he names Jesus.

- This passage provides the New Testament account of Mary's virgin birth and emphasizes the fulfillment of Isaiah's prophecy.

## 3. Luke 1:26-38

The image quotes the full passage in a modern copyrighted translation; in outline:

- In the sixth month Gabriel is sent to Nazareth, to a virgin betrothed to Joseph of the house of David; her name is Mary.
- He greets her as favored; she is troubled, and he tells her not to be afraid.
- She will conceive and bear a son named Jesus, the Son of the Most High, who will receive David's throne and reign without end.
- Mary asks, "How will this be, since I am a virgin?"
- The Holy Spirit will come upon her and the power of the Most High overshadow her; the child will be called holy, the Son of God.
- Elizabeth, called barren, has also conceived in her old age, for nothing is impossible with God.
- Mary answers that she is the servant of the Lord: let it be according to his word.

- This passage details the Annunciation, where the angel Gabriel announces to Mary that she will conceive Jesus through the Holy Spirit, emphasizing her virginity.

## Summary

These passages collectively affirm the doctrine of the Virgin Birth, highlighting its significance in the Christian faith as a miraculous event that underscores Jesus's divine nature and fulfillment of prophetic Scripture.
