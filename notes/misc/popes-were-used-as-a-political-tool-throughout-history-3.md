## 5. The Counter-Reformation and the Catholic Monarchs (16th–17th Century)

- **Spain and the Papacy**
    - **Philip II of Spain (Reigned 1556–1598)**
        - Used the Pope to justify his **wars against Protestantism** (e.g., in the Netherlands).
        - Claimed to be the **defender of Catholicism**, using the Pope’s authority to support his empire.
- **France and the Papacy**
    - **Louis XIV (Reigned 1643–1715)**
        - Forced **Pope Innocent XI** to recognize **Gallicanism**, which gave the French king more power over the Church in France.

## 6. The Napoleonic Era (19th Century) – Napoleon and the Pope

- **Napoleon Bonaparte (Reigned 1804–1815)**
    - In **1804**, forced **Pope Pius VII** to come to Paris for his **coronation as Emperor**.
    - Instead of allowing the Pope to crown him, Napoleon **placed the crown on his own head**, symbolizing that he ruled by his own authority, not by papal approval.
    - Later **imprisoned the Pope (1809–1814)** and used the Church as a political tool.

## 7. The Modern Era – The Papacy and Secular Politics (19th–20th Century)

- **Italian Unification (19th Century)**
    - The Papal States were taken by **King Victor Emmanuel II** of Italy in **1870**, ending the Pope’s political power over central Italy.
- **World War II and the Vatican**
    - **Pope Pius XII** was accused of trying to **maintain neutrality** while dealing with **Hitler and Mussolini**.
    - Both **Nazis and Fascists** tried to use the Pope for their own political gains.
