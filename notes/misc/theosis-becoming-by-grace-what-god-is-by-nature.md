The site has verse notes and a list of Fathers on deification but no main page. This is it. **Theosis** (θέωσις), "deification", also *theopoiesis*: being freed from sin and united with God, beginning now and completed in the resurrection. For the Orthodox this isn't one doctrine among others. It *is* salvation.

## 1. The One-Line Version

> "He was made man that we might be made God." (St. Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* 54)

- Lowercase "god", if you like. Not God by nature. Man can't become God *ontologically*, or another god.
- The usual formula: we become **by grace** what God is **by nature**.
- See [[St. Athanasius, On the Incarnation: Main Argument]], [[Church Fathers That Taught the Doctrine of the Deification of Human Nature Through the Incarnation]].

## 2. Scripture

- **2 Peter 1:4**: "partakers of the divine nature". The key text. See [[2 Peter 1:3-4: Theosis]].
- **John 17:21-23**: "that they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us... the glory which thou gavest me I have given them."
- **Psalm 82:6** / **John 10:34**: "I have said, Ye are gods."
- **2 Cor 3:18**: "changed into the same image from glory to glory." See [[2 Corinthians 3:18: We Move From Glory to Glory]].
- **1 John 3:2**: "we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is."
- **Phil 2:13**: God works in you "both to will and to do". (Theosis is synergy. See [[Synergy: Grace and Free Will]].)
- **Matt 17:2**: the Transfiguration, human nature shining with divine light. See [[The Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor]].

## 3. Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *hamartia* | "missing the mark": the *state* of sin, not just a sinful act (*hamartema*) |
| *zoe* vs *bios* | divine, everlasting life vs ordinary biological life. Theosis is receiving *zoe* |
| image / likeness | the image can't be lost; the likeness is what theosis grows into. See [[Image and Likeness]] |
| *podvig* (Russian) | ascetic struggle |
| *theoria* | vision / contemplation of God |
| *apotheosis* | deification **in God's essence**. Rejected as heresy |

## 4. Three Stages

This scheme isn't in the OrthodoxWiki article. It's the classic patristic one (Dionysius, Maximus, the Philokalia), and modern writers like Romanides and Met. Hierotheos Vlachos make it central.

| Stage | Greek | What happens | Mainly through |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purification** | *katharsis* | of the heart from the passions | repentance, fasting, the commandments, confession |
| **Illumination** | *photismos* | of the *nous*; unceasing prayer in the heart | prayer, the Jesus Prayer, watchfulness |
| **Glorification** | *theosis* proper | vision of the uncreated light | grace, given, not achieved |

- Dionysius has the same triad as purification, illumination, perfection, and maps it onto the orders of clergy. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]].
- Not strictly one after the other. More like overlapping. Purification never stops.
- See [[The Virtues and the Eight Passions]] for what purification is purifying *from*, and [[Nous]] for what illumination illumines.

## 5. Essence and Energies

The doctrine that makes theosis possible without pantheism.

- God's **essence** can't be known or shared by any creature, ever.
- God's **energies** (His life, glory, grace, light) are God Himself, uncreated, and *can* be shared.
- So union with God is real (not just moral imitation) and not absorption (we don't become the divine essence).
- St. Gregory Palamas defended exactly this against Barlaam. See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]], [[God's Uncreated Energies]], [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]].

Without the distinction you're stuck with two bad options: theosis is a metaphor (Western scholastic tendency), or we merge into God (pantheism, Neoplatonism).

## 6. More Than Getting Back to Eden

- Many Fathers: theosis goes *beyond* Adam's original state. Because divine and human nature are united in one Person in Christ, a closer union is now possible than Adam had.
- Some (Maximus especially, and some modern Orthodox theologians) hold the Incarnation would have happened **even without the Fall**, since union with God was the point of creation from the start.
- Irenaeus's recapitulation lies underneath: Christ goes through every stage of human life and heals it. See [[Doctrine of Recapitulation]].

## 7. How It Happens in Practice

- **Church and sacraments.** Baptism, Chrismation, above all the Eucharist: literally taking in the deified humanity of Christ. See [[The Eucharist in Type and Fulfilment]].
- **Prayer of the heart.** "Pray without ceasing" (1 Thess 5:17). The Philokalia.
- **Ascesis**: fasting, vigil, almsgiving. Body included, because the body is to be deified too.
- **Love of neighbour.** Not optional extra. "He that loveth not his brother... cannot love God" (1 John 4:20).
- **Not imitation only.** Imitating Christ from outside isn't theosis. Living from His life is.

## 8. Comparisons

**The West**

- Largely neglected the language, though not entirely. Aquinas speaks of beatitude as participation in divinity (*ST* III.1.2). Anglicans (Andrewes, the Wesleys' hymns, Pusey), and the Finnish Lutheran school (Mannermaa) argue Luther meant something like theosis by justification.
- Usual Protestant word: **sanctification**. Legal justification first, then progressive holiness. In Orthodoxy there's no such split: being saved *is* being healed and united.
- Methodist "entire sanctification": a sinless life attainable here. The Council of Vienne (1311) condemned the claim that one can become sinless and unable to grow further. Orthodoxy says theosis never ends: *epektasis*, endless progress (St. Gregory of Nyssa).

**Mormon "exaltation"**

- Sounds similar ("as God now is, man may become", Lorenzo Snow). Isn't.
- Mormonism: each person's core "intelligence" is uncreated and eternal; exalted humans may create worlds; God was once a man.
- Orthodoxy: one uncreated God; everything else created from nothing; creatures glorified by grace, forever creatures. The Creator–creature distinction is never crossed.
- Mormons sometimes quote the Fathers on deification. The same Fathers reject everything else in their theology, Trinity first.

## 9. Things I Want to Work Out

- How exactly do the energies relate to grace as Western theology uses the word ("created grace")? This seems to be the real point of difference.
- If theosis is endless (*epektasis*), is there any "rest" in the age to come? Maximus: "ever-moving rest". Read *Ambigua* on this.
