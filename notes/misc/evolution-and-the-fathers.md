The Orthodox Church has never issued a dogmatic statement on evolution. What's dogma: God created everything from nothing, freely, through the Son and in the Spirit; man is made in God's image; man fell and death came in through sin; Christ restores. *How* creation unfolded in time is open, though the death-before-the-Fall question pushes hard on it.

The site already has the philosophy-of-science angle: [[Evolution Can Be Understood as a Paradigm-Level Theory]], [[The Underdetermination of Data Thesis in the Context of Evolution]]. This note is about the theology.

## 1. How the Fathers Read Genesis

Not one way. Most read it on several levels at once.

| Father | Approach |
|---|---|
| **St. Basil**, *Hexaemeron* (c. 370) | mostly literal: real days, real kinds. Warns against allegorists who turn water into something else: "when I hear 'grass', I think of grass." But he isn't doing science: Genesis teaches what's useful for the soul, not cosmology |
| **St. Gregory of Nyssa**, *On the Making of Man*, *Hexaemeron* | God created all things together in **potency** in one instant; they unfolded in order. The "days" mark a logical sequence of appearance |
| **St. Augustine**, *On the Literal Meaning of Genesis* | creation was **instantaneous** (Sir 18:1, "he that liveth for ever created all things in general"); the six days are an order of angelic knowledge, not 24-hour periods. God placed **"seminal reasons"** (*rationes seminales*) in matter, which develop over time. Warns Christians not to talk nonsense about nature and make the faith look ridiculous to unbelievers |
| **Origen** | heavily allegorical: how could there be "days" before the sun (day 4)? |
| **St. Ephrem**, **St. John Chrysostom** | literal, against allegory |
| **St. Maximus**, **St. Gregory Palamas** | the *logoi*: each creature has its principle in the Word; creation is moving toward an end |

Common ground: Genesis is true; it's about God, man and the purpose of creation; it isn't a science textbook. They didn't have the question we have.

## 2. The Days and the Age

- **Day 1 to 3 have no sun.** Origen and Augustine both pointed this out. Basil says the light of day 1 is a different light.
- "One day is with the Lord as a thousand years" (2 Pet 3:8, Ps 90:4). Some Fathers (Irenaeus, Justin) used it for the 6,000-year world-week, not for long creation days.
- **The seventh day has no evening** (Gen 2:2-3). The Fathers note it: God's rest continues. And the **eighth day** is the Resurrection. So the "days" have a theological shape.
- Most Fathers assumed a young earth (c. 5,500 years before Christ by the Septuagint chronology). But it wasn't a doctrine they argued for; it was the history they had.

## 3. The Real Problem: Death Before the Fall

This is the hard part for any Orthodox view of evolution.

**The Orthodox view of death**
- Wis 1:13: "**God made not death**: neither hath he pleasure in the destruction of the living."
- Wis 2:23-24: "God created man to be immortal... Nevertheless **through envy of the devil came death into the world**."
- Rom 5:12: "by one man sin entered into the world, and **death by sin**." See [[Ancestral Sin vs Original Sin]].
- Rom 8:20-21: "**the creature was made subject to vanity**, not willingly... because the creature itself also shall be delivered from **the bondage of corruption**." See [[Romans 8:18-24: Creation Corruption]].
- 1 Cor 15:26: death is "the last enemy". Not part of God's good design.

**The problem**: evolution needs hundreds of millions of years of death, predation and extinction *before* humans. If death is the result of man's sin, how?

**Responses**

| Response | Idea | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| **No death before the Fall** (Seraphim Rose) | reject evolution: creation was incorrupt until Adam; animals didn't die | conflicts with geology and the fossil record |
| **Only human death** | Rom 5:12 means *human* death; animal death was always natural | Rom 8 speaks of the whole creation in bondage; and the Fathers (e.g. Basil) speak of animals as not carnivorous in Paradise (Gen 1:30) |
| **Retroactive Fall** (Dembski, some Orthodox) | God, foreseeing the Fall, made the world as it would need to be for fallen man. The Fall's effects reach backward | can sound arbitrary |
| **Fall out of time** | the Fall happened in a different mode of existence (paradise isn't in our history); the fallen world, *as we see it*, is entirely fallen, with its whole past | Maximus says Adam fell "at the moment he was created": creation and Fall are almost simultaneous for us. Harder to square with a historical Adam |
| **Paradise as a place within** | the world outside was already mortal; Adam in paradise was protected and called to extend paradise outward (Gen 1:28, "subdue"), and failed | close to some Fathers' view of paradise as a special place (Gregory of Sinai, Ephrem); keeps Rom 8 as frustration of what creation *should have* become |

See [[Death and Corruptibility as Privations of Life and the Ability to Sin]], [[The Fall of Humanity and Its Consequences]].

## 4. Modern Orthodox Positions

| Position | Who | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| **Creationist** | Fr. Seraphim Rose, *Genesis, Creation and Early Man* | evolution is a philosophy (materialism, progress), not just science. The Fathers' consensus is literal and incompatible with it. Creation was incorrupt before Adam |
| **Open / agnostic** | many bishops; the Church as such | no dogma on the mechanism; reject only materialist evolution (no God, no purpose) |
| **Theistic evolution** | Theodosius Dobzhansky (an Orthodox Christian; "Nothing in biology makes sense except in the light of evolution"); Fr. Doru Costache; Gayle Woloschak | evolution is how God brought creatures into being; the *logoi* unfold through it. Man is set apart not by biology but by the image and God's call |

- Rose's argument partly answers the "underdetermination" point: the same data, read through different metaphysics. See [[The Underdetermination of Data Thesis in the Context of Evolution]].
- Dobzhansky's point is the opposite: science shows *how*; faith shows *why*.

## 5. What Isn't Negotiable

Whatever view of the mechanism:
- God creates **from nothing**, freely. Not emanation, not eternal matter.
- Creation is **good** (Gen 1:31).
- Man is made in the **image of God**: a person, called to communion. Not just a clever animal. See [[Image and Likeness]].
- There's a real **Fall**, and death is an **enemy**, not God's tool. Christ destroys it.
- Creation has a **purpose**: deification, the new creation. Unguided, purposeless evolution (as metaphysics) is out.

## 6. Notes

*Mine.*

- Augustine is the Father most often cited for an old earth, but his reason (instantaneous creation) is the opposite of evolution's long time. Seminal reasons fit development, though, and he's clear that God wrote the "book of nature" too.
- Gregory of Nyssa's "everything created in potency" is the closest patristic idea to development.
- The real collision isn't Genesis's days but Rom 8 and Wis 1:13: is death natural or an intruder? I think the "Fall out of time" and "paradise as a place" answers are more patristic than the "only human death" one.
- Rose is right that "evolution" in popular use usually means a materialist worldview. A Christian can't accept *that*, whatever they think of common descent.
