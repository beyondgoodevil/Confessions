## Essence vs. Energies in the Context of Jesus Walking on Water

1. **Divine Essence:**
    - The essence of God refers to what God is in Himself—His incomprehensible and unchanging nature. In Christian theology, particularly within the Eastern Orthodox tradition, God's essence is transcendent and remains beyond human comprehension.
2. **Divine Energies:**
    - Energies are the ways in which God interacts with creation and reveals Himself to humanity. They are uncreated and allow for God's presence and action in the world while still being distinct from His essence.

## Key Problem: Temporal vs. Eternal Actions

1. **If Essence Equals Energies:**
    - If there is no distinction between essence and energies, then Jesus' walking on water would be seen as a necessary expression of His divine essence. This raises the question of whether the act is inherently **eternal** rather than a **temporal** event that occurred during His earthly ministry.
2. **Eternal Action:**
    - If walking on water is a reflection of the divine essence, it implies that the miracle is timeless and perpetually present, rather than a specific act that occurred at a defined moment in history. This leads to the idea that the miracle is always "happening" in a sense, which contradicts the understanding of it as a concrete historical event.
3. **Implications for Historical Events:**
    - The Gospel accounts present miracles, including Jesus walking on water, as specific actions in time. If these actions are eternal due to their connection to God's essence, it undermines the reality of the Incarnation, suggesting that Jesus' historical actions are merely manifestations of an eternal state rather than genuine interventions in human history.
4. **Authenticity of the Incarnation:**
    - The Incarnation emphasizes that Jesus, while fully divine, entered into human experience and engaged with the temporal world. If His actions, like walking on water, are seen as eternal expressions of divine essence, it raises questions about the authenticity of His human experience and the true nature of His interaction with creation.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, within the context of the essence-energy distinction, if there is no separation between God's essence and His energies, it implies that Jesus' walking on water is not just a historical miracle but an eternal act. This presents significant theological challenges regarding the nature of divine actions, the authenticity of Christ's human experience, and the significance of miracles as specific historical events. Maintaining the distinction allows for a coherent understanding of Christ's actions as genuine interventions in the temporal realm while affirming His divine nature.
