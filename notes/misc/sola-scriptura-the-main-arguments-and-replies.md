The formal principle of the Reformation. The site has a lot of notes on oral tradition and the canon; this pulls the arguments together in one place, with the Protestant case put fairly.

## 1. What It Claims

**Westminster Confession 1.6**: "The whole counsel of God concerning all things necessary for His own glory, man's salvation, faith and life, is either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men."

**Sola vs solo**
- ***Sola scriptura*** (classical Reformers, Mathison): Scripture is the only **infallible** rule; creeds, councils, Fathers are real but subordinate authorities, judged by Scripture.
- ***Solo scriptura*** (some evangelicals, "me and my Bible"): Scripture is the *only* authority of any kind. The Reformers would've rejected this too.

The claim is about **sufficiency** (everything necessary is there), **perspicuity** (clear enough for an ordinary reader on what's necessary), and **supremacy** (it judges everything else).

## 2. Protestant Arguments and Replies

**2 Tim 3:16-17**: "**All scripture is given by inspiration of God**, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness: That the man of God may be **perfect, throughly furnished unto all good works**."

| Protestant | Reply |
|---|---|
| Scripture is inspired and makes us complete; so it's sufficient | "profitable" isn't "sufficient". And "scripture" here is the OT (the NT wasn't complete): if this proves sufficiency, it proves the OT alone is sufficient |
| | the verse before: "**continue thou in the things which thou hast learned**... knowing **of whom thou hast learned them**" (3:14). Timothy is pointed to Paul's oral teaching too. And 2:2: "the things that thou hast heard of me... **commit thou to faithful men**" |
| | James 1:4 says patience makes us "perfect and entire, wanting nothing". Not sufficiency of patience alone |

**Mark 7:8-13** / Matt 15: "**laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men**... **making the word of God of none effect through your tradition**."

| Protestant | Reply |
|---|---|
| Jesus condemns tradition | He condemns *human* traditions that contradict God's command (Corban). Paul *commands* apostolic tradition: 2 Thess 2:15. The issue is which tradition, not tradition as such. See [[Overview of Where Jesus Refers to Oral Tradition]] |
| | and Jesus Himself accepts oral tradition: Matt 23:2-3 ("Moses' seat"), Matt 2:23 ("He shall be called a Nazarene", in no OT book) |

**Acts 17:11**: the Bereans "searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so."

| Protestant | Reply |
|---|---|
| they tested apostolic teaching against Scripture | they were testing whether Jesus was the Messiah against OT prophecy. They then accepted Paul's oral preaching as God's word (1 Thess 2:13). See [[1 Thessalonians 2:13: Paul Teaches Oral Tradition]] |

**Isa 8:20**: "**To the law and to the testimony**: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them."

| Protestant | Reply |
|---|---|
| everything is tested by Scripture | the Orthodox agree that true tradition doesn't contradict Scripture. It doesn't follow that Scripture is the *only* rule |

**Rev 22:18**: "**If any man shall add unto these things**, God shall add unto him the plagues."

| Protestant | Reply |
|---|---|
| don't add to Scripture | refers to the book of Revelation. Deut 4:2 says the same of the Law, and then came the Prophets and the NT |

**1 Cor 4:6**: "**not to think of men above that which is written**."

| Protestant | Reply |
|---|---|
| don't go beyond Scripture | the context is about pride among teachers; "that which is written" probably refers to the OT texts Paul just cited. Very unclear |

**Historical argument**: the Fathers constantly appeal to Scripture as the final authority (Athanasius: "the holy and inspired Scriptures are sufficient for the declaration of truth"; Cyril of Jerusalem: "do not believe me unless you receive the proof from the Scriptures").

- **Reply**: the same Fathers appeal to unwritten tradition. Basil, *On the Holy Spirit* 27: some doctrines "we have from written teaching, others from the tradition of the apostles, handed down in a mystery". Athanasius appeals to "the tradition, teaching and faith of the Catholic Church from the beginning". "Scripture is sufficient" in the Fathers means Scripture *as read in the Church*. See [[Paul Commands to Keep the Oral Traditions as Well as the Written Ones]].

## 3. Arguments Against

**1. The canon**
- Scripture doesn't list its own contents. Which books are Scripture is something known only by tradition.
- So sola scriptura relies on a tradition to get started. Either that tradition is reliable (and then why not the rest?) or the canon is uncertain. Sproul: the canon is a "fallible collection of infallible books."
- See [[Canon of the Bible: Its History]], [[Factors That Played a Role to Determine the Canon of the Bible]].

**2. It's self-refuting**
- Sola scriptura isn't taught in Scripture. No verse says "Scripture alone is the rule of faith". So by its own standard it should be rejected.
- Protestant reply: it's a "good and necessary consequence" of 2 Tim 3:16 etc. (sec. 2: contested).

**3. Scripture points to the Church and tradition**
- 1 Tim 3:15: "**the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth**."
- 2 Thess 2:15: "**hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle**." See [[2 Thessalonians 2:15: Stand Fast and Hold the Tradition Which You Were Taught by Word or Epistle]].
- 1 Cor 11:2: "keep **the ordinances** (*paradoseis*, traditions), as I delivered them to you."
- Matt 18:17: "tell it unto **the church**: but if he neglect to hear the church..."
- John 16:13: the Spirit "will guide you into all truth". See [[Jesus Promises That the Holy Spirit Will Guide the Church]].
- John 21:25 / 20:30: not everything is written. See [[John 20:30: Not Everything Jesus Did Has Been Written Down]].

**4. The first Christians didn't have it**
- For decades there was no NT; for centuries no fixed canon; most Christians couldn't read and had no Bibles. The Church lived by apostolic tradition, preaching and liturgy.

**5. Interpretation**
- 2 Pet 3:16: in Paul's letters "are **some things hard to be understood**, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest."
- Acts 8:30-31: "Understandest thou what thou readest? And he said, **How can I, except some man should guide me?**"
- 2 Pet 1:20: "no prophecy of the scripture is of any **private interpretation**."
- In practice: thousands of Protestant denominations disagreeing on baptism, the Eucharist, salvation, church order, all claiming Scripture. Without an authoritative interpreter, the "only infallible rule" gives no infallible answers.
- Arius, Nestorius etc. all argued from Scripture. The councils settled it not by more proof-texts but by the Church's tradition of reading them. See [[Protestant Heresy Falls Into Heresies Dealt With in the Ecumenical Councils]].

**6. Historical novelty**
- No Christian group held it in this form before the late Middle Ages (Wycliffe, Hus as forerunners). St. Vincent of Lérins (434) already answers: everyone quotes Scripture, so we hold "what has been believed **everywhere, always, and by all**."

## 4. The Best Protestant Replies

- **"Tradition" in Catholic/Orthodox practice has added things** (the papacy, indulgences, the Immaculate Conception). Sola scriptura was a reaction to real abuses. *Orthodox answer*: those are Roman developments, not apostolic tradition. Orthodoxy rejects them too, on the basis of tradition.
- **The Church was also wrong** (Arian councils, Robber Synod). So the Church isn't automatically infallible. *Answer*: a council is recognised as ecumenical by the Church's reception, not by its claim. The Church as a whole can't fail (Matt 16:18), even if parts do.
- **Canon**: God's providence gave the canon, not the Church's authority (books are self-authenticating to the Spirit-filled community). *Answer*: then why did it take centuries and why do Protestants have a different OT canon from every ancient Church? See [[Argument for the Validity of the EO Canon of Scriptures]].
- **Perspicuity** only claims that what's *necessary for salvation* is clear, not everything. *Answer*: Protestants still disagree on baptism, the Eucharist and whether one can lose salvation: things that seem necessary.

## 5. The Orthodox Alternative

*Mine.*

- Not "Scripture **plus** tradition" as two sources (the Tridentine-Catholic way of saying it). **Scripture is within Tradition**: the written part of the one Tradition handed down by the apostles, read by the Church that wrote and canonised it.
- Tradition is the **life of the Holy Spirit in the Church** (Lossky). It includes Scripture, the creeds, councils, Fathers, liturgy, icons, canons, the saints.
- Scripture has **primacy** within it: nothing in tradition contradicts it, and the Fathers are judged by it. That's what's right in sola scriptura.
- The test isn't "what does my reading of the text say?" but "how has the Church always read it?" (Vincent's canon; Irenaeus against the Gnostics: the heretics have the same texts, but arrange the mosaic of a king into a dog).
- See [[The Holy Spirit in Orthodox Theology]] sec. 5.
