Scientific scandals highlight how corruption, manipulation, and bias can undermine the reliability of scientific claims. Here are some key examples:

1. **Piltdown Man (1912)**: A deliberate hoax presented as the missing link between humans and apes, later proven false, highlighting the dangers of manipulated evidence.
2. **Andrew Wakefield and MMR Vaccine (1998)**: A fraudulent study linking the MMR vaccine to autism, which caused public panic and led to a decline in vaccinations, later retracted and discredited.
3. **The Lysenko Affair (1930s-1960s)**: Soviet biologist Trofim Lysenko promoted politically motivated pseudoscience, stifling legitimate research and causing agricultural failure.
4. **Tuskegee Syphilis Study (1932-1972)**: U.S. researchers deliberately withheld treatment from African American men to study syphilis, causing deaths and lasting harm to public trust in medicine.
5. **ClimateGate (2009)**: Leaked emails suggested some climate scientists manipulated data to support global warming, raising concerns about transparency in environmental research.
6. **Power Posing Study (2010)**: Claims that body posture affects confidence were discredited after failed replication and problems with the data, showing the danger of premature conclusions.
7. **Jan Hendrik Schön (2002)**: A physicist fabricated data in high-profile publications, highlighting how prestige can shield fraudulent research.
8. **Hwang Woo-suk Stem Cell Scandal (2004-2005)**: A stem cell breakthrough was later revealed to be based on fabricated data, damaging credibility in biomedical research.
9. **Haruko Obokata (2014)**: A claimed stem cell discovery was fabricated, undermining trust in groundbreaking claims in the field.
10. **"Science for Sale" Scandals**: Corporate-funded studies, particularly in pharmaceuticals, have led to biased research, such as in the opioid crisis, highlighting the danger of financial conflicts of interest.

These scandals show that scientific consensus doesn't always guarantee truth and emphasize the need for transparency, ethical standards, and critical examination in research.
