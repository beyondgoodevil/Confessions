**Epistemology** is the branch of philosophy that studies **knowledge**, its **nature**, **origin**, and **limits**. It seeks to answer fundamental questions such as:

1. **What is knowledge?** (Definition and criteria)
2. **How do we acquire knowledge?** (Sources and methods)
3. **What are the limits of human knowledge?** (Skepticism and certainty)
4. **How can we justify our beliefs?** (Standards of evidence and rationality)

## Key Concepts in Epistemology

1. **Justified True Belief (JTB)** – The classical definition of knowledge:
    - A belief must be **true**.
    - The person must **believe** it.
    - The belief must be **justified** (i.e., supported by valid reasoning or evidence).
2. **Sources of Knowledge**
    - **Empiricism**: Knowledge comes from sensory experience.
    - **Rationalism**: Knowledge comes from reason and innate ideas.
    - **Revelation**: In Orthodox Christianity, divine revelation is the highest source of knowledge.
3. **Skepticism vs. Certainty**
    - **Skepticism** questions whether true knowledge is possible.
    - **Foundationalism** asserts that some beliefs are self-evident and form a secure base for further knowledge.
4. **Epistemic Justification**
    - **Coherentism**: Beliefs are justified if they fit within a coherent system.
    - **Foundationalism**: Some beliefs (axioms) are self-justified and support others.

## Epistemology and Worldviews

A person’s **epistemology** determines how they evaluate **truth claims**. In **Eastern Orthodox thought**, epistemology is grounded in **divine revelation**, the **Church’s teaching**, and **spiritual experience**, rather than pure rationalism or empiricism.
