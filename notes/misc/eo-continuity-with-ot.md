## 1. The Church as the Fulfillment of Israel

The Eastern Orthodox Church believes that it is the New Israel, the continuation of God's covenant people:

- **Galatians 6:16:** Paul refers to the Church as the "Israel of God."
- **1 Peter 2:9-10:** The Church is described as *"a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God's special possession."* These titles were applied to Israel in the OT (Exodus 19:6), showing that the Church inherits Israel's role.
- **Romans 11:17-24:** Paul explains that Gentiles are grafted into the olive tree (Israel), signifying that the Church is the continuation of God's covenant people, combining Jewish and Gentile believers.

## 2. Liturgical Worship in Continuity with the Old Testament

The EO Church's liturgical practices, including its structure, sacrifices (spiritual, not blood), and use of sacred space, are rooted in OT worship.

### A. The Temple and the Liturgy

- **Exodus 25-30:** God gives Israel detailed instructions for constructing the Tabernacle and its worship, including the use of incense, priestly garments, and sacred objects.
- The EO Church mirrors these principles in its liturgical worship:
    - **Incense** (Malachi 1:11, Revelation 8:3-4): Incense was used in the Temple and is now used in the EO liturgy to signify prayers rising to God.
    - **Sacred Vestments** (Exodus 28:2): Orthodox priests wear liturgical vestments, as the OT priests did, signifying the sanctity of their role.
    - **Icons and Sacred Art:** The EO Church views icons as a continuation of sacred imagery, akin to the cherubim and other depictions in the Temple (Exodus 25:18-22).

### B. Sacrifice Fulfilled in Christ and the Eucharist

- **Hebrews 9-10:** Christ fulfills the sacrificial system of the OT through His once-for-all sacrifice.
- In the EO Eucharist, the Church offers the *bloodless* sacrifice of bread and wine, which becomes the Body and Blood of Christ, fulfilling the OT sacrifices:
    - **Malachi 1:11:** *"For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering."* This prophecy is fulfilled in the Eucharist, celebrated universally in the Orthodox Church.
    - **1 Corinthians 10:16-17:** The Eucharist is described as a participation in the Body and Blood of Christ.

### C. Sabbath and Feasts

- The EO Church celebrates a liturgical calendar rooted in OT practice:
    - Weekly worship echoes the Sabbath, now on Sunday, the *Lord's Day* (Acts 20:7, Revelation 1:10).
    - Feasts like Pascha (Easter) fulfill the Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7).
    - **Leviticus 23** prescribed holy days, which find their fulfillment in the Christian liturgical cycle.

## 3. Priesthood and Continuity of Covenants

The priesthood in the EO Church is a continuation of the priestly role seen in the OT, but fulfilled in Christ's eternal High Priesthood and mediated through the Church.

### A. Christ as the High Priest

- **Hebrews 7:17:** Christ is the eternal High Priest in the order of Melchizedek, a priesthood greater than Aaron's.
- The EO Church's priesthood participates in Christ's priesthood:
    - **John 20:21-23:** Christ gives the Apostles the authority to forgive sins, continuing the priestly role of mediation.
    - **1 Peter 2:5, 9:** All believers are called a "royal priesthood," but specific ministers are ordained for sacramental duties.

### B. Covenantal Continuity

- **Jeremiah 31:31-34:** God promises a *new covenant* written on the hearts of His people. This covenant is fulfilled in the Church through the sacraments (e.g., Baptism, Eucharist).
- **Matthew 26:28:** Jesus institutes the new covenant at the Last Supper, which the Church continues to proclaim through the Eucharist.

## 4. The Eastern Orthodox Church and the Ancient Covenants

The EO Church fulfills the OT covenants through Christ, who establishes a universal covenant for all nations:

- **Abrahamic Covenant (Genesis 12:3):** The promise that all nations would be blessed through Abraham is fulfilled in Christ and the Church, which includes both Jews and Gentiles (Galatians 3:7-9).
- **Davidic Covenant (2 Samuel 7:12-16):** Christ, as the Son of David, reigns eternally, and the Church is His Kingdom (Luke 1:32-33, Matthew 28:18-20).

## 5. Scriptural Evidence for Orthodox Practices

The EO Church maintains practices that align with Scripture:

- **Acts 2:42:** The early Church devoted itself to "the apostles' teaching, fellowship, the breaking of bread, and prayers," which matches the Orthodox emphasis on liturgy, sacraments, and prayer.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:15:** Paul exhorts believers to hold to the traditions handed down, which the EO Church preserves.

## Summary

The Eastern Orthodox Church's claim to continuity with the Old Testament is deeply rooted in Scripture. It sees itself as the fulfillment of God's covenant with Israel, with Christ as the High Priest, and the Church as the New Israel. Its liturgical practices echo and fulfill OT worship, while the sacraments and priesthood demonstrate the fulfillment of the OT covenants through Christ. This continuity is affirmed by prophecies, apostolic tradition, and the unbroken practice of worship dating back to the early Church.
