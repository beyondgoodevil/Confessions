title: Church Fathers That Taught the Doctrine of the Deification of Human Nature Through the Incarnation

summary: Seven Fathers, from Irenaeus to Photios, on deification through the Incarnation.
---

1. **St. Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 296–373):**
    - St. Athanasius famously articulated the doctrine of deification in his work *On the Incarnation*. He stated, "For the Son of God became man so that we might become god." This highlights the belief that the Son assumed human nature to elevate it to divine life.
2. **St. Irenaeus of Lyons (c. 130–202):**
    - St. Irenaeus emphasized that the Word of God became flesh for the salvation of humanity. He spoke of the necessity of the Incarnation for restoring humanity, emphasizing that by becoming human, Christ allows us to partake in divine grace.
3. **St. Gregory of Nazianzus (c. 329–390):**
    - St. Gregory, also known as Gregory the Theologian, contributed to the understanding of the Incarnation and deification. He stated, "What is not assumed is not healed," meaning that by assuming human nature, Christ heals and elevates humanity.
4. **St. Cyril of Alexandria (c. 376–444):**
    - St. Cyril elaborated on the union of divine and human natures in Christ. He taught that the Logos (Word) became flesh to restore humanity to its original glory and that through the Incarnation, believers can achieve deification.
5. **St. John of Damascus (c. 676–749):**
    - In his *Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, St. John of Damascus discussed the significance of the Incarnation in terms of deification, affirming that Christ's assumption of human nature makes it possible for humans to become partakers of the divine nature.
6. **St. Maximus the Confessor (c. 580–662):**
    - St. Maximus further developed the concept of theosis and emphasized the transformative power of Christ's Incarnation, teaching that believers can become united with God through grace, facilitated by the divine energies.
7. **St. Photios the Great (c. 810–893):**
    - St. Photios affirmed the teachings of the earlier Fathers on the Incarnation and deification, emphasizing that through participation in the divine grace provided by Christ, humanity can attain deification.
