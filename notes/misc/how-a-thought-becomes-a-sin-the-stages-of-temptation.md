---

The Fathers of the desert and the **Philokalia** describe sin not as a sudden act but as a **process** that begins with a passing thought (*logismos*) and, if it is not resisted, ends in a settled **passion**. Knowing the stages lets a person stop the process **early**, while resisting is still easy. Here's how it works:

### 1. The Scriptural Basis

"But each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death." (James 1:14-15)

- **Significance:** St. James already describes sin as a sequence: desire, conception, birth, growth, death. The Fathers fill in the stages in detail.
- "Whoever looks at a woman to lust for her has already committed adultery with her in his heart." (Matthew 5:28) Sin can be completed **inwardly**, before any outward act.

### 2. The Stages

St. John Climacus (*Ladder*, step 15) and other Philokalic Fathers, such as St. Philotheos of Sinai, list the stages as follows:

1. **Provocation** (*prosbolē*): a thought or image simply appears in the mind. This is **not a sin**. Even Christ was tempted in the desert.
2. **Coupling** (*syndiasmos*): the mind begins to converse with the thought, entertaining it and turning it over.
3. **Consent** (*synkatathesis*): the will agrees with the thought and takes pleasure in it. Here **sin begins**, even if no outward act follows.
4. **Captivity** (*aichmalōsia*): the thought now carries the mind away against its will, so that it returns again and again and is hard to resist.
5. **Passion** (*pathos*): through repeated consent, the sin becomes a **habit** rooted in the soul, a second nature that drives the person.

St. John Climacus also names **struggle** (*palē*): the battle between the mind and the thought, which can be fought at any stage and earns either a crown or a punishment.

### 3. Where to Stop It

- **At the Provocation**: the Fathers say the easiest victory is at the very first stage, before any conversation begins. Christ answered each temptation in the desert at once, with Scripture (Matthew 4:1-11).
- **The Babylonian Infants**: "Happy the one who takes and dashes Your little ones against the rock!" (Psalm 137:9). The Fathers read this spiritually: the "little ones" of Babylon are evil thoughts while they are still small, and the rock is Christ. Kill them while they are infants.
- **Watchfulness** (*nepsis*): "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life." (Proverbs 4:23) Guarding the heart means noticing thoughts as they arrive.
- **The Name of Jesus**: the Jesus Prayer is the chief weapon against thoughts, calling on Christ the moment a provocation appears (see [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]]).
- "Bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5): the goal is to take the thought captive before it takes the mind captive.

### 4. Comparison With Augustine

St. Augustine (*On the Lord's Sermon on the Mount*, book 1) gives a shorter, three-stage account:

1. **Suggestion**: the thought is presented.
2. **Delight**: the soul takes pleasure in it.
3. **Consent**: the will agrees, and the sin is complete.

- **Significance:** East and West agree that temptation itself is not sin and that **consent** is the decisive point. The Eastern account adds the later stages, captivity and passion, because its main concern is **healing** habits, not only judging acts.

### 5. Why It Matters

- **Freedom From Scrupulosity**: a person troubled by bad thoughts can know that provocations are not sins. What matters is the response.
- **Understanding Passions**: a passion is consent repeated until it becomes a habit. Healing therefore works backwards, by refusing consent until the habit weakens.
- **Repentance**: when a person has fallen, the stages show where the fall began and where to watch next time (see [[Repentance as Metanoia: A Change of Mind, Not a Payment]]).

### Conclusion

The Fathers teach that sin grows in stages: a thought appears, the mind converses with it, the will consents, the mind is taken captive, and finally a passion takes root. Only with consent does sin begin, and the earlier the thought is rejected, the easier the victory. Through watchfulness, Scripture and the Jesus Prayer, the Christian learns to dash the "infants of Babylon" against the rock, which is Christ, before they grow.
