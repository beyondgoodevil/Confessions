The term **hypostasis** (Greek: ὑπόστασις) is used in the New Testament in a few key passages, where it typically refers to the underlying reality or essence of something. Here are the notable instances:

1. **Hebrews 1:3:**
    - **"He is the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature (hypostasis), and he upholds the universe by the word of his power."**
    - In this context, **hypostasis** refers to the essence or nature of Christ as the representation of God's being.
2. **Hebrews 11:1:**
    - **"Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen."** (The term "assurance" in some translations is rendered as "hypostasis.")
    - Here, **hypostasis** implies a foundation or assurance, highlighting the certainty of faith in things hoped for.
3. **2 Corinthians 9:4:**
    - **"Otherwise, if some Macedonians come with me and find that you are not ready, we would be humiliated— to say nothing of you—for being so confident."** (In some translations, "confidence" may be linked to **hypostasis**.)
    - This passage uses the term in a context related to confidence and assurance.
4. **Hebrews 3:14** (in some translations):
    - **"For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end."** (The term "confidence" can also relate to **hypostasis** in certain translations, indicating a firm assurance in one's faith.)

While **hypostasis** is not used extensively in the New Testament, its appearances are significant in discussing the nature of faith, the essence of Christ, and the assurance that believers have in their relationship with God.
