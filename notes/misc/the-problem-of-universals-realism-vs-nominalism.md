Socrates and Plato are both men. Is there something real, "human nature", that they share? Or only two individuals and a word? That's the problem of universals: do general things (*humanity*, *redness*, *justice*) exist in reality or only in minds and language.

## 1. Terms

- **Particular**: an individual, one place, one time. *This* man, *this* apple.
- **Universal**: what many particulars have in common, what a general word names. *Man*, *red*.

The question is what a universal *is*, if anything.

## 2. Positions

| Position | Universals exist... | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Platonic (extreme) realism | on their own, apart from things and minds, as Forms | *ante rem*, before the thing |
| Aristotelian (moderate) realism | only in particulars, as their shared nature | *in re*, in the thing |
| Conceptualism | only as concepts in the mind | |
| Nominalism | nowhere. Only names | *post rem*, after the thing |

Notes on each:

- **Plato**: particulars are what they are by *participating* in Forms. Classic problem: the Third Man (if man and the Form of Man share something, do you need a third Form?).
- **Aristotle**: the form is real but in the thing. The mind *abstracts* it.
- **Conceptualism**: nothing common in things, only resemblances the mind groups. (Abelard is usually put here. Feels unstable: what makes the grouping correct?)
- **Nominalism**: Roscelin (11th c.), universals are "a breath of the voice". **Ockham** (14th c.) gave it its lasting form.

## 3. The Christian Synthesis

- Put the Forms in the mind of God. Then universals exist all three ways:
    - *before* things, as divine ideas
    - *in* things, as natures
    - *after* things, as our concepts
- Eastern version: the **logoi**. Each nature has its uncreated logos in the Logos (St. Maximus). See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].
- St. John of Damascus, *Dialectica*, gives the vocabulary: *ousia* / *physis* = what's common. *Hypostasis* = the particular.

Perl's reading of Plato says the Forms were never "outside" intellect to begin with. See [[The Forms and the Demiurge]].

## 4. Why Theology Cares

This looks like an abstract medieval quarrel until you see what hangs on it.

1. **Trinity.** Three hypostases, one essence. If a common nature is only a name, you have three gods. Roscelin was accused of exactly this.
2. **Christology.** Christ assumed *human nature*, not just one man's existence. "What is not assumed is not healed" (St. Gregory the Theologian) needs there to be a real common nature that got healed.
3. **Adam and Christ.** "As in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive" (1 Cor 15:22). Presupposes real solidarity.
4. **Ethics.** No real human nature → no natural law, no stable account of what's good for man. Good = whatever God, or society, happens to command.
5. **Knowledge.** Science studies kinds. If kinds are fictions, laws of nature describe nothing.

## 5. What Nominalism Led To

- **Voluntarism.** No natures to ground goodness, so moral law rests on bare divine will. Sharpens the Euthyphro problem. See [[The Euthyphro Dilemma and the Orthodox Answer]].
- **Church as a collection.** Individuals who happen to agree, not a real body.
- **Legal salvation.** Righteousness can be *imputed* by declaration if there's no shared nature to heal. Late-medieval nominalism is the background the Reformers were trained in (Luther called Ockham his master).
- **Scepticism.** Hume on causation and the self follows from allowing only particular impressions. See [[Critique of Empiricism]].

Caveat: "nominalism caused the Reformation and modernity" is a big-picture story and historians argue about it. As a logical connection it holds up. As history it's probably too tidy.

## 6. The Transcendental Point

- The nominalist argues using universals: "all", "every", "argument", "true". He relies on logic holding in every case.
- "Only particulars exist" is itself a universal claim.
- Universal, unchanging, immaterial realities need a ground that's universal, unchanging, immaterial. On the Orthodox view: the Logos. See [[TAG]].

Open question: modern "trope theory" and resemblance nominalism claim to do the work without universals. Haven't looked at whether the transcendental argument touches them.
