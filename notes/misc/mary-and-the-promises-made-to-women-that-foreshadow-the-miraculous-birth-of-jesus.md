1. Eve:

   "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel."

   **Genesis 3:15**

   This passage is often referred to as the Protoevangelium, the first announcement of the gospel. It speaks of a future battle between the seed of the woman and the serpent, which Christians interpret as being fulfilled in Christ, born of the Virgin Mary.

2. Sarah:

   "And the LORD said unto Abraham, Wherefore did Sarah laugh, saying, Shall I of a surety bear a child, which am old? Is any thing too hard for the LORD?"

   **Genesis 18:13-14**

   Sarah's miraculous conception of Isaac at an old age symbolizes God's power to fulfill His promises, which culminates in the birth of Jesus through Mary, emphasizing that nothing is impossible for God.

3. Rachel:

   "And when Rachel saw that she bare Jacob no children, Rachel envied her sister; and said unto Jacob, Give me children, or else I die."

   **Genesis 30:1**

   Rachel's longing for children and eventual birth of Joseph points to God's plan to fulfill His promises through miraculous births, culminating in Mary's role in the birth of Jesus.

4. Hannah:

   "And she was in bitterness of soul, and prayed unto the LORD, and wept sore. And she vowed a vow, and said, O LORD of hosts, if thou wilt indeed look on the affliction of thine handmaid, and remember me, and not forget thine handmaid, but wilt give unto thine handmaid a man child."

   **1 Samuel 1:10-11**

   Hannah's prayer for a child and the miraculous birth of Samuel foreshadows God's intervention in human history through Mary, who bears the Son of God.

5. Isaiah's Prophecy:

   "Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel."

   **Isaiah 7:14**

   This direct prophecy speaks of the virgin birth, which is ultimately fulfilled in Mary when she conceives Jesus by the Holy Spirit.

### Fulfillment in Mary:

- Annunciation:

  "And the angel said unto her, Fear not, Mary: for thou hast found favour with God. And, behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and bring forth a son, and shalt call his name Jesus."

  **Luke 1:30-31**

  Mary fulfills the prophecy of Isaiah by conceiving Jesus while remaining a virgin, completing the promises made to women in the Old Testament.

- Emphasis on Faith:

  "And Mary said, Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it unto me according to thy word. And the angel departed from her."

  **Luke 1:38**

  Mary's acceptance of God's plan signifies her faith and obedience, paralleling the faith exhibited by the women in the Old Testament.

These verses illustrate how the promises made to women in the Old Testament converge and find their ultimate fulfillment in the virgin birth of Jesus through Mary, establishing her as a pivotal figure in God's redemptive plan.
