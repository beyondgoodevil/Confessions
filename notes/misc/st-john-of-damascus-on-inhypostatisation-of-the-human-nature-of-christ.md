---
title: St. John of Damascus on inhypostatisation of the human nature of Christ
tags: [theology, christology]
summary: Christ's human nature has no hypostasis of its own but subsists in the Person of the Logos.
---

St. John of Damascus, in his theological works, particularly in his *Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, addresses the concept of *inhypostatisation* in relation to the human nature of Christ. The term refers to the idea that the human nature of Jesus, while truly human, is assumed by the divine Logos (the Second Person of the Trinity) and is given personal subsistence in the Logos Himself. This means that the human nature of Christ does not have an independent hypostasis (personhood) of its own but is always united to the divine Person of the Son of God.

St. John of Damascus explains that Christ's human nature, though fully human, finds its true identity and reality in the Person of the Logos. The human nature does not exist apart from the divine Word, and it is through this union that the two natures—divine and human—are perfectly united without confusion or separation. Thus, the human nature of Christ is "inhypostatised," meaning that it is subsumed into the person of the Logos and given personal existence through Him.

This is a crucial point in Christological doctrine, affirming that while Christ is fully divine and fully human, His humanity does not have a separate person but is assumed into the divine Logos, making the union of the two natures inseparable yet distinct. This was an important development in defending the orthodox understanding of the union of the divine and human in the person of Christ, especially in response to early heresies like Nestorianism, which suggested that Christ's two natures were separate persons.

In summary, St. John of Damascus teaches that Christ's human nature, though complete and real, is not independent but is given its personal existence through the divine Logos, an essential element of the Orthodox understanding of the hypostatic union.
