The dominant philosophy from the mid-3rd to the mid-7th century. "Neoplatonism" is a modern label. They called themselves Platonists, and aimed at one synthesis of Plato, Aristotle, Pythagoras and Greek religion (but not Epicureans or Stoic materialism).

Background I keep needing for [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]], [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]], [[Emanationism]] and [[Neoplatonic Idea of Divine Simplicity]].

## 1. Two Basic Ideas

1. **Mind before matter.** Consciousness (*nous*) is more real than the physical world, and prior to it. A form of idealism.
2. **One first principle.** Everything depends on one source: "the First", "the One", "the Good".

The problem that follows: how does a physical, many-sided world come from one principle that's neither physical nor many?

## 2. Who

| Who | Dates | Main works | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammonius Saccas | 3rd c. | none | teacher of Plotinus in Alexandria (perhaps of Origen too) |
| **Plotinus** | 204/5–270 | *Enneads* (6 × 9 treatises) | founder. Taught in Rome |
| **Porphyry** | c. 234–305 | *Life of Plotinus*, *Isagoge*, *Against the Christians* | edited the *Enneads*; the *Isagoge* became the medieval logic primer |
| **Iamblichus** | c. 245–325 | *On the Mysteries* | made ritual (theurgy) central |
| **Proclus** | c. 412–485 | *Elements of Theology*, *Platonic Theology*, commentaries | head of the Athenian Academy ~50 years; systematiser |
| Damascius | c. 462–540s | *Problems and Solutions on First Principles* | last head of the Academy |
| Simplicius | 6th c. | commentaries on Aristotle | preserved much of the Presocratics |
| John Philoponus | 6th c. | *Against Proclus on the Eternity of the World* | Christian; attacked the eternal world |

**529**: Justinian's law closes the Academy in Athens. Philosophy continues in Alexandria and Constantinople, taught by Christians.

## 3. The Three Hypostases

*Enneads* V.1, "On the Three Primary Hypostases".

| | What it is | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **The One** (the Good) | absolute unity, beyond being and thought | can't strictly be described. Not even "thinks" or "is" |
| **Intellect** (*Nous*) | the One's first "outer activity"; pure thinking, containing the Forms | turning back to know its source, it becomes two (thinker and thought), and the categories arise. Knower and known are one activity |
| **Soul** (*Psyche*) | Intellect's outer activity; carries the Forms down into the world | the World Soul animates the cosmos; individual souls are parts of it |

Below Soul: **Nature** (unconscious life, a dim contemplation), then **matter**.

So everything natural has something divine in it, and the material world is good and worth reverence. Not Gnostic.

Compare Perl on Plato's demiurge: intellect and forms as one. See [[The Forms and the Demiurge]].

## 4. Procession and Return

- **Procession** (*proodos*) and **return** (*epistrophe*). Proclus adds **remaining** (*mone*): every cause stays in itself, goes out, and its effect turns back.
- Principle: every activity has an inner side and an outer effect. The outer effect isn't intended; it's a by-product, already contained in the cause.
- **Not emanation in a crude sense**. No stuff flows out, nothing is manufactured. The sun-and-light image is admitted to be only an image.
- **Eternal**, not an event in time. The Neoplatonists weren't creationists: the world always proceeds from the One.

## 5. Matter and Evil

- **Plotinus**: matter is where the outflow runs out, like darkness at the edge of light. Formless, passive. And evil comes "from below": souls turning their attention down to matter instead of up (*Enneads* I.8).
- **Proclus** disagreed. Evil isn't matter. It's "parasitic" on good: a by-product of aims that miss (*On the Existence of Evils*). And the soul can be really corrupt, not just distracted.
- Dionysius took Proclus's version almost word for word, and through him it reached Aquinas. See [[The Problem of Evil and Theodicy]].

## 6. The Ascent

- Man contains every level (body, soul, intellect, and something of the One): a microcosm who "came from above".
- Goal: return. *Eudaimonia* understood as **becoming like god**.
- The virtues matter mainly as purifications that free the soul for union.
- Plotinus's last words (in Porphyry): "try to bring back the god in you to the divine in the All."
- The famous end of the *Enneads*: "the flight of the alone to the Alone".

## 7. Theurgy

| | Route to the divine |
|---|---|
| Plotinus, Porphyry | **philosophy**: contemplation. The highest part of the soul never fully fell |
| Iamblichus, Proclus | **ritual** (theurgy, "god-work"): the soul descended fully into the body, so it can't think its way back; it needs divinely given rites |

Proclus: theurgy has three levels; the highest is union with the One through negation, silence and faith (*pistis*).

That's where Dionysius gets the word "theurgy", but he turns it into God's work in the sacraments. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]] sec. 5.

## 8. Proclus's System

*Elements of Theology*: 211 propositions, proved in order like Euclid, starting from the One.

- **Triads** everywhere:
    - remaining – procession – return
    - **unparticipated – participated – participating** (the Form itself, its presence in things, the things)
    - **Being – Life – Intellect** (a refinement of Plotinus)
    - limit – unlimited – mixed (from Plato's *Philebus*)
- **Henads**: divine unities between the One and the many; Proclus identifies them with the traditional gods.

Dionysius's ordered ranks and triads clearly come from here. What he drops: the henads as gods, and the chain of intermediaries. For him each thing depends on God directly.

## 9. The Fathers and Neoplatonism

*Mine.*

**Contact was close.** The Cappadocians studied in Athens. Augustine read "the books of the Platonists" (Plotinus and Porphyry) and said they brought him close to the Trinity. By 500 the Alexandrian lecture halls were mostly Christian.

**What was taken**

- God beyond being and beyond names: the apophatic way.
- Creation as good, and every creature having something of God in it.
- Ascent and return; purification, illumination, union.
- Evil as privation.
- The vocabulary: *hypostasis*, *energeia*, *ousia*, procession.

**What was refused**

| Neoplatonism | Christianity |
|---|---|
| the world proceeds **necessarily** and **eternally** | God creates **freely**, **from nothing**, with a beginning |
| the One doesn't know or love the world | God loves the world, providentially, personally |
| hierarchy of lesser principles between the One and us | one Creator; everything depends on Him directly |
| the second and third principles are **lower** than the first | Son and Spirit **equal** to the Father (Arius was the Neoplatonist reading, and Nicaea rejected it) |
| salvation by philosophy (or ritual) | salvation by Christ, given, not achieved |
| union = merging into the One | union by grace, persons remaining; essence never shared |
| Incarnation absurd: the One can't become body | the Word became flesh |
| body as something to escape | resurrection of the body |

- **Emanation and the Trinity**: the danger was reading the Father → Son → Spirit as One → Intellect → Soul: descending, each less. That's subordinationism. See [[Emanationism]], [[Monarchical Trinitarianism]].
- **Origenism**: pre-existent souls falling from contemplation into bodies is the Plotinian story. Condemned in 553. Maximus reverses it. See [[Thomism and the Originist Problematic]].
- **Simplicity**: the Neoplatonic One is the model for absolute divine simplicity. The Orthodox charge is that Western scholasticism imported it. See [[Neoplatonic Idea of Divine Simplicity]], [[The Problem of the One and the Many]] sec. 6.

**Later**

- Islam: the "Theology of Aristotle" (really Plotinus), al-Farabi, Avicenna.
- Renaissance: Plethon, Bessarion, Ficino. See [[Emergence of Magic and Neoplatonism in the Renaissance]].
- Idealism: the Cambridge Platonists, Hegel, Schelling.
