In theological discussions, particularly in the context of Christology, the idea that "will is proper to nature" means that the will is associated with the nature in which it resides. This is important for understanding the two natures of Christ—His divine nature and His human nature—and how each nature possesses its own will, yet both are united in the one divine Person of the Son.

1. **Will and Nature**: In this view, each nature has its own will. The divine nature of Christ has the divine will, which is perfectly aligned with the will of the Father, as the divine will is always in perfect unity with the will of God. The human nature of Christ has a human will, which, while fully human, remains in perfect submission to the divine will because of the union with the divine Person of the Son.
2. **Christ's Two Wills**: The human will of Christ, being fully human, possesses the same kind of will as any human being. However, due to Christ's unique nature as both fully God and fully man, His human will is not opposed to His divine will. Instead, it is in perfect harmony with the divine will, as demonstrated in Christ's prayer in the Garden of Gethsemane ("Not My will, but Yours be done" – Luke 22:42), where His human will submits to the divine will.
3. **Patristic Support**: This understanding is based on the teachings of the Church Fathers, particularly in response to the heresy of Monothelitism, which posited that Christ had only one will. The Orthodox view, following the councils (such as the Sixth Ecumenical Council, 680-681), affirmed that Christ has two distinct wills corresponding to His two natures—one divine and one human—each will being proper to its respective nature.
4. **Avoiding Confusion**: The doctrine that will is proper to nature helps to preserve the integrity of both the divine and human natures of Christ. If the wills were not tied to the natures, it would lead to confusion or even the potential collapse of Christ’s full humanity and full divinity. By attributing the will to the respective nature, the distinctiveness of each nature is preserved while maintaining the unity of the person of the Son.

In summary, "will is proper to nature" means that Christ’s divine nature has a divine will, and His human nature has a human will, with both wills being in perfect harmony in the one divine Person of Christ. This helps preserve the fullness of both His divinity and humanity without confusion or division.
