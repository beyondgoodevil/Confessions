The break in communion between Rome and the Eastern patriarchates. Textbooks say **1054**. Really that year is one incident in a drift that took centuries and wasn't settled until well after.

## 1. Causes

### Doctrine

**Filioque**

- West added "and the Son" to the Creed where it says the Spirit proceeds from the Father.
- Two separate Eastern objections, and it helps to keep them apart:
    1. the doctrine (it compromises the monarchy of the Father)
    2. the *act*: changing an ecumenical creed with no council

**Papal authority**

- East: Bishop of Rome is first among equals, primacy of honour inside a conciliar Church.
- Rome: more and more, universal jurisdiction over every bishop.

Of the two, the papacy is the deeper one, I think. The Filioque could only be added unilaterally *because* of the claim about authority.

### Liturgy and discipline

- Eucharistic bread: unleavened in the West (azymes), leavened in the East
- Clerical celibacy required in the West, married priests in the East
- Rome's Saturday fast
- None of these caused the split. They turned into ammunition once things had already gone wrong.

### Politics and culture

- **Language.** Latin West and Greek East stopped being able to read each other. (Augustine barely had Greek. By the 9th c. almost nobody in Constantinople read Latin.)
- **Two empires.** Charlemagne crowned in 800 = a rival to the Roman emperor in Constantinople.
- **Turf.** Illyricum, southern Italy, the Bulgarian mission.

## 2. Timeline

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 330 | Constantinople founded as New Rome |
| 381 | 2nd Ecumenical Council: Constantinople second in honour after Rome |
| 451 | Chalcedon canon 28 confirms it. Rome objects |
| 484–519 | Acacian Schism, first long break |
| 589 | Toledo III: Filioque in the Creed in Spain |
| 692 | Council in Trullo condemns several Roman customs. Rome doesn't accept all its canons |
| 800 | Leo III crowns Charlemagne |
| 809 | Aachen endorses the Filioque. Leo III refuses to add it at Rome (has the Creed engraved without it on silver shields) |
| 863–867 | Photian controversy: Nicholas I vs St. Photios. Bulgaria, Filioque |
| 879–880 | Council of Constantinople restores Photios, forbids additions to the Creed |
| 1014 | Filioque sung in the Creed at Rome for the first time |
| 1054 | Humbert's bull on the altar of Hagia Sophia. Cerularius and synod anathematise the legates |
| 1098 | Council of Bari. Crusades put Latin bishops in Eastern sees |
| 1182 | Massacre of the Latins in Constantinople |
| 1204 | Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople, installs a Latin patriarch |
| 1274 | Lyons II: union accepted by the emperor, rejected in the East |
| 1438–39 | Ferrara-Florence: union signed. St. Mark of Ephesus refuses. East repudiates it |
| 1453 | Fall of Constantinople |
| 1484 | Synod of Constantinople formally rejects Florence |
| 1965 | Paul VI and Athenagoras lift the 1054 anathemas. Communion not restored |

Things that stand out: 809 (a pope *refusing* the Filioque in the Creed) and 879–80 (a council with Roman legates present forbidding additions). The East counts that council as authoritative.

## 3. What Actually Happened in 1054

- Leo IX sends legates to Constantinople under Cardinal **Humbert of Silva Candida**. Background: rows over Latin churches in Constantinople and Greek churches in southern Italy (Normans).
- Talks with Patriarch **Michael Cerularius** go nowhere. Neither man was a diplomat.
- 16 July: Humbert puts a bull of excommunication on the altar of Hagia Sophia during the Liturgy and walks out.
- Catch: Leo IX had **died in April**. So did the legates still have authority?
- The bull also, bizarrely, accuses the Greeks of *removing* the Filioque from the Creed.
- Patriarch's synod anathematises the legates in reply.
- Both excommunications were of persons, not whole Churches. Plenty of people then didn't think it was final. Communion carried on in various places for a long time.

## 4. Why It Stuck

- **1204** did more than 1054 to make it real for ordinary people. Latin soldiers sacking the city and its churches isn't a thing you forget.
- Lyons and Florence were pushed by emperors who needed Western troops. The faithful and the monks rejected both.
- Later Roman definitions widened it: Immaculate Conception (1854), papal infallibility and universal jurisdiction (Vatican I, 1870).

## 5. The Orthodox Reading

- Not "the Church split in two". The Orthodox Church holds that it carried on in the faith of the councils, and that Rome left by changing the Creed and claiming an authority the first millennium didn't know.
- Reunion = agreement in faith. Not a formula that papers over the disputed points (which is what Florence tried).
