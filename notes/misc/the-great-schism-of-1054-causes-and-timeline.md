The **Great Schism** is the break in communion between the Church of Rome and the Eastern patriarchates. It is dated to **1054**, but that year was one episode in an estrangement that grew over centuries and only became fixed afterwards.

## 1. Causes

### Doctrinal

1. **The Filioque**:
    - The West added "and the Son" to the Creed's statement that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father.
    - The East objected on two grounds: the doctrine itself, which compromises the monarchy of the Father, and the act of altering an ecumenical creed without a council.
2. **Papal Authority**:
    - The East held the Bishop of Rome to be first among equals, with a primacy of honour within a conciliar Church.
    - Rome increasingly claimed universal jurisdiction over all bishops.

### Liturgical and Disciplinary

- **Bread of the Eucharist**: unleavened in the West (azymes), leavened in the East.
- **Clerical celibacy**: required of priests in the West; married priests permitted in the East.
- **Fasting**: the Roman Saturday fast, rejected in the East.
- These were not causes of schism in themselves, but they became accusations once relations had broken down.

### Political and Cultural

- **Two languages**: Latin West and Greek East gradually ceased to read each other.
- **Two empires**: the crowning of Charlemagne in 800 created a rival to the Roman emperor in Constantinople.
- **Jurisdiction**: disputes over Illyricum, southern Italy and the mission to Bulgaria.

## 3. What Happened in 1054

- Pope Leo IX sent legates to Constantinople, led by Cardinal **Humbert of Silva Candida**, amid disputes over Latin churches in Constantinople and Greek churches in southern Italy.
- Negotiations with Patriarch **Michael Cerularius** failed.
- On 16 July 1054 Humbert placed a bull of excommunication against the patriarch on the altar of Hagia Sophia. Leo IX had died in April, so the legates' authority was doubtful.
- The patriarch's synod answered by anathematising the legates.
- The excommunications were aimed at persons, not at whole Churches, and many at the time did not regard the break as final.

## 4. Why the Break Became Permanent

- **1204** did more than 1054 to make the schism real for ordinary believers: Latin soldiers sacked the city and its churches.
- The reunion councils of **Lyons** and **Florence** were driven by imperial need for military help and were rejected by the Eastern faithful.
- Later Roman definitions widened the gap: the Immaculate Conception (1854) and papal infallibility and universal jurisdiction (Vatican I, 1870).

## 5. The Orthodox View

- The Church did not split into two halves. The Orthodox Church holds that it continued in the faith of the councils, and that Rome departed by altering the Creed and claiming an authority unknown to the first millennium.
- Reunion requires agreement in faith, not a formula that leaves the disputed points unresolved.
