Fideism and Evidentialism are two opposing positions in the philosophy of religion regarding the role of reason and evidence in faith.

## 1. Fideism: Faith Over Reason

Fideism (from Latin *fides*, meaning "faith") is the view that religious belief is independent of reason and evidence, or even that it is opposed to them. According to fideists, faith is the primary or sole foundation for religious truth, and attempts to justify belief through reason or empirical evidence are either unnecessary or even harmful.

**Key Features of Fideism:**

- **Faith is self-authenticating** – It does not need rational justification.
- **Reason is limited or corrupt** – Human reasoning is flawed due to sin or finite understanding.
- **Divine revelation is paramount** – Knowledge of God comes from revelation, not from rational arguments.

**Notable Fideists:**

- **Tertullian** – "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?" (suggesting faith is separate from philosophy).
- **Blaise Pascal (to some extent)** – He emphasized the limitations of reason and the importance of the "heart" in knowing God.
- **Søren Kierkegaard** – He emphasized the "leap of faith" as necessary for true belief.

## 2. Evidentialism: Reason and Faith

Evidentialism is the view that belief in God should be based on evidence and rational arguments. It holds that religious faith should be supported by logic, historical evidence, and empirical data.

**Key Features of Evidentialism:**

- **Faith should be rational** – Religious belief should be based on good reasons.
- **Evidence is necessary** – Arguments from natural theology (cosmological, teleological, moral, etc.) justify belief.
- **Historical and empirical proofs matter** – For example, historical evidence for Christ’s resurrection supports Christianity.

**Notable Evidentialists:**

- **Thomas Aquinas** – Used rational arguments (e.g., the Five Ways) to prove God's existence.
- **William Paley** – Advocated the teleological argument (watchmaker analogy).
- C.S. Lewis – Used moral and historical arguments to defend Christianity.

## 3. How They Relate and Conflict

- Fideism rejects evidentialism because it sees faith as independent of or superior to rational justification.
- Evidentialism rejects fideism because it insists that belief should be based on demonstrable reasons rather than blind faith.
- Some thinkers (e.g., Augustine) seek a middle ground: faith and reason working together, where faith seeks understanding (*fides quaerens intellectum*).

## 4. The Eastern Orthodox View

Eastern Orthodoxy rejects both extreme fideism and extreme evidentialism. Faith and reason are seen as complementary rather than opposing forces. The Church Fathers emphasize that:

- Faith is not irrational but is grounded in divine revelation.
- Reason is a gift from God but is insufficient without divine illumination.
- True knowledge comes through spiritual experience (theoria), not just logical proofs.

Thus, Orthodoxy sees faith as a living relationship with God rather than a mere intellectual assent based on evidence alone.
