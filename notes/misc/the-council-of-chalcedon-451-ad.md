The Council of Chalcedon (451 AD) defined the doctrine of Christ's two natures, fully divine and fully human, united in one person (hypostasis) without confusion, change, division, or separation. This is known as the Chalcedonian Definition. Specifically, it affirmed:

1. **Two Natures (Dyophysitism):** Christ has two complete and distinct natures—divine and human.
2. **Union in One Person:** These two natures are united in the one person (hypostasis) of Jesus Christ.
3. **Without Confusion or Change:** The divine and human natures are not mixed or altered into something different.
4. **Without Division or Separation:** The natures remain distinct but cannot be divided or separated within the person of Christ.

This council was convened to address controversies surrounding the teachings of Eutyches (who emphasized Christ’s divine nature to the exclusion of the human) and Nestorius (who was accused of dividing Christ into two persons). The Chalcedonian Definition sought to strike a balance between these extremes, reaffirming the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed and preserving the mystery of the Incarnation.
