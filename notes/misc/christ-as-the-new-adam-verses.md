## 1. 1 Corinthians 15:21-22

- **The passage:** death came by a man, and the resurrection of the dead has also come by a man; as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive.
- This verse contrasts Adam's role in bringing death to all with Christ's role in bringing life through resurrection.

## 2. 1 Corinthians 15:45-49

- **The passage:** the first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit. The natural comes first, then the spiritual. The first man was of the earth, of dust; the second man is from heaven. As we have borne the image of the man of dust, we will also bear the image of the man of heaven.
- This passage explains that Adam, formed from dust, was the first human, but Christ, as the last Adam, is from heaven. Believers, who bear Adam's nature, will also bear the resurrected and glorified nature of Christ.

## 3. Romans 5:12-14

- **The passage:** sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all because all sinned. Death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those who did not sin as Adam did, who was a type of the one who was to come.
- Here, Paul explains that Adam's sin brought death to all humanity, but Adam is also a type (or foreshadowing) of Christ, the one who is to come and bring life.

## 4. Romans 5:18-19

- **The passage:** as one trespass led to condemnation for all, so one act of righteousness leads to justification and life for all; as by one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by one man's obedience the many will be made righteous.
- This passage further contrasts Adam's disobedience, which made all sinners, with Christ's obedience, which leads to justification and life.

## 5. Philippians 2:8-9

- **The passage:** being found in human form, He humbled Himself and became obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross; therefore God highly exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name.
- This emphasizes Christ's obedience and how His humble submission to death on the cross reverses the disobedience of Adam, leading to exaltation and salvation for humanity.

## Theological Significance

The Bible repeatedly contrasts the actions and roles of **Adam** and **Christ**, showing how through Adam's **disobedience**, sin and death entered the world, while through Christ's **obedience**, righteousness and life are restored. Christ's resurrection not only reverses the effects of Adam's fall but also ushers in the new creation, where believers are "**made alive**" and transformed to bear the "**image of the man of heaven**" (1 Corinthians 15:49). These passages form the biblical foundation for the teaching of Christ as the **new Adam** in Christian theology.
