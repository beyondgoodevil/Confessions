## 1. The Disciples of John the Baptist in Ephesus (Acts 19:1-7)

Paul encounters a group of people in Ephesus who were disciples of John the Baptist but had not received full instruction about Jesus Christ and the Holy Spirit. He instructs them on the need for baptism in Jesus' name and the reception of the Holy Spirit.

- **Acts 19:1-7:** Paul asks whether they received the Holy Spirit when they believed; they have not even heard of Him and were baptized only into John's baptism. Paul explains that John pointed to Christ Jesus. They are baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus, Paul lays hands on them, the Holy Spirit comes upon them, and they speak with tongues and prophesy. There were about twelve men.

## 2. The Athenians in Athens (Acts 17:22-34)

Paul addresses the Athenians, who had an altar to an "unknown god." Paul uses this as an opportunity to teach them about the one true God, Jesus Christ, and the resurrection.

- **Acts 17:22-23:** Standing in the Areopagus, Paul says he sees they are very religious and has found an altar inscribed TO THE UNKNOWN GOD; the One they worship without knowing, he proclaims to them.
- **Acts 17:30-31:** God overlooked the times of ignorance but now commands all to repent, having appointed a day to judge the world by the Man He has ordained, and given assurance by raising Him from the dead.

## 3. The Philippian Jailer (Acts 16:25-34)

After the earthquake that freed Paul and Silas from prison, the Philippian jailer, who was not yet a believer, asks Paul what he must do to be saved. Paul instructs him to believe in Jesus Christ.

- **Acts 16:30-31:** The jailer asks what he must do to be saved; they answer that he should believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and he and his household will be saved.
- **Acts 16:33-34:** That same hour he washes their stripes, and he and all his family are baptized at once. He sets food before them and rejoices, having believed in God with all his household.

## 4. The Bereans (Acts 17:10-12)

Paul instructs the Bereans by preaching in their synagogue. The Bereans, after hearing Paul, diligently search the Scriptures to confirm what he said.

- **Acts 17:10-11:** Sent to Berea by night, Paul and Silas go into the synagogue. The Bereans are more fair-minded than those in Thessalonica: they receive the word readily and search the Scriptures daily to see whether these things are so.
- **Acts 17:12:** Many of them believe, along with a number of prominent Greek women and men.

## 5. Paul's Teaching in Antioch (Acts 13:13-52)

In Antioch, Paul and Barnabas preach to the synagogue congregation, explaining that Jesus is the fulfillment of God's promises to Israel. They teach the truth about Christ's death and resurrection, offering salvation through faith.

- **Acts 13:38-39:** Through this Man forgiveness of sins is preached, and by Him everyone who believes is justified from all the things from which the law of Moses could not justify.

## 6. The Conversion of Saul (Acts 9:1-19)

After Saul's encounter with Jesus on the road to Damascus, he is blinded and instructed to go to the city. There, Ananias, sent by God, lays hands on him to restore his sight and further instructs him in the gospel.

- **Acts 9:17-18:** Ananias enters the house, lays hands on him, and says the Lord Jesus has sent him so that Saul may receive his sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit. Something like scales falls from his eyes; he sees, rises, and is baptized.

## 7. The Conversion of Cornelius (Acts 10:34-48)

Cornelius, a Gentile, had been praying to God but had not received full knowledge of the gospel. Peter, after being called by God, preaches the gospel to Cornelius and his household, and they receive the Holy Spirit and are baptized.

- **Acts 10:44-47:** While Peter is still speaking, the Holy Spirit falls on all who hear. The Jewish believers are astonished that the gift is poured out on Gentiles too, hearing them speak with tongues and magnify God. Peter asks whether anyone can forbid water for baptizing those who have received the Holy Spirit as they have.

## Conclusion

Throughout the Book of Acts, Paul consistently instructed those who had either not received full teaching about Christ or who were in need of further clarification. From baptizing the disciples of John in Ephesus to preaching the gospel to the Gentiles in Athens and Caesarea, Paul's missionary work was deeply focused on instructing new believers and ensuring that they understood the full scope of the Christian faith. These examples illustrate how Paul not only evangelized but also edified the early church through teaching, baptizing, and guiding believers into the fullness of the gospel.
