---

## 1. Genesis 3:15 (The Protoevangelium)

- "I will put enmity between you and the ==woman==, and between your seed and her seed; he shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel."
- This is the first prophecy of the Messiah's victory over Satan, and "the ==woman==" is interpreted as a reference to Mary, whose seed (Christ) will defeat the devil.

## 2. John 2:4 (The Wedding at Cana)

- "And Jesus said to her, '==Woman==, what does this have to do with me? My hour has not yet come.'"
- Jesus addresses Mary as "==Woman==," a title pointing to her significant role in salvation history, particularly regarding her intercessory role at the wedding.

## 3. John 19:26 (At the Crucifixion)

- "When Jesus saw his mother and the disciple whom he loved standing nearby, he said to his mother, '==Woman==, behold, your son!'"
- Here, Christ again refers to Mary as "==Woman==" while entrusting her to John, symbolizing her as the mother of all believers.

## 4. Revelation 12:1 (The ==Woman== Clothed with the Sun)

- "And a great sign appeared in heaven: a ==woman== clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars."
- Though primarily a symbol of the Church, the ==woman== is also understood to refer to Mary, who gives birth to the Messiah and plays a vital role in the cosmic battle between good and evil.

## 5. Revelation 12:5

- "She gave birth to a male child, one who is to rule all the nations with a rod of iron, but her child was caught up to God and to his throne."
- This continues the symbolism from Revelation 12:1, where Mary is seen as the ==woman== who gives birth to the Christ child.

## 6. Galatians 4:4

- "But when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth his Son, born of a ==woman==, born under the law."
- While not directly naming Mary, this passage acknowledges her as the ==woman== through whom the Son of God was born.
