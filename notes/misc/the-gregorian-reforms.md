The **Gregorian Reforms**, initiated by **Pope Gregory VII** in the 11th century (named after him), were a series of changes aimed at reforming the Catholic Church’s structure, discipline, and independence. They addressed corruption, clerical behavior, and the relationship between the Church and secular rulers. Here are the key changes:

### 1. Clerical Celibacy

- **What changed?** Priests were forbidden from marrying or engaging in sexual relationships. This was intended to ensure their full devotion to the Church and avoid hereditary claims to Church property.
- **Before the reforms:** Many clergy were married or lived with concubines.

### 2. Simony (Buying and Selling of Church Offices)

- **What changed?** The practice of simony (the purchase of ecclesiastical positions) was condemned and strictly prohibited.
- **Before the reforms:** Wealthy individuals could buy bishoprics or other offices, leading to corruption and incompetence.

### 3. Lay Investiture

- **What changed?** Secular rulers were no longer allowed to appoint bishops or grant them spiritual authority. Only the Pope or Church officials could perform such appointments.
- **Before the reforms:** Kings and nobles often appointed bishops, who then became loyal to them instead of the Church. This reform sparked conflicts like the **Investiture Controversy** with Emperor Henry IV.

### 4. Papal Authority and Centralization

- **What changed?** The authority of the Pope over all aspects of the Church was emphasized, including the power to appoint bishops, depose kings, and excommunicate rulers.
- **Before the reforms:** Local bishops and secular rulers often operated with significant autonomy, limiting the Pope’s influence.

### 5. Moral Discipline for Clergy

- **What changed?** Strict rules were enforced for clergy to live moral lives. Corruption, luxury, and immoral behavior were addressed.
- **Before the reforms:** Many clergy were accused of living extravagantly and engaging in immoral conduct.

### 6. Focus on Church Independence

- **What changed?** The Church sought independence from secular influence in governance, appointments, and decision-making.
- **Before the reforms:** Secular rulers often controlled or influenced Church affairs, blurring the lines between Church and state.

### 7. Strengthened Canon Law

- **What changed?** The development and enforcement of Church laws (*canon law*) were formalized, giving the Pope and Church councils greater control over doctrine and discipline.
