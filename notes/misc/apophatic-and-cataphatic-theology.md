Two ways of speaking about God:

| | Cataphatic (*kataphasis*, affirmation) | Apophatic (*apophasis*, negation) |
|---|---|---|
| Says | what God is: good, wise, light, love | what God is not: not limited, not visible, not comprehensible |
| Based on | what God has revealed | that God's essence exceeds every concept |
| Risk if alone | an idol: God as one more thing we can define | empty agnosticism, or a God with nothing to say |

The site uses "apophatic" in a dozen notes without ever explaining it. This is that note.

## 1. Why Negation

- God's **essence** is unknowable. Not just "very hard to know": beyond knowing by any created mind, in this life or the next.
- Human words are made for created things. "Good", "wise", "exists" all carry creaturely limits that don't fit God.
- So the more accurate thing is often to say what God isn't.

OrthodoxWiki's example: "God is everywhere" is, apophatically, too small. God is also beyond creation altogether, and we can't know "every place God is". Even "God exists" needs qualifying: His existence is "altogether different than any existence we can imagine". Dionysius says *hyperousios*, "beyond being". See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]], [[Heidegger and the God Beyond Being]].

## 2. Scripture

- **John 1:18**: "No man hath seen God at any time; the only begotten Son... he hath declared him."
- **1 Tim 6:16**: "dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto; whom no man hath seen, nor can see." See [[1 Timothy 6:16: God Dwells in Unapproachable Light, aka His Uncreated Glory]].
- **Job 11:7-8**: "Canst thou by searching find out God?"
- **Rom 11:33**: "how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out!"
- **Ex 20:21**: "Moses drew near unto the thick darkness where God was."
- **Acts 17:23**: the altar "To the Unknown God". Paul's sermon is the setting of Dionysius's conversion.
- **Isa 55:8-9**: "my thoughts are not your thoughts."

Note John 1:18: the same verse that says no one has seen God says the Son has made Him known. Apophatic and cataphatic in one sentence.

## 3. The Fathers

| Father | What he says |
|---|---|
| **Philo** (1st c., Jewish) | God's existence can be known, not His essence. Moses in the darkness |
| **Clement of Alexandria** | reach God by abstraction: take away body, then dimension, then position... |
| **St. Gregory of Nyssa** (*Life of Moses*) | true knowledge of God is seeing that He is invisible; the darkness of Sinai is where God is. Ascent never ends (*epektasis*) |
| **St. Gregory the Theologian** (*Oration* 28) | quotes Plato that God is hard to know and impossible to speak of, and corrects him: impossible to *comprehend* |
| **St. Basil** (*Against Eunomius*) | Eunomius claimed to know God's essence ("unbegotten"). Basil: we know Him from His energies, not His essence. See [[St. Gregory of Nyssa's Writings Against Eunomius]] |
| **St. Dionysius** (*Mystical Theology*) | affirm, then deny, then deny the denials. The "brilliant darkness" |
| **St. Maximus** | apophatic theology together with stillness (hesychia) is the way to union |
| **St. John of Damascus** (*Exact Exposition* I.4) | the only thing comprehensible about God is His infinity and incomprehensibility. What we say positively tells us "the things around His nature", not the nature |
| **St. Gregory Palamas** | God is unknowable in essence and truly known in His uncreated energies. The darkness is the uncreated light |

OrthodoxWiki presents Dionysius as passing on an existing tradition, not inventing it. The table bears that out: Philo, Clement and the Cappadocians are all earlier. (The Philo, Clement, Basil and Palamas rows are mine; the OrthodoxWiki page doesn't cover them.)

## 4. How the Two Work Together

- **Cataphatic first**: we start with what Scripture and the Church say. God *is* good, *is* Trinity, *did* become man.
- **Then apophatic**: none of these names captures what He is. "Good" yes, but not good the way anything else is.
- **Then beyond both**: God isn't contained by our denials either. "Not good" is no closer than "good".
- So apophaticism isn't a separate doctrine about God. It's a **correction applied to every doctrine**: an attitude of worship toward what has been said.
- Lossky (*Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church*): apophaticism is the fundamental character of all Orthodox theology, not one branch of it.

## 5. What It Is Not

- **Not agnosticism.** The Fathers who insist God is unknowable are the same ones who fought over the exact wording of the Creed. Negation presupposes revelation. You can only say "God is not merely good" if you know He is good.
- **Not "all religions see the same unknown".** The God beyond concepts is the Trinity, revealed in Christ. Apophaticism doesn't make doctrine negotiable.
- **Not anti-rational.** Reason is used to its limit and then sees its limit. See [[Natural Theology: Is It Legitimate]] sec. 6.
- **Not only intellectual.** The "unknowing" is reached in prayer and purification, not just by logic. The aim is union, not a better definition. See [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]], [[Theosis: Becoming by Grace What God Is by Nature]].
- **Not a contradiction.** "God is and is not good" isn't "P and not-P" in the logic-book sense: the two "goods" mean different things. See [[Deviant Logics]] sec. 3, where Gensler uses Dionysius as an example.

## 6. Essence and Energies

The doctrine that holds the two together:

- **Apophatic** about the essence: wholly unknowable, unnameable.
- **Cataphatic** about the energies: God's goodness, wisdom, life, light are God Himself acting, and can be known and shared.
- So the divine names name God truly (in His energies) without defining what God is (in His essence).
- See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]], [[God's Uncreated Energies]].

## 7. East and West

OrthodoxWiki: apophatic statements are crucial in Orthodox theology; in Western theology the opposite tends to hold, with exceptions.

My notes on that:

- The exceptions are large: Augustine ("if you have understood, it is not God"), Aquinas ("we cannot know what God is, only what He is not", *ST* I.3 prologue), Eckhart, *The Cloud of Unknowing*, John of the Cross.
- The real difference may be **method**. Scholastic theology works mainly by **analogy**: our words apply to God neither univocally nor equivocally but analogically. That keeps a lot of cataphatic content and lets theology be a science.
- Orthodox worry: analogy can make God the highest instance of being, Heidegger's "ontotheology". See [[Heidegger and the God Beyond Being]].
- And absolute simplicity sits awkwardly with apophaticism: if God's essence = His attributes, then knowing His goodness is knowing His essence. See [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]].

## 8. Why It Matters in Practice

- **Against idols**, including mental ones. Our concept of God is not God.
- **Humility** in theology. St. Gregory the Theologian: not everyone should do theology, and not at every moment.
- **In the Liturgy**: "Thou art God ineffable, inconceivable, invisible, incomprehensible, ever-existing and eternally the same" (Anaphora of St. John Chrysostom). Apophatic theology is prayed every Sunday. See [[The Divine Liturgy Explained]].
- **Against some atheist arguments**: arguments against a God who is "one more being in the universe" usually don't touch the God of the Fathers.
