Fideism is the belief that faith, rather than reason or evidence, is the primary or sole basis for knowledge, especially in matters of religion. It asserts that religious truths are to be accepted by faith alone, without the need for rational justification or empirical evidence. This stance contrasts with positions that emphasize the role of reason and evidence in understanding religious beliefs or truths.

In other words, fideism holds that faith should be trusted even when it cannot be supported or validated by logical arguments, scientific proof, or philosophical reasoning. Some variations of fideism might argue that faith is independent of reason, while others may claim that reason is irrelevant or even obstructive when it comes to religious belief.

Fideism has been criticized by many philosophers, especially those in the tradition of naturalism or rationalism, who argue that belief should be based on evidence and logical reasoning. However, proponents of fideism may emphasize the limitations of human reason and suggest that divine truths transcend human understanding, making faith the most appropriate response to religious claims.
