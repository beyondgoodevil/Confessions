The concept of the **Office of the Keys** is more directly found in **Matthew 16:19** and **Matthew 18:18**, rather than in chapters 15 or 16 specifically. This doctrine refers to the authority to bind and loose sins, granted by Christ to His apostles, particularly to Peter.

## Matthew 16:19 (NKJV)

*"And I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven."*

Here, Christ entrusts Peter with a unique authority, symbolized by the "keys of the kingdom." This reflects the power to make binding decisions on doctrinal and disciplinary matters within the Church. It's a foundational verse for understanding apostolic authority and Church governance.

## Matthew 18:18 (NKJV)

*"Assuredly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven."*

This verse broadens the authority to include all the apostles, indicating the communal nature of Church leadership.

## Significance of the Office of the Keys

1. **Binding and Loosing Sins**: Represents the Church's authority to forgive or retain sins (see also **John 20:23**).
2. **Doctrinal Authority**: The Church can define and uphold doctrine, safeguarding the faith against heresies.
3. **Disciplinary Power**: Authority over excommunication and reconciliation, maintaining Church order.

This concept is central to ecclesiology, especially in discussions on apostolic succession and the role of bishops and priests in administering sacraments like confession.
