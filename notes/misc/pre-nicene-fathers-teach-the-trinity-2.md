## 4. Irenaeus of Lyons (c. 130–202 AD)

“The Father is God, and the Son is God, for the one who is begotten of God is God.” — *Against Heresies, 3.19.2*

“The Church... has received from the apostles and their disciples this faith in one God, the Father Almighty... and in one Christ Jesus, the Son of God, who became incarnate for our salvation, and in the Holy Spirit.” — *Against Heresies, 1.10.1*

### Irenaeus’ Teaching on the Trinity

- Calls **both the Father and Son "God"**, confirming Christ’s divinity.
- Lists the **Father, Son, and Holy Spirit** in the **Apostolic Tradition**, proving the early Church's Trinitarian belief.

## 5. Tertullian (c. 160–225 AD)

“We define that there are two, the Father and the Son, and three with the Holy Spirit, and this number is made by the pattern of salvation... They are three not in substance, but in form; not in power, but in order; not in aspect, but in kind.” — *Against Praxeas, 2*

“Thus the connection of the Father in the Son, and of the Son in the Paraclete (Holy Spirit), produces three coherent Persons, who are yet distinct one from another. These three are one essence, not one Person.” — *Against Praxeas, 25*

### Tertullian’s Teaching on the Trinity

- First known use of the word **"Trinity" (Trinitas)**.
- Clearly states **three distinct Persons in one essence**, showing that the doctrine existed before Nicaea.
