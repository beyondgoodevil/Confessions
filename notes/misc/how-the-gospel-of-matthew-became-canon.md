title: How the Gospel of Matthew Became Canon
tags: [theology, canon, church-history]
summary: Five stages in the recognition of Matthew's Gospel, from Irenaeus to Augustine.
---

1. **Early Christian Writings (2nd Century)**: Early church fathers, such as **Irenaeus** (c. 130–202 AD), acknowledged the Gospel of Matthew as authoritative. Irenaeus referred to it as the first Gospel in his writings.
2. **Muratorian Fragment (c. 170-200 AD)**: This early list of canonical scriptures, known as the **Muratorian Canon**, includes the Gospel of Matthew, indicating its acceptance in certain Christian communities.
3. **Council of Hippo (393 AD)**: This regional council in North Africa affirmed the canon of Scripture, including the Gospel of Matthew, as part of the New Testament.
4. **Council of Carthage (397 AD)**: This council further confirmed the canon established at Hippo, explicitly listing the Gospel of Matthew among the accepted texts.
5. **Influence of St. Augustine**: Augustine of Hippo (354–430 AD) played a significant role in advocating for the canon established by these councils, reinforcing the authority of the Gospel of Matthew within the broader context of Christian doctrine.
