Anthropomorphic language in the context of God and the Bible refers to the use of human traits, emotions, or actions to describe God, even though He is beyond human limitations. This is a way to make divine realities more understandable to human beings.

## Examples in Scripture

1. **God’s Hands and Eyes** – "The eyes of the Lord are in every place, beholding the evil and the good." — Proverbs 15:3 (LXX)
2. **God’s Anger** – "The Lord was angry with me for your sakes." — Deuteronomy 3:26 (LXX)
3. **God’s Walking** – "And they heard the voice of the Lord God walking in the garden in the cool of the day." — Genesis 3:8 (LXX)

## Eastern Orthodox Understanding

- **Symbolic Expression** – The Church Fathers explain that such descriptions help humans relate to God in a way they can grasp but do not imply that God has a physical body or human limitations.
- **Apophatic Theology** – Orthodox theology emphasizes that while we describe God with human-like terms, His essence remains beyond human comprehension.
- **Divine Economy** – Anthropomorphic language is used in Scripture because God condescends (κατάβασις) to our level for the sake of our salvation.

## Patristic Explanation

- St. John Chrysostom states: *“The Scripture speaks in a human manner because of our weakness.”* (Homily on Genesis)
- St. Basil the Great warns against taking anthropomorphic descriptions literally, saying that **God is beyond human form and passions.**

Thus, anthropomorphic language in the Bible is a necessary but limited tool to help humans understand God's actions and presence, while the Orthodox Church teaches that God is ultimately beyond all human attributes.
