The **World Council of Churches (WCC)** was officially founded in **1948** in Amsterdam, Netherlands. However, discussions and preparatory work for its formation began much earlier, particularly in the **1920s and 1930s**.

## Rockefeller Funding and Influence

While the **Rockefeller Foundation** did not directly establish the WCC, it played a significant role in **funding and supporting** the ecumenical movement that led to its creation.

1. **1920s–1930s: Early Ecumenical Movements**
    - The Rockefeller Foundation, particularly through its **philanthropic arms**, provided financial support for various Protestant and ecumenical initiatives, including the **Life and Work** and **Faith and Order** movements. These were two major efforts that eventually merged into the WCC.
    - John D. Rockefeller Jr., a well-known supporter of liberal Protestantism and globalist religious unity, funded many church-based initiatives that encouraged ecumenism.
2. **1937: Formation Plans for the WCC**
    - Representatives from Protestant and Orthodox churches met to plan for a global Christian organization. This meeting was **partially funded by Rockefeller-sponsored organizations**.
    - The Rockefeller Foundation also provided **funding for theological and missionary conferences** that pushed for greater unity among Protestant and Orthodox Christians.
3. **1948: Official Establishment of the WCC**
    - By this time, the groundwork had been laid through decades of financial and ideological support.
    - The WCC was created as a global ecumenical organization with participation from Protestant, Anglican, and Eastern Orthodox churches (though the Roman Catholic Church did not officially join).
