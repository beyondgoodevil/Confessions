---
title: "Sola fide still requires works: mental work"
tags: [theology, apologetics]
summary: Believing is itself an act of the will, so sola fide does not remove works but moves them inward.
---

1. **Mental Work of Faith:** The act of believing or trusting in Christ is itself a mental and spiritual effort. It demands intellectual assent and personal conviction, meaning that while physical works may be dismissed, the doctrine still insists on a form of **mental action** to achieve justification. This creates a paradox: while physical works are rejected, **mental belief becomes the "work"** required for salvation.
2. **Faith Requires Effort:** To truly believe in Christ is not passive; it involves a **decision of the will, a turning of the mind and heart** toward God. This decision-making process and continuous trust in God's promises require effort, so the notion that faith alone excludes any form of "work" isn't entirely consistent. Even the act of sustaining faith involves mental discipline and perseverance.
3. **Inconsistent Rejection of Works:** By rejecting physical works but requiring a form of mental effort, the doctrine of sola fide doesn't eliminate works entirely—it merely shifts the emphasis from external actions to internal conviction. Yet, this internal faith is treated as the sole requirement for salvation, despite the fact that **scripture frequently highlights both faith and external works as integral to the salvation process.**
