- **16:18** (highlighted) – Jesus tells Peter that he is Peter, that on this rock He will build ==My church==, and that the gates of Hades will not prevail against it.

## The Wordplay

- "Thou art **Petros**, and upon this **petra** I will build my church."
- In Aramaic (which Jesus probably spoke) both would be **Kepha** ("Cephas", John 1:42), which supports reading the rock as Peter.
- In Greek, *petros* (masculine, a stone) and *petra* (feminine, a rock mass): some Protestants stress the difference; most scholars think it's just grammatical (a man's name has to be masculine).

## "This Rock" in the Fathers

The Fathers give **several** readings, often the same Father in different places (Siecienski ch. 3):

| Reading | Who |
|---|---|
| **Peter's confession / faith** ("Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God", v. 16) | Origen (in part), Chrysostom (*Hom. on Matt.* 54: "upon the faith of his confession"), Hilary, Ambrose, Cyril of Alexandria; the most common Eastern reading |
| **Peter himself** | Tertullian, Cyprian, Ephrem, Leo the Great; frequent in the West, also in some Eastern hymns |
| **Christ** ("that Rock was Christ", 1 Cor 10:4; "other foundation can no man lay", 1 Cor 3:11) | Augustine (*Retractations* I.20.1: he says he often took the rock as Christ, but leaves the reader to choose) |
| **Every believer** who makes Peter's confession | Origen: "if we too say 'Thou art the Christ'... we become Peter" |

Siecienski's point: these readings weren't seen as exclusive, and **none** of them was taken in the first millennium to mean that Peter's **successors in Rome** have universal jurisdiction. Cyprian, who reads the rock as Peter, also says all bishops share Peter's chair (*On the Unity of the Church* 4): "the episcopate is one, each part of which is held by each one for the whole".

## The Gates of Hades

- Not "hell" as final damnation but **Hades**, the realm of death. Its "gates" are its power to hold the dead.
- So the promise: **death** will not overpower the Church. Fits the Descent into Hades: Christ breaks those gates. See [[The Descent into Hades]].
- Also read as: heresies and persecutions won't destroy the Church (Origen, Chrysostom).

## The Keys (v. 19)

- "I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth..." Background: Isa 22:22, the steward of David's house given the key.
- But in **Matt 18:18** the same power of binding and loosing is given to **all the disciples**; and John 20:22-23 ("whose soever sins ye remit") to all. See [[Matthew 15, 16: Office of the Keys]].

## In the Papacy Debate

*Mine.*

- Catholic reading: the rock is Peter; the keys make him steward; the office passes to his successors in Rome.
- Orthodox reading: the rock is Peter's confession (or Peter **as** confessor); the keys are given to Peter as **representative** of the apostles and of all bishops; Antioch also had Peter as first bishop.
- The weak point for the Catholic argument is not Peter's primacy among the apostles (generally granted) but the step to **Rome's** bishops having it **exclusively** and as **jurisdiction**.
