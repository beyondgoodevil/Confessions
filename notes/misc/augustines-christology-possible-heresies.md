## 1. Overemphasis on Christ's Divine Will

- Augustine often focused on Christ's divine will and sovereignty, emphasizing God's control over salvation and the divine nature of Christ. This strong emphasis can lead to a lack of attention on Christ's human will, which is essential for understanding His full humanity.
- **Potential Heresy: Monothelitism**
    - This heresy teaches that Christ had only one divine will after the Incarnation. If Augustine's framework is misinterpreted, it can imply that Christ's human experiences and choices were secondary or nonexistent, leading to a diminished understanding of His humanity.

## 2. Insufficient Emphasis on Mary as Theotokos

- Augustine did not place significant theological weight on Mary's role as **Theotokos** (Mother of God). He primarily viewed her as the mother of Christ's human nature, potentially separating Christ's divine and human natures.
- **Potential Heresy: Nestorianism**
    - Nestorius argued that since Mary gave birth only to Christ's human nature, she should not be called **Theotokos**. Augustine's lack of emphasis on this title could inadvertently support a separation between Christ's two natures, leading to the Nestorian view where Christ's humanity and divinity are seen as distinct and separate.

## 3. Emphasis on Divine Justice and Subordination

- Augustine's theology often emphasizes divine justice and the Father as the source of divinity. While affirming the equality of the Trinity, his language can sometimes imply a hierarchy, particularly in discussing predestination and the roles of the Father and Son.
- **Potential Heresy: Arianism**
    - This heresy claims that the Son is not of the same essence as the Father and is therefore subordinate. Although Augustine rejects Arianism, the potential misinterpretation of his emphasis on divine authority could lead some to view the Son as lesser than the Father.

## 4. Neglect of the Hypostatic Union in Practical Terms

- While Augustine acknowledges the unity of Christ's two natures, he does not delve deeply into the theological implications of the **hypostatic union** (the union of Christ's divine and human natures). This lack of exploration may leave ambiguity regarding how these natures coexist.
- **Potential Heresy: Apollinarianism**
    - Apollinaris claimed that Christ did not have a human mind or soul, suggesting the divine Logos took its place. If Augustine's discussions are seen as insufficiently robust, they might unintentionally support a view that minimizes Christ's full humanity.

## 5. Predestination and the Human Nature of Christ

- Augustine's doctrine of predestination emphasizes God's sovereignty in salvation. However, this can raise questions about the role and nature of Christ's humanity, suggesting that Christ's human experience may be limited or predetermined by divine will.
- **Potential Heresy: Nestorianism or Docetism**
    - This perspective could lead to Nestorianism, where Christ's human nature is perceived as distinct from His divine nature. Alternatively, it might drift toward **Docetism**, which denies the reality of Christ's human suffering and existence, portraying His humanity as merely an appearance.
