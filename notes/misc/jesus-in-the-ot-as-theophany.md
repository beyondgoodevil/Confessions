In the Old Testament, various theophanies (manifestations of God) are interpreted as appearances of the pre-incarnate Christ (the Logos). Here are some key examples:

1. **Genesis 18:1-3** – *"And the LORD appeared to him by the oaks of Mamre... three men stood in front of him."*  
   **Explanation:** This is often seen as a theophany of Christ, who appears as one of the three visitors to Abraham, though the text speaks of "the LORD."
2. **Exodus 3:2-6** – *"And the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush..."*  
   **Explanation:** The angel of the LORD who speaks to Moses from the burning bush is understood to be the pre-incarnate Christ, as He identifies Himself as God and tells Moses to remove his sandals, for he is on holy ground.
3. **Daniel 3:25** – *"But I see four men unbound, walking in the midst of the fire, and they are not hurt; and the appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods."*  
   **Explanation:** The fourth figure in the fiery furnace is often understood to be Christ, who miraculously appears to save Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.
4. **Joshua 5:13-15** – *"When Joshua was by Jericho, he lifted up his eyes and looked, and behold, a man was standing before him... and Joshua fell on his face to the earth and worshiped."*  
   **Explanation:** The figure Joshua encounters is regarded as a theophany of Christ, as Joshua worships him, which would not be permissible if it were not God.
5. **Isaiah 6:1-5** – *"In the year that King Uzziah died I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up; and the train of his robe filled the temple."*  
   **Explanation:** The prophet Isaiah's vision of God on His throne is understood as a vision of Christ, as John 12:41 explicitly connects this vision to Jesus.

These theophanies are understood as manifestations of the second person of the Trinity before His incarnation in the New Testament.
