title: "Penal substitution: Reformers that believed in penal substitution"

summary: Ten Reformers and Reformed theologians who taught penal substitution.
---

1. **Martin Luther:** Luther believed that Christ bore the punishment due to sinners, satisfying divine justice on their behalf. His writings on justification by faith are closely tied to the concept of penal substitution.
2. **John Calvin:** In his *Institutes of the Christian Religion*, Calvin extensively teaches penal substitution, describing Christ's atonement as taking the punishment sinners deserved in order to satisfy God's justice.
3. **Ulrich Zwingli:** Though Zwingli's focus was more on the symbolic nature of the sacraments, he supported the idea that Christ bore the wrath of God in place of sinners, aligning with penal substitution.
4. **Theodore Beza:** Calvin's successor in Geneva, Beza, developed Calvin's doctrine further and was a strong advocate of penal substitutionary atonement.
5. **John Knox:** The founder of the Presbyterian Church in Scotland, Knox embraced Calvinist theology, including the belief in penal substitution as essential to the gospel.
6. **Thomas Cranmer:** Cranmer, the architect of Anglicanism and the *Book of Common Prayer*, reflected penal substitution in his theological writings, particularly regarding Christ's atonement.
7. **Heinrich Bullinger:** A Swiss reformer and Zwingli's successor, Bullinger also affirmed penal substitution in his theology of the atonement.
8. **John Owen:** A prominent English theologian and Puritan leader, Owen defended penal substitution as central to Reformed theology, arguing that Christ's suffering was the exact payment for sin.
9. **Huldrych Zwingli:** Zwingli, one of the early leaders of the Reformation in Switzerland, while not as detailed as Calvin, still accepted the notion that Christ's death served as the punishment for human sin.
10. **Francis Turretin:** A 17th-century Reformed scholastic theologian, Turretin's defense of penal substitution was a crucial part of his broader theological system.
