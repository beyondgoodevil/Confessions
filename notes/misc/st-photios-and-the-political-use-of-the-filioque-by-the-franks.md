1. **Frankish Political Ambitions and the Filioque**
    - St. Photios argued that the Franks, particularly under **Charlemagne (r. 768–814)**, **introduced the Filioque as a means to challenge Byzantine influence** and assert their own theological authority.
    - Charlemagne sought to position himself as the true defender of Christianity in the West and used the **Filioque** as a way to **distance the Latin Church from Constantinople**.
    - By **altering the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed**, the Franks aimed to **justify their political and religious independence** from the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire.
2. **The Filioque as a Tool to Undermine Byzantine Authority**
    - St. Photios saw the **insertion of the Filioque** as a **deliberate innovation** meant to weaken the unity between East and West.
    - The Franks had **no theological basis** for the Filioque but insisted on its use to establish a **separate Latin ecclesiastical identity**.
    - This ultimately contributed to the **schism** between the Eastern Orthodox Church and the Roman Catholic Church.
3. **Photios' Encyclical Against the Filioque (867)**
    - In **867**, St. Photios wrote an **encyclical letter** condemning the **Filioque** and accusing the Frankish clergy of distorting the **Apostolic Faith**.
    - He addressed this letter to the Eastern Patriarchs, warning them that the **Franks were using doctrinal changes to assert political control**.
    - This controversy escalated into what is called the **Photian Schism**.
4. **Theological and Political Opposition to Frankish Rule**
    - Photios saw the **Filioque not only as a theological heresy but also as a political maneuver** by the Frankish rulers and bishops.
    - The Byzantines viewed the **Papacy’s increasing alignment with the Franks** as an attempt to break from the authority of **Rome-Constantinople's traditional unity**.
    - The Papacy, by the **9th century**, was already under strong Frankish influence, and the introduction of the **Filioque** into Latin liturgy signaled an assertion of **Frankish control over Rome**.

## Key Quote from St. Photios

One of St. Photios’ most famous critiques of the **Filioque** and the Franks’ use of it as a political tool can be found in his work **"Mystagogy of the Holy Spirit"**:

> *“The Latins, under the pretext of correcting the Faith, have introduced a blasphemous and unlawful innovation, changing the very words of the sacred Creed… They seek not the truth, but dominion, using doctrine as a weapon to subjugate the churches.”*

This shows how **Photios saw the Filioque as more than just a doctrinal error—it was a geopolitical strategy by the Franks** to reshape Christian authority.
