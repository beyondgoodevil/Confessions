The **Canons of the Sixth Ecumenical Council**, also known as the **Council in Trullo** or the **Quinisext Council** (691–692 AD), were convened to provide disciplinary and administrative rules to complement the doctrinal definitions of the **Fifth** (553 AD) and **Sixth Ecumenical Councils** (681 AD). This council is called "Trullo" because it met in the **Trullus Hall** of the imperial palace in Constantinople.

While the **Sixth Ecumenical Council** itself focused on theological issues (mainly the condemnation of **Monothelitism**), the **Canons of the Trullan Council** provide a comprehensive set of **disciplinary measures** for the Church, addressing various aspects of ecclesiastical life, including clergy conduct, laity responsibilities, and liturgical practices. These canons are recognized as authoritative primarily in the **Eastern Orthodox Church**.

## Major Canons and Their Content

1. **Canon 1**:
    - Affirms the canons of the previous **Ecumenical Councils** and earlier **local synods** (e.g., Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, Chalcedon).
2. **Canon 2**:
    - Recognizes and adopts the **85 Apostolic Canons**, along with the canons of specific **Fathers** and earlier **regional councils**, emphasizing their authority for the governance of the Church.
3. **Canon 13**:
    - **Clerical Marriage**: Bishops are to remain celibate after ordination, while priests and deacons can remain married to their wives, provided the marriage occurred before ordination.
4. **Canon 16**:
    - Forbids clergy from marrying after ordination. If a widowed cleric remarries, he is to be deposed from clerical office.
5. **Canon 20**:
    - **Fasting on Saturdays**: Condemns the Roman practice of fasting on Saturdays during Lent, asserting that this contradicts established Orthodox custom.
6. **Canon 28**:
    - Outlines proper conduct for **monastics**, prohibiting monks or nuns from leaving their monasteries without a compelling reason and without their superior's blessing.
7. **Canon 55**:
    - Commands that Christians abstain from **fasting on Saturdays** (except during Holy Week), in opposition to the Roman practice.
8. **Canon 62**:
    - Prohibits **pagan customs** and **superstitions**, such as attending theatrical performances, dances, or festivals that promote idolatry or immorality.
9. **Canon 69**:
    - Forbids **laity and catechumens** from entering the **altar area**, reserving it solely for ordained clergy.
10. **Canon 81**:
    - Mandates that every church must have a copy of the **Gospels**, reflecting the importance of Scripture in the liturgical life of the Church.
11. **Canon 82**:
    - Prohibits the depiction of **Christ as a lamb** in icons. Instead, He should be depicted in human form to affirm the reality of the **Incarnation**.
12. **Canon 91**:
    - Imposes a strict **penance** on those who commit **infanticide**, particularly focusing on women who kill their own children.
13. **Canon 99**:
    - Condemns the consumption of **blood** or animals that have died without being properly slaughtered, consistent with the Church's interpretation of **Acts 15:29**.
14. **Canon 102**:
    - Provides guidance for the practice of **confession** and **penance**, emphasizing that these should be applied pastorally, with discernment and care for the spiritual growth of the penitent.

## Key Themes

1. **Discipline of Clergy and Laity**:
    - Clear guidelines for the moral and spiritual behavior of both clergy and laity.
2. **Liturgical Uniformity**:
    - Establishes practices for fasting, worship, and iconography, seeking consistency across the Church.
3. **Monastic Life**:
    - Strict rules governing the conduct and discipline of monks and nuns.
4. **Defense Against Heresy and Pagan Practices**:
    - Continues the Church's battle against lingering heresies and pagan influences in Christian life.

## Importance of the Canons

The **Trullan Canons** are essential for the governance of the Orthodox Church, providing a **comprehensive framework** for ecclesiastical discipline and pastoral care. They reflect the Church's ongoing effort to balance **doctrinal purity** with **pastoral sensitivity**, ensuring the faithful's spiritual growth while maintaining the Church's unity and holiness.
