- Beginning about the reign of King Mu (r. 956—918 B.C.), a reign ushered in by a disastrous defeat in which the preceding king (King Zhao, r. 977—957 B.C.) was killed and the royal army was decimated, there emerge signs of an incipient bureaucracy:
	- Bronze inscriptions record appointment of individuals without relationship to the king.
	- These individuals were appointed to courtly positions, had specific powers and responsibilities.
	- Before this new bureaucratic government, kinship relationships (brothers/cousins) of the king deputed to rule colonized territories. 

- Two years after conquest of Shang, King Wu dies (1043 B.C.).
- Succession crisis ensued, which was monumental for history of Chinese statecraft.
	- Wu was to be succeeded by his eldest son, Song (according to preceding two generations).
	- However, Wu's younger brother Zhou Gong Dan declared Song too young to rule, declaring himself regent.
	- This caused his brothers, Guanshu Xian; Caishu Du and Huoshu Chu, as well as Wu Geng, to rebel against Dan in civil war.
	- Dan, alongside Song (now King Cheng) and older half-brother Shao Gong Shi launched another campaign east. 
	- In two years, they killed Guanshu Xian and Wu Geng, forced Caishu Du into exile. Also began colonizing farther east.

### Colonization East

- Royal family members were sent to defend strategic points along two main geographic axes of north China:
	- Yellow River
	- Taihang Mountains

### Philosophical Foundations of Zhou Rule

- New problem facing the Zhou leadership: how to establish a lasting form of government. 
- The three (Zhou Gong Dan, King Cheng, Shao Gong Shi) had disagreements on how to govern. 

#### The Role of Heaven

- King Wu's death had been followed by a civil war.
- The reasons for this aren't as simple as ambition:
	- King Chen performed turtle shell divination to determine whether or not to attack uncles.
	- Oracles were positive, but his advisors urged him to disregard (difficult task and disquietude of people).

- King Chen refused to go against the apparent will of Heaven:
	Stop! I the young son do not dare to disregard the command of the Lord on High. Heaven was beneficent to King Wen, raising up our little country of Zhou, and it was turtle-shell divination that King Wen used, succeeding to receive this mandate. Now Heaven will be helping the people; how much more so should it be turtle-shell divination that I too use. Wuhu! Heaven is brightly awesome — it helps our grand foundation.

- Cheng gives us the first instance of the Mandate of Heaven:
	How is it that I go to the limits with divination and dare not to follow you? It is that I will proceed through the borders and lands pointed out by our ancestors; how much more so now that the divination is also auspicious. And so, expansively I will take you east to campaign. Heavens mandate is not to be presumed upon; the divination is aligned like this.

- Zhou Gong Dan believed "Heaven helps those who help themselves."
- Meritocracy was his preferred form of governance.
