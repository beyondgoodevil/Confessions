The authority to forgive sins is explicitly granted in **John 20:22-23**. This passage occurs after Christ's resurrection, when He appears to His disciples in the upper room.

### John 20:22-23 (NKJV):

*"And when He had said this, He breathed on them, and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven them; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained.'"*

### Key Points from This Passage:

1. **Authority from Christ**: Jesus directly grants the apostles the power to forgive or retain sins. This authority is not self-derived but comes from Christ, the ultimate source of forgiveness.
2. **Connection to the Holy Spirit**: The gift of the Holy Spirit is closely linked with this authority. The Spirit empowers the apostles to act in Christ's name.
3. **Binding and Loosing**: This mirrors the authority given in **Matthew 16:19** and **18:18** but focuses explicitly on sin and reconciliation.

### Theological Implications:

- **Sacrament of Confession**: In the Eastern Orthodox and Catholic traditions, this passage forms the biblical basis for the sacrament of penance or confession.
- **Apostolic Authority**: It emphasizes the unique role of the apostles and their successors in mediating Christ's forgiveness.

This passage underscores the communal and sacramental nature of forgiveness within the Church, emphasizing both divine mercy and ecclesiastical authority.
