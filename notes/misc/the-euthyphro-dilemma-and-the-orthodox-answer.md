From Plato's *Euthyphro* (10a). Socrates asks: is the pious loved by the gods because it's pious, or pious because the gods love it? The modern version is aimed at any ethics grounded in God.

## 1. The Dilemma

> Is a thing good because God commands it, or does God command it because it's good?

Either answer is supposed to sink the theist.

## 2. The Horns

**Horn 1: good because commanded** (voluntarism)

- Then good is arbitrary. If God had commanded cruelty, cruelty would be good.
- And "God is good" is empty. It just means "God does what God wills."

**Horn 2: commanded because good**

- Then there's a standard above or apart from God that He measures up to.
- God isn't the source of everything, and morality doesn't need Him.

## 3. The Standard Reply: False Dilemma

- There's a third option the question skips: **goodness is God's own nature**.
- He doesn't invent the good by decree and doesn't look it up somewhere. He commands what's good because He *is* the Good. The commands express what He is.
- Not above God (horn 2). Not arbitrary (horn 1), because God can't will against His nature: "He cannot deny himself" (2 Tim 2:13).

Verses that talk this way:

- **Mark 10:18**: "There is none good but one, that is, God."
- **1 John 4:8**: "God is love."
- **James 1:17**: "Every good gift... cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness."
- **Ps 119:68**: "Thou art good, and doest good."
- **Mal 3:6**: "I am the LORD, I change not."

Funny thing is that Plato more or less gets there himself in the *Republic*: the Good isn't one more thing that's good, it's the source. See [[The Good in Plato]]. The dilemma only bites on the Homeric gods Euthyphro believes in, who disagree with each other.

## 4. The Orthodox Version

The "God's nature" reply is shared with Aquinas etc. Orthodoxy adds:

1. **Goodness is an uncreated energy.**
    - God's essence is beyond every name, "good" as we mean it included. His goodness as we know and share it is an energy: God Himself, present and acting.
    - So the good isn't a created rule, or an abstract Form, or a bare edict. It's God's life communicated.
2. **The Good is personal.**
    - Not a principle. The Trinity, an eternal communion of love. Which is why the highest good for us is love and not rule-keeping.
3. **Commandments fit our nature.**
    - Every creature is made according to its logos in the Logos. The commandments aren't imposed from outside. They describe how something made in God's image flourishes.
    - To sin is to act against your own logos. That's why it damages you. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].
4. **Morality is participation.**
    - Being good = sharing in God's goodness = theosis. Ethics and salvation are one road.
5. **Free but not arbitrary.**
    - God didn't have to create. Having created, what He wills for creatures comes from who He is.

## 5. Follow-Up Objections

**"You've only pushed it back. Is God's nature good because it's His, or because it meets a standard?"**

- Explanation stops somewhere for everyone. Either at an impersonal abstract standard that can't oblige anybody, or at a personal necessary being who is goodness. The second is the better stop, since obligations are owed to persons.
- I think this is the strongest objection and the reply is decent but not knock-down. The atheist can say "I stop at the standard."

**"Then 'God is good' tells you nothing."**

- It tells *us* something. We learn goodness first from created goods and then find their source is God. Informative in the order of knowing, though God is first in the order of being.

**"What about the hard commands in the OT?"**

- Different question (how to read particular texts, God dealing with a fallen world). Doesn't bring the dilemma back, which is about what goodness *is*.

## 6. Turning It Around

- The objector assumes there's real, binding goodness a god could be judged by.
- With no God, what grounds that? Preference, convention, evolved instinct describe what people *do* value, not what they *ought* to. See [[Is Ought]].
- So the dilemma presupposes objective morality, which is the thing in dispute.

Voluntarism is a real position though, not a straw man. Ockham and some Reformed writers do take horn 1. See the section on nominalism in [[The Problem of Universals: Realism vs Nominalism]].
