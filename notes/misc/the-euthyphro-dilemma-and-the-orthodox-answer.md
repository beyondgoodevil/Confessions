The **Euthyphro dilemma** comes from Plato's dialogue *Euthyphro*, where Socrates asks whether the pious is loved by the gods because it is pious, or is pious because it is loved by the gods. In its modern form it is aimed at any morality grounded in God.

## 1. The Dilemma

> Is something good because God commands it, or does God command it because it is good?

Each answer is said to be fatal.

## 2. The Two Horns

1. **Good because God commands it** (divine voluntarism):
    - Then goodness is arbitrary. Had God commanded cruelty, cruelty would be good.
    - "God is good" becomes empty: it means only "God does what God wills."
2. **God commands it because it is good**:
    - Then there is a standard of goodness above or apart from God, to which He conforms.
    - God is no longer the source of all things, and morality does not need Him.

## 3. The Classical Reply: A False Dilemma

- There is a third option the question leaves out: **goodness is God's own nature**.
- God neither invents the good by decree nor consults a standard outside Himself. He commands what is good because He *is* the Good, and His commands express what He is.
- The standard is therefore not above God (second horn) and not arbitrary (first horn), since God cannot will against His own nature. "He cannot deny himself" (2 Timothy 2:13).

Scripture speaks this way:

- **Mark 10:18** – "There is none good but one, that is, God."
- **1 John 4:8** – "God is love."
- **James 1:17** – "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning."
- **Psalm 119:68** – "Thou art good, and doest good."
- **Malachi 3:6** – "For I am the LORD, I change not."

## 4. The Orthodox Answer in Full

1. **Goodness is an uncreated energy of God**:
    - God's essence is beyond every name, including "good" as we understand it. His goodness, as we know and share it, is a divine energy: God Himself, truly present and acting.
    - So the good is not a created rule, nor an abstract Form, nor an arbitrary edict. It is God's own life communicated to creatures.
2. **The Good is personal**:
    - The standard is not a principle but the Holy Trinity, an eternal communion of love. This is why the highest good for man is love, not rule-keeping.
3. **Commandments fit our nature**:
    - Each creature is made according to its logos in the divine Logos. God's commandments are not impositions from outside. They describe how a being made in His image flourishes.
    - To sin is to act against one's own logos, which is why it harms.
4. **Morality is participation**:
    - To be good is to share in God's goodness, which is theosis. Ethics and salvation are the same road.
5. **God's will is free but not arbitrary**:
    - God did not have to create. Having created, what He wills for creatures flows from who He is.

## 5. Replies to Follow-Up Objections

- **"The dilemma just moves back: is God's nature good because it is His, or because it meets a standard?"**
    - Explanation has to stop somewhere. It can stop at an impersonal abstract standard with no power to oblige anyone, or at a personal, necessary being who is goodness itself. The second is the better stopping point, since obligation is owed to persons.
- **"Then 'God is good' says nothing."**
    - It says something about us: our knowledge of goodness comes first from created goods, and we discover that their source is God. The statement is informative in the order of knowing, even though God is first in the order of being.
- **"What about hard commands in the Old Testament?"**
    - This is a separate question about interpreting particular texts and God's dealings with a fallen world. It does not revive the dilemma, which concerns what goodness *is*.

## 6. Turning the Question Around

- The objector assumes there is real, binding goodness by which a god could be judged.
- If there is no God, what grounds that standard? Preference, convention and evolutionary instinct describe what people do value, not what they ought to.
- The dilemma therefore presupposes the objective morality that only theism can ground. It is an instance of the is-ought problem facing the atheist, not the believer.

## Summary

- The dilemma offers two options: arbitrary command or a standard above God.
- The answer is a third: God is the Good, and His commands express His nature.
- In Orthodox terms, goodness is an uncreated energy of the tri-personal God, and to be good is to participate in Him.
