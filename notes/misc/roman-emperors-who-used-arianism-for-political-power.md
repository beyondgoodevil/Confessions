### 1. Roman Emperors Who Used Arianism for Political Power

#### Constantius II (Reigned 337–361 AD)

- Son of **Constantine the Great** and a strong supporter of Arianism.
- Favored **Arian bishops**, exiling **Nicene (Orthodox) bishops** such as **Athanasius of Alexandria**.
- Sought to unify the empire under Arian theology, making it the dominant doctrine in the **Eastern Roman Empire**.
- Presided over several councils (e.g., the **Council of Rimini, 359 AD**) that attempted to weaken Nicene Christianity.

#### Valens (Reigned 364–378 AD)

- A committed Arian who continued **persecuting Nicene Christians**.
- Exiled Orthodox bishops and replaced them with Arian clergy.
- Engaged in wars against the **Goths**, many of whom were Arian.
- Died at the **Battle of Adrianople (378 AD)**, a devastating defeat against the **Visigoths**, marking the decline of Arian influence in the Eastern Roman Empire.

### 2. Germanic Kings Who Used Arianism

#### Alaric I (Reigned c. 395–410 AD) – King of the Visigoths

- Arian Christian who led the **Sack of Rome in 410 AD**.
- His Visigothic kingdom was predominantly Arian, but he tolerated Nicene Christianity among Romans.

#### Theodoric the Great (Reigned 493–526 AD) – King of the Ostrogoths

- Established the **Ostrogothic Kingdom in Italy**.
- A strong Arian who ruled over a largely Nicene (Catholic) Roman population.
- Although he was Arian, he generally **allowed religious tolerance** but maintained **Arian control over the state**.
- Clashed with the Catholic Church toward the end of his reign, imprisoning **Pope John I**, who later died in captivity.

#### Gaiseric (Reigned 428–477 AD) – King of the Vandals

- Established the **Vandal Kingdom in North Africa**, making Arianism the state religion.
- **Persecuted Nicene (Catholic) Christians**, exiling bishops and confiscating churches.
- His kingdom became a center of **Arian power**, but after the Byzantine conquest under **Justinian I (533–534 AD)**, Arianism was suppressed.

#### Leovigild (Reigned 568–586 AD) – King of the Visigoths

- Last Arian king of the **Visigothic Kingdom in Spain**.
- Persecuted Nicene Christians, including his own son, **Hermengild**, who converted to Nicene Christianity and was executed.
- Tried to unify the kingdom under Arianism, but after his death, his successor **Reccared I (586–601 AD)** converted to Nicene Christianity, ending Arian dominance in Spain.

### Decline of Arianism as a Political Tool

- By the **7th century**, most Arian kingdoms had either **converted to Nicene Christianity** or were conquered by Nicene rulers.
- The **Byzantine Empire under Justinian I** (527–565 AD) actively fought Arian kingdoms, restoring Nicene Orthodoxy.
- The **Franks under Clovis I** (converted in 496 AD) played a key role in **eliminating Arian rule in Gaul**.
