Islam honours Jesus (*ʿĪsā*) more than any other prophet except Muhammad. It affirms a lot that Christians affirm, and denies the three things that matter most: that He is **God's Son**, that He **died on the cross**, and that He **rose**.

## 1. What the Qur'an Affirms

| Claim | Qur'an | Gospels |
|---|---|---|
| **Virgin birth** | 3:45-47; 19:16-22 (Mary under a palm tree) | Matt 1:18-25; Luke 1:26-38 |
| **Mary's holiness** | 3:42: "chosen thee above the women of the worlds". The only woman named in the Qur'an; Surah 19 is named after her | Luke 1:28, 42 |
| **"The Messiah"** (*al-Masīḥ*) | 3:45; 4:171 | Matt 16:16 |
| **"Word from God"** (*kalimah*) | 3:45; 4:171: "his word which he conveyed unto Mary" | John 1:1, 14 |
| **"A spirit from Him"** | 4:171 | Luke 1:35 |
| **Miracles** | 3:49: heals the blind and lepers, raises the dead, makes clay birds live (the last from the apocryphal *Infancy Gospel of Thomas*); 5:110 | throughout |
| **Speaks as an infant** | 19:29-33 | (from apocryphal sources) |
| **Sinless** | implied: "a faultless son" (19:19) | Heb 4:15 |
| **Taken up to God** | 4:158 | Acts 1:9 |
| **Will return** | hadith; the "sign of the Hour" (43:61) | Acts 1:11 |
| **The Injil** (Gospel) given to Him | 5:46 | |

## 2. What the Qur'an Denies

**Divinity and sonship**
- 4:171: "the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, was only a messenger of Allah... So believe in Allah and His messengers, and **say not 'Three'**... Allah is only one God. Far is it removed from His transcendent majesty that **He should have a son**."
- 5:72-73: "They surely disbelieve who say: Lo! Allah is the Messiah... They surely disbelieve who say: Lo! **Allah is the third of three**."
- 5:116: Allah asks Jesus: "didst thou say unto mankind: **Take me and my mother for two gods** beside Allah?"
- 112: "He begetteth not nor was begotten."
- 3:59: "the likeness of Jesus with Allah is as the likeness of Adam. He created him of dust." Created, not begotten.

**The crucifixion**
- 4:157-158: "And because of their saying: We slew the Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, Allah's messenger; **they slew him not nor crucified him, but it appeared so unto them** (*shubbiha lahum*)... But Allah took him up unto Himself."

## 3. The Crucifixion Denial

The single verse 4:157 is the only place, and it's ambiguous. Muslim interpretations:

| Theory | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Substitution** (most common) | God made someone else look like Jesus (Judas, Simon of Cyrene, a volunteer disciple), who was crucified instead. Jesus was raised alive |
| **Swoon** (Ahmadiyya) | Jesus was crucified but didn't die; revived, went to Kashmir |
| **Only "they" didn't kill him** | a minority reading: the Jews didn't kill Him, God did (cf. 8:17, "ye slew them not, but Allah slew them"); the death is real but its agent is God |

**Problems**
- **Historically**, the crucifixion is about as certain as anything in ancient history: Paul (within 20 years), all four Gospels, Josephus, Tacitus, the Talmud. Even sceptical scholars (Crossan, Ehrman, Lüdemann) accept it.
- The substitution theory makes **God the deceiver** of the disciples, Mary, and all of history; and makes Christianity, which He then allowed to spread, a product of His deception.
- The verse comes **600 years later**, with no historical source. It resembles a **Docetist / Gnostic** idea (Basilides: Simon of Cyrene was crucified in Jesus' place; *Second Treatise of the Great Seth*).
- Jesus predicted His death repeatedly: Mark 8:31; 9:31; 10:33-34. If the Qur'an confirms Him as a true prophet, these predictions should be true.
- The OT foretold it: Isa 53:5, "he was wounded for our transgressions"; Ps 22:16, "they pierced my hands and my feet".
- Even the Qur'an elsewhere: 19:33, Jesus: "Peace on me the day I was born, and **the day I die**, and the day I shall be raised alive." Order: born, dies, raised. Muslims usually place the death after His return.

## 4. Tawhid vs the Trinity

- **Tawhid**: absolute oneness of God. Associating anything with God (**shirk**) is the unforgivable sin (4:48).
- The Qur'an seems to understand the Trinity as **Father, Mother (Mary), Son** (5:116), or as three gods. Christians reject both. The Trinity is one God, one essence, three Persons, and Mary is no part of it. See [[The Trinity]].
- **Christian reply**: "God is love" (1 John 4:8) requires an object of love. A solitary God is love only after creating. In the Trinity, love is eternal: the Father loves the Son "before the foundation of the world" (John 17:24). The Islamic God depends on creation to love.
- **The Word**: the Qur'an calls Jesus God's Word. Mainstream Sunni theology also holds that the Qur'an is God's **uncreated** speech, eternal with God but not identical to Him. That's structurally close to the Logos (John 1:1). St. John of Damascus used this in debate: if God's Word is eternal, Jesus as the Word is eternal; if created, God was once wordless. See [[Council of Nicea: Arianism]].

## 5. The Corruption Claim (Tahrif)

**The problem for Islam**: the Qur'an affirms the Torah and Gospel as revealed and tells Christians to judge by them:
- 5:47: "Let the People of the Gospel **judge by that which Allah hath revealed therein**."
- 10:94: "if thou art in doubt concerning that which We reveal unto thee, then **question those who read the Scripture** (that was) before thee."
- 6:115: "**There is none who can change His words**."
- 5:68: "ye have naught till ye observe the Torah and the Gospel."

But the Gospels flatly contradict the Qur'an on the crucifixion and divinity. So later Muslim scholars (Ibn Hazm, 11th c.) argued the Bible was **corrupted** (*tahrif*).

**Problems**
- The manuscripts we have (Codex Sinaiticus, Vaticanus, papyri like P66, P75) **predate Muhammad** by centuries and say what our Bibles say. So the Gospel the Qur'an told Christians to judge by in the 7th century is the one we have.
- If it was corrupted before Muhammad, why does the Qur'an tell Christians to judge by it? If after, the manuscripts show it wasn't.
- 6:115 says God's words can't be changed. So either the Gospel wasn't changed, or God's words *can* be.
- This is the "Islamic dilemma". See [[Quran Confirms the Bible]].

## 6. Who Is Greater?

Even on the Qur'an's own terms, Jesus stands out:
- Born of a virgin (Muhammad wasn't).
- Called **Word** of God and **Spirit** from God (Muhammad isn't).
- Sinless; the Qur'an tells Muhammad to ask forgiveness for his sins (47:19; 48:2).
- Raised to God alive, and returning (Muhammad died and is buried in Medina).
- Raises the dead, gives life (the Qur'an itself, 3:49).

## 7. Notes

*Mine.*

- St. John of Damascus (*On Heresies* ch. 100, 8th c.) treated Islam as a **Christian heresy** (the "heresy of the Ishmaelites"), since it keeps so much about Christ but denies His divinity, like Arianism. That's still a good way to see it.
- The Qur'an's Jesus material shows contact with apocryphal and heretical Christianity (Infancy Gospels, Docetism, maybe Collyridians who worshipped Mary). That would explain the "Father, Mother, Son" picture.
- The sharpest point: Islam cuts out the cross and Resurrection, which is the centre of the Gospel. "If Christ be not raised, your faith is vain" (1 Cor 15:17). See [[Resurrection Verses]].
