A double standard is when two similar situations, actions, or people are judged by different sets of rules, usually unfairly—favoring one side while criticizing the other.

### Full Explanation

A double standard occurs when a principle, rule, or moral judgment is applied inconsistently: it is enforced strictly in one case but relaxed or entirely ignored in another. This inconsistency is not based on a meaningful difference between the two situations, but rather on **bias, favoritism, or hypocrisy**.

### Structure of a Double Standard

1. **Two similar things**: Comparable actions, people, or ideas.
2. **Two different judgments**: One is praised or excused, the other is blamed or condemned.
3. **No just cause for the difference**: The differential treatment isn’t based on relevant or objective criteria.

### Examples (with clear asymmetry)

1. **Moral Behavior**
    - A man is praised for dating multiple people as “experienced”
    - A woman is shamed for the same behavior as “immoral”
2. **Politics**
    - A politician condemns corruption in the opposing party
    - But ignores or excuses it within his own party
3. **Religion**
    - A Protestant criticizes Orthodox Christians for honoring tradition
    - While holding to **sola scriptura**, which itself is a man-made 16th-century tradition
