The basis for the **Eastern Orthodox (EO) belief** is fundamentally rooted in **Holy Tradition** (*ἱερὰ παράδοσις*), which consists of:

1. **Sacred Scripture (Ἡ Ἁγία Γραφή)** – The divinely inspired writings of the Old and New Testaments, particularly the **Septuagint (LXX) for the Old Testament**, and the Greek manuscripts of the New Testament. Scripture is not an isolated authority but part of the larger **living Tradition** of the Church.
2. **The Holy Fathers (Οἱ Ἅγιοι Πατέρες)** – The teachings of the Church Fathers, such as St. Athanasius, St. Basil the Great, St. Gregory the Theologian, St. John Chrysostom, St. Maximus the Confessor, St. John of Damascus, and St. Gregory Palamas. Their writings interpret Scripture and defend Orthodox doctrine against heresies.
3. **Ecumenical Councils (Οἱ Οἰκουμενικαί Σύνοδοι)** – The Seven Ecumenical Councils (325–787 AD) which established and defended key dogmas such as the Trinity, Christology, and the veneration of icons. These councils were guided by the Holy Spirit (John 16:13).
4. **Liturgical Worship and the Holy Mysteries (Λειτουργία καὶ Μυστήρια)** – The Orthodox liturgical life, especially the **Divine Liturgy**, embodies the faith through prayer, hymnography, and the **Eucharist**, which is the real presence of Christ. The Church does not separate theology from worship (*lex orandi, lex credendi*—"the rule of prayer is the rule of belief").
5. **The Lives of the Saints (Συναξάρια)** – The experiences and miracles of the saints confirm the truths of Orthodox Christianity, demonstrating that holiness (*θέωσις* – theosis) is possible through divine grace.
6. **Apostolic Succession (Ἀποστολικὴ Διαδοχή)** – The uninterrupted transmission of spiritual authority from Christ and the Apostles through the bishops of the Orthodox Church. This ensures doctrinal continuity.
7. **Hesychasm and the Vision of Uncreated Light** – The experience of the **Uncreated Light** (such as by St. Gregory Palamas and the hesychast saints) confirms Orthodox theology as not merely intellectual but **experiential** (*θεωρία* – theoria).

## Core Theological Foundations

- **Monarchical Trinitarianism** – The Father is the source (*ἀρχή*) of the Son and the Holy Spirit.
- **Christology** – Christ is one Person in two natures (divine and human), without confusion or separation (*Chalcedonian Definition*).
- **Theosis (Θέωσις)** – The purpose of human life is to partake in the divine nature (2 Peter 1:4 LXX).
- **Holy Icons (Ἅγια Εἰκόνες)** – Icons are venerated, not worshiped, as affirmed by the **Seventh Ecumenical Council (787 AD)**.

Eastern Orthodoxy does not rely on **one** single foundation (such as "Scripture alone") but sees all these elements as **one unified Tradition**—a living faith passed down by Christ through His Church.
