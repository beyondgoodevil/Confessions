St. Cyril of Alexandria used the term **"perichoresis"** (Greek: περιχώρησις) to describe the intimate, mutual indwelling and co-inherence of the persons of the Holy Trinity—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. This term is often translated as "mutual interpenetration" or "circumincession" and is a key concept in Christian theology, especially in understanding the relationship between the divine persons within the Trinity.

St. Cyril’s use of perichoresis was aimed at emphasizing the deep unity and inseparability of the three persons of the Trinity, while also maintaining their distinct personal properties. For Cyril, the perichoresis between the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit meant that they share the same divine essence (ousia) and are inseparably united in their actions and will, yet each person is distinct and retains their own personhood (hypostasis).

## Key points of St. Cyril's understanding of perichoresis

1. **Inseparable Union**: The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are not three separate gods but are united in one divine essence. They cannot be divided or separated from one another. This mutual indwelling is central to understanding how they exist together in perfect unity while being distinct persons.
2. **Mutual Interpenetration**: Each of the divine persons penetrates and indwells the others, meaning the Father is in the Son and the Son is in the Father, and the Holy Spirit is in both the Father and the Son. This interpenetration does not diminish the distinctness of each person but shows the perfect unity and relationship between them.
3. **Cooperation and Action**: According to Cyril, this unity of being and action means that the three persons of the Trinity work together in perfect harmony in all divine actions. For instance, in the work of salvation, the Father sends the Son, the Son redeems, and the Holy Spirit sanctifies, but these actions are inseparable and act in perfect cooperation due to the perichoresis of the divine persons.
4. **Opposition to Separation**: Cyril used the concept of perichoresis to reject any notion of separation or division between the divine persons. This was especially important in his Christological debates with Nestorius, who was accused of dividing Christ into two separate persons (the divine and the human). Cyril emphasized that the divine and human natures of Christ, while distinct, are inseparably united in the one person of Jesus Christ.

In summary, **perichoresis** refers to the profound interrelationship and mutual indwelling of the three persons of the Trinity, a concept that highlights their unity, inseparability, and co-operation in divine action. For St. Cyril of Alexandria, this doctrine was central to defending the orthodox Christian understanding of the Trinity and the nature of Christ against various heresies, particularly those that sought to separate or divide the divine persons.
