Emanationism is a philosophical and theological belief that all things originate from a single, divine source through a process of gradual outflow or "emanation," rather than through direct creation. In this view, the divine essence becomes progressively diluted as it flows into lower realms of existence.

Regarding the Trinity, emanationism typically distorts the orthodox understanding by treating the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as different levels or stages of this divine outflow, rather than as co-equal and co-eternal Persons of the same essence. This contradicts the Orthodox Christian doctrine, which teaches that the three Persons of the Trinity are consubstantial and exist in an eternal relationship of love, not as hierarchical emanations.
