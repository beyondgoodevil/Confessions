What did Adam's sin do to the rest of us? West and East agree that something was passed on and that we need Christ to be saved from it. They disagree on *what*: **guilt**, or **death**.

## 1. The Two Views

| | Original sin (Augustine, the West) | Ancestral sin (the Greek Fathers, Orthodoxy) |
|---|---|---|
| What is inherited | Adam's **guilt**, and a corrupted nature (concupiscence) | **death** and **corruption** (mortality, the passions, a weakened will) |
| Are we guilty of Adam's sin? | yes; all sinned "in Adam" | no; each is guilty only of his own sins |
| Newborn infants | guilty, liable to damnation without baptism (Augustine); later softened to limbo | not guilty; but born mortal and subject to corruption |
| What sin is | primarily a legal state, a debt | primarily a sickness, a loss of communion and life |
| Why we sin | because our nature is guilty and disordered | because we are mortal: fear of death drives us to sin |
| What Christ does | pays the debt, removes the guilt | destroys death, heals and deifies nature |

The Greek word is *propatorikon hamartema*, "the sin of the forefathers". The Orthodox usually avoid the phrase "original sin" for this reason.

## 2. Romans 5:12

"Wherefore, as by one man sin entered into the world, and death by sin; and so death passed upon all men, **for that all have sinned**" (KJV).

The whole question turns on three Greek words: ***eph' hō pantes hēmarton***.

| Reading | Translation | Who |
|---|---|---|
| ***in quo*** (relative pronoun, masculine) | "**in whom** all sinned", i.e. in Adam | the Latin Vulgate / Old Latin; Augustine, who couldn't read Greek well |
| ***eph' hō*** as a conjunction | "**because** all sinned" | most modern translations, KJV ("for that") |
| ***eph' hō*** with "death" (neuter/masc.) | "**because of which** (death) all sinned" | Romanides, following some Greek Fathers (Cyril of Alexandria, Theodoret) |

- On the Latin reading, all men were in Adam and sinned in him: so all share his guilt. That's the textual root of Augustine's doctrine.
- On the third reading, the order is the reverse: Adam's sin brought death, and **death** is why everyone sins. The fear of death and the drive for self-preservation produce the passions. Heb 2:14-15: Christ came to "deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage."
- Either way the Greek doesn't say "in whom". Most modern Catholic scholars (e.g. Fitzmyer) accept this, though they keep the doctrine on other grounds.

## 3. The Rest of Romans 5

- 5:14: "death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over them that had **not sinned after the similitude of Adam's transgression**". So those after Adam didn't commit his sin, yet died. Death is the inheritance.
- 5:17: "by one man's offence **death reigned** by one".
- 5:19: "by one man's disobedience many were **made sinners**". The West reads this as imputation. The East: made sinners because made mortal and so inclined to sin.
- 1 Cor 15:22: "as **in Adam all die**, even so in Christ shall all be made alive". The parallel is death and life, not guilt and acquittal. See [[Christ as the New Adam: Verses]].

## 4. Against Inherited Guilt in Scripture

- Ezek 18:20: "The soul that sinneth, it shall die. **The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father**."
- Deut 24:16: "the fathers shall not be put to death for the children... every man shall be put to death for his own sin."
- Jer 31:29-30: "they shall say no more, The fathers have eaten a sour grape, and the children's teeth are set on edge."
- Matt 19:14: "Suffer little children... for of such is the kingdom of heaven."

The Western reply: Exod 20:5 ("visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children") and Ps 51:5 ("in sin did my mother conceive me"). The Orthodox read Ps 51:5 as David speaking of the sinful *condition* he was born into, not inherited guilt.

## 5. The Greek Fathers

- **St. Irenaeus**: Adam was created as a child, meant to grow; the Fall is a stumble on the way. Death is what came in. See [[Doctrine of Recapitulation]].
- **St. Athanasius**, *On the Incarnation*: by transgression man turned toward non-being and **corruption**; the Word became flesh to undo corruption by death and resurrection. Nothing about guilt passing down.
- **St. John Chrysostom**, on Romans 5:19: "made sinners" means made liable to punishment and death, since it would make no sense for one to become a sinner by another's disobedience. And in a baptism homily: "we baptise infants, although they are not guilty of any sins."
- **St. Gregory of Nyssa**, *On Infants' Early Deaths*: infants who die aren't punished, since they've done nothing.
- **St. Maximus**: Adam's sin introduced *passibility, corruptibility and mortality* into nature; Christ took on this blameless passibility without sin.
- **St. Cyril of Alexandria**: we became sinners through Adam not by sharing his transgression, but because nature fell sick with the corruption of sin.

The Orthodox position in brief: the inheritance is in **nature** (mortality, corruption), and guilt belongs to the **person**. See [[Death and Corruptibility as Privations of Life and the Ability to Sin]].

## 6. Consequences

| Question | Augustinian answer | Orthodox answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Why baptise infants?** | to wash away inherited guilt | to give them new life in Christ, unite them to His Body, and free them from death's dominion. Not for guilt |
| **Unbaptised infants** | damned (Augustine), then limbo (medieval), now "hope" (2007 Catholic document) | in God's mercy; no punishment for others' sin |
| **The Theotokos** | must be preserved from original sin at conception: the **Immaculate Conception** (1854) | unnecessary: there's no guilt to be preserved from. She was born mortal like us and freely pure |
| **Christ's humanity** | sinless because conceived without male seed (which transmits sin) | He took on our mortal, passible nature, without sin |
| **The atonement** | the debt of guilt must be paid: Anselm, then penal substitution | death must be destroyed: Christ enters death and empties it. See [[Recapitulation Doctrine vs PSA]] |
| **Total depravity** (Calvin) | follows: the will is enslaved | rejected: the image remains; synergy. See [[Synergy: Grace and Free Will]] |

So the Immaculate Conception and penal substitution both rest on the Augustinian view. Remove inherited guilt and neither is needed.

## 7. Objections

**"If there's no inherited guilt, why does everyone sin?"**
- Because everyone is born mortal into a fallen world, with a weakened will and disordered passions. Sin is universal in fact, without being inherited as guilt.

**"Then babies don't need baptism."**
- They need it, but not for guilt. They need life and union with Christ. Baptism is more than forgiveness. See [[Augustinian View on Baptism]].

**"Isn't 'ancestral sin' just a Romanides novelty?"**
- The 20th-century term and its sharp East/West contrast come largely from Romanides (1957). But the content (inherited death, not guilt) is in Athanasius, Chrysostom, Cyril, Maximus. Some later Orthodox texts (e.g. Peter Mogila's 17th-century Confession) do use Latin language of inherited guilt; those are usually considered Western-influenced.

**"Death is the punishment for sin, so death implies guilt."**
- Death is the *consequence* of turning away from the source of life, not a sentence passed on each newborn. Like a branch cut off from the vine: it withers, it isn't charged. See [[The Fall of Humanity and Its Consequences]].

## 8. Notes

*Mine.*

- The distinction lines up with the person/nature distinction. Nature can be inherited; guilt is personal and can't be.
- It also changes how death is seen: in the West death is the penalty; in the East death is the enemy (1 Cor 15:26) and the root of sin. See [[1 Corinthians 15:26: The Last Enemy That Will Be Destroyed Is Death]].
