Genesis says God made man **"in Our image, according to Our likeness."** Many Fathers read these as two related gifts: the **image** is what every human being is given at creation, and the **likeness** is what each person is called to grow into. This distinction is the foundation of the Orthodox teaching on **theosis**. Here's how it works:

### 1. The Text

"Then God said, 'Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness...'" (Genesis 1:26)

"So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them." (Genesis 1:27)

- **Significance:** verse 26 states God's intention with both words, but verse 27, describing what God actually did, mentions only the **image**. Several Fathers saw in this that the image was given, while the likeness was left to be attained.

### 2. The Image: What We Are

The Fathers locate the image in what sets human beings apart and makes communion with God possible:

1. **Reason and the Nous**: the capacity to know God and the world (see [[Nous]]).
2. **Freedom**: the power of self-determination. St. John of Damascus says "after His image" refers to the mind and free will (*Exact Exposition*, book 2, ch. 12).
3. **Dominion**: man is set over creation as its priest and king (Genesis 1:26).

Christ Himself "is the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15). Man is made **according to** the Image, that is, according to the Son. The human person is an icon of the Icon.

### 3. The Likeness: What We Are Called to Become

- St. John of Damascus: "after His likeness" means likeness in **virtue**, as far as that is possible for man.
- St. Irenaeus (*Against Heresies*, book 5) connects the likeness with the gift of the **Holy Spirit**: the image belongs to man's nature, while the likeness is perfected by the Spirit.
- A saying attributed to St. Basil puts it this way: by creation we have the image; by our free choice we become the likeness.
- **Significance:** the likeness is not a static possession but a **direction of growth**. To acquire the likeness of God is to become by grace what God is by nature, which is theosis (see [[2 Peter 1:3-4: Theosis]]).

### 4. What the Fall Did

- **The Image Is Darkened, Not Destroyed**: after the Fall the image remains, but it is obscured, like a painting covered with grime. Human beings still have reason, freedom and the capacity for God.
- **The Likeness Is Lost**: the movement toward God was turned aside, and death and corruption entered (see [[Death and Corruptibility as Privations of Life and the Ability to Sin]] and [[Adam and Eve Sin]]).
- **Contrast with Total Depravity**: the Reformed teaching that fallen nature is wholly corrupted goes further than the Fathers. For the Orthodox, the image cannot be lost, which is why every human being retains a dignity no sin can erase.

### 5. How Christ Restores the Image

- St. Athanasius' illustration (*On the Incarnation*, 14): when a portrait painted on wood has been ruined, the subject of the portrait must come again so that the image can be renewed on the same wood. So the Word, the original Image, came Himself to renew the image in man (see [[St. Athanasius, On the Incarnation: Main Argument]]).
- In baptism the image is cleansed; in the life of the Church, through the sacraments and the struggle for virtue, the likeness is acquired.
- "But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord." (2 Corinthians 3:18)
- "We know that when He is revealed, we shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is." (1 John 3:2)

### 6. Why the Distinction Matters

1. **Human Dignity**: every person bears the image, including the unbeliever and the sinner, so every person is to be honoured as an icon of God.
2. **Freedom and Synergy**: because the likeness is reached through free cooperation with grace, salvation is not imposed (see [[Synergy: Grace and Free Will]]).
3. **Salvation as Growth**: salvation is not only being declared righteous, but being **transformed** from glory to glory.

Not every Father separates the two words; some, including St. Athanasius, often use them together as one idea. But the distinction is widespread and shapes the whole Orthodox account of the spiritual life.

### Conclusion

Man was created in the image of God, with reason, freedom and dominion, and was called to grow into His likeness through virtue and the Holy Spirit. The Fall darkened the image and turned man away from the likeness, but it did not destroy the image. Christ, the true Image of the Father, came to renew it, and in the Church the faithful are transformed into His likeness from glory to glory. This is the meaning of theosis.
