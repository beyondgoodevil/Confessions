## 3. The High Middle Ages (12th–13th Century) – Popes as Pawns in European Politics

- **English Kings and the Papacy**
    - **King John of England (Reigned 1199–1216)**
        - Excommunicated by **Pope Innocent III** over a dispute concerning the appointment of the **Archbishop of Canterbury**.
        - To regain favor, John **surrendered England to the Pope** and received it back as a **papal fief**, effectively making England a **vassal of the Pope** (1213).
        - This was a political move to gain papal support against rebellious English barons.
- **French Kings and the Papacy**
    - **Philip IV ("the Fair") of France (Reigned 1285–1314)**
        - Used **Pope Clement V** as a tool by pressuring him to **move the Papacy to Avignon (1309–1377)** in what became known as the **Avignon Papacy**.
        - This allowed France to **control the Pope** and use him for **French political interests**.
        - Philip also pressured **Clement V** into **dissolving the Knights Templar (1312)** and seizing their wealth.

## 4. The Renaissance Papacy (15th–16th Century) – Popes as Political Tools of Families

- **Spanish and French Influence over the Papacy**
    - **During the Renaissance, Popes were controlled by powerful Italian families** (e.g., the Borgias, Medicis).
    - **Pope Alexander VI (Borgia, 1492–1503)**
        - Used by **Spain** (his home country) to advance Spanish interests in Italy.
        - His **son Cesare Borgia** used papal power to carve out a personal kingdom in Italy.
- **Habsburg Control of the Pope**
    - **Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor (Reigned 1519–1556)**
        - Used **Pope Clement VII** to try to prevent the annulment of **King Henry VIII’s marriage**, leading to the English Reformation.
        - Pope Clement VII was **politically weak**, caught between the **French and the Habsburgs**.
