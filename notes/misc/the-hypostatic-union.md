## 1. The Hypostatic Union

- Christ is **one divine Person (hypostasis), the Second Person of the Holy Trinity, the Son of God**.
- He possesses **two natures**:
    - **Divine nature**: Fully God, consubstantial (ὁμοούσιος) with the Father.
    - **Human nature**: Fully man, consubstantial with us, taken from the Theotokos.
- These **two natures** exist **without confusion, change, division, or separation** (ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιαιρέτως, ἀχωρίστως)—**the Chalcedonian Definition**.

## 2. The Distinction Between Nature and Person

- **Nature (οὐσία, φύσις)** answers **what** Christ is: He is fully God and fully man.
- **Person (ὑπόστασις)** answers **who** Christ is: He is the one eternal Son of God.
- His divine Person **pre-exists eternally** and assumed human nature **in time**.

## 3. Refuting Confusions About Christ’s Identity

If one denies the distinction between **nature and person**, it leads to serious **Christological errors**:

### A. If Christ were two persons (Nestorianism)

- He would not be a single subject of worship and salvation.
- The Son of God and the man Jesus would be separate, meaning **God did not truly die on the Cross**.

### B. If Christ had only one nature (Monophysitism)

- Either His humanity would be absorbed into His divinity (like a drop in the ocean), making Him no longer truly human.  
  Or His divinity would be diminished, making Him a creature.

### C. If there were no distinction between nature and person

- Every **human person** would be the same being, as all men share the same **human nature**.
- This would lead to absurdity: If **nature and person were identical**, then **all humans would be one person**.
