The Spirit is the least talked-about Person in a lot of Western theology. In Orthodoxy He is everywhere: every service starts with a prayer to Him ("O Heavenly King"), every sacrament is completed by calling Him down, and the goal of the Christian life is described as **acquiring** Him.

## 1. The Spirit Is God

**Scripture**
- Acts 5:3-4: "why hath Satan filled thine heart **to lie to the Holy Ghost**... thou hast not lied unto men, but **unto God**."
- 1 Cor 2:10-11: "**the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God**... the things of God knoweth no man, but the Spirit of God."
- 1 Cor 3:16: "ye are **the temple of God**, and that **the Spirit of God dwelleth in you**." Indwelt by the Spirit = temple of God.
- Matt 28:19: baptising "in the **name** (singular) of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost."
- Ps 139:7: "Whither shall I go from **thy spirit**?" Omnipresent.
- Heb 9:14: "**the eternal Spirit**."
- Matt 12:31: blasphemy against the Spirit is unforgivable.

**History**
- 4th c.: the **Pneumatomachians** ("Spirit-fighters", or Macedonians) accepted the Son's divinity but called the Spirit a creature, a higher angel.
- **St. Basil, *On the Holy Spirit*** (375): defends giving the Spirit the same glory as the Father and Son. The doxology "Glory to the Father *with* the Son *together with* the Holy Spirit". Doesn't quite say "God" outright, for pastoral reasons, but means it.
- **St. Gregory Nazianzen**, *Fifth Theological Oration*: the OT revealed the Father clearly and the Son dimly; the NT revealed the Son and suggested the Spirit's divinity; now the Spirit dwells among us and gives us clearer knowledge of Himself. Revelation is gradual.
- **Council of Constantinople (381)** expanded the Creed: "the Lord, the Giver of Life, who proceeds from the Father, who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified, who spoke by the prophets."

## 2. Procession: From the Father

| | Orthodox | Filioque (West) |
|---|---|---|
| Creed | "proceeds from the Father" (John 15:26) | "proceeds from the Father **and the Son**" |
| Eternal source | the Father alone, the one cause (*monarchia*) | Father and Son as one principle |
| The Son's role | the Spirit is **sent** through the Son in time, and in eternity "rests on" the Son / shines through Him | the Son is co-cause of the Spirit's existence |

- John 15:26: "the Comforter... whom **I will send** unto you **from the Father**, even the Spirit of truth, **which proceedeth from the Father**." Two different verbs: *pempsō* (send, in time) and *ekporeuetai* (proceed, eternal origin). Lossky's point: the Filioque confuses the two.
- The Spirit is called the "Spirit of the Son" (Gal 4:6) and "Spirit of Christ" (Rom 8:9): that's about mission and manifestation, not origin.
- The Filioque was added in Spain (Toledo, 589), pushed by the Franks, and adopted in Rome only around 1014. See [[Filioque and the Franks]], [[EO vs RC Trinity]].
- Why it matters (Orthodox view): it makes the Spirit secondary, a bond of love between Father and Son, and in the Church a function of Christ's Body rather than a Person giving Himself to each. See [[Monarchical Trinitarianism]], [[St. Maximus on the Holy Spirit]].
- St. Gregory of Cyprus and St. Gregory Palamas: the Spirit's **eternal manifestation** through the Son (as energy) is distinct from His **existence** from the Father alone. That gives a sense in which "through the Son" is true.

## 3. The Spirit in the Old Testament

- Creation: "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters" (Gen 1:2). Ps 104:30: "Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created."
- Life: "the breath of life" (Gen 2:7). Job 33:4: "The Spirit of God hath made me."
- Prophecy: "who spoke by the prophets" (Creed); 2 Pet 1:21: "holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost."
- On individuals, for a task: Bezaleel (Exod 31:3), the judges, Saul, David (1 Sam 16:13).
- The promise: Joel 2:28, "I will pour out my spirit **upon all flesh**"; Ezek 36:27, "I will put my spirit within you"; Ezek 37, the dry bones.

Before Pentecost the Spirit came **on** certain people for certain tasks. After, He **dwells in** all the baptised.

## 4. Pentecost

- Acts 2:1-4: the sound of wind, "cloven tongues like as of fire" resting on **each** of them, all filled.
- Peter: "this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel" (Acts 2:16).
- **Reversal of Babel** (Gen 11): there, one language split into many and people were scattered; here, many languages understood, people gathered. The Pentecost kontakion says exactly this.
- **Fulfilment of Sinai**: Pentecost (50 days after Passover) was the feast of the giving of the Law. Now the law is written on hearts (Jer 31:33; 2 Cor 3:3).
- The birth of the Church. See [[Christ Set Up His Church at Pentecost, and the Church Is Referred to as His]].
- Lossky: Christ unites human **nature** in Himself; at Pentecost the Spirit is given to each **person**, the tongues dividing to rest on each one.

## 5. The Spirit in the Church

- John 16:13: "when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, **he will guide you into all truth**." See [[Jesus Promises That the Holy Spirit Will Guide the Church]].
- Acts 15:28: the Council of Jerusalem: "**it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us**." The model for councils.
- **Tradition** is the life of the Spirit in the Church (Lossky). Not a deposit of documents but the Spirit's continuing presence. See [[2 Thessalonians 2: Tradition and Holy Spirit]].
- **Irenaeus**: "where the Church is, there is the Spirit of God; and where the Spirit of God is, there is the Church."
- The **gifts** (1 Cor 12): different gifts, "but the same Spirit". Unity without uniformity.

## 6. The Spirit in the Sacraments

Every Orthodox sacrament has an **epiclesis**, a calling-down of the Spirit.

| Sacrament | The Spirit's work |
|---|---|
| **Baptism** | "born of water and of the Spirit" (John 3:5); the water is blessed by calling the Spirit on it |
| **Chrismation** | "the seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit" (2 Cor 1:21-22; Acts 8:14-17). Each person's own Pentecost |
| **Eucharist** | the epiclesis: "send down Thy Holy Spirit upon us and upon these gifts here offered", "making them" the Body and Blood. See [[The Divine Liturgy Explained]] |
| **Ordination** | "The divine grace, which always heals that which is infirm..." |
| **Confession, unction, marriage** | each has its prayer for the Spirit |

- West vs East on the Eucharist: Latin theology tended to locate the change at the **words of institution**; Orthodoxy says the Spirit makes the change, with the whole anaphora.

## 7. Acquiring the Spirit

- **St. Seraphim of Sarov** to Motovilov: "the true aim of our Christian life consists in the **acquisition of the Holy Spirit of God**." Prayer, fasting, almsgiving are means, not the goal.
- Gal 5:22-23: the **fruit** of the Spirit, "love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance."
- Rom 8:14-16: "as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God... the Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit."
- Rom 8:26: "the Spirit itself maketh intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered."
- Theosis is the Spirit's work in us, conforming us to Christ. See [[Theosis: Becoming by Grace What God Is by Nature]].
- Lossky: the Spirit "effaces Himself" as a Person in the economy: He makes Christ known rather than Himself (John 16:14, "He shall glorify me"). So the Spirit has no icon of His own except the saints.

## 8. "O Heavenly King"

The prayer that begins almost every Orthodox service:

> O Heavenly King, the Comforter, the Spirit of Truth, who art everywhere present and fillest all things, Treasury of blessings and Giver of life: come and abide in us, and cleanse us from every impurity, and save our souls, O Good One.

Every phrase is Scripture or the Creed: Comforter (John 14:16), Spirit of Truth (John 14:17), everywhere present (Ps 139:7), Giver of life (Creed; John 6:63).
