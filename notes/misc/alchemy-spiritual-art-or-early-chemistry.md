The popular picture of alchemy goes like this:
- turning lead into gold was only a cover;
- the real work was the transformation of the alchemist's soul;
- the strange images (dragons, kings and queens, the chymical wedding) are symbols of an inner journey.

That picture is mostly Jung's, and before him the Victorian occultists'. Over the last forty years historians of science (Principe, Newman and others) have taken it apart. This note sets the two side by side.

## 1. What Alchemy Was (Principe)

**Three periods:**
1. **Greco-Egyptian** *chemeia* (1st–4th centuries AD)
2. **Arabic** *al-kīmiyā'* (8th–12th)
3. **Latin** *alchemia* (12th–17th)

The *al-* is the Arabic article, a sign of the route the subject took.

**Origins in workshops**: the Leiden and Stockholm papyri, about 250 recipes for dyeing, imitating gold and silver, making gems. **Zosimos** of Panopolis (c. 300) turned this into a theory of transmutation: metals have a "body" and a "spirit" (*pneuma*); change the spirit and you "tinge" (*baphē*) the metal.

**Mercury–sulfur theory** (Jābir, Arabic): all metals are made of two principles, a "mercury" and a "sulfur", in different proportions and purities. Gold is the perfect mixture. So transmutation is in principle **natural**: speed up and correct what nature does slowly underground.

**Goals.** Besides gold making (*chrysopoeia*), alchemy made medicines (*chemiatria*, Paracelsus), dyes, pigments, acids, glass, salts, alcohol ("water of life"). It was often hard to tell where alchemy stopped and practical chemistry began.

**"Chymistry."** Before about 1700, *alchemy* and *chemistry* were interchangeable. Libavius's *Alchemia* (1597) is a lab manual with little gold making. Historians now use the old spelling "chymistry" to avoid reading the later split back.

**Secrecy and Decknamen** ("cover names"):
- Zosimos already says alchemical writers "call a single thing by many names while they call many things by a single name".
- "Our lead" means antimony ore; "the green lion", "the grey wolf", "the king" (gold), "Diana" (silver), "Saturn" (lead), "Mars" (iron).
- Jābir's method of **dispersal**: "to present knowledge by cutting it up and dispersing it into many places".
- Principe's key point: Decknamen "simultaneously conceal and reveal". They have to be **logical**, not arbitrary, or no one could decode them, "and if the intent were to conceal information entirely, it would be far simpler for alchemists to have written nothing at all".

## 2. How Alchemy Lost Its Reputation (Principe ch. 4)

- **Around 1720 gold making collapsed**, though no experiment had shown it impossible. What happened was an image problem.
- Chymistry, newly professionalized (five places at the Paris Academy of Sciences from 1699), wanted respectability. So it **quarantined** the embarrassing part (transmutation, the Stone) under the name "alchemy" and called it fraud, keeping everything useful as "chemistry". "Alchemy became the scapegoat for chymistry's sins."
- **Geoffroy's 1722 exposé** of alchemical cheats was copied from a century-old book written *for* alchemists. His library was full of transmutation books, and he seems to have gone on experimenting quietly.
- **The Enlightenment** put alchemy in one bin with witchcraft, astrology and necromancy: Adelung's *History of Human Foolishness*. Historically most alchemists had nothing to do with magic. They thought they were working with nature.
- **James Price** (1782) claimed transmutations, was challenged by the Royal Society, and poisoned himself on the day of the demonstration.

## 3. The Invention of "Spiritual Alchemy"

**Mary Anne Atwood**, *A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery* (1850):
- Alchemy was never chemistry. Lab language is "wisdom's envelope".
- The real matter is a universal **ether**; the vessel is the alchemist himself, who in a trance draws it in and condenses it into the Stone. "Man is the true laboratory of the Hermetic Art."
- **Source**: the English craze for **Mesmerism** in the 1840s. Atwood's "ether" is Mesmer's magnetic fluid; the trance is "magnetic somnambulism". Her father had already found Mesmerism hidden in the Homeric Hymns.
- She bought up the print run and burned it on the lawn, but the surviving copies were widely read.
- Principe: "As a historical interpretation or description of pre-nineteenth-century alchemy, however, it is simply wrong."

**Ethan Allen Hitchcock**, US general, *Remarks upon Alchemy and the Alchemists* (1857): alchemy is a moral and Christian allegory. "Man was the subject of Alchemy." Mercury is a clean conscience; the Stone is holiness. Secrecy was needed because of the "intolerance of the Middle Ages", though he never explains why preaching morality would have been heretical.

**The occult revival**: Westcott (the Golden Dawn, 1888), Blavatsky's Theosophy, A. E. Waite. Waite took a "middle course": physical processes as outer forms of a universal spiritual development. Then in 1926 he reversed himself: before Luther there is "no vestige" of alchemy as anything but "a record of experimental physics".

**The exoteric/esoteric split** (outer chemical language, inner spiritual meaning) came from Atwood and was taken over, unknowingly, by many 20th-century historians of science.

## 4. Jung: Alchemy as Projection

**The claim** (*Psychology and Alchemy*, 1944):
- Alchemy "does not deal at all, or for the most part at least, with chemical experiments, but probably with something like psychic processes expressed in pseudochemical language".
- In the dark of the lab, staring into the vessel, the alchemist **projected** the contents of his unconscious onto the matter. "During the practical work, hallucinatory or visionary perceptions take place, which can be nothing other than projections of unconscious contents."
- The experience "had nothing to do with matter in itself".

**Why he thinks so:**
- Part II of the book: about 400 dreams of a young scientist (in fact the physicist Wolfgang Pauli) who knew nothing of alchemy, yet dreamed alchemical images: mandalas, the quaternity, the world clock.
- The same images turn up in all periods and cultures. For Jung that points to the **collective unconscious** and its archetypes.

**The opus as individuation**: the stages of the work map onto psychological development.
- ***nigredo*** (blackening, putrefaction, the "raven's head") = meeting the **shadow**, depression, dissolution
- ***albedo*** (whitening) = purification, the anima
- ***rubedo*** (reddening) = completion
- the ***coniunctio***, the chymical wedding of king and queen, sun and moon = **union of opposites**, conscious and unconscious
- the **Stone** (*lapis*) = the **self**, the whole psyche

**The axiom of Maria Prophetissa**: "One becomes two, two becomes three, and out of the third comes the one as the fourth". Jung reads it as the move from three to four: the missing fourth function, the feminine, the shadow, completing the Trinity into a quaternity.

**Other key texts in Jung:**
- "*Aurum nostrum non est aurum vulgi*" (our gold is not common gold)
- "*tam ethice quam physice*" (as much ethically as physically)
- "the philosopher is not the master of the stone but rather its minister" (*Rosarium*)
- **Gerhard Dorn**: the operator "must accomplish in his own self the same process that he attributes to matter".

**The lapis–Christ parallel** (Part III.5). Medieval alchemists compared the Stone with Christ: it dies, is buried, rises glorified, and redeems base matter.
- **Petrus Bonus** (14th century) says the ancients foresaw the Virgin birth in the Stone.
- **Melchior Cibinensis** (16th century) writes the opus as a Mass.
- ***Aurora consurgens*** (14th century, falsely attributed to Aquinas) is a Song of Songs of Wisdom (Sapientia) and the Stone. Jung and von Franz half-accepted the Aquinas attribution.

Jung's twist: the Christian receives grace *ex opere operato*, through the Church's rites, but the alchemist works *ex opere operantis*, "in the most literal sense". He is a "redeemer" of the spirit trapped in matter, completing what Christ left undone. Alchemy is an **undercurrent** compensating for the Church's one-sidedness: it takes in matter, the feminine, evil, the body.

**On Christianity directly** (Part I): many Christians have "all God outside" and are "undeveloped and unchanged in [their] inmost soul". An archetype differs from Christian allegory in that "the patristic allegory *ad Christum spectat*... whereas the psychic archetype is simply itself".

## 5. Eliade: Alchemy as Initiation

*The Forge and the Crucible* (1956):
- Alchemy continues the archaic **sacred crafts** of miners and smiths, with ores growing in the womb of Mother Earth (*terra mater*) and the smith as "master of fire".
- "The alchemist, while pursuing the 'perfecting' of the metal, its 'transformation' into gold, pursued in fact his own perfection."
- The matter is treated like the god of the mysteries. It "suffers", "dies" and "is reborn". *Nigredo* is initiatory death; the *regressus ad uterum* (return to the womb) and *coincidentia oppositorum* are universal religious symbols.
- **Time**: alchemists wanted to do "better and faster than Nature", speeding up the slow maturing of metals. Modern technology inherits this ambition "without... the liturgical dimension which in other societies made work bearable".
- Principe groups Eliade with Jung. Both were influenced by occultism; both separate alchemy from chemistry; both claim that "his real quest concerned the soul".

## 6. The Historians' Answer (Principe ch. 6; Newman)

**1. Decoding the texts.** Read with chemical knowledge, the allegories turn out to be **recipes**.
- **Basil Valentine's *Twelve Keys***: "the king" is gold, "the grey wolf" is stibnite (antimony ore, which purifies gold). The emblems describe dissolving gold in aqua regia ("the salty sea") and distilling it off again.
- **Zosimos's "Visions"** (men of copper, an altar like a vessel) are "personified Decknamen woven into a coherent and extended narrative": allegorized distillation, not trance experiences.

**2. Replicating the experiments.** Principe redid the processes in his own lab:
- Valentine's glass of antimony, which "doesn't work" with modern pure reagents, works exactly as described with real ore. His iron tools added iron compounds.
- **Starkey's "Philosophical Mercury" with gold**, sealed in a "philosophical egg", **grew a metallic tree**, branching and silvery, just as Starkey ("Eirenaeus Philalethes") described it, "like coral". What Jung would read as a projected vision was a real chemical result.
- So many "symbolic" descriptions are accurate reports of what alchemists saw in the flask.

**3. Newton.** William Newman, *Newton the Alchemist*:
- Newton left over a million words on alchemy. Keynes (1947): "Newton was not the first of the age of reason. He was the last of the magicians."
- **Dobbs and others** tied it to his heterodox anti-Trinitarian religion and a lost "ancient wisdom". Newman: "Few topics in Newton scholarship have led to more misleading claims." Newton's rules for reading prophecy reject "private imagination"; his alchemy is decoding plus **experiment**, not mysticism.
- Newton spent decades decoding Philalethes, Snyders, Flamel and Sendivogius, compiling dictionaries of Decknamen ("Jove's sceptre", "the doves of Diana", "the net"), and testing them at his furnace.
- **His 1693 breakdown** has been blamed on mercury poisoning or on an alchemical "delusion". Newman (ch. 18): the late manuscript *Praxis* is a rational, if over-optimistic, attempt to decode texts.
- It was **part of his science**. Boyle's chymical analysis and resynthesis shaped Newton's optics; his "active principles" (fermentation, attraction between particles) in the *Opticks* queries come from his chymistry.
- Epilogue: Newton was not irrational. Nowhere else does he combine "textual scholarship and experiment" to the same degree.

**4. The verdict.** Principe: the Jungian, Eliadean and occultist claims about alchemy's "true nature" "are simply not supported by the historical record, and therefore... they are now rejected by historians of science as valid descriptions of alchemy". These ideas belong to the history of the 19th and 20th centuries, not to the alchemists.

## 7. Where Religion Really Did Enter

Principe doesn't deny that alchemy had religious dimensions. They were simply not the Jungian ones.
- **Divine gift** (*donum Dei*). Starkey's notebook: "At Bristol, on 20 March 1656, God revealed to me the whole secret of the liquor alkahest". That doesn't mean ecstasy. It means a believer giving thanks for a eureka moment, with God present in ordinary work: "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights" (James 1:17).
- **Christian legitimation** (14th century). John of Rupescissa, a Franciscan, wrote on the "quintessence" (alcohol) as medicine for the coming tribulations of Antichrist. Pseudonymous works appeared under Lull, Arnald of Villanova and Aquinas.
- **Paracelsian theology**:
  - *spagyria*, separating and recombining, mirrors death, purification and resurrection;
  - God is "a chymist at both the beginning and the end of time";
  - since poisons entered the world with the Fall, making medicines returns things to their prelapsarian state, so the chymist is "a co-redeemer of a fallen world";
  - Böhme and the Rosicrucians take this further.
- **Analogies used by preachers**: purification, distillation and the refiner's fire. "He is like a refiner's fire... And he shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver" (Mal 3:2–3); "I have refined thee, but not with silver; I have chosen thee in the furnace of affliction" (Isa 48:10).

**So**: chymists were Christians who sometimes drew spiritual parallels from their work, the way any trade does. They were not secretly doing psychology.

## 8. Comparison

| | **spiritual / psychological reading** (Atwood, Jung, Eliade) | **historical / chymical reading** (Principe, Newman) |
|---|---|---|
| what the texts describe | inner states projected or symbolized | laboratory processes in code |
| why the strange language | the unconscious speaks in images; initiation secrecy | trade secrecy, Decknamen, dispersal; protecting economies and the unworthy |
| the Stone | the Self; inner gold | a real (hoped-for) transmuting agent |
| relation to chemistry | opposite; chemistry is "merely physical" | continuous; much of chemistry was made by alchemists |
| where the idea comes from | Mesmerism, Victorian occultism, Jung's own séance research | the sources read with chemical knowledge and tested in the lab |
| best evidence | the universality of the images; Pauli's dreams | replications that work; Newton's notebooks |

## 9. Assessment

*Mine.*

**On the history**: Principe and Newman win. The "spiritual alchemy" thesis has a traceable 19th-century origin, and the replications are decisive: images Jung read as projections describe real chemical events. It is the same mistake Dodds warned about with Orphism: projecting modern religious longings onto the past. See [[Dionysus, Orpheus and the Mysteries: Dodds and the Irrational]].

**On Jung's psychology**:
- He is an acute observer of imagery, and the parallels he collected are real. But "collective unconscious" explains the parallels in a way that can't be tested.
- **The Christian problem**:
  - He makes Christ one symbol of the Self among others ("the psychic archetype is simply itself").
  - He wants God to integrate the shadow and evil into a quaternity.
  - He sets "ex opere operantis" self-redemption beside grace.

  That's Gnosticism in psychological form, as he half admits by calling the Gnostics his precursors (cf. *Answer to Job*). The Fathers' answer is the same one given to the Gnostics: God is light "and in him is no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5). Evil is a privation, not a quarter of God to be integrated. See [[Death and Corruptibility as Privations of Life and the Ability to Sin]].
- **"All God outside"** is a fair criticism of formal religion, but Orthodoxy already has the answer: the Kingdom "within you" (Luke 17:21), the prayer of the heart, deification. It is real union with God by grace, not integration of an inner archetype. See [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]], [[Theosis: Becoming by Grace What God Is by Nature]].

**On Eliade**: the link between alchemy and the sacred crafts and the transformation of matter is suggestive. The theological point worth keeping is about **matter**. The Orthodox view of creation as good and destined for transfiguration (the Eucharist, relics, icons, the "new earth") is what the Paracelsian chymists were reaching for in their talk of redeemed matter. It comes by grace through Christ's body, not by the adept's art. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].

**On Newton**: a useful corrective to the Enlightenment myth that science and "superstition" are opposites. The same mind, the same method. Compare [[Emergence of Magic and Neoplatonism in the Renaissance]].
