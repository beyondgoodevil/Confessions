**Prior Assumptions** and **Presuppositions** are foundational beliefs or ideas that shape our thinking but are typically **unexamined** and **implicit**. They form the base for further reasoning or understanding.

## Key Differences

- **Prior Assumptions:**  
  These are beliefs we hold **before** analyzing a topic. They guide how we interpret information. For example, assuming the world operates by consistent laws affects how we approach scientific experiments.
- **Presuppositions:**  
  These are **necessary background beliefs** that must be in place for reasoning or communication to be possible. For example, assuming that words have meaning or that others understand language is crucial for any conversation to happen.

## Examples

1. **Science:**  
   The belief that **natural laws govern the universe** is a prior assumption in science.
2. **Philosophy:**  
   Philosophers might presuppose that **logic is valid** or that humans can reason correctly.
3. **Everyday Life:**  
   We presuppose that **social norms** (like greeting others) are shared by people around us.

## Why It Matters

Recognizing prior assumptions and presuppositions helps us understand why we think the way we do and allows us to critically evaluate our reasoning.
