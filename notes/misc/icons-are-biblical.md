---
title: Icons are biblical
tags: [theology, icons-saints, scripture]
summary: Ten biblical cases of sacred images and material things used by God.
---

1. God commands the use of sacred images:
    - **Exodus 25:18-22:** God instructs Moses to make two cherubim over the Ark of the Covenant. These are images, but they are not idols—they serve a holy purpose.
2. Bronze serpent:
    - **Numbers 21:8-9:** God commands Moses to make a bronze serpent, which is used to heal the people, demonstrating that images can be used as instruments of divine grace, not objects of worship.
3. Solomon's Temple:
    - **1 Kings 6:23-29:** The temple built by Solomon, which God approves, contains numerous images of cherubim, palm trees, and flowers. These were part of worship but were not worshipped themselves.
4. Aaron's breastplate:
    - **Exodus 28:15-22:** The high priest's breastplate contains engraved stones with the names of the tribes of Israel. These engraved items are part of the sacred vestments for worship.
5. The Ark of the Covenant:
    - **1 Samuel 6:19-20:** The Israelites did not worship the Ark, though it contained sacred items and images (the cherubim). The Ark itself was revered, not worshipped.
6. God's glory fills the Temple with images:
    - **2 Chronicles 3:10-14:** Solomon decorates the Temple with images of cherubim and other creatures, but these images were not considered idols.
7. Revelation vision of Heaven:
    - **Revelation 4:6-8:** In John's vision of Heaven, there are images of creatures around God's throne. These are heavenly realities depicted in images, showing the use of images even in the divine realm.
8. Veneration of relics:
    - **2 Kings 13:20-21:** A dead man is raised to life when his body touches the bones of Elisha. This shows that God can work through material objects without those objects becoming idols.
9. God works through material things:
    - **Acts 19:11-12:** People are healed through handkerchiefs and aprons that had touched Paul. The objects are venerated for their connection to God's power, but they are not worshipped.
10. Incarnation of Christ:
    - **John 1:14:** "The Word became flesh." If God Himself can take on material form in Christ, then the material world is not inherently evil, and physical representations of holy figures can serve as a reminder of spiritual realities, not idols.
