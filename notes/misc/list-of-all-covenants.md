## 1. The Covenant with Adam (Adamic Covenant)

Passage: Genesis 1:28-30, Genesis 2:16-17  
This is often considered a **two-part covenant**:

- **Covenant of Creation:** God's command to Adam and Eve to be fruitful, multiply, fill the earth, and have dominion over it (Genesis 1:28). Adam was given dominion as a priestly figure over creation, tasked with tending to it in harmony with God's will.
- **Covenant of Life:** The warning that disobedience (eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil) would result in death (Genesis 2:16-17). This covenant reflects humanity's role in maintaining communion with God and the consequences of breaking that communion, which introduced death and corruption into the world. Adam's failure signifies the need for a greater restoration through Christ.

## 2. The Covenant with Noah (Noahic Covenant)

Passage: Genesis 9:8-17  
This is an **unconditional covenant** where God promises never to destroy the earth with a flood again. The **rainbow** is given as a sign of this covenant with all creation. The Noahic Covenant reaffirms God's sovereignty over creation and His ongoing relationship with it, even after the corruption brought by the Fall. This covenant foreshadows the eventual restoration of the cosmos, anticipating the ultimate renewal through Christ.

## 3. The Covenant with Abraham (Abrahamic Covenant)

Passage: Genesis 12:1-3, Genesis 15, Genesis 17:1-14  
God makes several promises to Abraham, including:

- The promise of a great nation (Israel), through whom the blessings would come to all nations.
- Blessings for Abraham and his descendants.
- The land of Canaan as an everlasting possession.
- The **covenant of circumcision** as a sign.  
  This covenant establishes Abraham as the father of a people who would carry the divine promise, culminating in the coming of the Messiah. The blessings promised to Abraham are ultimately realized through Christ, the true seed of Abraham, who brings salvation to all nations.

## 4. The Covenant with Moses (Mosaic Covenant)

Passage: Exodus 19-24, Deuteronomy  
This is a **conditional covenant** made at Mount Sinai, where God gave Israel the **Law** (the Ten Commandments and other laws) and promised to bless them if they obeyed. Disobedience, however, would bring curses. The Mosaic Law governed Israel's relationship with God, serving as a guide toward righteousness and maintaining Israel's identity as God's chosen people. The Law is understood as a **pedagogue** leading to Christ, preparing humanity for the full revelation of God's will in the New Covenant.

## 5. The Covenant with David (Davidic Covenant)

Passage: 2 Samuel 7:12-16, 1 Chronicles 17:11-14  
God promises David that his descendants would reign on the throne of Israel forever. This is an **unconditional covenant**, with the promise that a king from David's line would establish an eternal kingdom. This is ultimately fulfilled in **Jesus Christ**, the eternal King and the true Son of David, whose kingdom is not of this world but transcends it, ruling in the hearts of believers and culminating in His final reign over all creation.

## 6. The New Covenant (Covenant of Christ)

Passage: Jeremiah 31:31-34, Luke 22:20, Hebrews 8:6-13  
This covenant is fulfilled in **Jesus Christ**, who establishes a new relationship between God and humanity. It promises:

- **Forgiveness of sins.**
- **The law written on believers' hearts**, not merely as an external code but through the grace of the Holy Spirit.
- **A personal relationship with God** for all who are united to Christ through His Church.  
  This is an **unconditional covenant** based on Christ's **sacrifice**, offering salvation, the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, and participation in the **uncreated divine energies**. It is the fulfillment of all previous covenants, bringing about the **deification (theosis)** of believers, restoring humanity to its original purpose and communion with God.
