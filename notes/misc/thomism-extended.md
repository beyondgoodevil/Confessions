## I. Core of Thomism

At its foundation, Thomism is the synthesis of natural reason and divine revelation, where reason serves theology but is also capable of reaching truths about God independently. Thomism is the dominant theological and philosophical tradition in Roman Catholicism and was formally endorsed by the Church—especially from the time of Pope Leo XIII’s encyclical *Aeterni Patris* (1879) onward.

## II. Key Philosophical and Theological Doctrines of Thomism

1. Essence and Existence Distinction:
    - In every created being, essence (what a thing is) and existence (that a thing is) are distinct. Only in God are essence and existence identical.
    - This serves to explain contingency in creatures and the necessity of God.
2. Act and Potency:
    - Beings are composed of actuality and potentiality. Change is the actualization of a potential.
    - God is Pure Act (Actus Purus)—without potentiality, fully realized being.
3. Five Ways (Quinque Viae):
    - Aquinas presents five rational arguments for the existence of God based on motion, causation, contingency, degrees of being, and finality (teleology).
    - These are a posteriori proofs and rest heavily on Aristotelian metaphysics.
4. Natural Law Ethics:
    - Moral law is rooted in human nature and reason, discoverable without divine revelation.
    - Human actions are good when they conform to natural ends (e.g., procreation, preservation of life).
5. Analogia Entis (Analogy of Being):
    - Human language about God is analogical, not univocal or equivocal.
    - We speak of God’s goodness, power, and wisdom by analogy, not by exact identity.
6. Hylomorphism:
    - All physical beings are composed of matter (hyle) and form (morphe).
    - The human soul is the form of the body, giving it life and function.
7. Epistemology:
    - All human knowledge begins in sense experience (*nihil est in intellectu quod non prius fuerit in sensu*—“nothing is in the intellect that was not first in the senses”).
    - The intellect abstracts universal concepts from individual sensory experiences.
8. Grace and Nature:
    - Grace does not destroy nature but perfects it (*gratia non tollit naturam, sed perficit*).
    - Man can know moral truths and basic theology by reason, but salvation requires grace.
9. The Beatific Vision:
    - Ultimate happiness consists in seeing God “face to face” (visio beatifica), a supernatural end that cannot be attained by nature alone.
10. Divine Simplicity and Immutability:
    - God is simple (without parts) and immutable (unchanging).
    - God’s attributes (justice, mercy, knowledge) are identical with His essence.

## III. Major Thomists and Revivals

- Scholastic Thomists: Cajetan, John of St. Thomas, and Capreolus.
- Neo-Thomism (19th–20th century): Led by Pope Leo XIII, figures such as Jacques Maritain, Étienne Gilson, and Reginald Garrigou-Lagrange systematized and revitalized Thomism.
- Contemporary Thomism: Divided between Strict Observance Thomists (following classical scholastic categories) and Existential Thomists (emphasizing the dynamic metaphysics of being).

## IV. Thomism in Contrast with Other Systems

1. vs. Augustinianism:  
   Augustinianism emphasizes divine illumination and the will, whereas Thomism is more intellect-centered and Aristotelian.
2. vs. Scotism:  
   Scotus denies the real distinction between essence and existence and upholds the univocity of being.
3. vs. Nominalism:  
   Thomism insists on the real existence of universals in the intellect and in things (*in re*), contrary to the nominalist idea that universals are just names.
4. vs. Eastern Orthodoxy (Palamism):  
   Thomism affirms absolute divine simplicity, while Orthodoxy, via St. Gregory Palamas, affirms a real distinction between the essence and energies of God—this is a key theological disagreement.

## V. Criticism of Thomism (from an Eastern Orthodox perspective)

1. Over-rationalization of Theology:  
   Thomism tends to subordinate mystical experience to reason, whereas Orthodoxy sees theosis and union with God as surpassing human understanding.
2. Essence/Existence vs. Essence/Energies:  
   The real distinction between essence and energies in Orthodox theology is essential for God’s immanence and man's participation in divine life, which Thomistic simplicity arguably makes impossible.
3. Scholastic Method:  
   The quaestio-method of Aquinas is perceived as overly juridical and systematic, alien to the patristic and liturgical approach of the East.
4. Created Grace:  
   Thomists view grace as a created effect in the soul, while Orthodoxy sees grace as uncreated—the very energies of God Himself.
5. Filioque:  
   Thomistic theology defends the Latin doctrine that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. This is in direct conflict with Orthodox teaching that the Spirit proceeds from the Father alone.
6. Beatific Vision vs. Theosis:  
   For Thomists, salvation culminates in a vision of the divine essence; for Orthodox Christians, theosis is participation in the energies, not a vision of the essence, which remains inaccessible.
