## Hasty Generalization

This fallacy occurs when someone makes a broad or universal statement based on a limited or insufficient sample of evidence. It involves drawing a conclusion about all or most cases based on a few specific instances. This type of reasoning is problematic because it assumes that what is true for a small subset must be true for the entire group, which is not logically sound.

## Example

- **Faulty Universal Claim**: "I met two people from City A, and both of them were rude. Therefore, everyone from City A must be rude."
- This generalization is fallacious because it extrapolates the behavior of two individuals to an entire population, which is insufficient and overly broad.

## Why It’s a Fallacy

The issue with a hasty generalization is that a limited number of observations can easily lead to incorrect or unfair conclusions. In order for a claim to be universal or applicable to all members of a group, the sample from which the claim is derived must be representative and substantial.

## Related Fallacy - "Overgeneralization"

Overgeneralization is closely related to hasty generalization, but it refers more specifically to making sweeping statements that apply to all situations or people without considering exceptions. For example: "All politicians are corrupt." This kind of claim is an overgeneralization because it doesn’t take into account any exceptions or nuances, making it an unfairly broad statement.

## Conclusion

The **Hasty Generalization** fallacy is problematic because it assumes that limited evidence is enough to support universal claims. A properly reasoned argument requires a more substantial and representative sample of evidence before making universal conclusions.
