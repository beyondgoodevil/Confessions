## 1. The Claim

**Absolute divine simplicity (ADS)**:

1. God has **no parts** of any kind. Not physical (no body), and not metaphysical either:
    - no matter and form
    - no potency and act
    - no essence and existence
    - no substance and accidents
2. God **is** His attributes. He doesn't *have* goodness; He *is* goodness. And so each attribute is identical with every other: God's justice = God's mercy = God's knowledge.
3. God's essence **is** His existence. He doesn't receive being; He is "subsistent being itself" (*ipsum esse subsistens*).

God is "uniquely unique". Not just the greatest thing, but different from everything in how He exists and has properties.

**Sources**: Augustine (*City of God* XI.10: God is what He has), Anselm (*Monologion*, *Proslogion*), **Aquinas** (*ST* I q. 3), Maimonides. Also in Islamic and Neoplatonic thought: Plotinus's One is the purest form. See [[Neoplatonism: Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus]].

## 2. Why Hold It

| Motive | Argument |
|---|---|
| **Aseity** | if God had parts or properties distinct from Him, He'd depend on them. Then He isn't wholly from Himself |
| **Necessity** | what's composed could be decomposed. Only what isn't composed exists necessarily |
| **Euthyphro** | if God *is* goodness, there's no standard above Him and no arbitrary command. See [[The Euthyphro Dilemma and the Orthodox Answer]] |
| **Other attributes** | immutability, eternity, immateriality all follow from having no parts and no potential |
| **First cause** | every composite needs a cause of its composition; the first cause can't be composite. Feser's Neo-Platonic proof. See [[The Neo-Platonic Proof]] |
| **Trinity** (Athanasius) | the Son is the Father's own Wisdom and Word; since God is simple, He was never without them, so the Son is eternal |

## 3. Objections

**Plantinga, *Does God Have a Nature?* (1980)**

- If God = His properties, and properties are abstract objects, then God is an abstract object: a property. A property can't know, love or create.
- And if goodness = power, then goodness = power, which is absurd.

**Distinct attributes**

- "God is merciful" and "God is just" don't mean the same thing. If they're identical in God, what makes them two truths?

**Contingent knowledge and free choice**

- God freely chose to create this world. He knows that it exists. That knowledge could have been otherwise (He might not have created).
- If God's knowledge and will = His essence, and His essence is necessary, then His knowledge and choices are necessary.
- An "aporetic pentad": libertarian freedom, simplicity, contingent divine knowledge, knowledge requiring intrinsic states, necessary existence. Not all five can be true.

**Modal collapse**

1. God's act of creating = God's essence (simplicity).
2. God's essence exists necessarily.
3. So God's act of creating is necessary.
4. So creation is necessary: nothing could have been otherwise.

No response to this is free of problems. See [[Thomism Leads to Eternal Creation]], [[The Leibnizian Cosmological Argument]] sec. 4.

**Trinity**

- If God is identical to His essence, and each Person is God, how are there three? Aquinas: the Persons are distinguished only by **relations of opposition** (begetting/begotten), which are "subsistent relations", not parts. See [[The Distinction Between Relation of Origin and Relation of Opposition]], [[EO vs RC Trinity]].

## 4. Replies

| Reply | Who | Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Wrong framework** | many | Plantinga assumes properties are abstract objects outside things. Classical defenders used a **constituent ontology**: a thing's nature is a concrete part of it, not an abstract property it relates to |
| **Truthmaker** | Bergmann and Brower | "God is omniscient" has God Himself as its truthmaker. "God's omniscience" refers to God, not to a property. No identity with properties needed |
| **Analogy** | Miller, Oppy | divine "perfections" aren't instances of the properties we have; "perfect" changes the meaning |
| **Mystery** | Dolezal | simplicity and freedom are both true; how is beyond us. Critics: retreat into mystery |
| **Wave-particle** | Stump | God is both *esse* and *id quod est*, the way light is both wave and particle |
| **Creatures distinct from acts** | Greeley and others | against modal collapse: the act is necessary, its effects aren't |
| **Give it up** | Plantinga, Hasker | the doctrine is incoherent; drop it, keep the God of Scripture |

## 5. Where That Leaves It

- The modern debate is mostly analytic philosophers arguing over Aquinas's version.
- The two hardest problems are the same as the Orthodox objections in sec. 6: **free contingent acts** (modal collapse) and **real distinctions** (attributes, Persons).

## 6. The Orthodox View

*Mine.*

**Orthodoxy affirms simplicity.** St. John of Damascus: God is "simple and uncompounded". The Cappadocians: God is not composite. So this isn't Orthodoxy against simplicity. It's about what simplicity *means*.

**Simple, but with real distinctions**

| | ADS (Thomism) | Orthodox (Cappadocians, Palamas) |
|---|---|---|
| Essence and attributes | identical; the distinctions are only in our concepts | the essence is distinct from the energies (goodness, wisdom, life) |
| Energies | God's acts toward creation = His essence | uncreated, really distinct from the essence, really God |
| Persons | distinguished by relations within one essence | the hypostases are distinct by their manner of existence, from the Father as source |
| What "simple" means | no distinctions of any kind | **not composed**: distinctions without parts |

The key move: a **real distinction is not a composition**. The sun and its rays are distinct; the sun isn't assembled out of them. Three hypostases aren't three parts of God. Palamas says the energies don't make God composite. See [[Distinction Does Not Necessitate Separation, Division, or Composition]].

**How that handles the objections**

- **Distinct attributes**: they really are distinct (as energies), while the essence stays one and unknowable.
- **Modal collapse**: God's *essence* is necessary; His *energies toward creation* (creating, providence) are free acts of will. Creation could have not been. See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]], [[Implications of No Essence-Energies Distinction]].
- **Contingent knowledge**: God's knowing of creatures is an energy, related to what He freely wills, not His necessary essence.
- **Trinity**: the Persons don't have to be squeezed into "relations" within an identity, because the starting point is the Persons (the Father as source), not an abstract essence. See [[Monarchical Trinitarianism]].
- **Plantinga's "God is a property"**: doesn't arise, because God isn't identified with His attributes.

**The Orthodox charge against ADS**

- It's the Neoplatonic One with Christian names: the One has no distinctions, so the many have no ground in it. See [[Neoplatonic Idea of Divine Simplicity]], [[The Problem of the One and the Many]] sec. 5–6.
- It makes the Incarnation hard to state: if every act of God = His immutable essence, what does "the Word became flesh" add? See [[God Being Pure Act Makes Incarnation Impossible]].
- And it threatens apophaticism: if God's goodness *is* His essence, knowing His goodness is knowing His essence. See [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]].

**The Thomist reply** (to be fair): Palamas makes the energies a second divine reality alongside the essence, and that *is* composition; and the essence/energies distinction is only "formal" or "in our understanding". The Orthodox answer: then the energies aren't really God's, and theosis becomes a metaphor. See [[God as Pure Act: RC vs EO]].
