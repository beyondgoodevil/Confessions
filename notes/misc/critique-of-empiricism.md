title: Critique of Empiricism

summary: Seven reasons not all knowledge can come from the senses.
---

1. **Sense Data Are Contingent and Limited**:  
   All sense data come through material means (sight, sound, touch, etc.), which are contingent and bound by space and time. If all knowledge were derived solely from these finite means, then knowledge itself would be equally contingent and limited, leaving no foundation for universal truths. For example, we cannot derive the principle of non-contradiction from empirical observation—it must be presupposed.
2. **Logical and Mathematical Truths Are A Priori**:  
   Logical principles (e.g., the law of non-contradiction) and mathematical truths (e.g., 2+2=4) exist independently of sensory experience. One cannot "observe" the number 2 or see the abstract relationship between addition and equality in the natural world. These truths are known **a priori**—they are apprehended through reason and must exist before any empirical observation can even occur.
3. **The Problem of Induction**:  
   Following David Hume's critique, empirical observation cannot guarantee universal truths about the future or the past. For example, observing that the sun rises every day does not prove it will rise tomorrow. The uniformity of nature, which science relies upon, cannot be justified through sense data alone. Thus, an **a priori** principle (e.g., the rational structure of reality) must undergird inductive reasoning.
4. **Abstract Universals**:  
   Concepts like "justice," "truth," or "beauty" cannot be reduced to mere sensory impressions. We can see instances of justice or beauty, but the abstract universal itself is not derived from sense data. These universals are apprehended through intellect and exist as immaterial realities that transcend empirical observation. This points to a metaphysical dimension of knowledge.
5. **Knowledge of God and Metaphysical Truths**:  
   If God exists as the necessary and ultimate reality, then knowledge of Him cannot be limited to sensory experience, as God is immaterial and beyond the physical world. The very concept of God as infinite, eternal, and unchanging cannot be derived from observing the finite, temporal, and changing material world. Revelation and **noetic** (spiritual) experience provide a form of knowledge that transcends empirical observation.
6. **Empiricism Undermines Itself**:  
   The claim that "all knowledge comes from sense data" is itself a philosophical proposition that cannot be verified empirically. No sense data can prove that all knowledge must be empirical—it is a self-refuting position. To even argue for empiricism, one must rely on logical reasoning (a priori), which exists prior to and independently of sensory input.
7. **Orthodox Theology Supports This View**:  
   In Orthodox Christian theology, the human person is created in the image of God and possesses a **nous** (spiritual intellect) capable of perceiving divine truths directly, beyond sensory or discursive reasoning. For example, the experience of God in the **uncreated energies** is a direct, noetic form of knowledge, not mediated by empirical sense data.

## Conclusion

Sense data provide a limited and contingent form of knowledge, but they cannot account for universal truths, logical principles, abstract realities, or metaphysical knowledge. These must come from **a priori** reasoning and noetic apprehension, which point to the existence of an immaterial and transcendent foundation for truth. This aligns with both philosophical critique and Orthodox theology, affirming that not all knowledge is derived from the senses.
