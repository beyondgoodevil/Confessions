The **fallacy of circular reasoning** (also known as *begging the question* or *petitio principii*) occurs when an argument assumes its conclusion as a premise, essentially using what it is trying to prove as evidence for itself. Instead of providing independent support, the argument simply restates the claim in a slightly different way.

## Structure of Circular Reasoning

- **Premise:** X is true.
- **Conclusion:** Therefore, X is true.

The problem is that the reasoning goes in a loop, without actually proving anything. It does not provide new or external justification for the claim being made.

## Examples of Circular Reasoning

1. **Religious Example:**
    - *"The Bible is the word of God because it says so in the Bible."*
    - This assumes the Bible’s authority instead of proving it.
2. **Legal Example:**
    - *"Laws must be obeyed because it is illegal to break them."*
    - This argument does not justify why laws should be followed, only restating the premise.
3. **Philosophical Example:**
    - *"I trust my reason because my reason tells me to trust it."*
    - This assumes the reliability of reason without providing external justification.
