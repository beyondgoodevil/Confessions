The **First Council of Nicaea**, held in **325 AD**, was the first ecumenical council of the Christian Church, convened by Emperor Constantine in Nicaea (modern-day İznik, Turkey). Its primary purpose was to address the Arian controversy and establish a unified Christian doctrine.

## Key Aspects of the Council

1. **Arian Controversy**:  
   The council addressed the teachings of **Arius**, a priest from Alexandria, who argued that Christ was a created being and not co-eternal with God the Father. Arius claimed, *"There was a time when the Son was not,"* denying the full divinity of Christ.
2. **Nicene Creed**:  
   To counter Arianism, the council formulated the **Nicene Creed**, affirming that Christ is *"of one essence with the Father"* (*homoousios*). This proclaimed Christ’s full divinity and eternal existence.
3. **Condemnation of Arianism**:  
   Arius and his teachings were condemned as heretical. He and his followers were exiled, and their writings were ordered to be burned.
4. **Theological Decisions**:
    - Christ is *begotten, not made* and co-eternal with the Father.
    - Emphasis on the Holy Trinity as central to Christian faith.
    - The term *homoousios* was crucial, signifying that the Father and the Son share the same divine essence.
5. **Other Topics**:
    - Standardization of the date for **Easter** (to be celebrated on the first Sunday after the first full moon following the spring equinox).
    - Rules for clergy conduct and administration within the Church.
6. **Attendees**:  
   Around **318 bishops** attended, primarily from the Eastern part of the Roman Empire. Notable figures included **St. Athanasius** (defender of Orthodoxy) and **Eusebius of Caesarea**.
7. **Legacy**:  
   The council was foundational in unifying Christian doctrine, establishing the groundwork for future ecumenical councils, and preserving the Orthodox understanding of the Trinity.
