**1 Corinthians 15:26** · New King James Version

**26** The last enemy *that* will be destroyed *is* death.

## Context: 1 Corinthians 15

- vv. 3-8: the early creed: Christ died, was buried, rose, appeared. See [[The Case for the Resurrection of Jesus]].
- vv. 12-19: some Corinthians said "there is no resurrection of the dead". Paul: then Christ isn't risen, and "your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins".
- vv. 20-22: Christ is the **firstfruits**; "as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive". See [[Christ as the New Adam: Verses]].
- vv. 24-28: the order of the end: Christ reigns "till he hath put all enemies under his feet" (Ps 110:1), and **the last enemy is death**. Then God will be "all in all".
- vv. 54-55: "Death is swallowed up in victory. O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?" quoting Isa 25:8 and Hos 13:14 (from the Septuagint). See [[Paul Quotes the Septuagint Part 3]].

## Death as an Enemy

- Death is **not natural** in the sense of being God's will. "God made not death" (Wis 1:13). It came in through sin (Rom 5:12). See [[Ancestral Sin vs Original Sin]].
    - Schmemann (*For the Life of the World* ch. 6): death is an abomination, the fruit of separation from God.
    - Bulgakov (*The Lamb of God* ch. 5): there is "no death as a positive principle of being, for God did not create it": so the Lord's death is called a "dormition".
- It's an **enemy**: personal language. Heb 2:14 links it to the devil, "him that had the power of death".
- It's the **last** enemy: still active now (Christians die), but already defeated in principle by Christ's resurrection; finally abolished at the general resurrection.
    - Since the Saviour died for us, "we no longer die as those condemned", but are "dissolved only for the time which God has set for each", like **seeds** sown that will rise (Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* ch. 21, quoting 1 Cor 15:53-55).
- "Destroyed" (*katargeitai*): made powerless, abolished.
- Neither hidden nor consoled away (Schmemann, ch. 6): modern culture either **hides** death (the funeral industry, "natural death") or makes religion a **consolation** about it ("he's in a better place"). Christianity does neither: it proclaims death as **the enemy**, and **Christ as Life**, who entered death and destroyed it from within.
    - "Christianity is not reconciliation with death. It is the revelation of death... because it is the revelation of Life."

## The Fathers and the Hymns

- **Athanasius** (*On the Incarnation* 27-29): the proof that death is destroyed is that Christians, even women and children, **despise** it and go to martyrdom laughing at it, "as a tyrant defeated".
    - **Third day**: long enough to prove He was really dead, soon enough that the witnesses were still alive "while the word was still echoing in their ears" (ch. 26).
    - Even boys and young girls "practice dying" and rush to martyrdom. Before Christ, everyone wept for the dying "as if they were perishing" (ch. 27–28, Behr trans.).
    - Images:
        - a defeated **tyrant** bound hand and foot, mocked by passers-by: "O death, where is thy victory?"
        - **asbestos**: someone who doubts fire can be beaten should put it on and touch the fire
        - a **snake** trampled, a **lion** played with by children: obviously dead or powerless (ch. 29)
    - A dead man can't act; but Christ is daily changing adulterers, murderers and the impious: "This is not the work of one dead, but of one alive" (ch. 30).
- **Chrysostom's Paschal Homily**: "O Death, where is thy sting? O Hell, where is thy victory? Christ is risen, and thou art overthrown... Christ is risen, and life reigns."
- **Paschal troparion**: "Christ is risen from the dead, **trampling down death by death**." See [[The Descent into Hades]].
    - Bulgakov: His death isn't an undoing of the Incarnation but "the deepest point of His in-humanization": Life tastes death to take its sting into itself, "trampling down death by death". Like God resting on the seventh day, the Son of Man rests "like a corn of wheat in the heart of the earth".
- **The Christian funeral** is **Paschal**: not "dealing with" death but announcing its defeat (Schmemann).

## Notes

*Mine.*

- The opposite of Epicurus's "death is nothing to us": for Christians death is very much something, an enemy, but a beaten one. See [[Epicurus and Lucretius]].
- The verse is why the Orthodox treat the body with such care (burial, not cremation; relics): the body is going to be raised.
