### 1. Emperor Leo III (r. 717–741) – The First Iconoclast Emperor

- **Political Motive:** Strengthening Imperial Authority
- **How He Used Iconoclasm:**
    - Leo III **banned the veneration of icons** around **726**, claiming that icons caused divine punishment, including recent **earthquakes and Muslim victories**.
    - He sought to **reduce the power of monastic iconodules (those who venerated icons), who had strong influence among the people**.
    - By attacking icons, he **asserted imperial authority over the Church**, challenging the Patriarch of Constantinople and the Pope of Rome.
    - He also **hoped to appease Muslims and Jews**, who saw Christian icons as idolatrous.

### 2. Emperor Constantine V (r. 741–775) – The Most Aggressive Iconoclast

- **Political Motive:** Suppressing Monastic and Church Power
- **How He Used Iconoclasm:**
    - Constantine V **intensified** iconoclasm, ordering the **destruction of icons, persecution of iconophiles (icon supporters), and confiscation of monastery wealth**.
    - He convened the **Council of Hieria (754)**, which **officially condemned icons** as heretical.
    - He saw **monasteries as centers of opposition** to his rule, so he targeted them, confiscated their land, and forced monks into military service.
    - His **brutality against iconodules was extreme**, leading to the **beating, exile, and execution** of those who resisted.
    - He promoted **imperial-controlled theology**, reducing the influence of bishops who supported icons.

### 3. Emperor Leo V (r. 813–820) – Reviving Iconoclasm for Political Stability

- **Political Motive:** Reuniting the Empire After Military Defeats
- **How He Used Iconoclasm:**
    - After the Byzantine defeat against the Bulgars at the **Battle of Pliska (811)**, Leo V revived iconoclasm, blaming icon veneration for the empire’s military weakness.
    - He sought to restore the policies of Constantine V, believing iconoclasm had brought **military success in the past**.
    - He removed **Patriarch Nicephorus I**, who supported icons, and replaced him with an **iconoclast-friendly patriarch**.
    - Leo V’s policy was **less extreme than Constantine V’s**, but it still aimed at **strengthening the emperor’s control over religious affairs**.

### 4. Emperor Theophilos (r. 829–842) – The Last Iconoclast Emperor

- **Political Motive:** Modernizing the Empire and Weakening Monastic Influence
- **How He Used Iconoclasm:**
    - Theophilos continued his predecessors' **anti-icon policies**, enforcing icon destruction and **harshly punishing iconophiles**.
    - He viewed iconodules as **rebellious monks** who resisted **imperial control**.
    - He also **sought to align Byzantine religious policies with Islamic neighbors**, who rejected images in worship.
    - Though harsh, he **allowed theological debate** on the matter rather than simply persecuting iconodules outright.
