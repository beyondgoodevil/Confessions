The Council of Ephesus, convened in 431 AD, was the third Ecumenical Council. It was called by Emperor Theodosius II in response to the Nestorian controversy. This controversy revolved around the teachings of Nestorius, the Patriarch of Constantinople. The issue was the relationship between Christ's divine and human natures and the title of Mary as Theotokos (God-bearer).

## 1. The Controversy

- **Nestorius:** Two separate persons in Christ.
- Divinity and humanity united in will, not substance.
- Rejected the title Theotokos for Mary.
- **Cyril of Alexandria:** One person in Christ.
- Mary as Theotokos, the bearer of God.

## 2. The Council's Proceedings

- Held in Ephesus in 431 AD.
- Over 200 bishops attended.
- Pope Celestine I supported Cyril.
- Nestorius condemned and excommunicated.
- Christ affirmed as one person, Theotokos upheld for Mary.

## 3. Theological Implications

- **Hypostatic union:** One person, two natures.
- **Theotokos reaffirmed:** Mary bore the Word made flesh.

## 4. The Aftermath

- Nestorius exiled.
- Followers became the Nestorian Church.
- Orthodox Christology strengthened.
- Mary's role clarified.
