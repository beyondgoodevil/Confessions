## Biblical Account of the Transfiguration

The Transfiguration is recorded in three Synoptic Gospels:

1. Matthew 17:1-9
    - **Key Verses:** "And He was transfigured before them. His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light."
2. Mark 9:2-8
    - **Key Verses:** "And He was transfigured before them. His clothes became shining, exceedingly white, like snow, such as no launderer on earth can whiten them."
3. Luke 9:28-36
    - **Key Verses:** "And while He prayed, the appearance of His face was altered, and His robe became white and glistening."

## Significance of the Transfiguration

1. Revelation of Divine Glory:
    - The Transfiguration reveals the true divine nature of Christ, displaying His glory as the Son of God. This moment is crucial for understanding Jesus' identity and His relationship with the Father.
2. Divine Energies:
    - In Eastern Orthodox theology, the radiance that emanates from Christ during the Transfiguration is seen as a manifestation of **divine energies**. These energies are the uncreated, active presence of God that can be experienced by believers.
    - The light that envelops Jesus signifies the glory of God, indicating that while His essence remains transcendent and unknowable, His energies are accessible and transformative.
3. Participation in Divine Energies:
    - The event illustrates that the divine energies of God can be experienced by humanity. The disciples, witnessing this event, are given a glimpse of the divine life, suggesting that through Christ, believers are invited to partake in these energies.
    - This participation leads to a deeper communion with God, facilitating spiritual transformation and growth in holiness.
4. Preparation for the Passion:
    - The Transfiguration also serves as a preparatory moment for Jesus' impending suffering and death. It provides the disciples with a glimpse of the resurrection glory that will follow the crucifixion, affirming their faith amid the coming trials.
5. The Presence of Moses and Elijah:
    - The appearance of Moses and Elijah during the Transfiguration signifies the continuity of God's revelation throughout salvation history. It connects the Law and the Prophets with Christ, affirming His fulfillment of both.

## Conclusion

The Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor is a profound revelation of His divine nature, marked by the radiating divine energies that transform Him and provide a glimpse of God's glory. In Eastern Orthodox thought, this event emphasizes the invitation for believers to partake in God's uncreated energies, facilitating spiritual transformation and a deeper relationship with the divine. The experience of the Transfiguration underscores the essential Christian belief in the interplay between the divine and human, calling believers to embrace the transformative power of God's grace in their lives.
