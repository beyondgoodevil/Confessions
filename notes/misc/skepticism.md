## What is Skepticism?

Skepticism is an epistemological position that doubts or questions the possibility of knowledge. Skeptics argue that humans cannot achieve certainty about the external world, other minds, or even the validity of our perceptions and reasoning processes. In its extreme form, skepticism can claim that we know nothing for sure, or that knowledge is fundamentally inaccessible to us.

There are different types of skepticism, such as:

1. **Global Skepticism**: The belief that it is impossible to know anything at all, including knowledge of the external world or our own minds.
2. **Local Skepticism**: Doubts about certain kinds of knowledge, such as skepticism about the existence of the external world, the reliability of sense perception, or the possibility of metaphysical knowledge.

## Reductio Against Skepticism

1. **Self-Defeating Argument**: If skepticism is correct and we cannot know anything, then the skeptic’s statement itself—that "we cannot know anything"—would also be unknowable. This leads to a **self-defeating** position: if we cannot know anything, then we cannot even know that we can't know anything, which undermines the very foundation of the skeptical claim.
2. **Impossibility of Knowledge**: Skepticism, especially in its extreme forms, leads to the conclusion that knowledge is impossible. If we cannot know anything with certainty, then all beliefs, including skepticism itself, are subject to doubt. This paradoxical conclusion suggests that adopting skepticism as a philosophy undermines the possibility of any coherent knowledge, making it a **logical contradiction**.
