The Neoplatonic idea of **divine simplicity** stems from the philosophical system established by **Plato** and further developed by later thinkers like **Plotinus**. Here are the key aspects of this concept:

## Key Aspects of Neoplatonic Divine Simplicity

1. **The One:** At the pinnacle of Neoplatonism is **the One** (or **the Good**), which is the ultimate reality and source of all existence. The One is characterized by absolute simplicity, meaning it is indivisible, unchanging, and beyond all categories of being. It transcends existence itself and cannot be fully comprehended or described.
2. **Lack of Distinction:** In the Neoplatonic framework, the One possesses no internal distinctions or multiplicity. Unlike created beings, which have parts and attributes, the One is entirely self-sufficient and does not rely on anything outside itself. This simplicity means that the essence of the One is its existence; there is no separation between what it is and how it exists.
3. **Emanation:** From the One emanates a hierarchy of reality, including the **Intellect** (or **Nous**) and the **World Soul**. Each of these levels retains a degree of simplicity while also reflecting the richness of the One. Emanation is not a creation in the traditional sense but rather a process of unfolding or radiating from the One, maintaining a connection to the divine simplicity.
4. **Unity and Plurality:** Neoplatonism emphasizes that all things ultimately derive from the One and strive to return to it. While the material world is diverse and complex, it is seen as participating in the unity of the One. This participation allows for the multiplicity of existence while remaining grounded in the absolute simplicity of the One.
5. **Implications for Knowledge and Existence:** Neoplatonists believe that true knowledge comes from understanding the unity behind the multiplicity of the material world. The more one moves away from the One, the more complex and less perfect the forms become. Thus, attaining knowledge of the One involves transcending the limitations of material existence and recognizing the simplicity that underlies all reality.

## Conclusion

In summary, the Neoplatonic idea of divine simplicity centers on the concept of the One as an absolute, indivisible reality that transcends all forms of existence and knowledge. This notion emphasizes unity and the process of emanation, where all things emerge from the One while retaining their connection to it. The goal of Neoplatonism is to recognize and return to this fundamental simplicity through philosophical contemplation and spiritual practice.
