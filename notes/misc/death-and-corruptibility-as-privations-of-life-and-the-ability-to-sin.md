title: Death and corruptibility as privations of life and the ability to sin

### 1. **St. Athanasius** – Corruptibility as Privation of Immortality

In **On the Incarnation**, St. Athanasius speaks of death and corruption as a privation of the original state of immortality and life:

- "God made man for incorruption and as an image of His own eternity, but men, turning from eternal things, sinned, and thus came under the law of death and corruption." (*On the Incarnation*, Chapter 4)

Athanasius makes it clear that death and corruption are not natural to man but are the result of turning away from God, thus losing the gift of life and immortality.

### 2. **St. Gregory of Nyssa** – Sin as Absence of the Good

St. Gregory of Nyssa, in his theological writings, explains sin and corruption as a privation of good, stating that evil is the absence of good rather than a substance of its own:

- "Evil is not a substance, but a condition of the soul opposed to virtue, arising in the soul from the neglect of the good." (*The Great Catechism*, Chapter 5)

This emphasizes that corruption (the inclination to sin) is a deprivation of virtue and participation in God, not a created entity.

### 3. **St. Maximus the Confessor** – Privation of Divine Life

St. Maximus teaches that the Fall brought death and corruption as a privation of participation in the divine life. The separation from God leads to mortality:

- "Man was created to share in the incorruptible divine life, but by turning away from God, he has subjected himself to corruption and death." (*Ambigua*, 7.22)

### 4. **St. John of Damascus** – Death as Privation of the Natural Good

In **An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith**, St. John of Damascus writes that death and corruption are not natural but entered the world as a result of the Fall:

- "Death is the separation of the soul from the body, and it is a privation of life, which God had originally intended for man." (*Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, Book 4, Chapter 27)

Here, John emphasizes that death is a privation of the life God intended for humanity, and corruptibility arises from this loss.

### 5. **Scripture** – Death and Sin as Privation

**Romans 5:12** – *"Therefore, just as through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and thus death spread to all men, because all sinned."*  
This shows how sin and death entered the world as a consequence of humanity's fall from grace, losing the original immortality.

**Wisdom 2:23-24** – *"For God created man for incorruption and made him in the image of His own eternity, but through the devil's envy, death entered the world."*  
This verse reflects the Eastern Orthodox understanding that humanity was created for eternal life, but death entered as a result of sin and a privation of divine life.

### 6. **St. Gregory Palamas** – Separation from Divine Life as Corruption

St. Gregory Palamas emphasizes that separation from God's divine energies leads to corruption and death:

- "For the man who has separated himself from the divine and life-giving grace of the Spirit is truly dead, though still living, because he is separated from God." (*Homilies*, 16.24)
