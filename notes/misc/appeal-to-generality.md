**Appeal to Generality** is an informal logical fallacy where someone makes a vague, broad, or overly general claim without sufficient specificity or evidence. It often occurs when a person avoids engaging with the specifics of an argument and instead relies on sweeping statements that are difficult to challenge because they lack clear definition.

## Characteristics of Appeal to Generality

1. **Vagueness** – The claim is too broad to be meaningfully evaluated.
2. **Lack of Specific Evidence** – There is no concrete support for the statement.
3. **Avoidance of Nuance** – It ignores relevant distinctions or counterexamples.
4. **Shifting the Burden of Proof** – Forces the opponent to disprove an undefined or generalized assertion.

## Examples

- **“Science has already disproven religion.”**
    - This is vague. What aspect of religion? What scientific discipline? What specific evidence?
- **“Philosophy is just mental gymnastics.”**
    - Which philosophy? What makes it unimportant?
- **“Everyone knows that X is true.”**
    - An appeal to an undefined consensus without proof.

## Why It's Fallacious

- It **fails to provide a falsifiable claim** because it is too broad.
- It **avoids dealing with specific arguments** that could challenge the position.
- It **creates an illusion of certainty** without proving anything.

To counter this fallacy, demand **specific definitions, examples, and precise evidence** rather than engaging with vague assertions.
