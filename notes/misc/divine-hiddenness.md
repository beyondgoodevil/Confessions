"Verily thou art a God that hidest thyself" (Isa 45:15). Two senses:

- **Existential**: the believer's experience of God's absence. The Psalms of lament, "My God, why hast thou forsaken me?", the dark night of the soul.
- **Evidential**: the fact that many people don't believe, used as an argument that God doesn't exist. Most of this note.

Companion to [[The Problem of Evil and Theodicy]]: many of the moves are the same.

## 1. Schellenberg's Argument

J. L. Schellenberg, *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason* (1993).

1. If a perfectly loving God exists, He is always open to a personal relationship with any finite person capable of one.
2. A relationship requires believing the other exists, and belief isn't under our control.
3. So if God exists, anyone capable of relating to Him who isn't **resisting** Him will believe.
4. But there are **nonresistant nonbelievers**: people capable of a relationship with God who don't believe, through no fault of their own.
5. So a perfectly loving God doesn't exist.

Who counts as nonresistant (Schellenberg's examples): honest seekers who search and don't find; whole cultures without the idea of a personal God; prehistoric hunter-gatherers; ordinary secular people who just never had it come up.

Why relationship would be good for us: help in overcoming our faults (moral), and peace, security, God's presence (experiential).

Later versions (2015 on): premises stated as necessary truths; "culpable" replaced by "resisting"; stress on "meaningful conscious relationship".

## 2. Two Related Arguments

**Demographics** (Maitzen 2006)

- Belief is very unevenly spread: nearly universal in Saudi Arabia, rare in Thailand.
- Individual-level explanations (this person is resisting) don't explain *national* patterns. Culture does.
- A just God wouldn't treat whole peoples so differently. So naturalism explains the pattern better.
- Replies: belief depends on testimony and community, so clustering is just as expected on theism (Baker-Hytch); God might allow social "defeaters"; Molinist and Calvinist versions (God placed people where they'd respond as they would anyway). The last two are controversial and morally troubling.

**Prehistoric nonbelief** (Marsh 2013)

- For most of human history, people had no concept of God. Millions of natural, nonresistant nonbelievers.
- Surprising if God wants relationship; not surprising if religion evolved.
- Replies: the cognitive science of religion may show humans are *built* to believe in some higher power, which fits theism; and what counts is comparative likelihood, not surprise.

## 3. Theistic Responses

**Reasons God might allow nonbelief** (each from someone in the literature):

| Reason | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Freedom / moral autonomy** (Hick, Swinburne, Murray) | God's obvious presence would overwhelm free moral choice: we'd be good out of fear or self-interest |
| **Right motives** | belief now might come with the wrong motives (fear of punishment); first those must change |
| **Owning one's character** | goodness should first be freely chosen against the pull of the opposite |
| **Intensity of seeking** (Kierkegaard) | easy belief breeds complacency; the seeking is part of the good |
| **Humility** (Pascal) | God gives enough light to those who seek, enough darkness to those who don't. Belief now might bring presumption, not contrition |
| **Mutual help** | nonbelief creates room for people to help each other toward God |
| **Not coercion** | widespread obvious belief would force theism on resisters |

**Attacking the premises:**

- **Premise 1** (love = openness to relationship): perfect love might aim at the person's greatest good, which may not be relationship *now*. A surgeon loves the patient by operating, not chatting.
- **Premise 2** (relationship needs belief): maybe faith without belief is enough: hope, acceptance, acting as if. Or relating to God without the proposition "God exists" (as a child relates to a parent before having that concept).
- **Premise 4** (there are nonresistant nonbelievers): maybe everyone has *some* knowledge of God (Rom 1:19-20; Calvin's *sensus divinitatis*) that's suppressed or not recognised as such. Contested.
- **Skeptical theism**: we're not placed to know God's reasons, so we can't infer He has none.

## 4. The Orthodox Answer

*Mine.*

**God isn't hidden. We are blind.**

- The Orthodox frame changes the problem. The issue isn't that God fails to give enough evidence. It's that the faculty that perceives Him, the *nous*, is darkened by the passions. "Blessed are the pure in heart: for they shall see God" (Matt 5:8). See [[Nous]].
- Like light and a damaged eye: the sun isn't hiding. St. Gregory Palamas: the uncreated light was there on Tabor for everyone, but only the three disciples were given eyes to see it. See [[The Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor]].
- So the "nonresistant nonbeliever" is a strange category on this view. Not because they are wicked, but because no one is purified enough by default. All are in need of healing; nonbelief is one symptom of the common illness, not a personal crime.

**Revealed, not hidden**

- From the Christian side God has done the opposite of hiding: "the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, and we beheld his glory" (John 1:14). "He that hath seen me hath seen the Father" (John 14:9). See [[John 14:9: Jesus Explicitly States That He Is the Image of the Father]].
- Creation testifies (Rom 1, Ps 19), the Church is visible, the saints are visible. The question becomes why people don't see what is shown. See [[Natural Theology: Is It Legitimate]], [[Religious Experience as Evidence]].

**Freedom and love**

- God doesn't override the will. Love that's compelled isn't love. A God who made Himself undeniable to everyone at every moment would make the free "yes" impossible. See [[Synergy: Grace and Free Will]].
- God knocks; He doesn't break the door (Rev 3:20).

**Those who never heard**

- The Orthodox Church doesn't presume to say who is saved outside her, and leaves those who never heard to God's mercy and justice. "God is not the God of the dead, but of the living" (Matt 22:32); Christ descended to Hades and preached to the dead (1 Pet 3:19, 4:6).
- So prehistoric and isolated peoples aren't written off. The argument assumes their fate is sealed by their nonbelief now; Orthodoxy doesn't.

**The existential side**

- The saints speak of God's apparent withdrawal as part of the spiritual life: so the soul seeks Him more, and doesn't attribute grace to itself. St. Silouan was told "Keep thy mind in hell, and despair not". The experience of absence isn't evidence of nonexistence; it's a stage of love.
