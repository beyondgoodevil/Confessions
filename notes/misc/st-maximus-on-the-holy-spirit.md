Saint Maximus the Confessor emphasized the omnipresence of the Holy Spirit while also distinguishing how His activity operates uniquely within the Church. This distinction is rooted in the Eastern Orthodox understanding of grace, synergy, and ecclesiology.

1. **Omnipresence of the Holy Spirit**:  
   The Holy Spirit, as God, is omnipresent and fills all creation. This reflects His role in sustaining and enlivening the cosmos, as the Spirit gives life to all things (Psalm 139:7-8, Wisdom of Solomon 1:7). Saint Maximus would affirm that the Holy Spirit is everywhere present by virtue of His divine nature.
2. **Specific Activity in the Church**:  
   While the Holy Spirit is omnipresent, His *energies* (uncreated grace) work in a particular way within the Church. The Church, as the Body of Christ, is the locus of salvation and the primary sphere of the Spirit’s sanctifying activity. Maximus links this to the Church’s sacramental life, where the Holy Spirit works through the mysteries (sacraments) to deify and unite the faithful with God. For Maximus, the Church is the continuation of Christ’s Incarnation, making it the unique dwelling of the Spirit's full activity.
3. **Synergy and Free Will**:  
   The Holy Spirit works in synergy with human freedom. Although the Spirit is present everywhere, His transformative work depends on human receptivity and the proper disposition of the heart. The Church, as the assembly of the faithful, provides the necessary context for this synergy through right doctrine and sacramental grace.
4. **Against Heresy and Schism**:  
   Saint Maximus insisted that the Holy Spirit does not work fully or salvifically outside the true Church, as heresy and schism separate individuals from the fullness of truth. While the Spirit may still call those outside the Church to repentance (since His love extends to all), His sanctifying grace is fully realized only within the Orthodox Church, the pillar and ground of truth.
