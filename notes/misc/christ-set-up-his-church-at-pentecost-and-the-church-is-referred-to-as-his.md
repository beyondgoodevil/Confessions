## 1. The Establishment of the Church at Pentecost

**Acts 2:1-4**  
"And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place. And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting. And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them. And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance."

**Acts 2:41**  
"Then they that gladly received his word were baptized: and the same day there were added unto them about three thousand souls."

These verses mark the moment when the Holy Spirit descended upon the apostles, empowering them to preach, thus establishing the Church.

## 2. The Church as the Body of Christ

**1 Corinthians 12:12-13**  
"For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ. For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit."

**Ephesians 1:22-23**  
"And hath put all things under his feet, and gave him to be the head over all things to the church, which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all."

**Ephesians 5:29-30**  
"For no man ever yet hated his own flesh; but nourisheth and cherisheth it, even as the Lord the church: For we are members of his body, of his flesh, and of his bones."

**Colossians 1:18**  
"And he is the head of the body, the church: who is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead; that in all things he might have the preeminence."

These passages show that the Church is the body of Christ, with Him as its head, uniting all believers into one through baptism.

## 3. How the Church is Christ's Body

The Church is Christ's body because believers are united with Him through the Holy Spirit at baptism (1 Corinthians 12:12-13). Christ, as the head, governs the Church (Ephesians 1:22-23) and provides it with life and direction. The Church participates in Christ's death and resurrection, especially through sacraments like the Eucharist (Ephesians 5:29-30). Though diverse, believers form one body under Christ (Colossians 1:18).
