> "And the serpent said unto the woman, Ye shall not surely die: For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods." (Gen 3:4–5)

*Notes from Underground* (1864) is a short, nasty, unhappy book. Most readers of Dostoevsky agree that his great period begins with it. A nameless retired official, forty years old, writes from his "underground" in St Petersburg, rejecting in turn the science, utilitarian ethics and socialist utopias of the 1860s, then confessing three humiliations from his youth. Out of it came three readings, each of which this note follows:

- **Shestov's**, a heroic revolt of the person against necessity;
- **Girard's**, the anatomy of pride;
- **Fr. Seraphim Rose's**, a symptom of the nihilism that runs from liberalism to the Antichrist.

Behind all three stands Nietzsche's madman announcing that "God is dead".

## 1. The Book Itself

**Part One, "Underground"**
- He is sick, spiteful, and **"hyper-conscious"**: "to be overly conscious is an illness".
- His mind runs into **the stone wall**: "the laws of nature, the deductions of the natural sciences, mathematics". "Once they prove to you that you're descended from a monkey, there's no point in frowning about it." Science will show that man is "nothing more than a kind of piano key or organ peg", his acts calculable "like a table of logarithms".
- The revolt: "Good Lord, what do I care about the laws of nature and arithmetic if I have my reasons for disliking them, including the one about two and two making four!" "**Twice two is four is no longer life, gentlemen, but the beginning of death.**" It is "a cocky-looking fop, who stands in your way, arms akimbo, and spits". And "two times two is five is a nice little thing too."
- **The Crystal Palace.** He has in mind the London Exhibition building, which Dostoevsky saw in 1862 and compared to "Babylon… some kind of prophecy from the Apocalypse", and Chernyshevsky's utopian palace in *What Is to Be Done?* (1863). People will not live in it. Man wants, above all, "one's own free, unrestrained choice… one's own whim".
- **No one to blame.** "One cannot forgive a law of Nature." "I'm guilty without guilt." Thompson's point: conscience needs a *person* to answer to, and a law of nature is no one.
- **"Show me something better."** He knows "the underground is not at all better, but something different… which I long for but will in no way find!" Dostoevsky's original chapter 10, which "deduced the need for faith and Christ", was cut by the censor and never restored.

**Part Two, "Apropos of the Wet Snow"**
- An officer moves him aside in a tavern like a piece of furniture. He spends years plotting revenge and finally bumps into him on the Nevsky Prospect.
- His old schoolmates hold a farewell dinner. He despises them, forces himself in, and humiliates himself.
- He lectures the young prostitute Liza on her fate. When she comes to him with real love, he hurts her and pushes money into her hand.
- His summary: "**I am alone and they are everyone.**"

## 2. Shestov: The First True Critique of Reason

Lev Shestov (1866–1938), the Russian-Jewish philosopher of "Athens and Jerusalem", wrote *Dostoevsky and Nietzsche: The Philosophy of Tragedy* (1903). That book is in my library only as an unreadable scan, so I take his reading from *Athens and Jerusalem*, where he returns to the Underground Man at length.

- **The target is necessity.** Since Aristotle, European thought has bowed to "self-evident truths" (identity, non-contradiction, causality) as to a power "that does not allow itself to be persuaded". Spinoza's God himself cannot transcend them. "Cry halt before Necessity" (Aristotle).
- **Dostoevsky's achievement.** In the *Notes*, "with an audacity and clear-headedness that we seek in vain in the author of the *Critique of Pure Reason*", Dostoevsky "hurls himself in an attack on the 'eternal truths'". He asks "the question of the conclusive value of proofs, of the source of that constraint which the self-evidences exercise" that Kant turned aside from. Shestov notes he did it after four years in prison reading only the Bible.
- **Philosophers worship the wall.** For them a wall "has something calming, final, perhaps even mystical about it". But "God does not constrain", while "two times two makes four… constrain[s] not only man but also the Creator".
- **Necessity and the Inquisition.** Self-evidence is "only a hypocritical *sine effusione sanguinis* behind which pyres and tortures are hidden". Duns Scotus would torture whoever denies contingency, and Epictetus threatened whoever disputes non-contradiction. Hence a Christianity that defends revelation by reason's methods ends by needing the Inquisition.
- **Shestov's theology.** The Fall was the replacement of faith by *knowledge*: the tree of knowledge, "the Original Lie". Biblical faith is "a completely different dimension of thought", whose substance is the "daring… conviction that **all things are possible**" (Mark 9:23). Philosophy must begin from "Out of the depths I cried unto Thee" and "My God, my God, why hast Thou forsaken me?"

## 3. Thompson: The Person Against the Organ Peg

Diane Oenning Thompson, in the Cambridge Companion, reads the *Notes* as a defence of **personhood** against scientific reductionism.

- The Underground Man is "the most isolated of all Dostoevskii's ideological heroes". He has "an animated relationship with quasi-personified ideas, but no communion with real, living persons".
- Science's word "is no one's word". It "cannot offer a word of love or hate". Twice-two-is-four "robs the person of potentials and reduces him to finitude… the end of dialogue".
- **The theological root.** Thompson quotes Zizioulas: the person "resists being 'regarded as an arithmetical concept'… personhood is the total fulfilment of being". "It is atheistic humanism which has detached the concept of the person from theology." For Dostoevsky truth "resides not in concepts, but in a person, in Christ". Hence his letter of 1854: if it were proved that Christ is outside the truth, he would rather "remain with Christ".
- **The later novels.** The same pattern recurs:
  - Raskolnikov's "percentage" of girls who must go to ruin;
  - Ippolit before Holbein's *Dead Christ*, where "nature" seems to have swallowed even Him;
  - Shigalev's scientific despotism in *Demons*;
  - Ivan's "all is permitted" if there is no one to answer to.

## 4. Girard: Underground Psychology, Underground Metaphysics

Girard's short book (*Dostoïevski, du double à l'unité*, 1963) reads all of Dostoevsky as one spiritual journey, "a death and a resurrection", and the underground as its turning point. See [[Mimetic Desire: Girard, Oughourlian and Lacan]].

**The early works lie (ch. 1)**
- From *Poor Folk* to *The Insulted and Injured* (1861) the same triangle recurs: the hero helps his rival to win the woman he loves and calls it self-sacrifice. Dostoevsky lived it himself in Siberia. He promoted the career of his rival Vergunov for Maria Isaeva, and lost interest in her once no one contested her.
- The romantic rhetoric of "magnanimity" hides "masochism and voyeurism to the second power". "These love affairs are born only from the obstacle that opposes them by means of a third person."

**Underground psychology (ch. 2)**
- With *Notes* and *The Eternal Husband*, "behind the transformation of Dostoevsky's art there is a veritable psychological conversion". The key is **pride**. "The most fanatical pride is the one most bound to humiliate itself before the other". "At the least defeat the most extreme egoism makes voluntary slaves of us."
- **The rival-model.** The officer who brushed him aside becomes monstrous. "The prestige with which we endow a rival who is too successful is always the measure of our vanity." The dreamer rises "to the seventh heaven" in fantasy, then "falls as deeply down in reality as he ascended".
- **Against the famous Part One.** Girard calls the "right to caprice" "very inferior" to the narrative. It opposes utilitarianism with "an empty and abstract freedom… which really does not refute anything at all", a "vague irrationalism", and it is where Gide got his "gratuitous act". Here Girard directly opposes Shestov. Dostoevsky's genius lies in the novels, not the polemic. In them freedom chooses among models: "individual existence remains basically imitative". "The church fathers held as evident a truth which later became obscured".
- **The banality of uniqueness.** "All underground individuals believe they are all the more 'unique' to the extent that they are, in fact, alike." Masochist and sadist find each other. "**The double**" (Golyadkin) is the hallucination produced when Self and Other merge in rivalry.

**Underground metaphysics (ch. 3)**
- **Raskolnikov** (*raskol*, schism) kills "to place his pride on an unshakable foundation", yet still depends "on the verdict of the Other". Nietzsche found the *Notes* in a bookshop and recognised in them his own *ressentiment*.
- **Myshkin and Stavrogin** (*The Idiot* and *Demons*) are "contradictory responses… to one and only one question: the spiritual meaning of detachment". "The difference… is simultaneously immense and minuscule." "The most extreme pride… is more easily confused with authentic humility."
- **Stavrogin as negative messiah.** His disciples call him "light", "sun", "Most High". Pyotr Verkhovensky says: "You are my idol… you are the sun, and me, I am nothing but an earthworm." "**Whoever revolts against God in order to adore himself always ends up adoring the Other.**" Stavrogin ("bearer of the cross") with Pyotr and Stepan Verkhovensky forms "a sort of demoniac counter-trinity". "The universe of hate parodies, in the least details, the universe of divine love."
- **Kirillov** kills himself to prove that he is god and end the Christian era. "He does not imitate Christ, he parodies him. He does not seek to collaborate in the work of redemption but to correct it." "The very essence of the underground is finally revealed."
- **Modern individualism**, from Descartes to Nietzsche, "took over little by little the prerogatives that had belonged to God". "Each mode of subjectivity must… affirm I am who I am." "Here the question really is who will be the heir, the only son of the dead God."

**Resurrection (ch. 4)**
- *The Brothers Karamazov* reaches the root: the father, parricide, and the shame of being one's father's son. "Because father and son are, in a sense, identical, parricide is simultaneously murder and suicide."
- **The Grand Inquisitor** is "the repetition and expansion" of the **three temptations** (Matt 4:1–11), which are Dostoevsky's own: "social messianism, doubt, and pride". The Inquisitor accuses Christ of *overestimating* man, and Simone Weil saw in him "the archetype of all totalitarian solutions".
- "There is no 'refuting' of the Legend since, from a Christian point of view, it is the devil… who is right" *about the world*. The answer is not argument but Christ's silent **kiss**, which Alyosha copies with Ivan. "Everything that you say serves not to blame, but to praise Christ."
- **Ivan's devil** is the double made plain: the parodist, "the father of lies… at once both seducer and adversary". Exorcised, evil "seems even derisory and ridiculous, nothing more than a bad nightmare".
- **Providence through idolatry.** "Satan, divided against himself, expels Satan. The idols destroy the idols." Dostoevsky's art is "prophetic… in a truly biblical sense, for he untiringly denounces the fall of the people of God back into idolatry".
- Dostoevsky's own line: "It is not as a child that I believe in Christ and confess him. It is through the crucible of doubt that my Hosanna has passed."

## 5. The Death of God

**Nietzsche's madman (*Gay Science* §125, 1882)** lights a lantern in the morning and cries "I seek God!" to unbelievers who laugh.
- "**We have killed him, you and I.** All of us are his murderers… What were we doing when we unchained this earth from its sun?… Are we not straying as through an infinite nothing? Do we not feel the breath of empty space?"
- "God is dead. God remains dead. And we have killed him… **Must we ourselves not become gods simply to appear worthy of it?**"
- Then: "I have come too early… This tremendous event is still on its way". The churches are now "the tombs and sepulchers of God".

**Dostoevsky had already drawn the conclusions.**
- Kirillov: "If there is no God, then I am God." "The attribute of my godhead is self-will." "They will divide history into two parts: from the gorilla to the annihilation of God, and from the annihilation of God to the transformation of the earth, and of man physically."
- Ivan Karamazov: if there is no immortality, "all things are lawful".

See [[Nietzsche on Nihilism and Its Overcoming]] and [[Dionysus versus the Crucified: Girard's Reading of Nietzsche]].

## 6. Rose: The Stages of Nihilism and the Worship of Nothingness

Eugene (later Fr. Seraphim) Rose wrote this in the early 1960s in a San Francisco basement, before becoming a monk. He had passed through Nietzsche, Zen and despair to Orthodoxy.

**The definition.** Nietzsche's: "That there is no truth; that there is no absolute state of affairs." "All truth is relative" is "the popular translation". It is self-contradictory, an absolute statement and a "negative metaphysics". "If there is no Revealed Truth, there is no truth at all." "He who will not live by the Christian Revelation must live by a false revelation".

**Four stages of "the Nihilist dialectic"**
1. **Liberalism**, a "passive Nihilism". Truth is "idle and unfruitful capital left over from a previous age". God is "a 'constitutional monarch' Whose authority has been totally delegated to the people". Its fatal blindness is to death. "If there is no immortality, the Liberal believes, one can still lead a civilized life; 'if there is no immortality'—is the far profounder logic of Ivan Karamazov—'all things are lawful.'"
2. **Realism**: Turgenev's Bazarov, Pisarev's frog, scientism, Marx. It is "nothing-but" reduction. The Christian love of truth, "frustrated of its proper object", is prostituted.
3. **Vitalism**: Symbolism, occultism, Nietzsche, Bergson, "blood and soil", and "the cult of 'awareness'". "The final divorce of life from truth." Rose sets its senseless crimes, its speed and its noise beside the underground man's caprice.
4. **The Nihilism of Destruction**: Stirner, Nechayev (the original of Pyotr Verkhovensky), Bakunin ("The passion for destruction is also a creative passion!"), Lenin, Dada, Hitler.

He compares the stages, after St John of Kronstadt, to a diseased eye that goes from blurring to blindness.

**The theology of nihilism**
- **Antitheism, not atheism.** Proudhon: "God, if there is a God, is [humanity's] enemy". "No one denies the true God except to devote himself to the service of a false god."
- **The worship of nothingness.** "Nothingness" in the modern sense "is a concept unique to the Christian tradition", the inverse of *creatio ex nihilo*. Maistre: "there never was before the eighteenth century… an insurrection against God". Dostoevsky: "the will is closest to nothing; the most assertive are closest to the most nihilistic."
- **The program**: destroy the Old Order, make the "new man", and prepare the "new age". Its final form is the Man-god, the Antichrist, "the Satanic imitation and inversion of the Kingdom of God".

**The answer**
- Hell is the final refutation of nihilism, since there is no annihilation. Rose quotes Zosima: "There are some who remain proud and fierce even in hell… they cry out that the God of life should be annihilated… And they will burn in the fire of their own wrath for ever and yearn for death and annihilation. But they will not attain to death." "It is the love of God itself which torments those who refuse it." See [[Hell as the River of Fire]].
- **The Christian's "nihilism of abundance"**: "to him, in the end, the world is nothing, and God is all", the opposite of a nihilism "that proceeds from the Abyss".

## 8. Assessment

*Mine.*

**Girard is right about the root**
- The underground is **pride**, and the Fathers had already mapped it. Vainglory and pride are the last and worst of Evagrius' eight thoughts. Climacus calls vainglory "a change of nature, a perversion of character, a taking note of criticism", and says "the proud man is a pomegranate, gone bad within, radiant outside" (*Ladder* 22–23). The underground man's swing "from contempt to the pedestal" is exactly the *Ladder*'s description of the vainglorious: never alone, always before an audience, even in solitude. See [[The Virtues and the Eight Passions]] and [[The Ladder of Divine Ascent]].
- Girard's formula ("whoever revolts against God in order to adore himself always ends up adoring the Other") is Romans 1:25 rewritten: they "worshipped and served the creature more than the Creator". It is also the Fall, as the serpent's "ye shall be as gods". The underground is the self trying to become god *by grasping*, and so becoming a slave to everyone who seems to have what it lacks. Christ's way is the opposite: He "thought it not robbery to be equal with God: but made himself of no reputation" (Phil 2:6–7). That is why Orthodoxy can speak of **theosis** without blasphemy. Deification is received as gift by the humble, never seized. See [[Theosis: Becoming by Grace What God Is by Nature]].

**Shestov is right about necessity, wrong about reason**
- He is right that the God of the Bible is not bound by any "law" above Him. The Fathers deny the Greek *anankē* exactly as he does. God creates freely, *ex nihilo*. And Orthodox personhood is precisely **freedom from the necessity of nature** (Zizioulas, Lossky). The Underground Man's cry that he is not an organ peg is a true cry of the image of God. See [[Being as Communion]] and [[Creation out of Nothing]].
- But Shestov sets faith against **logos** as such, and the Fathers would not. The Word is *Logos*. Reason's laws are not a wall against God but the shape of His wisdom in the creation (the *logoi*). The sin in Eden was not knowing but **disobedient self-assertion**: knowledge seized rather than received. "Twice two is four" does not spit at anyone. The underground man hears an insult in it because he hears everything as an insult. Girard's diagnosis covers Shestov's hero better than Shestov does. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].

**Rose is right about the end**
- Rose's stages fit the modern West painfully well, and his central point is the Gospel's: no neutral ground exists. "He that is not with me is against me; and he that gathereth not with me scattereth" (Matt 12:30). Girard quotes the same verse.
- But a reader should correct Rose's tone with Dostoevsky's method. Dostoevsky answered nihilism not by diatribe but by **descending into it** and finding Christ at the bottom: Sonya with Raskolnikov at Lazarus, the kiss for the Inquisitor, Zosima's "each of us is guilty before all, for all". Rose's own life, from Nietzsche to the monastery at Platina, is the same descent and ascent.

**The death of God and the Resurrection**
- Nietzsche's madman is right that "we have killed him", and the Church says so every Holy Friday: "Today He who hung the earth upon the waters is hung upon the Cross." But the Church's next word is the one Nietzsche never heard: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death." The murder of God has already happened, once, and it ended in Pascha. The madman's "I have come too early" is answered by the women who came *very early* to the tomb (Mark 16:2) and found it empty. See [[The Descent into Hades]] and [[The Case for the Resurrection of Jesus]].
- **Ivan's "ticket"**, returned because of the suffering of children, is the strongest form of the problem of evil. Dostoevsky answers it, as Girard says, not with a theodicy but with Alyosha's care for Ilyusha and with the Cross. See [[The Problem of Evil and Theodicy]].
