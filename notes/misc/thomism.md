**Thomism** is the philosophical and theological system based on the teachings of **Thomas Aquinas**, a 13th-century Catholic philosopher and theologian. Thomism seeks to reconcile **faith and reason**, drawing from Christian theology, especially Catholic doctrine, and the philosophy of ancient thinkers like **Aristotle**. The core of Thomism is Aquinas' belief in the **existence of God** and the use of **natural reason** to understand divine truths.

Aquinas' Thomism emphasizes concepts such as the **existence of God**, **the nature of being**, **moral philosophy**, and the relationship between **grace** and **nature**. It holds that through **natural reason**, one can arrive at certain truths about God, the world, and morality, and that these truths align with **revelation** in Scripture.

## "God is Pure Act"

In Thomistic theology, when it is said that **"God is pure act"**, it refers to the concept of **"actus purus"** in Latin, meaning that God is **completely actualized** and lacks any potentiality. This idea is derived from Aquinas' interpretation of Aristotle's **Metaphysics**.

1. **Act vs. Potency**: Aquinas differentiates between **act** (the actualization of being) and **potency** (the potential for being). For created beings, there is always a combination of both: things are potentially something (they can change, grow, or develop) and actually something (they exist as they are). In contrast, God has no potentiality. He is **pure act** in that He is fully actualized in every way and lacks any possibility of change or development.
2. **God's Perfection**: By being pure act, God is **perfect**. In Thomism, **perfection** means completeness, and since God is **pure act**, He does not need to undergo change or development. He is fully **actualized** and exists in a state of absolute perfection, unlike anything in the created world, which is always in a state of becoming and change.
3. **No Potentiality**: The concept of "pure act" also implies that God is **immutable** (unchanging) and **eternal**. Unlike created things, which can move from potentiality to actuality (for instance, a seed growing into a tree), God does not change or grow. He is always fully Himself in an eternal present.
4. **Theological Implications**: This concept of God as pure act is crucial for understanding God’s nature. It means that God is not contingent on anything else; He is **necessary**, **self-existent**, and not dependent on the world or anything else for His existence. This idea underlines God's **sovereignty**, **infinite perfection**, and **theological transcendence** in Thomistic thought.

In summary, **"God is pure act"** in Thomism means that God is fully actualized, eternal, and unchanging, with no potential for development or improvement—He is perfect and complete in every aspect of His being.
