### Essence and Energies

1. **Divine Essence:** Refers to what God is in Himself—His incomprehensible nature that remains transcendent and cannot be fully grasped by created beings.
2. **Divine Energies:** These are the ways in which God interacts with the world and reveals Himself to creation. They are uncreated and allow for God's presence and action in the universe.

### Implications for Free Will

1. **If Essence Equals Energies:** If God's essence were the same as His energies, it would mean that His nature determines His actions entirely. This would imply that God's actions are necessary and dictated by His nature.
2. **Necessity vs. Freedom:** In this scenario, God would act out of necessity rather than free will. Since His energies (how He acts) would be inseparable from His essence (who He is), every action would be a direct result of His nature, leaving no room for choice or freedom.
3. **Divine Freedom:** For God to possess true free will, His essence must be distinct from His energies. This distinction allows God to act freely and sovereignly, choosing how to manifest His will without being constrained by His nature. Thus, while God's actions are perfectly good and aligned with His essence, they are not determined by it in a way that negates His freedom.

### Conclusion

In summary, if God's essence were the same as His energies, it would imply that His actions are necessary and not chosen, which would negate the concept of divine free will. This could also suggest that God is as old as His creation since His actions would be tied to the existence of creation. However, traditional Christian theology holds that God is eternal and exists outside of time, meaning He is not bound by creation or its temporal nature. The distinction between essence and energies preserves the understanding that God freely chooses how to engage with creation while remaining perfectly consistent with His nature.
