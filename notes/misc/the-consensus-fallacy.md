title: The Consensus Fallacy
tags: [theology, fallacies]

The Consensus Fallacy, also known as the Appeal to Popularity (*Argumentum ad Populum*), occurs when someone argues that a belief must be true simply because a large number of people accept it. This is a logical fallacy because truth is not determined by the number of people who believe something but by objective evidence and sound reasoning.

### Structure of the Fallacy:

- *Premise:* "Most people believe X."
- *Conclusion:* "Therefore, X must be true."

### Why Is It a Fallacy?

1. Majority opinion is often wrong – History shows that widely accepted beliefs can be false (e.g., geocentrism, the idea that smoking is harmless).
2. Truth is independent of popularity – Something is true or false based on reality, not human agreement.
3. Bandwagon effect – People often accept ideas due to social pressure rather than rational evaluation.

### Examples of the Consensus Fallacy:

- "Millions of people believe in astrology, so it must be true!"
- "Most scientists agree with theory X, so it cannot be questioned!"
- "All the major news outlets report this, so it must be factual!"

In epistemological debates, this fallacy is often used to silence dissenting views without addressing their actual arguments.
