The **Canons of the First Council of Nicaea (325 AD)** were a set of church laws created to address disciplinary and doctrinal issues. They formed the foundation of canon law in the early Church. Here's a summary:

## Key Canons

1. **Canon 1: Eunuchs and Clergy**
    - Those who became eunuchs voluntarily cannot join the clergy. However, those made eunuchs by necessity (e.g., through medical or violent circumstances) can.

---

2. **Canon 2: Clerical Promotion**
    - New converts should not be immediately ordained as bishops, priests, or deacons. There should be a period of instruction to ensure they are well-versed in the faith.

---

3. **Canon 3: Moral Purity of Clergy**
    - Clerics (bishops, priests, and deacons) must not live with women unless they are close relatives (e.g., mothers or sisters) to prevent scandal.

---

4. **Canon 4: Election of Bishops**
    - Bishops must be appointed with the approval of all neighboring bishops. A minimum of three bishops should confirm the election, with the metropolitan bishop's approval.

---

5. **Canon 5: Excommunication**
    - A person excommunicated by one bishop cannot be received by another without proper investigation. This canon ensured consistency and avoided "forum shopping."

---

6. **Canon 6: Jurisdiction of Major Sees**
    - Recognized the jurisdictional primacy of certain sees, such as Alexandria over Egypt, Libya, and Pentapolis; Rome over the western territories; and Antioch over the East.

---

7. **Canon 7: Honor of Jerusalem**
    - While Jerusalem didn't hold jurisdiction like other major sees, it was to be given special honor due to its significance in salvation history.

---

8. **Canon 8: Novatians**
    - Provided for the reintegration of Novatian schismatics into the Church if they accepted orthodox doctrine and adhered to Church rules.

---

9. **Canon 10: Clergy from the Lapsed**
    - Those who denied the faith during persecution and later repented could not serve as clergy.

---

10. **Canon 13: Communion at Death**
    - Even those excommunicated should be given communion if they are on their deathbed.

---

## Importance of the Canons

These canons established norms for ecclesiastical governance, addressing issues of authority, discipline, and heresy. They also affirmed unity under the Church's hierarchical structure and clarified procedures for resolving disputes, which contributed to stabilizing the early Church.
