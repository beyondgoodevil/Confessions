Between Good Friday and Pascha Christ's body lay in the tomb and His soul, united to His divinity, went down to **Hades**, the place of the dead. Not to suffer there, but to **destroy it** from the inside and bring out the righteous dead. The Apostles' Creed says "He descended into hell"; the Nicene Creed doesn't mention it, but in the East it's at the centre of the Paschal faith.

*Hades* (Hebrew *Sheol*) isn't "hell" in the sense of the final punishment (*Gehenna*). It's the realm of the dead, where everyone went before Christ, righteous and wicked alike.

## 1. Scripture

**1 Peter**
- 1 Pet 3:18-19: "Christ also hath once suffered for sins... being put to death in the flesh, but quickened by the Spirit: by which also **he went and preached unto the spirits in prison**; which sometime were disobedient... in the days of Noah."
- 1 Pet 4:6: "For this cause was **the gospel preached also to them that are dead**, that they might be judged according to men in the flesh, but live according to God in the spirit."

**Psalm 16 / Acts 2**
- Ps 16:10: "For **thou wilt not leave my soul in hell**; neither wilt thou suffer thine Holy One to see corruption."
- Acts 2:24, 27, 31: Peter at Pentecost applies it to Christ: "he seeing this before spake of the resurrection of Christ, that **his soul was not left in hell**, neither his flesh did see corruption."

**Ephesians 4**
- Eph 4:8-9: "When he ascended up on high, **he led captivity captive**... Now that he ascended, what is it but that **he also descended first into the lower parts of the earth**?" (quoting Ps 68:18).

**The sign of Jonah**
- Matt 12:40: "as Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly; so shall the Son of man be three days and three nights **in the heart of the earth**."
- Jonah 2:2: "out of the belly of hell cried I."

**The strong man bound**
- Matt 12:29: "how can one enter into a strong man's house, and spoil his goods, except he **first bind the strong man**?"

**Death defeated**
- Hos 13:14: "I will ransom them from the power of the grave; I will redeem them from death: **O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave, I will be thy destruction**." Quoted 1 Cor 15:55.
- Heb 2:14-15: "that **through death he might destroy him that had the power of death**, that is, the devil; and deliver them who through fear of death were all their lifetime subject to bondage."
- Rev 1:18: "I am he that liveth, and was dead... and **have the keys of hell and of death**."
- Matt 27:52-53: "the graves were opened; and many bodies of the saints which slept arose."
- Isa 45:2: "I will **break in pieces the gates of brass**, and cut in sunder the bars of iron." Ps 107:16 the same.
- Zech 9:11: "by the blood of thy covenant I have **sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit** wherein is no water."

## 2. The Fathers

- **St. Irenaeus**: the Lord went to those under the earth, preaching His coming and the forgiveness of sins to those who believed in Him (the patriarchs and prophets).
- **St. Ignatius**, *Magnesians* 9: the prophets He waited for, He raised from the dead when He came.
- **St. Melito of Sardis**, *On Pascha*: "I am the one that destroyed death and triumphed over the enemy and trampled Hades under foot and bound the strong one."
- **St. Cyril of Jerusalem**: death was struck with dismay at seeing a new visitor descend into Hades, not bound by its chains.
- **The ancient Holy Saturday homily** (read in Catholic Office too): Christ goes looking for Adam "as for a lost sheep", takes him by the hand: "Awake, O sleeper... I am your God, who for your sake became your son."
- **The Gospel of Nicodemus** (apocryphal, 4th–5th c.) gives the dramatic version: Hades and Satan arguing as the gates break. It shaped the medieval "Harrowing of Hell" plays.

## 3. Death Swallowed by Deception

A theme in the Fathers (Gregory of Nyssa, Chrysostom, Cyril): death took Christ as just another man, and swallowed what it couldn't hold.

- Gregory of Nyssa's image: the divinity hidden in the humanity like a hook in bait. Death took the bait.
- Chrysostom's **Paschal Homily**, read at every Orthodox Pascha:
    - "Hell was embittered when it encountered Thee below."
    - "It took a body, and met God face to face. It took earth, and encountered Heaven. It took what it saw, and fell upon what it did not see."
    - "O Death, where is thy sting? O Hell, where is thy victory? Christ is risen, and thou art overthrown."

## 4. The Hymns

**The Paschal troparion**, sung dozens of times during the season:

> Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life.

**Holy Saturday** is the "Blessed Sabbath": Christ resting in the tomb as God rested on the seventh day.

- Vespers and Liturgy of St. Basil on Holy Saturday morning: the clergy change from dark to bright vestments, scatter bay leaves, "Arise, O God, judge the earth" (Ps 82:8). The first announcement of the Resurrection before Pascha.
- The **Lamentations** (Matins of Holy Saturday, served Friday night): Hades groaning at His coming. One verse, roughly: Hades was embittered, for it received a corpse and found God.
- The hymns' theme: the One who holds all things lies in the tomb, and Hades is emptied by His presence.

## 5. The Icon

The Orthodox icon of the Resurrection isn't Christ stepping out of the tomb (the Gospels don't describe that moment). It's the **Anastasis**, the descent:

- Christ in white, in a mandorla of light, standing on the **broken gates of Hades**, laid in the form of a cross.
- Keys, locks and nails scattered in the dark below. Sometimes **Satan bound** in the pit.
- Christ pulls **Adam and Eve** out of their tombs **by the wrists** (not the hands: they can't save themselves; He does the lifting).
- Around them: David and Solomon (crowned), John the Baptist (who went ahead to preach to Hades too), Abel, the prophets.

It shows the Resurrection as the event that affects the whole of humanity, starting from the first man.

## 6. Orthodox vs Western Views

| | Orthodox | Catholic | Some Protestants |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where | Hades, the realm of all the dead | the "limbo of the fathers" (a part of Hades for the righteous) | Calvin: the descent = Christ's suffering of hell's torments on the cross. Some deny it |
| Who He frees | the righteous at least; hymns can sound as if Hades is emptied | the righteous of the OT only | — |
| Why | to destroy death and Hades from within | to bring the just into heaven, opened by His death | to bear the full punishment |
| Mood | victory | victory | suffering (Calvin), or omitted |

- The Calvinist reading ties into penal substitution: Christ suffers the damned's punishment. Orthodoxy has Christ going down as **conqueror**, not victim. See [[Recapitulation Doctrine vs PSA]].
- Whether everyone in Hades was freed, or only those who accepted His preaching, is left open by the hymns. They speak broadly ("Hades emptied"), but the Church doesn't teach that all were saved.

## 7. Notes

*Mine.*

- Fits the [[Ancestral Sin vs Original Sin]] picture: if what we inherited from Adam is death, the cure is the destruction of death. Christ's descent is the point where He meets humanity at the bottom.
- Connects with recapitulation: Christ goes through every stage of human existence, including being dead. See [[Doctrine of Recapitulation]].
- Answers the "what about those who lived before Christ?" question in part: the righteous dead heard the Gospel too (1 Pet 4:6).
