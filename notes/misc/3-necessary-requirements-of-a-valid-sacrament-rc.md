In **Roman Catholic Canon Law**, three essential elements are required for the valid celebration of any sacrament:

---

## 1. Proper Matter

- Each sacrament has specific physical elements or actions as its matter.
    - **Baptism**: Water.
    - **Eucharist**: Bread (made of wheat) and wine (from grapes).
    - **Confirmation**: Sacred Chrism (oil).
    - **Holy Orders**: Laying on of hands.
    - **Matrimony**: Mutual consent of the couple.
    - **Penance**: Confession of sins and contrition.
    - **Anointing of the Sick**: Oil of the sick.

---

## 2. Proper Form

- The specific words or prayers used in the administration of the sacrament.
    - **Baptism**: “I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.”
    - **Eucharist**: Words of Consecration: “This is my body... this is my blood.”
    - **Confirmation**: “Be sealed with the gift of the Holy Spirit.”
    - **Penance**: Words of absolution: “I absolve you from your sins…”
    - **Marriage**: Exchange of vows.
    - **Holy Orders**: Consecratory prayer during laying on of hands.
    - **Anointing of the Sick**: “Through this holy anointing, may the Lord in his love and mercy help you…”

---

## 3. Proper Minister with Intent

- The sacrament must be administered by someone with the proper authority and **intention**:
    - **Baptism**: Normally a priest or deacon, but in emergencies, anyone can baptize if they intend to do what the Church does.
    - **Eucharist**: Only a validly ordained priest.
    - **Confirmation**: A bishop, or a priest with delegated authority.
    - **Holy Orders**: A bishop.
    - **Marriage**: The spouses themselves are the ministers, with the priest or deacon as the Church’s witness.
    - **Penance**: A validly ordained priest with faculties.
    - **Anointing of the Sick**: A priest.

---

## Summary

For a sacrament to be valid according to Roman Catholic law, it must have the **proper matter**, **proper form**, and be administered by a **proper minister** with the **intention** to perform the sacrament in accordance with the Church. Without these three elements, the sacrament is considered invalid.
