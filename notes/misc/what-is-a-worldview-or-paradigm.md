A **worldview** (or **paradigm**) is the **set of presuppositions, beliefs, and interpretative frameworks** through which a person understands reality. It functions like **a lens** that shapes how we perceive truth, knowledge, morality, and existence itself.

## 1. The Structure of a Worldview

A worldview consists of answers to fundamental questions, such as:

- **Metaphysics (Reality):** What is the nature of existence? Is reality purely material, or is there something beyond the physical?
- **Epistemology (Knowledge):** How do we know what we know? What justifies our beliefs?
- **Ethics (Morality):** What is good and evil? How do we determine right from wrong?
- **Anthropology (Human Nature):** What is man? Does he have a soul, or is he just a biological machine?

Each worldview **assumes certain foundational truths** that it does not continuously question—these are called **presuppositions**.

## 2. Paradigms as Interpretative Frameworks

A **paradigm** is a specific way of organizing knowledge and interpreting facts within a worldview. Different paradigms lead to different conclusions, even when people examine the same evidence.

For example:

- A materialist (atheistic) paradigm assumes that **all reality can be explained through physical processes**.
- A theistic paradigm assumes that **there is a transcendent God who grounds existence, knowledge, and morality**.

Because of these **underlying presuppositions**, individuals with different paradigms will interpret the same data in **radically different ways**.

## 3. The Inescapability of Worldviews

Some claim that they have **no worldview** and simply follow “evidence.” However, this is impossible because:

- **Every interpretation of evidence relies on assumptions.**
- **Neutrality is itself a worldview**—the belief that one can be purely "neutral" is already a commitment to a certain epistemology.

Thus, **everyone operates within a worldview**, whether consciously or unconsciously. The key question is not whether one has a worldview, but whether one’s worldview is **coherent, consistent, and justifiable**.
