The Orthodox Church recognizes nine key ecumenical councils as foundational for its theology, doctrine, and ecclesiastical order. Below is a summary of these councils and their major decisions:

## 1. First Ecumenical Council: Nicaea I (325 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned Arianism (which denied the divinity of Christ).
- **Creed**: Formulated the Nicene Creed, affirming that Christ is "of one essence with the Father" (*homoousios*).
- **Easter Date**: Standardized the calculation of the date of Easter.

## 2. Second Ecumenical Council: Constantinople I (381 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned Macedonianism (which denied the divinity of the Holy Spirit).
- **Creed**: Expanded the Nicene Creed to include a fuller statement on the Holy Spirit.
- **Trinitarian Doctrine**: Affirmed the full divinity of the Holy Spirit.

## 3. Third Ecumenical Council: Ephesus (431 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned Nestorianism (which separated the natures of Christ too sharply).
- **Theotokos**: Affirmed Mary as *Theotokos* ("God-bearer"), emphasizing the unity of Christ's divine and human natures.

## 4. Fourth Ecumenical Council: Chalcedon (451 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned Eutychianism/Monophysitism (which argued for one blended nature of Christ).
- **Definition of Faith**: Declared Christ as fully God and fully man in two natures, "without confusion, change, division, or separation."

## 5. Fifth Ecumenical Council: Constantinople II (553 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned certain writings and interpretations associated with Nestorianism.
- **Theology**: Reinforced the decisions of Ephesus and Chalcedon regarding Christology.

## 6. Sixth Ecumenical Council: Constantinople III (680-681 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned Monothelitism (which taught Christ had only one will).
- **Doctrine of Two Wills**: Affirmed that Christ has two wills, divine and human, in harmony.

## 7. Seventh Ecumenical Council: Nicaea II (787 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Condemned Iconoclasm (the rejection of holy icons).
- **Veneration of Icons**: Affirmed the proper veneration (not worship) of icons as a legitimate practice rooted in the Incarnation.

## 8. Fourth Council of Constantinople (879-880 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Affirmed the restoration of Patriarch Photius and clarified the Orthodox position on the Filioque (rejecting its addition to the Creed).
- **Filioque**: Maintained that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone, not "and the Son."

## 9. Fifth Council of Constantinople (1341-1351 AD)

- **Key Decision**: Defended Hesychasm and the theology of St. Gregory Palamas.
- **Essence-Energies Distinction**: Affirmed that God's essence is unknowable, but His energies can be experienced through prayer and communion.

## Summary

These councils shaped core Orthodox teachings:

1. **Trinity**: Nicaea I, Constantinople I.
2. **Christology**: Ephesus, Chalcedon, Constantinople II, Constantinople III.
3. **Veneration and Worship**: Nicaea II.
4. **Church and Doctrine**: Constantinople IV and V.

They collectively provide the theological framework and safeguard against heresies, ensuring continuity with Apostolic Tradition.
