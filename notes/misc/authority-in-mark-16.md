## 1. Authority in Mark 16:17-18

In Mark 16:17-18, Jesus says that certain signs will accompany those who believe: in His name they will cast out demons and speak in new tongues; they will pick up serpents, and deadly poison will not hurt them; they will lay hands on the sick, who will recover. *(Paraphrased; the image quotes a modern copyrighted translation.)*

These promises of miraculous signs apply broadly to believers, affirming the power of faith. However, this does not equate all believers with the same authority in governance or ministry.

## 2. Differentiated Roles in Acts

The **Book of Acts** demonstrates that while all believers share in faith, spiritual authority is exercised differently:

- **Apostolic Leadership**:
    - Acts 1:21-26 shows how the apostles chose Matthias to replace Judas, indicating a special role for the apostles in governance.
    - Acts 6:1-6 highlights the appointment of deacons to specific tasks by the apostles.
- **Delegated Authority**:
    - Paul and Barnabas appoint elders in each church (Acts 14:23), emphasizing structured leadership.
    - Acts 15:19-22 describes the **Council of Jerusalem**, where decisions about doctrine were made by apostles and elders, not by general believers.
- **Special Powers Granted to Apostles**:
    - Acts 5:1-11: Peter exercises unique authority in the judgment of Ananias and Sapphira.
    - Acts 8:14-17: Apostles, specifically Peter and John, impart the Holy Spirit by laying on hands, a role not given to all believers.

## 3. Key Points of Distinction

- **Teaching Authority**: Only appointed leaders (apostles, elders) are seen teaching authoritatively (Acts 2:42; 20:28).
- **Miracles and Signs**: While miracles accompany various believers, governance and doctrinal authority remain with a specific hierarchy.

## Conclusion

Mark 16 speaks of signs that accompany believers, but Acts clearly distinguishes between the general empowerment of believers and the unique authority granted to church leaders. Claiming all believers have equal authority misrepresents the structured hierarchy God established through the apostles.
