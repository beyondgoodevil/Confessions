**Mortalium Animos** is an encyclical issued by Pope Pius XI on January 6, 1928. In this document, the pope strongly condemned the idea of ecumenism and the notion of Christian unity that involved the recognition of non-Catholic Christian communities, particularly Protestant denominations, as legitimate parts of the Church. It was issued in response to efforts, especially in the Anglican Communion, to promote the "Branch Theory" and attempts at rapprochement between Catholics and non-Catholic Christians.

### Key Points of Mortalium Animos:

1. **Rejection of Ecumenism**:  
   Pope Pius XI argued that efforts to unite with non-Catholic Christians under the "Branch Theory" were misguided. He stated that there is only one true Church, the Roman Catholic Church, and it is through it that unity should be sought.
2. **Warning Against False Unity**:  
   The encyclical warned Catholics against participating in interdenominational movements that sought unity without the Catholic Church. The pope emphasized that these movements could lead to relativism and the undermining of the truth of Catholic doctrine.
3. **The Importance of the True Church**:  
   The document affirmed the Catholic Church as the only true Church of Christ, and it rejected the idea that Protestant communities, or any other non-Catholic Christian groups, could be regarded as legitimate branches of this one true Church.
4. **Call for True Unity**:  
   Pope Pius XI emphasized that true Christian unity could only be achieved through the return of non-Catholic Christians to full communion with the Catholic Church.

### Theological Implications:

- **Mortalium Animos** reaffirmed the Catholic position on the exclusivity of the Church as the repository of the fullness of truth.
- It set the tone for the Catholic approach to ecumenism until Vatican II, which introduced a more open approach to dialogue with other Christian denominations.

This encyclical played a significant role in the Church's stance against ecumenical movements before the more inclusive approach of Vatican II in the 1960s.
