1. **Life:** God's sustaining power that gives life to all creation.
2. **Grace:** The uncreated energy that brings divine help and transformation to believers.
3. **Love:** God's perfect and infinite love, actively drawing creation to Himself.
4. **Wisdom:** The manifestation of divine wisdom, guiding creation and human history.
5. **Glory:** The radiance of God's presence that can be experienced by those who are spiritually purified.
6. **Power:** God's omnipotence, shown through His governance and actions in the world.
7. **Holiness:** The manifestation of God's sanctity and purity, making things holy.
8. **Truth:** The energy of God's absolute truth, leading believers to knowledge of Him.
9. **Justice:** God's righteous actions, establishing order and equity in creation.
10. **Mercy:** God's compassionate action towards creation, offering forgiveness and help.

## What the Energies Are

Palamas's account in the *Triads*, with the earlier Fathers behind each point (Bradshaw).

- *Energeia* = activity, operation. God's energies are **God Himself in action**, going out toward creation and making Himself known and shared.
    - **John of Damascus**: the energies are the "movement" of the divine nature.
    - Dionysius: when we call God "life" or "essence" we mean "the providential powers produced from the imparticipable God" (*Triads* III.ii).
- **Uncreated**: they are God, not created effects. "The deifying gift of the Spirit is not the essence of God, but it is an energy of the essence, uncreated" (*Triads* III.1).
    - Palamas's argument (*Triads* III.ii.5): if only the essence is uncreated, does it have knowledge, providence and the power to deify? If not, it isn't God. If it got them later, it was once imperfect. So they're **eternal and uncreated**, but not the essence.
    - **Maximus**: "existence, life, holiness and virtue are works of God that do not have a beginning in time"; "there was never a time when virtue, goodness, holiness and immortality did not exist."
    - Creation has a beginning, but "beginning and end must be ascribed... to its activity... as directed towards created things", not to the creative power itself.
- **Really distinct** from the essence, but **not separate**: the sun and its rays. The essence is unknowable and unparticipable; the energies are known and participated.
    - **Basil**, *Letter 234*: "We know our God from His energies, but we do not claim to approach His essence. His energies come down to us, but His essence remains unapproachable."
    - Two kinds of glory (Maximus, quoted by Palamas): one "beyond participation", identical to the essence; one participable.
    - God "communicates to us not His nature, but His proper glory and splendour". If deification were participation in the essence, the deified would be "God by nature".
    - **Dionysius**: the divine names; God's "processions" (*proodoi*) in which creatures participate. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]].
- **Many**, but undivided: goodness, wisdom, life, power, light, grace; and one, since God is one.
    - **Gregory of Nyssa**: the names of God (good, wise, just) name His energies, not His essence. See [[Saint Gregory of Nyssa: Essence Energies]].
- **Common** to the three Persons: every energy is from the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit.
- **Not a third thing** between God and creatures: "the whole God" is present in each energy.
- Known by the saints as **light**: the light of Tabor, seen by the apostles and the hesychasts, is an uncreated energy. See [[The Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor]].
    - The saints call the light "divinity" because it deifies. Dionysius to Gaius: God is "beyond the thearchy" if "divinity" means the deifying gift; so God is "**more-than-God**" only because the deifying grace is called "divinity".
    - The light is "**enhypostatic**": it has no hypostasis of its own but is seen in the hypostasis of another (the saint), and is never separate from the Spirit who gives it.
    - It's seen neither by the intellect nor by the body but "by the Spirit": "spiritual sensation". Beyond prayer there is "the ineffable vision, and ecstasy in the vision"; images are used to speak of it "not because that is the way in which these things are seen". Dionysius: better to call it **union** than knowledge.
    - Andrew of Crete: the angels "celebrate this light in an immaterial manner". Christ "has always possessed it, and always will".

## Barlaam's Objection and Palamas's Answers

The *Triads* (nine treatises, 1338–41, in three groups of three) answer first Barlaam's spoken teaching, then his writings (Meyendorff's introduction):

- **Barlaam**, a Calabrian Greek, had argued (on the *filioque*) that reasoning about the Trinity can't reach "apodictic" certainty, only dialectic. Some historians compare the dispute with Western Nominalism, though the contexts differ.
- **Akindynos**: God is identical with His essence, so any vision of God is of the essence or of something created; no real distinctions in God. **Gregoras** took his side. Councils in **1347** and **1351** upheld Palamas, who became archbishop of Thessalonica; his disciples became patriarchs.
- Pelikan's preface: older Western reference works treated Palamas as a scandal. One encyclopedia said the controversy "ended with a resurrection of polytheism".

| Barlaam / later Western critics | Palamas |
|---|---|
| God is simple; any distinction in Him makes Him composite | a distinction isn't a composition; the sun isn't composed of its rays. See [[Distinction Does Not Necessitate Separation, Division, or Composition]] |
| if the light of Tabor is uncreated, it is God's essence, which no one can see | it is uncreated but not the essence: an energy |
| so it must be created, a symbol | then the apostles saw a created phenomenon, and theosis is metaphor |
| the light is a "symbol" of divinity | a natural symbol or not? If not, it's a passing phantom; if natural, it belongs to Christ's divinity |
| knowledge of God comes through philosophy and Scripture's concepts | God is known by **experience** in prayer, by those purified; the hesychasts see what philosophers can't |

On philosophy (*Triads* I): its natural power comes from God, but its use can be "wisdom become folly". There's medicine in **serpent's flesh**, but you must first "kill the serpent" (the pride) and purify the rest, like separating honey from hemlock; "all or most of the harmful heresies" came from it. Against the "iconognosts", who think the image of God is restored by knowledge: Christ didn't come to teach philosophy but to take away sin. Barlaam's side made knowledge the highest illumination; Palamas: the light is "neither an opinion nor a conclusion reached by syllogistic argument". The method of prayer itself is in [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]].

## Why It Matters

- **Theosis** is real participation in God, not in a created grace. 2 Pet 1:4: "partakers of the divine nature" (by energy, not essence).
    - **Maximus**: two natural energies in Christ; deification as sharing God's energy (Bradshaw). See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].
    - Palamas's **Christological argument**: "How would we come to know Christ in two energies and two natures, if the natural energies of God are not uncreated?" A nature whose natural energies are created is itself created. So denying uncreated energies undermines Constantinople III.
    - "The end of prayer is to be snatched away to God." The mind leaves first evil things, then neutral, then even higher things. Paul became "light by grace, and nonbeing by transcendence". At the Last Day the light will deify "the sons of the Resurrection" openly. "The prize of virtue... is to become God."
    - Knowing creatures and inferring God by analogy isn't the same as having God dwell in you.
- **Creation is free**: creating is an energy of God's will, not a necessity of His essence. See [[How the Absolutely Simple Creator Escapes a Modal Collapse]].
- **Apophatic and cataphatic** together: unknowable essence, knowable energies. See [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]].
