## 1. Ignatius of Antioch (c. 35–107 AD)

“For our God, Jesus Christ, was conceived by Mary according to God’s plan, of the seed of David, but also by the Holy Spirit.” — *Letter to the Ephesians, 18*

“There is one Physician, who is both flesh and spirit, born and unborn, God in man, true life in death, from Mary and from God, first subject to suffering and then beyond it—Jesus Christ our Lord.” — *Letter to the Ephesians, 7*

### Ignatius' Teaching on the Trinity

- Clearly calls **Jesus Christ "God"**, confirming His divinity.
- Mentions the **Holy Spirit** in connection with Christ’s conception, showing a triadic understanding.

## 2. The Didache (1st Century)

“Baptize in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.” — *Didache, 7.1*

### Didache’s Teaching on the Trinity

- Uses the same Trinitarian baptismal formula found in **Matthew 28:19**, proving that this belief existed in the earliest Christian communities.

## 3. Justin Martyr (c. 100–165 AD)

“For, in the name of God, the Father and Lord of the universe, and of our Savior Jesus Christ, and of the Holy Spirit, they then receive the washing with water.” — *First Apology, 61*

“The most true God is the Father of righteousness... and His Son, who came forth from Him and taught us these things, and the host of the other good angels who follow and are made like Him, and the prophetic Spirit, we worship and adore.” — *First Apology, 6*

### Justin Martyr’s Teaching on the Trinity

- Confirms **Trinitarian baptism**, as practiced in the early Church.
- Identifies **the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit** as objects of worship, showing their divine status.
