> "Vanity of vanities, saith the Preacher, vanity of vanities; all is vanity." (Ecclesiastes 1:2)

> "For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: and whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall find it." (Matthew 16:25)

> "Where is the wise? where is the scribe? where is the disputer of this world? hath not God made foolish the wisdom of this world?" (1 Corinthians 1:20)

The *Zhuangzi* is the strangest and funniest book of ancient philosophy. It is a heap of fables, jokes, dialogues and arguments: talking trees, a cook who never blunts his knife, a man who sings when his wife dies, a butterfly who may be dreaming she is a man. Underneath the jokes is a serious attack on the confidence of every school that claims to know **the right way** (*dao*), and a picture of life as **free, skilful responsiveness** rather than rule-following.

## 1. The Book and the Man

- **Zhuang Zhou** (c. 369–286 BC) came from Meng in the state of Song. Sima Qian's short biography says he was a minor official in a lacquer-tree park. It also tells a story: offered a prime ministership, he compared himself to the sacrificial ox, fed and dressed in silk for years only to be led to the temple for slaughter, and said he would rather wallow in the mud.
- **The text.** The book we have is Guo Xiang's edition (d. 312 AD), cut down from 52 chapters to 33 and divided into **Inner** (1–7), **Outer** (8–22) and **Miscellaneous** (23–33) chapters. Most scholars think only the Inner Chapters are close to Zhuangzi himself.
  - A. C. Graham and Liu Xiaogan sort the rest into layers: his school (17–22), "primitivists" (8–11), "Yangists" (28–31) and syncretists (11–16, 33).
  - Ziporyn's preface lays out three ways to read it: as an **anthology**, as a **genius's notebook**, or (as most premodern readers did) as **one deliberate journey** from the inner teachings outwards.
- **Laozi came later?** The Inner Chapters never quote the *Daodejing*. Graham thought they were older than it. The Guodian bamboo slips (late 4th c. BC) make that less likely, but the book in its present form, and its link to "Lao Dan", are not attested until after Zhuangzi's death. So the traditional order, Laozi first and then Zhuangzi, is not safe (SEP).
- **Huizi** (Hui Shi), the logician, is Zhuangzi's friend, sparring partner and straight man. He turns up in about ten chapters. The last chapter mourns him. One story has Zhuangzi pass Huizi's grave and say he has no one left to talk to.

## 2. The Setting: Many *Daos*

The Warring States period (5th–3rd c. BC) was a "marketplace" of schools selling rulers a *dao*.
- **Confucians**: humaneness (*ren*), responsibility (*yi*), ritual (*li*) and filial piety, modelled on the family. Mencius, Zhuangzi's contemporary: "human nature is good".
- **Mohists**: impartial love for all, utility, frugal funerals, and a Heaven that rewards and punishes.
- Each has a *dao* in the old sense: a **"guiding discourse"** (Chad Hansen's phrase), a set of words and practices that tells you how to divide the world and what to value. Each says *shi* ("this, right") where the other says *fei* ("not this, wrong").
- Zhuangzi doesn't pick a side. He asks how anyone could ever ground the choice between such *daos* at all. As Ziporyn puts it, knowledge, will, tradition, society, intuition, logic and faith all prove unreliable as guides. "But what else is there?"

## 3. Big and Small (Chapter 1)

- **Kun and Peng.** A fish thousands of miles long in the Northern Oblivion turns into the bird Peng, whose wings are like clouds, and flies 90,000 miles south. A cicada and a little dove laugh at him: we fly up to the elm tree and that's far enough, so why go so high? Then the cicada's mockery is turned back on it: small understanding can't reach large understanding.
- The point is **perspective**. "Large" and "small", long life and short, depend on where you stand. Peng, looking down, sees only blue, just as we see blue looking up.
- **The useless gourd and tree.** Huizi grows a gourd so big it's useless as a ladle, so he smashes it. Zhuangzi: why not make it a boat and float on the lakes? Huizi has a huge gnarled tree no carpenter will look at. Zhuangzi: plant it in the wilds and loaf under it. No axe will touch it, precisely *because* it is useless.

## 4. "Equalizing Assessments of Things" (Chapter 2)

The philosophical core (*Qiwulun*; Graham renders it "the sorting which evens things out").

1. **"I have lost me."** Sir Shoestrap (Ziqi) sits like a withered tree. He has heard the **piping of Heaven**: the wind blows through ten thousand hollows, each howls in its own way, and when the wind stops they fall silent. Who blows? No "blower" can be found behind the sounds; each sound comes from its own hollow.
2. **The anxious mind.** Joy, anger, plans and regrets come up day and night "like mushrooms of steam", and we can't find their source. We spend our lives "grinding and lacerating" against things and achieve nothing. "Is it not sad?"
3. **"This" and "that" are born together.** Every thing is a "this" from its own side and a "that" from another's. Affirmation and denial generate each other. So the sage doesn't take any one side but lets them all "bask in the light of Heaven". When "this" and "that" no longer face each other as opposites, this is the **axis of the Way**: a pivot at the centre of a ring, from which one can respond to every position endlessly.
4. **"Courses are formed by walking them; things are so by being called so."** Every thing is right from somewhere. A beam and a pillar, a leper and the beauty Xishi: "the Course opens them into one another."
5. **Three in the Morning.** A monkey keeper offers three nuts in the morning and four at night. The monkeys are furious. "All right, four in the morning and three at night." They're delighted. Nothing changed in name or fact; he simply **went along with** their "this". So the sage harmonises rights and wrongs and rests at the centre of "Heaven the Potter's Wheel". This is called **"Walking Two Roads"**.
6. **Who knows the right place to live?** People sleeping in damp places get sick, but eels don't. People in trees are terrified, but monkeys aren't. Humans think Lady Li and Mao Qiang beautiful, but fish dive away from them and deer bolt. So which of us "knows" what is rightly alluring?
7. **The unwinnable debate.** If you beat me in argument, does that make you right? Whom shall we get to judge? Someone who agrees with you, with me, with both, with neither? None can settle it.
8. **Lady Li's tears and the great awakening.** Lady Li wept when she was carried off to the palace of Jin. Once she shared the king's bed and ate fine meats, she regretted her tears. "How do I know that the dead don't regret clinging to life?" We dream, and in the dream interpret our dreams. Perhaps a **great awakening** will show all this to be a great dream. Fools think they're already awake.
9. **The butterfly.** Zhuang Zhou dreamed he was a butterfly, fluttering happily, not knowing he was Zhou. He woke and was Zhou, and didn't know whether he was Zhou who had dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly dreaming he was Zhou. "Between Zhou and a butterfly there must be some distinction." This is called **the transformation of things**.

Note the last line. The story doesn't say the distinction is unreal. It says there *is* one, and that things pass into one another across it. Graham stresses that this is not a Western "reality behind appearance". The point is that, waking or dreaming, you can't tell which you're in.

## 5. Skeptic, Relativist, Anti-rationalist?

Ziporyn lists the labels that have been aggressively argued: mystic, skeptic, monist, dualist, intuitionist, theist, agnostic, relativist, fatalist, nihilist, existentialist. All have some support in the text. The main lines:

| Reading | Who | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Therapeutic skeptic** | Kjellberg; Raphals; Schwitzgebel | Like Sextus Empiricus, he uses arguments to **unsettle**, not to prove that nothing can be known. Raphals: skeptical **method and attitude** but no self-refuting skeptical **thesis**. Schwitzgebel: the radical doubt is a kind of therapy that brings us to an "everyday skepticism", humility about our views. |
| **Relativist** | Chad Hansen (SEP) | "Indexical relativism": each *dao* is right relative to a standpoint, as in physics a frame of reference. Nature offers many paths and picks none as *the* Dao. |
| **Not a relativist** | Ivanhoe | He plainly recommends some ways of living over others, so he can't think all accounts equally good. The skepticism is "therapy" against self-aggrandisement, so that we fit as "things among things". |
| **Anti-rationalist** | Graham | Not an *irrationalist*: the world is really there, and you must take it as it is. He denies only that **analytic reason** can show it. Two stages: (1) all principles for grounding conduct are groundless; (2) at the bottom remain spontaneity and **one imperative, "mirror things as they are"**. He distinguishes the fluid "'this' that goes by circumstances" (*yinshi*) from the rigid "'this' that deems" (*weishi*). |
| **Nondual** | Loy | Read with Nāgārjuna: reason used to show the limits of reason, and "nonduality [as] the great dream which we awaken not from but to". |
| **Genuine pretender** | Moeller & D'Ambrosio | Against the Confucian "regime of sincerity". The *zhenren* (genuine person) plays social roles without identifying with them. Humour, parody and tricksters are the method. |

Graham's observation about mood is worth keeping: in Zhuangzi, as in Nietzsche, there is **no vertigo in the doubt**. Ethical skepticism only hurts if you must choose without grounds. Zhuangzi's ideal is not to need to choose.

## 6. Knack: Cook Ding and the Skill Stories (Chapter 3)

- **Cook Ding** carves an ox for Lord Wenhui. Every movement is like a dance in time to music. He says: at first I saw the whole ox; after three years I no longer saw the ox; now I meet it with **spirit**, not with my eyes. The senses stop and the spirit moves where it wants. I follow the **"Heavenly pattern"** (*tianli*: the natural grain, the joints and hollows) and my blade has never touched a ligament. A good cook changes his knife every year because he cuts; a poor one every month because he hacks. Mine is nineteen years old and as sharp as new. When I come to a hard place, I slow down, take care, and with a tiny move it falls apart "like a clod of earth".
  - Lord Wenhui: "From the words of Cook Ding I have learned how to **nourish life**."
- **The other craftsmen** in the "school" chapters include the swimmer under a waterfall ("I go in with the inflow and come out with the outflow, follow the way of the water"), the cicada-catcher, the wheelwright and the bell-stand carver who fasts until he forgets reward, praise and even his own body.
- **What is learned** is **knowing-how**, not knowing-that (Eno, Graham, citing Ryle's distinction). It can't be put into rules.
  - **Eno** pushes this to a troubling conclusion: Zhuangzi's *dao* is **amoral**, and the *dao* of butchering men could give the same spontaneity as that of butchering oxen.
  - **Yearley** and **Kupperman** read it as **trained** spontaneity, "educated emotions" or "transcendent drives". It is second nature reached *through* reflection, not mere impulse.

## 7. The Fasting of the Mind, the Mirror and Hundun (Chapters 4, 7)

- **Fasting of the mind** (ch. 4). Yan Hui wants to go and reform a cruel king. Confucius tells him to fast. "I'm poor, I haven't had wine or meat for months." "That is the fasting for sacrifices, not the fasting of the mind." Don't listen with the ears, or even with the mind, but with the *qi*, which is **empty and waits on things**. The Way gathers in emptiness. Yan Hui: "Before I heard this I was certainly Hui. Now, there has never been a Hui." Then you can go into the king's cage without being moved by names.
- **The mirror** (ch. 7). The utmost person uses the mind **like a mirror**. It doesn't send off or welcome. It responds and doesn't store. So it can deal with things without being harmed.
- **Hundun's death** (ch. 7). The emperors of the South Sea (Swoosh) and the North Sea (Oblivion) were well treated by Hundun (Chaos), emperor of the middle. Wanting to repay him, they said, "All men have seven holes to see, hear, eat and breathe, but he has none. Let's drill him some." They drilled one a day, and on the seventh day Hundun died.
  - Moeller reads this as a **parody** of a creation myth. Well-meant "improvement" by imposed form kills the undifferentiated source.

## 8. The Uses of Uselessness (Chapters 4, 20)

- **The oak at the shrine** (ch. 4). Carpenter Shi passes a vast oak without a glance: useless wood, makes boats that sink and coffins that rot. That night the oak appears in a dream. Fruit trees are stripped and broken because they're useful, it says. I've worked a long time at being useless, and it nearly killed me, but now it's my great use. The joke on the joke: a disciple asks why, if it wants to be useless, it's serving as a shrine tree. Keep quiet, says Shi; it's only using the shrine as cover.
- **The goose** (ch. 20). Zhuangzi praises a tree that lives because it's useless. Then the host kills the goose that **can't** cackle. The disciples ask which, then, is right. Zhuangzi: I'd take a place **between** useful and useless, and even that isn't quite it. Better to drift with the Way and the Virtue, now a dragon, now a snake. **No rule, even "be useless", can be made into a safe *dao*.**

## 9. Death and the Happiness of Fish

- **The washtub** (ch. 18). When his wife died, Huizi came to mourn and found Zhuangzi squatting, pounding a washtub and singing. "Isn't that going too far?" Zhuangzi: when she first died, how could I not grieve? But then I looked back to before her birth: no life, no form, not even *qi*. In the mingling, a change produced *qi*, *qi* became form, form became life, and now another change and she is dead. It's like the four seasons. She's lying asleep in the great room, and if I followed her bawling, I'd show I didn't understand fate. So I stopped.
- **His own death** (ch. 32). The disciples want a grand funeral. Zhuangzi: Heaven and earth will be my coffin, the sun and moon my jade discs, the stars my pearls, all things my funeral gifts. "But the crows and kites will eat you." Above ground the crows and kites will eat me, below ground the ants and crickets. You'd rob one to feed the other: what favouritism!
- **The fish** (ch. 17). On the bridge over the Hao. Zhuangzi says the minnows swimming freely are happy. Huizi: you're not a fish, so how do you know the fish are happy? Zhuangzi: you're not me, so how do you know I don't know? Huizi: I'm not you, so I don't know you; you're not a fish, so the argument stands. Zhuangzi: go back to the beginning. You asked *how* I know, so you already knew that I knew. I know it **from here, above the Hao**.
  - Is this a cheap pun on "how/from where"? Or the point itself, that all knowing is knowing **from somewhere**? Ziporyn reads the second: perspectival knowing, the knowing of play.

## 10. Comparison

| | Zhuangzi | Sextus Empiricus | Nāgārjuna | Christian (Fathers) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem** | rival *daos*, anxious fixed judgments | dogmatic beliefs disturbing peace | clinging to "own-being" (*svabhāva*) | sin: the will turned from God; pride |
| **Method** | stories, paradox, parody, "goblet words" | opposing arguments, *epochē* | reductio of every view | repentance, ascesis, the commandments |
| **Goal** | free wandering, mirror-mind, skill | *ataraxia* | nirvana as emptiness | union with the personal God (theosis) |
| **Ultimate** | the Course that is not a course; transformation | (none claimed) | emptiness, itself empty | the Trinity, beyond being but personal |
| **Language** | can't fix the Way; "the Course spoken is not the Course" | appearances reported, not asserted | conventional truth only | apophatic and cataphatic; the Word made flesh |
| **Death** | one more transformation; sing | (not a theme) | samsara to be ended | "the last enemy", defeated by Resurrection |

## 11. Assessment

*Mine.*

**Where Zhuangzi and the Fathers meet**
- **Against the "disputer of this world"** (1 Cor 1:20). Zhuangzi's unwinnable debate and Paul's "foolishness of God" both deny that arguing well puts you in possession of the truth. The Orthodox distrust of theology as a debating science, and the claim that the true theologian is "one who prays" (Evagrius), sit closer to Zhuangzi than to Huizi. See [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]] and [[Skepticism]].
- **The fasting of the mind and *nepsis*.** "Listen not with the ears but with the *qi*", emptiness that waits, a mirror that doesn't store: this is strikingly like the hesychasts' stillness and the warning against **holding onto images** in prayer, the mind "naked" of concepts (Evagrius: prayer is "laying aside of thoughts"). Yan Hui's "there has never been a Hui" resembles the death of the self-image. See [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]] and [[Nous]].
- **Cook Ding and the *logoi*.** The cook succeeds by following the **"Heavenly pattern"** in the ox, not by imposing his will. St Maximus would call this attending to the *logos* of each thing, the meaning the Word has put in it, so that work becomes a kind of natural contemplation (*theoria physike*). The difference: for Maximus the grain of things is *spoken* by a Person and leads back to Him. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].
- **Uselessness and the Gospel.** "Whosoever will lose his life… shall find it." Merton sees in Zhuangzi's useless tree the same reversal as the Gospel's childlikeness and humility, and as St John of the Cross: you enter the way by leaving all ways. The cross itself is supremely "useless" to the world. But see the next point.

**Where they part**
- **Distinction is not the Fall; grasping it is.** Zhuangzi's myth of decline runs like this: first no things, then things without boundaries, then boundaries without right and wrong; "when right and wrong wax bright, the Way wanes". Genesis has it the other way round. **God's first acts are distinctions**: He "divided the light from the darkness", the waters from the waters, and called it good. Adam's naming of the animals is a vocation, not a corruption. Hundun's death would be no tragedy in Genesis: the formless deep (*tohu wa-bohu*) is *meant* to be given form.
  - And yet Zhuangzi is onto something real. The Fall in Genesis is precisely eating of the tree of the **knowledge of good and evil**, that is, seizing the right to declare *shi* and *fei* for ourselves. Many Fathers read it so. Zhuangzi's anxious, "lacerating" judging mind is a sharp phenomenology of fallen *discursive* reason. The cure, though, is not to dissolve the distinctions but to receive them again from God. See [[The Fall of Humanity and Its Consequences]].
- **Perspective needs a Person.** "Who knows the right place to live: man, eel or monkey?" is a fine joke against human chauvinism, and Ivanhoe is right that Zhuangzi doesn't really believe all perspectives are equal: he plainly prefers the sage. But he has nowhere to stand to say *why*. The Christian answer is that there **is** a "view from everywhere": not a theory but a Person. "I am the way, the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). Truth is not a guiding discourse but *Someone*, and the Chinese Bible translates John 1:1 "In the beginning was the *Dao*" (太初有道). The Logos is the Dao who turned out to have a face. See [[Relativism]].
- **Death is not a season.** Zhuangzi's washtub is moving, and far better than the Epicurean shrug. But "she is lying asleep in the great room" means her personal existence is over and the "transformation of things" goes on. The Fathers grant that death is natural to a creature made from nothing, then call it **the last enemy** (1 Cor 15:26), defeated not by acceptance but by **Resurrection**. Christ wept at Lazarus' tomb before He raised him (John 11:35). Lady Li's regretted tears find their real fulfilment in Paul: "to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better" (Phil 1:23). That is not because death is just another change, but because Someone is on the other side. See [[1 Corinthians 15:26: The Last Enemy That Will Be Destroyed Is Death]] and [[Homer on Life and Death]].
- **The mirror and the image.** For Zhuangzi the mirror reflects whatever comes, storing nothing. For Paul, "we all, with open face beholding **as in a glass** the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory" (2 Cor 3:18). The mirror is meant to be filled, by one Face, and so transfigured. That's the difference between attunement and **theosis**. See [[Theosis: Becoming by Grace What God Is by Nature]].

**What I take from it**
- Hold your *shi* and *fei* with a looser grip. Most of our quarrels are "three in the morning" quarrels, about nothing in name or fact. Schwitzgebel's "everyday skepticism" is just Christian humility in a philosopher's coat.
- Work like Cook Ding: slow down at the hard joints, follow the grain, don't hack.
- Don't be so eager to be useful. The tree that's left alone, and the monk in his cell, may be doing more than the busy reformer.
- But don't drill holes in Hundun **or** leave him faceless. The answer to the anxious mind isn't a mirror that holds nothing, but a heart that holds One.
