title: The First Followers of Christ Were Jews
tags: [theology, scripture, church-history]
summary: Scripture showing that Jesus' ministry, disciples and the early Church were Jewish first.
---

1. **Jesus’ ministry was directed to the Jews first**:
    - **Matthew 10:5-6**: When Jesus sends out the Twelve, He explicitly tells them: *“Do not go among the Gentiles or enter any town of the Samaritans. Go rather to the lost sheep of Israel.”*

      This shows that Jesus initially focused His ministry on the Jewish people.
2. **The disciples and early followers were Jewish**:
    - **Matthew 4:18-22**: Jesus calls His first disciples (Peter, Andrew, James, and John), who were Jewish fishermen from Galilee.
    - **John 1:45**: Philip tells Nathanael: *“We have found the one Moses wrote about in the Law, and about whom the prophets also wrote—Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph.”* This reflects their Jewish understanding of the Messiah.
3. **Pentecost and the early Church were predominantly Jewish**:
    - **Acts 2:5-11**: At Pentecost, the Holy Spirit descends on Jewish followers of Jesus, and it is stated that *“there were staying in Jerusalem God-fearing Jews from every nation under heaven.”*
    - Peter’s sermon in Acts 2 is directed to this Jewish audience, whom he calls to repentance (Acts 2:14-39). About 3,000 Jews believed in Jesus that day (Acts 2:41).
4. **Paul acknowledges the Jewish roots of Jesus’ followers**:
    - **Romans 1:16**: Paul writes, *“For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes: first to the Jew, then to the Gentile.”* This affirms that salvation was first brought to the Jews before it was extended to Gentiles.
    - **Acts 13:46**: Paul and Barnabas say, *“We had to speak the word of God to you (Jews) first. Since you reject it and do not consider yourselves worthy of eternal life, we now turn to the Gentiles.”*
5. **The Apostles and the early Church leaders were Jewish**:
    - The Twelve Apostles (Matthew 10:2-4) and key figures in the early Church, such as Mary, Jesus’ mother, and James, Jesus’ brother, were Jewish.

Thus, scripture consistently demonstrates that Jesus’ first followers were Jews, reflecting His mission to fulfill the promises made to Israel before extending salvation to the Gentiles.
