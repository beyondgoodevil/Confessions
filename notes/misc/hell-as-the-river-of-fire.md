---

A common Orthodox way of describing the Last Judgment is that **heaven and hell are not two different places**, but the **same presence of God**, His uncreated glory, experienced in two different ways. For those who love God, His presence is **light and joy**; for those who have turned away from Him, the same presence is **fire and torment**. Here's why:

### 1. God Is a Consuming Fire

"A fiery stream issued And came forth from before Him." (Daniel 7:10)

"For our God is a consuming fire." (Hebrews 12:29)

- **Significance:** the fire in these passages comes **from God Himself**. It is not a separate place of punishment set apart from Him, but His own glory. This is the "river of fire" from which the teaching takes its name.

### 2. The Same Fire, Two Effects

"Each one's work will become clear; for the Day will declare it, because it will be revealed by fire; and the fire will test each one's work, of what sort it is." (1 Corinthians 3:13)

- The same fire that **purifies gold burns up straw**. The difference lies in the material, not in the fire.
- St. Basil, commenting on "The voice of the LORD divides the flames of fire" (Psalm 29:7), says that the burning power of fire will be separated from its light: the light for the enjoyment of the righteous, the burning for the punishment of sinners.
- **Significance:** God does not change at the Judgment. What differs is the state of the one who stands before Him.

### 3. Love as Torment

- St. Isaac the Syrian (*Ascetical Homilies*) teaches that those in Gehenna are **"scourged by the scourge of love."** The love of God works in two ways: it is torment to those who have sinned against love, and joy to those who have kept it.
- He insists it is wrong to think that sinners in Gehenna are deprived of God's love. What torments them is precisely that love, received by hearts that refuse it.
- "And this is the condemnation, that the light has come into the world, and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil." (John 3:19)

### 4. The Contrast With the Juridical Model

1. **The Juridical Model**: in much Western theology, hell is primarily a **punishment inflicted** by God to satisfy His justice. God turns His wrath toward the damned and His favour toward the saved.
2. **The Orthodox Model**: God's love is unchanging and is offered to all. Hell is the condition of a person who has made himself unable to bear that love. The "wrath of God" describes how His goodness is experienced by those opposed to it, not a change of mood in God.
3. **The Connection to Theosis**: those who have been purified and illumined in this life are able to receive the uncreated light as glory (compare the disciples on Tabor in [[The Transfiguration of Jesus on Mount Tabor]]). Those who have not are burned by the same light (see [[God's Uncreated Energies]]).

Alexandre Kalomiros' lecture *The River of Fire* (1980) made this contrast widely known, sometimes in sharp terms.

### 5. Cautions

- **Hell Is Real and Eternal**: this teaching does not soften the reality of judgment. "And these will go away into everlasting punishment, but the righteous into eternal life." (Matthew 25:46)
- **The Fathers Also Speak of Punishment**: the Fathers use the language of judgment and punishment as well. The river of fire describes **how** the punishment works, not that there is none.
- **Not Every Critic of Kalomiros Is Western**: some Orthodox writers think he overstated the contrast with the West and underplayed the patristic language of divine justice. The core image, however, is genuinely patristic.
- **Universal Salvation Is a Separate Question**: some, following St. Isaac, have hoped the fire might finally heal all. The Church's condemnation of the Origenist teaching on the restoration of all things (*apokatastasis*) makes this a hope at most, not a doctrine.

### Conclusion

The river of fire is the uncreated glory of God, flowing from His throne at the Judgment. It is one and the same for all, but it is received as light by those who love God and as fire by those who have rejected Him. On this view, God does not inflict hell out of a changed attitude; hell is love refused. This keeps the reality and seriousness of judgment while preserving the unchanging goodness of God, and it ties the Last Judgment directly to the life of theosis begun now.
