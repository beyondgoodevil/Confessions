## 6. Origen (c. 185–254 AD)

“For we who say that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are united in honor and divinity, and in substance and majesty, also think that the Son is distinct from the Father in hypostasis and rank.” — *De Principiis, 1.3.5*

### Origen’s Teaching on the Trinity

- Affirms that the **Father, Son, and Holy Spirit share the same divine nature**.
- Uses the term **hypostasis**, which would later be crucial in Nicene theology.

## 7. Cyprian of Carthage (c. 200–258 AD)

“If anyone could be baptized by heretics, he could also obtain remission of sins. But since this is not allowed, and it is clear that heretics cannot baptize or give remission of sins, the question is whether one baptized outside the Church can receive the Holy Spirit. We must remember that Jesus said: ‘Go, therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.’” — *Letter 73*

### Cyprian’s Teaching on the Trinity

- Defends the necessity of Trinitarian baptism, showing that **belief in the Trinity was the orthodox position**.

## 8. Hippolytus of Rome (c. 170–235 AD)

“The Word alone of this God is from God Himself; wherefore also the Word is God, being the substance of God.” — *Refutation of All Heresies, 10.29*

“God the Father Almighty... and Christ Jesus, the Son of God, who was incarnate, died, and rose again... and the Holy Spirit.” — *Apostolic Tradition, 21*

### Hippolytus’ Teaching on the Trinity

- Calls **Jesus Christ "God"** and affirms **the Holy Spirit** in the triadic formula.

## Conclusion: The Trinity Was Taught Before Nicaea

These direct quotes from pre-Nicene Church Fathers prove that the doctrine of the Trinity was **not created at the Council of Nicaea** but was an **established belief from the Apostles onward**.

- **Ignatius, Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, and Hippolytus** openly taught the divinity of Christ and the unity of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
- **The Didache, Cyprian, and Origen** affirmed Trinitarian baptism as the rule of faith.
- **Tertullian explicitly formulated the doctrine of the Trinity** long before Nicaea.

Thus, the **Council of Nicaea did not invent the Trinity**; it merely defended what had already been universally believed against the Arian heresy.
