### Key Old Testament Foreshadowings of Heavenly Worship:

#### 1. The Tabernacle and Temple Worship

- **Exodus 25:8-9**: God commands the construction of the Tabernacle as a dwelling place, with its design reflecting a heavenly pattern:  
  *"And let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell in their midst. Exactly as I show you concerning the pattern of the tabernacle, and of all its furniture, so you shall make it."*
- The Tabernacle and later the Temple in Jerusalem are earthly reflections of God's heavenly throne room. Revelation 4–5 reflects this same imagery of God's throne and worship.

#### 2. The Use of Incense

- **Exodus 30:1-10**: The Altar of Incense is established in the Tabernacle as part of Israel's worship:  
  *"And Aaron shall burn fragrant incense on it. Every morning when he dresses the lamps he shall burn it, and when Aaron sets up the lamps at twilight, he shall burn it, a regular incense offering before the LORD throughout your generations."*
- Revelation 5:8 and Revelation 8:3-4 show incense being offered with the prayers of the saints before God's throne, connecting Old Testament worship to the heavenly reality.

#### 3. The Priesthood and Sacrifices

- **Leviticus 16:2-34**: The Day of Atonement (Yom Kippur) includes the high priest entering the Holy of Holies to offer blood for the sins of the people. This anticipates Christ, the ultimate High Priest, and the Lamb of God in Revelation 5, who offers Himself for the redemption of all.
- **Psalm 110:4**: The promise of a priestly Messiah:  
  *"The LORD has sworn and will not change his mind, 'You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek.'"*  
  This points to Jesus' eternal priesthood and His role in mediating heavenly worship.

#### 4. Songs and Hymns in Worship

- **Isaiah 6:1-3**: The vision of God's throne room includes the seraphim singing:  
  *"Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts; the whole earth is full of his glory!"*  
  This is echoed in Revelation 4:8 and forms part of the Orthodox Trisagion hymn.
- **Psalm 96:1-3**: The call to universal worship:  
  *"Oh sing to the LORD a new song; sing to the LORD, all the earth! Sing to the LORD, bless his name; tell of his salvation from day to day. Declare his glory among the nations, his marvelous works among all the peoples!"*  
  This parallels the universal praise seen in Revelation 5:13.

#### 5. The Ark of the Covenant as God's Throne

- **1 Chronicles 28:2**: The Ark is described as God's footstool, symbolizing His presence:  
  *"I had it in my heart to build a house of rest for the ark of the covenant of the LORD and for the footstool of our God."*  
  The Ark's significance points to God's throne in Revelation 4, surrounded by worshipers.

#### 6. All Nations Joining in Worship

- **Isaiah 56:6-7**: A prophecy that all nations will worship God:  
  *"For my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples."*  
  This foreshadows the multitude from every nation in Revelation 7:9-10, worshiping before God and the Lamb.

---

### How This Points to Continuity in Orthodox Worship:

The Old Testament worship, with its detailed liturgy, priesthood, and sacrificial system, is fulfilled and transformed in Christ and the New Covenant. The Church's liturgical practices mirror the heavenly worship described in Revelation, integrating these Old Testament patterns:

1. **Incense** in Orthodox worship reflects the prayers of the saints.
2. **Hymns and Psalms** connect directly to the worship traditions of Israel.
3. **The altar** in Orthodox churches mirrors the Holy of Holies, symbolizing God's presence.
4. The Church's liturgical year and feasts align with the biblical calendar, continuing the rhythm of worship established in the Old Testament.

In this way, the Eastern Orthodox Church sees itself as fulfilling and continuing the covenantal worship of God's people, now centered on Christ, the eternal High Priest, and the Lamb.
