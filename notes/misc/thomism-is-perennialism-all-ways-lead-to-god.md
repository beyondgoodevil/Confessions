## Perennialism

Perennialism is the belief that all major religions contain a shared, underlying truth that leads to the same ultimate divine reality. Despite outward differences, Perennialists argue that the esoteric or mystical core of each tradition is a path to the same transcendent truth.

## How Thomism Ends in Perennialism Due to Absolute Divine Simplicity

**Thomism**, rooted in the thought of St. Thomas Aquinas, teaches **absolute divine simplicity**, meaning that God's essence is entirely simple, without parts, and identical to His attributes. In this view, God's essence is the same as His existence—there is no distinction between who God is and what God does.

This doctrine of simplicity can lead to a **perennialist conclusion** because:

1. **God as One Reality:** If God is absolutely simple and indivisible, then He is the ultimate, singular reality behind all existence. Therefore, any religious tradition that reaches toward the divine could be understood as participating in or expressing this same reality, even if its outward forms differ.
2. **No Real Distinction in God’s Attributes:** Since there are no real distinctions within God’s essence, His attributes (e.g., wisdom, love, justice) are all the same. This leads to the idea that different religions, though expressing different aspects of God, are ultimately pointing to the same divine reality, just in different ways.
3. **Knowledge of God:** Thomism asserts that God’s essence is incomprehensible and can only be known through His effects (i.e., creation, revelation). This could imply that different religious traditions, by reflecting on the divine through creation and experience, are glimpsing the same ultimate truth, reinforcing the Perennialist idea that all religions point toward the same God.

Because **absolute divine simplicity** asserts that there are no distinctions within God, it can be used to argue that various religious experiences and doctrines are different expressions of the same singular divine reality. This, in essence, brings Thomism close to the core idea of Perennialism: that all paths lead to the one, undivided God. However, traditional Thomists might resist this conclusion, emphasizing the uniqueness of Christian revelation.
