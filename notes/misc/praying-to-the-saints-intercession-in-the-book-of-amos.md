## Intercession in the Book of Amos

1. Amos 7:1-6 – Amos Intercedes for Israel:
    - **Verses:**
        - **Amos 7:1-3:** "Thus the Lord God showed me: Behold, He formed locust swarms at the beginning of the late crop; indeed, it was the late crop after the king's mowings. And so it was, when they had finished eating the grass of the land, that I said: 'O Lord God, forgive, I pray! Oh, that Jacob may stand, for he is small!' So the Lord relented concerning this. 'It shall not be,' said the Lord."
        - **Amos 7:4-6:** "Thus the Lord God showed me: Behold, the Lord God called for conflict by fire, and it consumed the great deep and devoured the territory. Then I said: 'O Lord God, cease, I pray! Oh, that Jacob may stand, for he is small!' So the Lord relented concerning this. 'This also shall not be,' said the Lord God."
    - **Significance:** In these visions, God shows Amos two impending judgments: a plague of locusts and a consuming fire. In both instances, Amos, seeing the devastating effects these would have on Israel, pleads with God to spare them. His intercessory prayer reflects his concern for the people and his belief in God's mercy. God responds to Amos' plea by relenting and withholding the judgment.

## Lessons from Amos' Intercession

- **Prophetic Role:** Amos, though sent to warn Israel of impending doom, also acts as an intercessor, seeking to avert the complete destruction of the people. This shows that the prophet's role is not only to announce judgment but also to plead for mercy on behalf of the people.
- **God's Relenting:** The fact that God listens to Amos and relents in response to his prayers highlights the power of intercession. Even in a context of judgment, God is shown to be merciful and responsive to genuine pleas for forgiveness and restraint.
- **Judgment and Mercy:** The book illustrates the balance between God's justice and mercy. Though Israel's sins merit punishment, the intercession of Amos reveals that God is willing to show mercy when His people (through their representatives, like prophets) seek it earnestly.

## Conclusion

In the Book of Amos, intercession plays a crucial role in demonstrating the possibility of divine mercy. Though the book is filled with messages of judgment against the corruption and injustices of Israel, the prophet's intercessory prayers show that God's heart remains open to forgiveness when sincere appeals are made. Amos' intercession provides a powerful example of the effectiveness of prayer, even in dire circumstances.
