## 1. Definition and Origin of the Term

The term *ἐνέργεια* originates with Aristotle, meaning "activity" or "actualization"—that which a being *does* according to its *nature*. In Christian theology, especially in the Eastern Orthodox tradition, it was taken up and deepened by the Cappadocian Fathers and later by **St. Gregory Palamas** to articulate the **distinction between God's essence (οὐσία) and His energies (ἐνέργειαι)**.

- **Essence** refers to *what God is in Himself*, utterly transcendent and unknowable.
- **Energy** refers to *how God makes Himself known and active in the world*, while remaining unchanged.

## 2. God’s Energies – Uncreated

God's energies are:

- **Uncreated**
- **Inseparable from God**, yet **distinct from His essence**
- The **means by which God creates, reveals, sustains, illumines, sanctifies, and deifies**
- The **basis for real communion with God**, without collapsing into pantheism or essence-mysticism

For example:

- When God creates, that is an energy.
- When He illumines a prophet or deifies a saint, that is an energy.
- When Moses encounters the glory of God on Sinai, or the Apostles behold the uncreated light on Mount Tabor, they are participating in God’s energies, not His essence.

This preserves **both** God’s *absolute transcendence* **and** His *real immanence*.

## 3. Energies of Angels and Demons – Created

Created beings also act according to their nature. These actions—whether mental, volitional, or spiritual—are called their **energies**.

- Angels possess **created spiritual energies**: their intellectual operations, ministering actions, and praises.
- Demons, as fallen angels, still retain created energies, but these are now **corrupted** by their willful rebellion. They use their energies to deceive, tempt, and destroy.

**Key differences**:

- God's energies are **uncreated, infinite, and divine**.
- Created energies are **finite, temporal, and contingent**.

## 4. Theological Implications

- This distinction is foundational to Orthodox soteriology: **deification (θέωσις)** means participation in the divine energies, not in the divine essence.
- It avoids **pantheism** (which would collapse God into the world) and **absolute apophaticism** (which would deny any true knowledge of God).
- It also rejects the **Thomistic identity of essence and act (actus purus)**, which denies real distinction in God and leads to an abstract, impersonal view of divine operation.

## 5. Fathers Who Taught This

- **St. Basil the Great**: distinguished between God’s essence and activities in his *Letter 234*.
- **St. Gregory of Nyssa**: taught that God’s power (*dunamis*) is revealed through His actions without compromising His changelessness.
- **St. Maximos the Confessor**: emphasized the *logoi* (divine principles or words) through which creatures participate in God.
- **St. Gregory Palamas**: systematized the essence-energies distinction during the Hesychast controversies of the 14th century.

## Summary

In Orthodox theology:

- **God’s energies** are **uncreated manifestations of His will and life**, allowing real participation in God.
- **Creaturely energies** are **created expressions of their nature**, limited and finite, whether angelic, demonic, or human.

This distinction is not optional; it is **dogmatic**, affirmed by the **Palamite councils** of the 14th century and upheld in the **Synodikon of Orthodoxy**. Without it, **theosis** becomes metaphysically impossible, and Orthodox spirituality collapses into either rationalism or mysticism without Christ.
