title: King David Prophesied the Incarnation of the Messiah
tags: [theology, scripture, covenant]
summary: Five Psalms read as prophecies of the Messiah's sonship, resurrection, suffering, kingship and exaltation.
---

1. Psalm 2:7:
    - **Verse:** "I will declare the decree of the Lord: He said to Me, 'You are My Son, today I have begotten You.'"
    - **Significance:** This psalm speaks of the Sonship of the Messiah, indicating a unique relationship between God and the anointed one. It is often interpreted as referring to the incarnation, where Jesus is recognized as the Son of God.
2. Psalm 16:10:
    - **Verse:** "For You will not abandon my soul to Sheol, or let Your holy one see corruption."
    - **Significance:** This verse is seen as a prophecy of the resurrection of Christ, indicating that the Messiah would not remain in death but would be raised to life, which is a key aspect of the incarnation narrative.
3. Psalm 22:
    - **Verses:** This psalm contains vivid imagery of suffering and anguish, particularly in verses like "My God, my God, why have You forsaken me?" (Psalm 22:1).
    - **Significance:** It is seen as a prophetic foreshadowing of Christ's crucifixion and suffering. The psalm reflects the experience of the Messiah, linking the incarnation to His sacrificial role.
4. Psalm 45:6-7:
    - **Verses:** "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever; a scepter of righteousness is the scepter of Your kingdom. You love righteousness and hate wickedness; therefore God, Your God, has anointed You with the oil of gladness more than Your companions."
    - **Significance:** This passage refers to the divine nature of the Messiah and His kingship, affirming both His humanity and divinity, central to the understanding of the incarnation.
5. Psalm 110:1:
    - **Verse:** "The Lord said to my Lord: 'Sit at My right hand, till I make Your enemies Your footstool.'"
    - **Significance:** This verse points to the exaltation of the Messiah, highlighting the dual nature of Christ as both Lord and servant, fulfilling the prophetic role of the incarnation.
