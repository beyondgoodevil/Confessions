## Adam's State in Paradise

Two strands in the Fathers:

| | Irenaeus | Later Fathers (Damascene, Maximus, Rose's reading) |
|---|---|---|
| Adam created | as a **child** (*nēpios*): innocent but immature, meant to grow into the likeness | in a high state of grace: his nous turned to God, wise (he named the animals) |
| the Fall | a stumble of immaturity, being hurried before he was ready | a fall from a real height, by pride |
| the goal | growth to maturity was always planned | restoration and more: theosis |

They're not really opposed: both say Adam was **not yet** deified, but **called** to it. Irenaeus, *AH* IV.38: God could have given perfection from the start, but man couldn't have received it, "being as yet an infant".

- **Man** (Damascene, *Exact Exposition* II.12): a "**second cosmos**", a microcosm combining visible and invisible creation; "an animal being deified" (*zōon theoumenon*) by inclination toward God.
    - Damascene's fuller description: "a sort of miniature world within the larger one, another adoring angel... an eye-witness of the visible creation, an initiate of the invisible... earthly and heavenly, passing and immortal... spirit by grace and flesh by pride". Made to live here and then be moved elsewhere, "becoming divine through reversion to God... not by being transformed into the divine substance, but by participation in the divine illumination" (following Gregory Nazianzen, *Or.* 38.11).
    - Why combine the two? God had made the intellectual (angels) and the material (the world); "as a mark of greater wisdom" there had to be "a bond between the visible and invisible natures".
    - "According to His image" = intellect and free will; "according to His likeness" = "likeness in virtue as is possible". Rose: most Fathers put the image in the soul, especially its highest faculty, the **nous**.
    - Body and soul were made **at the same time**, "not one before and the other afterwards, as the ravings of Origen would have it" (Damascene); Rose adds the opposite error, that the body came first and was nobler.
- Created **free**, with the possibility of turning to evil. Damascene: "sinless" doesn't mean unable to sin ("only the Divinity is incapable of sinning"), but that the tendency to sin lay not in his nature but in his choice: able to "persevere and progress in good with the help of divine grace" or to fall. "That which is done by force is not an act of virtue." Theophilus of Antioch put it as: **neither mortal nor immortal** by nature, able to become either.
- Rose's reading: Adam's virtue was **untested**. Everything had been given by grace, but he wasn't yet practised at "tilling and keeping" his inward state.
- His **mind**: naming the animals shows "prophetic grace along with... ineffable intelligence" (Rose, quoting the Fathers). He saw at once what each creature was and what to call it.

## Paradise and the Two Trees

- **Paradise** (Damascene, *Exact Exposition* II.11): both a **physical** place in the East, higher than the earth, temperate and beautiful, and a **spiritual** one: "a twofold paradise", for man is twofold (soul and body). Adam lived in the body in the one, and in the soul in the other.
    - Physically: "Eden" means "delight"; "flooded with light", with no brute beasts in it, a kingdom prepared for man as "king and ruler over the whole earth".
    - Spiritually: "he had the indwelling God as a dwelling place and wore Him as a glorious garment", and like the angels fed on "that one most sweet fruit which is the contemplation of God". Damascene says both groups of Fathers, those who taught a material and those who taught a spiritual Paradise, "taught rightly".
    - Rose: the four rivers show Paradise was "a definite place on earth". Adam's "work" there was prayer: "working in the mind alone, that is, in the sweetest vision of God".
- **The two trees** (Damascene):
    - The **tree of knowledge** was "a sort of trial, test, and exercise of man's obedience". It was "the power of discernment by multiple vision... the complete knowing of one's own nature": good "for them that are full-grown" and settled in contemplation, but bad for the young and greedy, "as strong meat is to them that... are still in need of milk". After eating, Adam noticed he was naked and started worrying about his body; God wanted him free from care, singing God's praises like the angels.
    - The **tree of life** was either a tree with life-giving power, or (spiritually) contemplation of God, which "communicates a life uninterrupted by death". "Of every tree... thou shalt eat" means: through all created things be drawn up to Me, "and from them reap the one fruit which is Myself".
    - Alternatively, the forbidden tree is **material food** that seems sweet. Material food passes into corruption, so "it is impossible for him who partakes of material food to remain incorruptible".
- Chrysostom (in Rose): the "slight command" was given to teach man "that he has a Creator and Craftsman" and is under God's dominion.
- "Naked, and... not ashamed" (Gen 2:25): Ephraim: "they were not ashamed because they were clothed with glory". Rose: the sign of their dispassion. Several Fathers taught that they lived in **virginity** before the Fall, and that marriage came in afterwards. Gregory of Nyssa read "male and female created he them" as a second, added creation, made with the Fall foreseen.

## Eve from the Rib

- "Bone of my bones": man and woman are **one nature**, equal in dignity, not two kinds (Chrysostom).
    - God took "not a part from Adam's soul but a rib from his body... not soul from a soul" (in Rose). Rose: the whole human nature was created in one man, Adam, and the first woman was taken from his flesh. That's why one man can be "like humanity", and why the one who restores it is called the Second Adam.
- From the **side**, not the head (to rule him) or the feet (to be ruled), but from beside (an old commentary, popular in the West too).
- **Typology**: Adam's sleep and Eve from his side = Christ's death on the cross and the **Church** from His pierced side, the blood and water (John 19:34) of the Eucharist and baptism. See [[Adam as a Type of Christ and Eve as a Type of the Church]].
- Eve as type of **Mary**: the "new Eve" obeys where the first disobeyed (Justin, Irenaeus).

## The Fall

- Not the eating itself but the **disobedience** and the desire to be "as gods" (Gen 3:5) **without God**: deification by grasping instead of receiving.
    - Chrysostom: the devil's motive is **envy** of man, who is called to the place the devil lost. He offers not an obvious evil but "something which seems good and true".
    - "Where art thou?" (Gen 3:9) isn't a question but a reproof and a **call to repent**: God knew, but "condescends... and calls them to confess their sin".
    - The **seed of the woman** (Gen 3:15) is Christ, who came "to destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8).
    - Rose's note: the devil, being bodiless, chose evil immutably; humans in the body can still repent.
- Then the shift of blame: Adam blames Eve and God ("the woman whom **thou** gavest"), Eve the serpent. Repentance refused.
- Consequences: death, corruption, the darkening of the nous, passions. See [[The Fall of Humanity and Its Consequences]], [[Ancestral Sin vs Original Sin]].
    - **The garments of skin** (Gen 3:21). Damascene, *Exact Exposition* III.1: man "was stripped of grace", "clothed with the roughness of his wretched life (for this is what the fig leaves signify) and put on death, that is to say, the mortality and grossness of the flesh, for this is what the garment of skins signifies". Gregory of Nyssa (in Rose): literal coats of skins, but also a changed kind of flesh. Athanasius: skins from dead animals, "a symbol of the mortality that through sin was added". Not a punishment only but a mercy: death puts a limit on evil, so it doesn't become immortal.
    - Expulsion from Paradise is the same mercy: eating of the tree of life would have made man "immortal without being good", so he's sent out (Rose).
    - **Creation** fell with him: "the creation which served him... was taken captive together with him" (Rom 8:20-22, in Rose). Rose's editor's appendix argues the whole world was created in **incorruption** and that death in nature followed man's fall.

## A Historical Adam?

- Fr. Seraphim Rose: the Fathers read Adam and Eve as **historical** persons, the first humans, and the Orthodox teaching depends on it (Christ as **New** Adam, Luke's genealogy up to "Adam, which was the son of God", 3:38; Rom 5).
- Modern Orthodox views range from Rose's literalism to symbolic readings that keep a real Fall in some form. See [[Evolution and the Fathers]].
