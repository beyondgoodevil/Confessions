The site has many notes that assume Thomist terms (pure act, divine simplicity, the Five Ways). This is the toolkit itself. Gorman's *Contemporary Introduction* (it starts from **substance**, then change, then form and matter, then essence and existence) is a "substance-centred metaphysics": the world is made basically of **things**, not events, processes or bundles of properties.

## 1. Substance and Accidents

- A **substance**: something that exists **in itself**, not in another: this dog, this oak, this man.
- **Accidents**: exist **in** a substance: its size, colour, location, relations. Nine kinds (Aristotle's categories): quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, passion.
- **Essential** features: what a thing must have to be what it is (rationality for a human). **Accidental**: it can lose them and remain itself (being tanned).
- **Essence** is partly **normative**: a dog *should* have four legs; a three-legged dog is defective, not a new kind. This is where natural law comes from. See [[The Last Superstition]].

## 2. Change

- **Accidental change**: the same substance gains or loses an accident (the leaf goes from green to red).
- **Substantial change**: a substance comes to be or perishes (a dog dies and becomes a corpse: no longer a dog).
- The principles of change: **subject**, **form**, **privation**, and **actualisation** of a potency. See [[Physics: Nature, Change and the Four Causes]].
- **Endurance**: a substance is **wholly present** at each moment (it doesn't have temporal parts). Against "four-dimensionalism".

## 3. Form and Matter (Hylomorphism)

- Every material substance is a composite of **matter** (*hylē*) and **form** (*morphē*).
- **Substantial form**: what makes this matter a dog rather than a pile of chemicals. One substantial form per substance (Aquinas against the Franciscans' plurality of forms).
- **Prime matter**: pure potency, with no form of its own; never exists alone; the ultimate subject of substantial change.
- The **soul** is the substantial form of a living body. The human soul is subsistent because the intellect operates without a bodily organ, so it survives death; but it's incomplete without the body, which is why resurrection matters. See [[Personal Identity and the Resurrection]].

## 4. Act and Potency

- **Act**: what a thing actually is or does. **Potency**: what it can be or do.
- Potency is real (an acorn really can become an oak, a stone can't), but it's not actual.
- **Principle**: nothing passes from potency to act except by something already in act. The basis of the First Way. See [[The Unmoved Mover]].
- Act **limits** nothing; potency limits act. Form is act relative to matter; existence is act relative to essence.
- **Pure act**: a being with no potency at all: unchangeable, perfect, one. God. See [[Aristotle and God as Pure Act]].

## 5. Essence and Existence

**The real distinction**
- What a thing **is** (essence) is distinct from **that** it is (existence, *esse*).
- *De Ente et Essentia* ch. 4: I can understand what a man or a phoenix is without knowing whether it exists. So existence isn't part of the essence.
- In every creature, essence and existence are **really distinct**: essence is potency, existence is act.

**The argument for God** (*De Ente* ch. 4; Kerr's *Aquinas's Way to God*)
1. In everything except possibly one thing, essence and existence are distinct.
2. Whatever belongs to a thing but isn't part of its essence is caused, either by its essence or by something outside.
3. A thing's essence can't cause its own existence (it would have to exist before it existed).
4. So everything whose essence differs from its existence is caused to exist by another.
5. That series can't go on to infinity (per se, essentially ordered).
6. So there is something whose essence **is** its existence: ***ipsum esse subsistens***, subsistent being itself. God.
7. There can only be one such thing (nothing to distinguish two of them).

**Participation**: creatures **have** being; God **is** being. Creatures participate in *esse* received from God, limited by their essence.

## 6. Analogy of Being

- "Being", "good", "wise" aren't said of God and creatures:
    - **univocally** (same meaning): that would make God one more being among others
    - **equivocally** (completely different): then we'd know nothing about God
- But **analogically**: the meaning is related, with God as the primary analogate and creatures as dependent. "God is good" means goodness exists in God in a higher way, as its cause.
- Scotus disagreed: being must be univocal for natural theology to work.

## 7. The Transcendentals

Properties that go with being as such, everywhere, across the categories:
- **one** (being as undivided)
- **true** (being as intelligible)
- **good** (being as desirable)
- (often) **beautiful**, **thing**, **something**

"Being and good are convertible": everything that exists is good insofar as it exists; evil is a **privation**. See [[Beauty as a Way to God]].

## 8. Orthodox Points of Disagreement

*Mine.*

- Much of this is shared common ground: substance, hylomorphism, act and potency, privation theory of evil all appear in the Fathers too.
- The disagreements start with **God**:
    - **God as *ipsum esse***: the Fathers (Dionysius) put God **beyond being** (*hyperousios*). Being is God's energy, not His essence. See [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]].
    - **Pure act / absolute simplicity**: no real distinction in God, so no real distinction between essence and energies. See [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]], [[God as Pure Act: RC vs EO]].
    - **Participation in *esse***: creatures participate in **created** being; the Orthodox speak of participation in God's **uncreated** energies. See [[Aristotle East and West]].
- So the Orthodox reading of Aquinas tends to keep his philosophy of nature and drop or reinterpret his doctrine of God. (*Orthodox Readings of Aquinas*, in the same folder, argues they can be closer than that.)
