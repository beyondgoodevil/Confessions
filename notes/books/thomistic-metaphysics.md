The site has many notes that assume Thomist terms (pure act, divine simplicity, the Five Ways). This is the toolkit itself. Gorman's *Contemporary Introduction* (it starts from **substance**, then change, then form and matter, then essence and existence) is a "substance-centred metaphysics": the world is made basically of **things**, not events, processes or bundles of properties.

- Gorman's chapters: Introduction; 1 Basic Beings and Their Features; 2 Change; 3 Parts; 4 Causes; 5 Universals and Particulars; 6 The Way Things Might Have Been; 7 Thoughts about Everything; 8 To the Edge of Metaphysics; 9 Metaphysics outside Normal Operating Conditions; 10 Not the Conclusion. Examples throughout use his two cats, **Rusty** and **Mickey**.
- Aquinas was a **scholastic**: he worked in a university ("school") setting, not a monastery.
- Metaphysics = "first philosophy", "wisdom", even "theology" (Aristotle). Its principles constrain other fields: a theologian colleague told Gorman, "You philosophers think you can do everyone else's thinking for them."
- "Ontology" = the science of being; the "transcendental properties of being" are what all beings share; the "categories" are its basic divisions.

## 1. Substance and Accidents

- A **substance**: something that exists **in itself**, not in another: this dog, this oak, this man. Paradigm substances: a cat, a tree, you. Rusty's redness "leans on" him: he "stands under" it.
- **Accidents**: exist **in** a substance: its size, colour, location, relations. Nine kinds (Aristotle's categories): quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, passion.
    - **Categories**: highest genera. The tree: substance → animal (sensation) → vertebrate → mammal → cat (lowest species) → [Mickey, a member not a subclass].
    - **Being is not a genus**: no generic sense of "being", so multiple categories. "Accident" is not one category but a label for several (quantity, quality, relation, place...). Relations (Vader is Luke's father) belong to both relata as accidents.
    - *Ens* is a present participle used as a noun: "a thing that is be-ing".
- **Essential** features: what a thing must have to be what it is (rationality for a human). **Accidental**: it can lose them and remain itself (being tanned). Mickey can't change from feline to canine and remain himself.
    - **Necessary accidents**: features a thing can't lack but which aren't essential (the ability to laugh in humans). Against the **modal theory** of essence (essential = can't lack), Gorman defends a "**foundationality**" theory: a feature is essential if it's the foundation others depend on; F is accidental if the thing has F **because of** something else.
    - Each thing has only **one** essential feature (with a determinate/determinable distinction: "animal" names no separate property beyond "human"). Possible exception: Christ's two natures.
- Theories of features: **bundle theory** (a thing is just its features; universals or tropes: D. C. Williams's "trope" = "the occurrence of an essence"); **bare particulars** (a featureless substrate: "Bertrand Russell could have been a poached egg"). Both fail: two things with all the same parts would be one; a bare substrate gives no reason for having these features rather than others.
- Aquinas's way: the **essence constitutes** the substance at its most fundamental level; features aren't mini-things but **ways substances are**. Essential features have no "bearer" (they make the bearer); accidental features do.
- **Essence** is partly **normative**: a dog *should* have four legs; a three-legged dog is defective, not a new kind. This is where natural law comes from. See [[The Last Superstition]].

**Universals and particulars**
- The **One over Many** problem. Square-circleness can't have instances: non-instantiable universals.
- **Platonic** ("extreme") realism: universals outside space and time. **Aristotelian** ("moderate", "immanent") realism: universals in things. **Nominalism**: no universals; the most extreme form: "Not one of them is like another" (the **Dr Seuss** view). "Extreme isn't automatically bad; sometimes moderation is just timidity."
- **Real conspecificity**: Rusty and Mickey are objectively alike in kind. Is that because of something numerically one (Felinity)? If a moderate realist says yes, his view really differs from moderate nominalism.
- Gorman's tentative conclusion: **accidents are tropes, essences are not**. In his sense, Aquinas might even be called a "nominalist", though in other senses that would be absurd.
- The linguistic argument ("Red exists, so Redness exists") fails: "red" isn't a name (except for Red Skelton).
- **Individuation**: matter, or a *haecceity* ("thisness")? See [[The Problem of Universals: Realism vs Nominalism]].

## 2. Change

- **Accidental change**: the same substance gains or loses an accident (the leaf goes from green to red; Mickey stands up).
- **Substantial change**: a substance comes to be or perishes (a dog dies and becomes a corpse: no longer a dog; Mickey dies, leaving "**feline remains**", a pile of chemicals). Treating conception and death as rearrangements of atoms would make them **accidental** changes: wrong.
- The principles of change: **subject**, **form**, **privation**, and **actualisation** of a potency. See [[Aristotle's Physics: Nature, Change and the Four Causes]]. Read Aquinas's *On the Principles of Nature*.
- **Endurance**: a substance is **wholly present** at each moment (it doesn't have temporal parts). Against "four-dimensionalism" (perdurance: temporal parts). Thomists are **three-dimensionalists**.

## 3. Form and Matter (Hylomorphism)

- Every material substance is a composite of **matter** (*hylē*) and **form** (*morphē*).
- **Substantial form**: what makes this matter a dog rather than a pile of chemicals. One substantial form per substance (Aquinas against the Franciscans' plurality of forms).
- **Parts**: **whole-dependent parts** (legs, tails: a severed leg "isn't really a leg at all") vs **whole-independent parts** (grains of sand in a heap). The organism is **prior** to its parts.
    - A "dead cat" isn't a cat or even a cat's body but non-feline chemicals.
    - Does science say Mickey is made of whole-independent atoms? Maybe atoms in an organism exist only **virtually**.
- **Prime matter**: pure potency, with no form of its own; never exists alone; the ultimate subject of substantial change.
- The **soul** is the substantial form of a living body. The human soul is subsistent because the intellect operates without a bodily organ, so it survives death; but it's incomplete without the body, which is why resurrection matters. See [[Personal Identity and the Resurrection]].

## 4. Act and Potency

- **Act**: what a thing actually is or does. **Potency**: what it can be or do.
- Potency is real (an acorn really can become an oak, a stone can't), but it's not actual.
- **Principle**: nothing passes from potency to act except by something already in act. The basis of the First Way. See [[The Unmoved Mover]].
- Act **limits** nothing; potency limits act. Form is act relative to matter; existence is act relative to essence.
- **Pure act**: a being with no potency at all: unchangeable, perfect, one. God. See [[Aristotle and God as Pure Act]].

**Causes**
- "Because" answers needn't be about processes: a turtle's shell is hard to break "because it's made of keratin".
- **Hume**: we only ever see **constant conjunction**; ideas are copies of impressions. Aquinas: causation needs an **agent** (active power) and a **patient** (passive power, from *pati*, to undergo).
- "Movie" vs "snapshot" causation.
- The four causes; natural processes "don't just happen", they aim at an end (*telos*). A cat is as it is because of its body, its substantial and accidental forms, its parents and other agents, and "so that it might live a certain kind of feline life".

## 5. Essence and Existence

**The real distinction**
- What a thing **is** (essence) is distinct from **that** it is (existence, *esse*).
- *De Ente et Essentia* ch. 4: I can understand what a man or a phoenix is without knowing whether it exists. So existence isn't part of the essence.
- In every creature, essence and existence are **really distinct**: essence is potency, existence is act.
- Rusty is the kind he is because of his **essence**, and is something rather than nothing because of his **existence-as-act**.

**The argument for God** (*De Ente* ch. 4; Kerr's *Aquinas's Way to God*)
1. In everything except possibly one thing, essence and existence are distinct.
2. Whatever belongs to a thing but isn't part of its essence is caused, either by its essence or by something outside.
3. A thing's essence can't cause its own existence (it would have to exist before it existed).
4. So everything whose essence differs from its existence is caused to exist by another.
5. That series can't go on to infinity (per se, essentially ordered).
6. So there is something whose essence **is** its existence: ***ipsum esse subsistens***, subsistent being itself. God.
7. There can only be one such thing (nothing to distinguish two of them).

- Gorman also gives the **Five Ways**; the First Way: an unchanged changer, "and this everyone calls God". What's proved at first is "quite minimal".
- God's existence can't be caused from outside, or He wouldn't be God.
- Essences define things, and to be defined is to have a limit; so "God's essence is existence" means God has **unlimited** existence, not that He's "just barely there".
- No parts, form/matter, act/potency, essence/accident in God. "**Categories are for creatures.**"

**Participation**: creatures **have** being; God **is** being. Creatures participate in *esse* received from God, limited by their essence.
- Creatures **participate** in what God has essentially and maximally; they're partial imitations of Him, because He creates them so (Pseudo-Dionysius).
- **Pure** vs **impure** perfections: being a rock is a perfection mixed with imperfection (not being intelligent).

**Possibility**
- Five modal concepts: actual, possible, necessary, contingent, impossible.
- **Lewis**: possible worlds are real concrete worlds; individuals are "world-bound"; "actual" means "the world I'm in"; **counterpart theory**. The **Humphrey objection**: "Humphrey could have won" would mean someone else won in another world, which Humphrey doesn't care about.
- **Plantinga**: possible worlds are abstract states of affairs.
- **Modality without possible worlds**: possibility grounded in **powers** of real things. "Essentially possible" vs "existentially possible".
- **From possibility to God** (Ross, "God, Creator of Kinds and Possibilities"): possibilities beyond any creature's powers need a ground: God.

## 6. Analogy of Being

- "Being", "good", "wise" aren't said of God and creatures:
    - **univocally** (same meaning): that would make God one more being among others
    - **equivocally** (completely different): then we'd know nothing about God
- But **analogically**: the meaning is related, with God as the primary analogate and creatures as dependent. "God is good" means goodness exists in God in a higher way, as its cause.
- Gorman's example: "healthy" said of a cat, its food, its urine sample: not univocal, not equivocal (like "bat"), but related to one primary use ("analogy of one to another").
- **Metaphorical** vs **literal** predication of God: "God is powerful" (literal, valid inferences) vs "God is a rock" (metaphorical: we can't infer He's igneous or sedimentary). Literal predication is **analogical**.
- Scotus disagreed: being must be univocal for natural theology to work.
- Philosophy vs theology: whether **revelation** is used. Philosophy studies God from creation. Gorman's two case studies of metaphysics "outside normal operating conditions": the **Incarnation** (Christ has two natures; one substance = one basic being; persons are substances with reason) and **transubstantiation** (bread and wine becoming Christ's body and blood while keeping appearances). See [[Nature and Person Distinction in Christology]].

## 7. The Transcendentals

Properties that go with being as such, everywhere, across the categories:
- **one** (being as undivided)
- **true** (being as intelligible): "everything is knowable"
- **good** (being as desirable)
- (often) **beautiful**, **thing**, **something**. Aquinas never lists beauty; some Thomists argue for it.

"Being and good are convertible": everything that exists is good insofar as it exists; evil is a **privation**: a bird without wings is bad because birds are supposed to have wings. Hence **natural law** morality. See [[Beauty as a Way to God]].

## 8. Orthodox Points of Disagreement

*Mine.*

- Much of this is shared common ground: substance, hylomorphism, act and potency, privation theory of evil all appear in the Fathers too.
- The disagreements start with **God**:
    - **God as *ipsum esse***: the Fathers (Dionysius) put God **beyond being** (*hyperousios*). Being is God's energy, not His essence. See [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]].
    - **Pure act / absolute simplicity**: no real distinction in God, so no real distinction between essence and energies. See [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]], [[God as Pure Act: RC vs EO]].
    - **Participation in *esse***: creatures participate in **created** being; the Orthodox speak of participation in God's **uncreated** energies. See [[Aristotle East and West]].
- So the Orthodox reading of Aquinas tends to keep his philosophy of nature and drop or reinterpret his doctrine of God. (*Orthodox Readings of Aquinas*, in the same folder, argues they can be closer than that.)
