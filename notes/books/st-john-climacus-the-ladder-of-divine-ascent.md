The most read book in Orthodoxy after the Bible and the service books. Read in monasteries every Lent, and its author has a Sunday of Lent named after him.

## 1. The Author

- **St. John Climacus** ("of the Ladder"), also John Scholasticus, John of Sinai. c. 579 – c. 649.
- Became a monk at St. Catherine's monastery, **Mount Sinai**, at about 16.
- Later lived for decades as a hermit nearby (from the traditional *Life*).
- At the end of his life, abbot of Sinai. Wrote the *Ladder* at the request of John, abbot of nearby Raithu.
- Also wrote ***To the Shepherd***: a short appendix on how an abbot should guide others.
- Feast **March 30**; and the **4th Sunday of Great Lent**.

## 2. The Image

- Jacob's ladder: "a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it" (Gen 28:12).
- **30 steps** for the 30 years of Christ's hidden life before His baptism.
- Climbing is gradual and from the bottom. You can't start at the top.
- The famous icon: monks climbing toward Christ at the top, some being pulled off by demons with hooks, angels helping, the fallen going down into a dragon's mouth. Its message: don't stop, and don't be proud of the height you've reached.

## 3. The 30 Steps

(Titles from the standard English translations; wording varies.)

**Breaking with the world (1–3)**

1. Renunciation of the world
2. Detachment
3. Exile (pilgrimage)

**The foundations (4–7)**

4. Obedience
5. Repentance
6. Remembrance of death
7. Mourning that brings joy

**Against the passions (8–26)**

8. Freedom from anger, and meekness
9. Remembrance of wrongs
10. Slander
11. Talkativeness and silence
12. Lying
13. Despondency (acedia)
14. Gluttony
15. Chastity
16. Love of money
17. Non-possessiveness (poverty)
18. Insensibility (deadness of soul)
19. Sleep, prayer and psalmody in church
20. Vigil
21. Cowardly fear
22. Vainglory
23. Pride (and blasphemous thoughts)
24. Meekness, simplicity, guilelessness
25. Humility
26. Discernment

**The summit (27–30)**

27. Stillness (*hesychia*)
28. Prayer
29. Dispassion (*apatheia*): "heaven on earth"
30. Faith, hope and love: the top rung is **love**

Note how 13–23 track the eight passions of Evagrius and Cassian. See [[The Virtues and the Eight Passions]].

## 4. Key Teachings

**Repentance** (step 5)

- A "renewal of baptism", a "contract with God for a second life".
- The voluntary bearing of afflictions.
- The penitent buys humility and stays wary of bodily comfort.
- See [[Repentance as Metanoia: A Change of Mind, Not a Payment]].

**Remembrance of death** (step 6)

- Not morbid. Keeping death in mind cuts through distraction, idleness and attachment. "Remember thy last end, and thou shalt never sin" (Sirach 7:36).

**Mourning** (step 7)

- *Charmolypi*, "joy-making mourning". Grief over sin that becomes joy, because it's met by mercy.
- Tears as a second baptism.
- A day without mourning over sin, he says, is effectively a day lost.

**Against despair**

- God's mercy is greater than any sin, so despair is the worst error: spiritual suicide.
- If you fall, get up. Falling isn't a reason to stop climbing; your angel honours patience.

**Spiritual warfare**

- Face the unseen enemies with courage. Fear invites attack (step 21).
- The passions come as *logismoi*, thoughts, and the battle is at that stage. See [[How a Thought Becomes a Sin: The Stages of Temptation]].

**Discernment** (step 26)

- The ability to tell good thoughts from bad, real virtue from its imitation, and what God wills. Comes with humility and experience.
- For beginners it's found in obedience to a spiritual father, not in one's own judgment.

**Stillness and prayer** (27–28)

- *Hesychia*: "the stillness of the body is the knowledge and composure of the habits and feelings; stillness of soul is the knowledge of one's thoughts".
- "Let the remembrance of Jesus be united with your breathing." An early witness to the Jesus Prayer. See [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]].
- Prayer without many words: the single word of the publican and the prodigal is enough.

**Dispassion and love** (29–30)

- *Apatheia* isn't lack of feeling. It's freedom from being ruled by the passions, so the soul can love purely. "Heaven on earth."
- Love is above faith and hope, because "God is love" (1 John 4:8). The ladder ends not in a technique but in God.

## 5. How to Read It

- Written for monks; most of it applies to anyone.
- Short sayings, examples from the lives of monks he knew, vivid images. Not systematic.
- Read slowly, a step at a time; Lent is the traditional time. The Sunday of St. John Climacus (4th Sunday of Lent) puts it at the centre of the fast.
- Pairs naturally with [[The Virtues and the Eight Passions]] (the map) and [[Theosis: Becoming by Grace What God Is by Nature]] (the goal).
