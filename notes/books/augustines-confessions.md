Written c. 397–400, when Augustine had become bishop of Hippo. The first autobiography in the Western sense, but it's really one long **prayer**, addressed to God throughout. *Confessio* means three things: confession of **sin**, confession of **praise**, confession of **faith**. Thirteen books: I–IX his life up to his mother's death, X his present state (memory), XI–XIII on Genesis 1 (time, creation).

## Books I–IX: The Story

| Book | Content |
|---|---|
| **I** | "**Thou hast made us for Thyself, and our heart is restless until it rest in Thee.**" Infancy: already sin (the baby jealous of its foster-brother at the breast). Schooling, Latin and Greek, weeping for Dido |
| **II** | Age 16. **The pear theft**: stealing pears he didn't want, just for the sake of doing wrong with friends. Analysing why: love of evil itself, a perverse imitation of God's freedom |
| **III** | Carthage: "I came to Carthage, where a cauldron of unholy loves sang all around me." Cicero's *Hortensius* turns him to love wisdom. Joins the **Manichees**: two principles, good and evil, God as a kind of material light |
| **IV** | Teaching rhetoric. Living with a concubine (unnamed), a son, Adeodatus. **Death of a friend**: grief because he loved a mortal as if immortal. See [[Friendship and Love in Ancient and Medieval Thought]] |
| **V** | Disillusioned with the Manichee bishop Faustus. To Rome, then **Milan**: hears **Ambrose** preach; allegorical reading of the OT removes his objections |
| **VI** | Monica follows; his concubine sent away for an arranged marriage. "Give me chastity and continence, but not yet." |
| **VII** | **The books of the Platonists** (Plotinus, Porphyry): God is immaterial; evil is privation, not a substance. A glimpse of God "in the flash of a trembling glance". But they didn't tell him that "**the Word was made flesh**". See [[Christian Platonism]] |
| **VIII** | **The garden in Milan**: torn between two wills. Hears a child's voice: "**tolle, lege**" (take up and read). Opens Paul: Rom 13:13-14, "put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh". Converts. "The divided will" |
| **IX** | Baptised by Ambrose (Easter 387). **The vision at Ostia** with Monica: together they rise in conversation beyond all creatures to Wisdom itself, touch it for an instant. Monica dies days later. Augustine's grief |

**Infancy, schooling and ambition**
- "Since nothing that is could exist without You, You must in some way be in all that is: therefore also in me." (I)
- His nurses' milk: "it was not my mother or my nurses who stored their breasts for me: it was Yourself, using them" (I).
- "The innocence of children is in the **helplessness of their bodies** rather than any quality in their minds." (I)
    - Christ commended "the low stature of childhood": **humility**, not innocence (I).
- Grammar school: everyone can spell "Aeneas" by agreed convention. A man who "drops an h" and says "'uman being" is more despised than one who **hates** a human being: "thus breaking a law of God" (I).
- "I make no accusation against the words, which are choice and precious vessels, but against the **wine of error** in them, poured out by teachers already drunk with it." (I)
- His father's sacrifice for his studies was praised, but from "vain ambition"; Monica thought study would help bring him to God (II).
- The "**Overturners**" (*eversores*): students who bullied newcomers (III).
- Carthage students rioted; Rome's students cheated teachers of fees by switching masters together; so Milan (V).
- The **drunken beggar** in Milan: "he was the happier man": he got wine by wishing luck to all; Augustine by lying got only empty praise (VI).

**Love, fear and grief**
- Adolescence: "I did not keep the measure of mind to mind, which is **the luminous line of friendship**; but mists steamed up to cloud my heart so I could not distinguish the white light of love from the fog of lust." (II)
- The concubine: a woman "not joined to me in lawful marriage" (IV).
- **Fear** "is the recoil from a sudden threat to something one holds dear... but nothing new or sudden can happen to You... where is safety secure save in You?" (II)
- Theatre: why do we enjoy watching suffering? "When a man suffers himself it is called **misery**; when he suffers in another's suffering it is called **pity**." He wanted sorrows that only "scratched the surface", which became inflamed like fingernail scratches (III).
- The friend's death: "I had spilt out my soul upon the sand, in loving a mortal man as if he were never to die." (IV)
    - "The Word Himself calls you to return; with Him is the place of peace that shall not be broken, where your love will not be forsaken unless it first forsake." (IV)

**Wisdom, the Manichees and the Platonists**
- Cicero's *Hortensius*: to love "not this or that school but **Wisdom itself**". But it lacked "the name of Your Son, which my infant heart had drunk in with my mother's milk" (III).
- Scripture at first seemed crude next to Cicero. The Manichees "cried out 'Truth, truth'... but the thing was nowhere in them". Col 2:8: "beware lest any man cheat you by philosophy." (III)
- He consulted **astrologers** ("mathematicians") since they offered no sacrifices; they make the stars, not man, guilty of sin (IV).
    - **Firminus** and the slave boy born at the same instant with opposite fates: so horoscopes, when right, are right by chance (VII).
- His first book, *On the Beautiful and the Fitting*: beauty as what is so of itself, the fitting as what suits another. He couldn't conceive the incorporeal: "because I did not see these things in my mind, I concluded I could not see my mind" (IV).
- The Manichee bishop **Faustus**: charming, but caught in errors about astronomy; Augustine learned "a thing is not true because eloquently said, nor false because badly said" (V).
- He wasn't yet Catholic, but no longer thought the Catholic view indefensible (V).
- He refused to entrust "my sick soul to the philosophers, because they were without the saving name of Christ" (VI).
- Authority of Scripture: "since men had not the strength to discover truth by pure reason, we needed the authority of Holy Writ." (VI)
- Epicurus would have won "the palm in my judgment" if Augustine hadn't believed in the soul's life after death and judgement (VI).
- **Nebridius**'s argument against the Manichees: if the dark power could harm God, God is corruptible; if not, why fight? The Manichees "preferred to hold that Your substance suffered evil than that their own substance committed it" (VII).
- The incorruptible is greater than the corruptible, so God must be incorruptible (VII).
- The **books of the Platonists**, "translated from Greek into Latin", given by "an incredibly conceited man". They said "In the beginning was the Word", but not "He came unto His own, and His own received Him not" (VII).
- He then thought of Christ as an excellent man with "more perfect participation in wisdom", not "the very Person of Truth" (VII).
- On evil: "Every part is defective that is not in harmony with the whole"; acts can seem blameworthy to men and approved by God, depending on the doer's mind (III).
    - Evil as discord: some things seem evil because they don't harmonise with others, but harmonise with yet others and are good in themselves (VII).

**Conversion, Monica and Ostia**
- **Alypius**, addicted to gladiator shows (VI).
- Monica on the sea voyage reassured the **sailors** in a storm (VI).
- Simplicianus tells the conversion of **Marius Victorinus**, the famous rhetorician with a statue in the Forum, who insisted on professing his faith publicly. "Victory over the enemy is greater when we win from him a man whom he holds more strongly." (VIII)
- **Two wills**: like a man who wants to get up but sinks back into a doze. "The first course delighted and convinced my mind, the second delighted my body and held it in bondage." "There are two wills in us because neither is entire." (VIII)
- **Ponticianus** tells of St. **Antony** and of two officials converted by reading his *Life* (VIII).
- "Take and read": "a boy's voice or a girl's voice, I do not know, a sort of sing-song" (VIII).
- He resigned his teaching (citing lung trouble) and was baptised with **Alypius** and Adeodatus (IX).
- Monica as a girl: an old servant kept the girls from drinking water between meals: "now you drink water because you're not allowed wine; when you're mistress of the cellars, you'll despise water but the habit of drinking will remain." Yet Monica developed a secret habit of sipping wine, cured when a maid called her a "wine-bibber" (IX).
- Ostia: "What am I still doing here?" Monica no longer cared to be buried in her own land. Wisdom "simply is, for it is eternal" (IX).
- After her death he went to the **bath** "because I had heard it drives anxiety from the mind": it didn't (IX).

## Book X: Memory

- Who am I **now**? "I have become a question to myself."
    - Why confess to men? "As if it were they who would cure all that is evil in me." "There is something of man that even the spirit of man in him does not know."
- Where is God found? Not in the earth, sea, sky: "**we are not your God; seek above us**". He turns inward, to **memory**.
    - He **asks creation**: "I asked the winds... and the whole air answered: 'Anaximenes was wrong; I am not God.' I asked the whole frame of the universe and it answered: 'I am not He, but He made me.'"
- Memory is a "**vast palace**", "fields and spacious halls": images of things sensed; skills and learning (not as images but the things themselves: numbers, logic); feelings remembered without being felt; even forgetting is remembered.
    - I distinguish the scent of lilies from violets while smelling nothing; recall joy without being joyful.
    - Searching for a forgotten **name**, we reject wrong names until the right one comes, which we recognise.
- We seek the **happy life** (*beata vita*); everyone wants it, so somehow we remember it. Happiness is "**joy in the truth**", and truth is God.
    - But "the flesh lusts against the spirit", so they settle for what they can.
- So God is in the memory, though not in any place there: "**Late have I loved Thee, O Beauty so ancient and so new, late have I loved Thee! Thou wast within, and I without.**"
- The rest of X: his present temptations: the lust of the flesh, of the eyes (curiosity), and the pride of life (1 John 2:16).
    - **Lust of the flesh**: food: "Who is not carried somewhat beyond the necessary?"
    - **Lust of the eyes**: curiosity, "experiments with the body's aid cloaked under the name of learning": we say "see how it sounds" for all the senses.
    - **Pride of life**: "the desire to be feared and loved by men for no other reason than the joy I get from it".
- Augustine on memory became the basis for his psychological image of the Trinity: memory, understanding, will (*De Trinitate*).

## Book XI: Time

Starts from "In the beginning God created": what was God doing before He made heaven and earth? (One joker's answer: preparing hell for those who ask such questions.) Augustine's answer: there **was no before**: time is a creature; God made time with the world. God is in an eternal present.

- "We exist because we were made; we did not exist before we existed to give ourselves existence."
- God didn't create by words sounding in time, or there'd have been a creature before creation.
- On the joker: "I would much rather say '**I don't know**', when I don't, than ridicule someone who asked a profound question."

**What is time?** "If no one asks me, I know; if I want to explain it to one who asks, I don't know."
- Past is no longer; future not yet; present has no duration (it passes instantly). So how can time be measured, or exist at all?
- Answer: past, present and future exist **in the soul**:
    - the present of things past: **memory**
    - the present of things present: **attention** (*contuitus*)
    - the present of things future: **expectation**
- Time is a ***distentio animi***: a **stretching of the mind**. When reciting a psalm, expectation shrinks, memory grows, attention passes the words from one to the other.
    - Measuring a line of verse: "*Deus creator omnium*", eight syllables, short and long alternately. "It is not the syllables I measure, for they are now no more, but something which remains engraved in my **memory**." A long future is "a long expectation"; a long past "a long memory".
- Our life is scattered, "distended" through time; we're called to be gathered into the One, to **extension** toward what is ahead (Phil 3:13), not distension.
- Contrast Aristotle (time is the number of motion). See [[Aristotle's Physics: Nature, Change and the Four Causes]], [[Presentism vs Eternalism]].

## Books XII–XIII: Creation

- **XII**: "heaven and earth" in Gen 1:1: the **heaven of heaven** (the intellectual creation that contemplates God, outside time) and the **formless matter**. Many interpretations can be true if they're true and fit the faith; Moses may have meant all of them.
    - Formless matter is "first" not in time, "because the forms of things give rise to time".
    - When interpreters fight ("Moses meant what I think"): "**Why should he not have meant both, if both are true**... why should we not believe that Moses saw them all?"
- **XIII**: allegory of the days of creation as the creation of the **Church**: the firmament is Scripture, the waters are the nations, the lights are the saints. The seventh day, without evening: eternal rest in God. "Thou wilt rest in us, as now Thou workest in us."
    - "I call Thee into my soul, which Thou dost make ready to receive Thee by the desire Thou dost inspire in it."

## Notes

*Mine.*

- The book shows **conversion** as a reorientation of desire, not just a change of opinions: the "restless heart" and the divided will. Very close to the Orthodox view of repentance (*metanoia*). See [[Repentance as Metanoia: A Change of Mind, Not a Payment]].
- The theory of time is still discussed (Husserl and Heidegger build on it).
- **Augustine and Orthodoxy**: he's a saint in the Orthodox Church (Blessed Augustine; *The Place of Blessed Augustine in the Orthodox Church*, Fr. Seraphim Rose). But the Orthodox reject the later Augustinian doctrines that came to dominate the West: inherited guilt, predestination, the psychological Trinity grounding the Filioque, created grace. See [[Ancestral Sin vs Original Sin]], [[Augustine's Christology: Possible Heresies]], [[Divine Illumination]].
- The *Confessions* itself is the least controversial part of him: the Orthodox read it as spiritual literature.
