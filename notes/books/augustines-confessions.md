Written c. 397–400, when Augustine had become bishop of Hippo. The first autobiography in the Western sense, but it's really one long **prayer**, addressed to God throughout. *Confessio* means three things: confession of **sin**, confession of **praise**, confession of **faith**. Thirteen books: I–IX his life up to his mother's death, X his present state (memory), XI–XIII on Genesis 1 (time, creation).

## Books I–IX: The Story

| Book | Content |
|---|---|
| **I** | "**Thou hast made us for Thyself, and our heart is restless until it rest in Thee.**" Infancy: already sin (the baby jealous of its foster-brother at the breast). Schooling, Latin and Greek, weeping for Dido |
| **II** | Age 16. **The pear theft**: stealing pears he didn't want, just for the sake of doing wrong with friends. Analysing why: love of evil itself, a perverse imitation of God's freedom |
| **III** | Carthage: "I came to Carthage, where a cauldron of unholy loves sang all around me." Cicero's *Hortensius* turns him to love wisdom. Joins the **Manichees**: two principles, good and evil, God as a kind of material light |
| **IV** | Teaching rhetoric. Living with a concubine (unnamed), a son, Adeodatus. **Death of a friend**: grief because he loved a mortal as if immortal. See [[Friendship and Love in Ancient and Medieval Thought]] |
| **V** | Disillusioned with the Manichee bishop Faustus. To Rome, then **Milan**: hears **Ambrose** preach; allegorical reading of the OT removes his objections |
| **VI** | Monica follows; his concubine sent away for an arranged marriage. "Give me chastity and continence, but not yet." |
| **VII** | **The books of the Platonists** (Plotinus, Porphyry): God is immaterial; evil is privation, not a substance. A glimpse of God "in the flash of a trembling glance". But they didn't tell him that "**the Word was made flesh**". See [[Christian Platonism]] |
| **VIII** | **The garden in Milan**: torn between two wills. Hears a child's voice: "**tolle, lege**" (take up and read). Opens Paul: Rom 13:13-14, "put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh". Converts. "The divided will" |
| **IX** | Baptised by Ambrose (Easter 387). **The vision at Ostia** with Monica: together they rise in conversation beyond all creatures to Wisdom itself, touch it for an instant. Monica dies days later. Augustine's grief |

## Book X: Memory

- Who am I **now**? "I have become a question to myself."
- Where is God found? Not in the earth, sea, sky: "**we are not your God; seek above us**". He turns inward, to **memory**.
- Memory is a "**vast palace**", "fields and spacious halls": images of things sensed; skills and learning (not as images but the things themselves: numbers, logic); feelings remembered without being felt; even forgetting is remembered.
- We seek the **happy life** (*beata vita*); everyone wants it, so somehow we remember it. Happiness is "**joy in the truth**", and truth is God.
- So God is in the memory, though not in any place there: "**Late have I loved Thee, O Beauty so ancient and so new, late have I loved Thee! Thou wast within, and I without.**"
- The rest of X: his present temptations: the lust of the flesh, of the eyes (curiosity), and the pride of life (1 John 2:16).
- Augustine on memory became the basis for his psychological image of the Trinity: memory, understanding, will (*De Trinitate*).

## Book XI: Time

Starts from "In the beginning God created": what was God doing before He made heaven and earth? (One joker's answer: preparing hell for those who ask such questions.) Augustine's answer: there **was no before**: time is a creature; God made time with the world. God is in an eternal present.

**What is time?** "If no one asks me, I know; if I want to explain it to one who asks, I don't know."
- Past is no longer; future not yet; present has no duration (it passes instantly). So how can time be measured, or exist at all?
- Answer: past, present and future exist **in the soul**:
    - the present of things past: **memory**
    - the present of things present: **attention** (*contuitus*)
    - the present of things future: **expectation**
- Time is a ***distentio animi***: a **stretching of the mind**. When reciting a psalm, expectation shrinks, memory grows, attention passes the words from one to the other.
- Our life is scattered, "distended" through time; we're called to be gathered into the One, to **extension** toward what is ahead (Phil 3:13), not distension.
- Contrast Aristotle (time is the number of motion). See [[Aristotle's Physics: Nature, Change and the Four Causes]], [[Presentism vs Eternalism]].

## Books XII–XIII: Creation

- **XII**: "heaven and earth" in Gen 1:1: the **heaven of heaven** (the intellectual creation that contemplates God, outside time) and the **formless matter**. Many interpretations can be true if they're true and fit the faith; Moses may have meant all of them.
- **XIII**: allegory of the days of creation as the creation of the **Church**: the firmament is Scripture, the waters are the nations, the lights are the saints. The seventh day, without evening: eternal rest in God. "Thou wilt rest in us, as now Thou workest in us."

## Notes

*Mine.*

- The book shows **conversion** as a reorientation of desire, not just a change of opinions: the "restless heart" and the divided will. Very close to the Orthodox view of repentance (*metanoia*). See [[Repentance as Metanoia: A Change of Mind, Not a Payment]].
- The theory of time is still discussed (Husserl and Heidegger build on it).
- **Augustine and Orthodoxy**: he's a saint in the Orthodox Church (Blessed Augustine; *The Place of Blessed Augustine in the Orthodox Church*, Fr. Seraphim Rose). But the Orthodox reject the later Augustinian doctrines that came to dominate the West: inherited guilt, predestination, the psychological Trinity grounding the Filioque, created grace. See [[Ancestral Sin vs Original Sin]], [[Augustine's Christology: Possible Heresies]], [[Divine Illumination]].
- The *Confessions* itself is the least controversial part of him: the Orthodox read it as spiritual literature.
