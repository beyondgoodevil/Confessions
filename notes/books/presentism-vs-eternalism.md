**Presentism**: only present things exist. **Eternalism**: past, present and future things all exist; "now" is like "here". Crisp defends presentism. Read the first half properly and the relativity and grounding sections; skimmed the two sections on singular propositions.

## 1. The Views

| View | What exists | Does the present move? |
|---|---|---|
| **Presentism** | present things only | yes (A-theory) |
| **Growing block** (Broad, Tooley) | past and present, no future | yes |
| **Moving spotlight** | everything, with a privileged "now" sliding along | yes |
| **Static eternalism** ("block universe") | everything | no (B-theory) |

- **A-theory / B-theory** (from McTaggart): A = there's an objective fact about which time is present, and it changes. B = there are only earlier-than / later-than relations.
- Crisp groups the middle two as "dynamic eternalism": the *totality of events that exist* changes over time.
- Warning on words: "four-dimensionalism" sometimes means eternalism, sometimes means things have temporal parts. Crisp uses it for the first and "perdurantism" for the second.

His official statement:

> **Presentism**: it is always the case that, for every x, x is present.

"Always" is there to rule out the growing block, which would satisfy "everything is present" at the very first moment.

## 2. Is It Even a Real Thesis?

The **triviality objection**: "only present things exist". What tense is "exist"?

- present tense → "only present things exist *now*". Trivially true.
- "existed, exist or will exist" → obviously false. The Roman Empire existed and isn't present.

Crisp: that trades on a ***de re* / *de dicto*** slip.

- *de dicto*: it WAS the case that (something is the Roman Empire). True, and the presentist agrees.
- *de re*: among absolutely everything there is, there's a thing that is the Roman Empire. That's what the presentist denies, and it isn't trivially true.

So the question is what's in the widest domain of "everything". Same box-scope move as in [[Modal Logic: Necessity and Possibility]] sec. 3 and 6, with tense in place of □.

## 3. Why Be a Presentist?

Crisp admits there's no knock-down argument. He says presentism is the only view that keeps three things most people believe:

1. **Endurantism.** Things persist by being *wholly present* at each time, not by having a different temporal part at each time (perdurance).
    - His reasons: I have my headache *directly*, not because a part of me has it. And the person who started this sentence is strictly the same one who finished it.
2. **Things really change.** I have properties now that I lacked ten years ago.
3. **Tense is univocal.** There's one ordinary meaning of "was". In that meaning it *was* true that Lincoln is president.

The argument:

- **Static eternalism** fails 1 + 2. If I'm wholly present at 1996 and wholly present now, and both times are equally real, then one and the same thing is (tenselessly) both bent and straight. The eternalist has to make "bent" a relation to a time, or give me temporal parts. Either way real change of an enduring thing goes. (This is Lewis's **problem of temporary intrinsics**, turned round.)
- **Dynamic eternalism** fails 3: it needs two senses of "was", one inside the block and one for the block itself changing.
- Presentism keeps all three.

Honest about the weakness: an eternalist will just reject 1. Crisp says so himself.

## 4. Objections

### 4.1 Talking about things that don't exist

- "Lincoln was tall" seems to be *about* Lincoln. If Lincoln isn't in reality at all, what's it about?
- Sharper form: a singular proposition has the individual as a constituent. No Lincoln, no proposition.
- (Skimmed. The replies involve denying that such propositions need their subject to exist, or paraphrasing with past-tense operators.)

### 4.2 Relativity

Crisp calls this one "sadly" real.

- Special relativity: no absolute simultaneity. What counts as "now" across the universe depends on your frame of reference. So there's no single present for only-the-present-exists to be about.
- Presentist fix: add a **preferred foliation**, one privileged way of slicing spacetime into moments.
- Cost: that's exactly what Einstein is supposed to have got rid of. And the privileged slicing would be **undetectable**.
- Crisp's replies:
    - Undetectable doesn't mean unreal. That inference is leftover positivism.
    - It isn't idle: for a presentist the job of the model is to represent how the 3-D world evolves, and that needs a slicing.
- Most philosophers treat relativity as decisive against presentism. Crisp obviously doesn't, but concedes it's a real price.

### 4.3 Grounding

- **Truthmaker principle**: every truth is made true by something that exists.
- "There were woolly mammoths" is true. What *present* thing makes it true? The present could be exactly as it is with the world five minutes old.
- Presentist could deny truthmaker (it has trouble anyway with "there are no unicorns"). But there's a weaker version that's hard to deny: **truth supervenes on being**. No difference in what's true without a difference in what exists or what properties things have.
- So the presentist needs something present whose existence or properties fix the past.
- Options I know of from elsewhere (didn't get to Crisp's own in detail):
    - the world *now has* past-directed properties, like "having contained mammoths" (Bigelow's "Lucretian" properties)
    - abstract times: complete descriptions of how things were, standing in earlier/later relations (I believe this is the direction Crisp takes)
    - God's memory (!). This has actually been proposed.

Compare [[Free Will and Divine Foreknowledge]] sec. 4, where Conee answers the fatalist by putting the truthmakers for predictions *in the future*. A presentist can't say that, since for him the future isn't there. So presentism and that reply to fatalism don't sit together easily. Open futurists use this on purpose.

## 5. Why I Care About This

- **God and time.** Boethius's answer to foreknowledge (God sees all times in one eternal present) seems to need all times to *be there* to be seen. That sounds like eternalism. But if the block is real and static, in what sense did creation *begin* or will history *end*?
- Other direction: if presentism is true and God is timeless, how does God relate to a "now" that keeps changing?
- Maybe the two-level picture helps: creatures really are in passing time; God's relation to time isn't one more position *in* time. Then neither view of created time is forced by theology. Not sure.
- **Liturgy.** "Today is born of the Virgin", "Today Christ is risen". The Church talks as though past saving events are present, not just remembered. That's not eternalism exactly, but it's not flat presentism either.
- **Resurrection.** On presentism the dead simply don't exist as bodies now; on eternalism they exist "back there". Neither is the resurrection.
- [[Nietzsche: Eternal Recurrence]]: Loeb's Nietzsche needs time itself to loop. On presentism that's hard to even state.
