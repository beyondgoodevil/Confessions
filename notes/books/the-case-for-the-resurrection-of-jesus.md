Written as a handbook for sharing faith, with quizzes. The method is what's important: argue only from facts that are **well evidenced** and accepted by the **vast majority** of scholars, including sceptics. Not from the inerrancy or even the general reliability of the Gospels.

## Structure

- **Part 1**: A Life to Die For (evangelism; history 101)
- **Part 2**: "Just the Facts" (the minimal facts)
- **Part 3**: "Yes, but..." (opposing theories: legends, lies, lapses; psychological phenomena; more critical comebacks)
- **Part 4**: There's More (vision or bodily appearance? who did Jesus think He was? what does God have to do with it? final issues)

## 1. Doing History

- Criteria for historical facts:
    - **multiple independent sources**
    - **early** testimony (close to the events)
    - **eyewitness** testimony
    - **enemy attestation** (opponents admit it)
    - **embarrassment** (a detail that hurts the author's cause)
- The **minimal facts** have to pass several of these, *and* be granted by most critical scholars. Habermas surveyed over 2,000 scholarly publications (1975–2003) to establish which facts qualify.

## 2. The Minimal Facts ("4 + 1")

| Fact | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **1. Jesus died by crucifixion** | all four Gospels, Paul; Josephus (*Ant.* 18.3), Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44), Lucian, Mara bar Serapion, the Talmud. Roman crucifixion was deadly; John 19:34 (blood and water) fits medical understanding |
| **2. The disciples believed He rose and appeared to them** | **1 Cor 15:3-8**: a creed Paul "received", dated by most scholars within 2–5 years of the crucifixion. Lists appearances to Peter, the Twelve, 500, James, all the apostles, Paul. Oral formulas in Acts (Acts 2, 13). The disciples' **transformation** and willingness to suffer and die for it (Clement, Polycarp, Tertullian, Origen) |
| **3. Paul, a persecutor, was converted** | Paul's own letters (Gal 1:13-16; 1 Cor 15:9; Phil 3:6); Acts. An enemy converted by what he took to be an appearance |
| **4. James, a sceptic, was converted** | the Gospels say Jesus' brothers didn't believe (Mark 3:21, John 7:5); 1 Cor 15:7 lists an appearance to James; he then leads the Jerusalem church (Gal 1:19, Acts 15) and is martyred (Josephus, *Ant.* 20.9) |
| **+1. The tomb was empty** | accepted by about 75% of scholars, so a "plus one": Jerusalem factor (they'd have produced the body); enemy attestation (the Jewish leaders said the disciples **stole** it, Matt 28:13: which admits it was empty); **women** as first witnesses (embarrassing in that culture: an inventor would have used men) |

## 3. Alternative Theories

| Theory | Reply |
|---|---|
| **Legend**: the story grew over time | the 1 Cor 15 creed is too early; and it doesn't explain Paul's conversion |
| **Fraud**: the disciples stole the body / lied | liars don't die for what they know is false; doesn't explain Paul or James |
| **Wrong tomb** | the authorities would have found the right one; doesn't explain the appearances |
| **Swoon**: Jesus didn't die | Romans were expert executioners; a half-dead man wouldn't inspire belief that He had conquered death (Strauss's own objection) |
| **Hallucination** | hallucinations are private, not group experiences (the 500; the Twelve); they don't happen to enemies like Paul or sceptics like James; and they don't explain the empty tomb |
| **Conversion disorder / grief visions** | same problems: groups, enemies, the tomb |
| **Different Jesus / twin** | no evidence; ad hoc |
| **Copycat of pagan dying-rising gods** | the parallels are late, vague, or not resurrections (Osiris rules the underworld); Jews weren't borrowing from pagan myth |

The resurrection explains **all** the facts; each rival explains at most a few, and needs to combine several.

## 4. Vision or Body?

- Some scholars accept the appearances but say they were **visions** (subjective or "spiritual").
- But the Jewish word "resurrection" (*anastasis*) always meant the **body** raised. A vision would have been called an angel or a ghost (Luke 24:37-39: "a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have").
- Paul's "spiritual body" (1 Cor 15:44) means a body animated by the Spirit, not a body made of spirit. And "sown... raised" implies the same body.

## 5. Who Did Jesus Think He Was?

- Even critical scholars allow that He called Himself **Son of Man** (Dan 7), claimed authority to forgive sins, spoke of His death and vindication, acted with divine authority. So the resurrection would be **vindication** of claims He made, not a random marvel. See [[Kingship of Christ]].

## 6. What Does God Have to Do with It?

- The atheist can say: even if we can't explain it, a miracle is the least likely explanation. Reply: if there's any reason to think God exists (cosmology, design, morality), a resurrection in this context, of this man, isn't improbable. See [[Hume on Causation and Induction]] sec. 5, [[Is There a God?]].

## Notes

*Mine.*

- The method is valuable because it doesn't need anyone to accept the Bible as inspired. But it reduces Christian faith to a historical inference, which the Fathers wouldn't: the resurrection is known by the Church's witness and in the Eucharist.
- 1 Cor 15:3-8 is the key text. Learn it.
- N. T. Wright's *The Resurrection of the Son of God* gives the longer version (what "resurrection" meant to Jews and pagans).

## Chapter by Chapter

**1. Unwrapping the Gift: Evangelism and the Resurrection**
- The Resurrection is at the centre of doctrine and practice: "If the dead are not raised, let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die" (1 Cor 15:32).
- Jesus' **predictions** of His resurrection are probably authentic by the **principle of embarrassment**: they show the disciples misunderstanding, which they wouldn't invent.
- "Reasonable historical certainty": we can't be 100% certain George Washington was president rather than a myth, but we're reasonably certain.
- 1 Pet 3:15: "always be ready to make a defence... with gentleness and reverence."
- Only God draws people to salvation; and many reject Christianity for emotional reasons (Jesus as the only way, sexual ethics), not evidence.

**2. History 101**
- **Multiple independent sources**: the car crash: one driver's word vs the other's, but several witnesses at the bus stop tip the scale.
- **Testimony against interest** (enemy/embarrassment): the Jefferson–Hemings case: a Jefferson relative's damaging admission was strong evidence. "People do not make up lies in order to hurt themselves."
- **Eyewitness and early** testimony; secondhand testimony is still usable.
- No video of antiquity: these are commonsense guides. When the scale tips significantly, we say the event is "historical".

**3–4. A Quintet of Facts (4 + 1)**
- Keep "the main thing the main thing" (Covey): the argument doesn't depend on the Bible's reliability or inspiration.
- **Fact 1, death by crucifixion**: Cicero calls crucifixion the most horrendous torture; "the very word 'cross' should be far removed not only from the person of a Roman citizen but from his thoughts, his eyes and his ears." Tacitus: "the extreme penalty". All four Gospels; Josephus, Tacitus, Lucian, Mara bar Serapion, the Talmud.
- **Fact 2, the disciples believed He rose and appeared to them**: Paul knew the apostles personally (Gal 1–2) and says "whether it was I or they, this is what we preach" (1 Cor 15:11). Oral formulas, early sermons in Acts. **Willingness to suffer**: Clement of Rome on Peter's and Paul's deaths; Tacitus on Nero's persecution; Polycarp, Ignatius, Tertullian, Origen. The disciples' claims weren't **myth-making** because they were the original witnesses.
- **Fact 3, Paul the persecutor converted**: "He who once persecuted us is now preaching the faith he once tried to destroy" (Gal 1:23): multiply attested.
- **Fact 4, James the sceptic converted**: Josephus; Hegesippus (in Eusebius): James was called "the Just" and "Oblias", "bulwark of the people". The "James, son of Joseph, brother of Jesus" ossuary is mentioned (its authenticity is disputed).
- **Fact +1, the empty tomb**: the Jerusalem factor, enemy attestation, and the **women** as first witnesses. The disciples called the women's report "idle tales" (Luke 24:11: Greek "nonsense, humbug"); women's testimony counted little in that culture. Accepted by about 75% of scholars.
- These facts exclude "the disciples lied and stole the body", since they truly believed He appeared.

**5. Always Looking for a Way Out: Legends, Lies, and Lapses**
- **Legend / embellishment**: the original disciples themselves claimed the appearances; Paul's conversion was through an experience he took as meeting the risen Jesus, not a story.
- **Non-historical genre** (the Gospels are honorific fiction): wouldn't have convinced Paul; doesn't explain the facts.
- **Pagan parallels**: Philostratus's *Life of Apollonius* postdates Jesus by nearly 200 years and may be "a conscious reaction against Christianity"; most parallels speak of a vague distant past.
- **Fraud**: Watergate: **John Dean** turned state's evidence "to save his own skin" within two weeks. The apostles never did, under far worse pressure. (Sincerity doesn't prove the Resurrection, but it rules out lying.)
- **Wrong tomb / stolen body**: the empty tomb didn't lead anyone except John to believe; Mary Magdalene concluded someone had taken the body.
- **Combination theories** are weak: each piece explains little.
- **Swoon / apparent death**: scourging and crucifixion; crucified victims had to push up on pierced feet to exhale; a half-dead Jesus wouldn't inspire belief in a conqueror of death.

**6. Mind Games: Psychological Phenomena**
- **Illusion** (misperceiving something real, like an optical illusion) vs **hallucination** (seeing what isn't there).
- **Group appearances**: hallucinations are private. The ship analogy: if one person on a raft sees a ship and the others don't, it's a hallucination; if everyone sees it, start yelling, the ship is real. Group appearances are in the 1 Cor 15 creed, the Gospels and Acts.
- **"Vision"** is vague ("like nailing jello to a wall"): if it means a literary genre, see legend; if objective, it's the risen Christ; if subjective, it's hallucination. Neither explains the empty tomb or the bodily nature of the appearances.
- Critics argue Paul's experience was visionary and he lists it with the others, so theirs were visionary too.
- **Conversion disorder** (Paul's guilt): implausible; guilt would more likely make him stop persecuting ("they learned their lesson") than become a Christian. The "**power theory**" (Paul wanted power): his life of suffering shows no sign of it.

**7–8. Stopping at Nothing / Naturally Speaking**
- **Discrepancies** in the Gospels: at most challenge complete accuracy, not general trustworthiness; historians don't conclude events didn't happen because accounts differ. They may even show **independence**.
- **Bias**: recognising an author's interest doesn't mean he distorted facts: Jewish historians of the Holocaust are passionately committed and careful; revisionists downplay.
- **Naturalism**: "truth is found only in what science can test" is self-refuting. A scientist or historian could evaluate "Jesus was seen alive after his death".
- **God of the gaps**: past bad "God" explanations no more undermine current arguments than discarded scientific theories undermine science.
- "**Miracles in other religions**" count against Christian ones? The issue is **antecedent probability**: most supernatural claims may be untrustworthy, but that doesn't settle one well-evidenced case.
- **Near-death experiences** are a challenge to naturalism.

**9. Heavenly Vision or Bodily Appearance?**
- Paul says believers' future body will be like Christ's risen body (Phil 3:21).
- Paul saw "a blinding light", the disciples touched Jesus: four reasons this doesn't rule out bodily resurrection (Paul's was after the Ascension, etc.).
- **1 Pet 3:18** ("made alive by the Spirit"): if it meant spiritual-only, the "evolution" would go backwards, from bodily to spiritual.
- **Mark 16:6-7**: "he is risen... he goeth before you into Galilee": "goes before" is the common meaning, not "leading" in a vision.
- **1 Cor 15:44-50**: "spiritual body" and "flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom": Rom 8:11 ("give life to your mortal bodies"), Phil 3:21 ("change our vile body"): "flesh and blood" means our bodies in their mortal form, not physicality as such.

**10. Who Did Jesus Think He Was?**
- **Son of Man** (Dan 7): emphasises humanity at first glance, so unlikely to be a later invention.
- **Son of God**: Hick and Crossan say it meant something less than divine (Augustus called son of a god in Suetonius). But in **Mark 13:32** (an authentic saying because embarrassing: the Son doesn't know the hour) the Son is ranked above humans and angels and below only the Father. Also Matt 11:27, Mark 14:36.
- So the disciples weren't inventing His divinity to honour Him.
- The resurrection happened to the one person who made such unique claims about Himself.

**11. What Does God Have to Do with This?**
- Atheist Frank Zindler: what do we do with the Wizard of Oz once we realise there's no Oz? So the atheist must refute the evidence **and** show there's no God.
- **Big Bang**: "almost a fingerprint of the Creator". **Fine-tuning** and **specified complexity** (Dembski): like a player dealt a perfect hand repeatedly: "the deck has been stacked".
- Given God, the Resurrection becomes "enormously more likely".

**12. Some Final Issues**
- The verdict: the Resurrection vindicates Jesus' life and claims.
- **Muslims** (like the Gnostics 500 years earlier) say Jesus never died. But then what caused the disciples' sincere belief? See [[Islam's View of Jesus vs the Gospels]].
- Martyrdom for other beliefs (Joseph Smith's eleven witnesses): sincerity shows belief, not truth; the minimal facts do the work.
- **Ad hominem** arguments against Christian scholars ("you're biased") are fallacies.
- **"Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence"**: group hallucination is itself an extraordinary claim.
- "If historians dismissed data merely because a doubt could be raised... virtually nothing could be held as knowable history."

**13. People Skills: The Art of Sharing**
- Model a changed life; gentleness. "When you wrestle a pig in the mud, sooner or later you realise the pig is enjoying it"; "do not throw your pearls before swine".
- **Anchor** a conclusion before moving on. For each objection: (1) would it refute the Resurrection if true? (2) Which of the 4 + 1 facts does it leave unexplained?
- Don't overclaim ("I'll prove beyond any doubt"); say "there's pretty good evidence". Understatement, like the Scot who called the Blitz "a bit of a nuisance".
- The **Romans Road** (Rom 3:23; 6:23; 5:8; 10:9) as a gospel outline.
- "I can't base my life on what isn't 100% certain": then no one could live as if their own past were real. "Does your friend deny the Holocaust because he wasn't there?"
- The appendix summarises every objection and reply in outline (a useful study sheet). Also: the "**principle of dissimilarity**" (Meier); Jonah's "three days and three nights" emphasises burial and rising, not exact duration (against Ahmed Deedat).
