Descartes (1596–1650) is called the father of modern philosophy because he started again from the **thinking self**: find something certain, then rebuild knowledge on it. The *Meditations* are six "days" of meditation (on the model of spiritual exercises; see [[Philosophy as a Way of Life]]).

## Meditation I: Doubt

- Goal: "to demolish everything and start again right from the foundations".
- Not doubting each belief but the **foundations**:
    1. **Senses** sometimes deceive (a tower looks round from afar). But surely not that I'm sitting by the fire?
    2. **Dreaming**: there are no sure signs to tell waking from dreaming. But even in dreams, simple things (shape, number, 2 + 3 = 5) seem true.
    3. **The evil demon** (*malin génie*): a powerful deceiver could make me wrong even about maths. So everything can be doubted.

## Meditation II: The Cogito

- Even if a demon deceives me, **I** must exist to be deceived. "**I am, I exist**, is necessarily true whenever it is put forward by me or conceived in my mind." (The famous form "*cogito, ergo sum*" is from the *Discourse on Method*.)
- What am I? A **thinking thing** (*res cogitans*): something that doubts, understands, affirms, denies, wills, refuses, imagines and senses.
- **The wax**: melt a piece of wax and every sensed quality changes (smell, colour, shape, hardness), yet it's the same wax. So I know it not by senses or imagination but by the **intellect** alone. And the mind is better known than the body.

## Meditation III: God (the Trademark Argument)

- **Rule**: whatever I perceive **clearly and distinctly** is true. But a deceiving God could undermine even this; so I need to know God exists and isn't a deceiver.
- Ideas have **objective reality** (what they represent). The cause of an idea must have at least as much **formal reality** (actual being) as the idea has objective reality.
- I have the idea of an **infinite, perfect** being. I'm finite and imperfect; I couldn't have produced it. So only an infinite being could be its cause. God exists.
- God put this idea in me "like the mark of the craftsman stamped on his work": hence "trademark".

## Meditation IV: Error

- If God is no deceiver, why do I err? Because my **will** (infinite in scope) goes beyond my **understanding** (finite). I affirm what I don't clearly perceive.
- Fix: suspend judgement unless clear and distinct.

## Meditation V: Matter and the Ontological Argument

- **Essence of matter**: **extension** (length, breadth, depth), knowable mathematically.
- **Ontological argument**: existence belongs to God's essence as having three angles = two right angles belongs to a triangle's. So God exists. See [[The Ontological Argument]].

## Meditation VI: Mind and Body

**The real distinction**
1. I can clearly and distinctly conceive mind without body, and body without mind.
2. Whatever I can clearly and distinctly conceive apart, God can make exist apart.
3. So mind and body are **really distinct** substances.

Also the **divisibility** argument: the body is divisible, the mind isn't (I can't find half a mind). So they differ.

- Mind (*res cogitans*): thinking, unextended. Body (*res extensa*): extended, non-thinking.
- The external world exists: God gave me a strong inclination to believe bodies cause my sensations; if they didn't, God would be a deceiver.
- Yet mind and body are **closely united**: "I am not merely present in my body as a sailor in a ship"; pain is felt, not just observed.

## Objections

| Objection | Who | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Cartesian circle** | Arnauld | clear and distinct perception proves God; God guarantees clear and distinct perception. Circular |
| **Interaction** | Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia (1643) | how can an unextended mind move an extended body? Causation by contact needs extension. Descartes's answer (the pineal gland) didn't help. See [[Mind and Body: Dualism, Materialism, Emergence]] |
| **Conceivability ≠ possibility** | Arnauld | I can conceive a right triangle without knowing Pythagoras's theorem holds; that doesn't make them separable |
| **"I think" assumes an "I"** | Lichtenberg, Nietzsche | at most "there is thinking" |
| **Animals** | many | Descartes said animals are machines without souls. Absurd consequences |

## Death of the Soul: The Story Afterwards

Barrett's narrative in three parts:
- **Gathering of a new age**: 17th c. science treats nature as mechanism; the soul is the one thing left outside it (Descartes, Leibniz); then the empiricists (Locke, Berkeley, Hume) reduce the mind to sensations.
- **The pivot**: Kant: the mind as active synthesiser of experience, but the self as substance unknowable.
- **Dispersion**: 19th–20th c.: psychology without a soul, behaviourism, the mind as computer. The soul disappears.

The upshot: once nature was taken to be **mere extension**, the mind had nowhere to fit, and in the end it was eliminated.

## Notes

*Mine.*

- Descartes's dualism is **not** the patristic or Aristotelian view. For Aristotle and the Fathers the soul is the **form of the body**, and a human is **one** thing, soul and body together. Descartes makes two substances and then can't connect them. Feser's account of how the moderns created this problem is in [[The Last Superstition]] sec. 5.
- The method (start from the self, doubt everything) became the default of modern philosophy: the self as a private inner theatre. Orthodox critics (and Heidegger) see it as the root of modern individualism. Compare [[Being as Communion]]: the person exists in communion, not as a self-enclosed thinker.
- But the cogito is still a good reply to total scepticism, and the argument that the mind isn't extended is close to Augustine's ("if I am deceived, I am": *City of God* XI.26).

## From the Meditations and the Objections and Replies

**Background (introduction)**
- Descartes was trained in the **scholastic** (Aristotelian) tradition at La Flèche. His *The World* and *Treatise on Man* explained the universe and human physiology by mechanical laws.
- *Je pense, donc je suis* is from the *Discourse* (French); *Cogito ergo sum* from the Latin *Principles*. "Rationalist" doesn't mean reason alone settles science: experiments decide between explanations.
- The **deceiving God** is a dilemma: if God exists He could make me err; if not, I come from a less perfect cause and have even less reason to trust myself.
- Three "**primitive notions**" (letter to Elisabeth): extension (for body), thought (for mind), and their **union** (for sensations and passions).

**Dedicatory letter to the Sorbonne**
- Unbelievers would judge proving God from Scripture circular, so reason is needed. Wis 13 and Rom 1: those who knew the world are "without excuse" for not finding its Lord.
- The 1641 title promised "**the immortality of the soul**"; the 1642 edition changed it to "the distinction between the soul and the body": Descartes shows only that the body's decay doesn't imply the mind's destruction, since immortality would need a full physics.

**The Meditations themselves**
- First: "the result is that I begin to feel dazed, and this very feeling only reinforces the notion that I may be asleep."
- Second: "I am, then, in the strict sense only a thing that thinks; that is, a mind, or intelligence, or intellect, or reason, words whose meaning I have been ignorant of until now." "Having a sensory perception" in the strict sense "is simply thinking".
- Third: **formal** vs **objective** reality (the scholastic distinction): if an idea A represents X which is F, F-ness is in X "formally" and in A "objectively". "My perception of the infinite, God, is in some way **prior** to my perception of the finite, myself": I know I'm "incomplete and dependent... aspiring without limit to ever greater things", so I know there's one who has them actually and infinitely.
- Fourth: error isn't pure negation but **privation**. Freedom is greatest when "from a great light in the intellect there followed a great inclination in the will": the less indifference, the more freedom.
- Fifth: God without existence is as contradictory as "a mountain without a valley". "The certainty and truth of all knowledge depends uniquely on my awareness of the true God."
- Sixth: the mind isn't "in" the body like a pilot; but if a foot or arm is cut off, "nothing has been taken away from the mind". **Phantom pain**: a motion anywhere along the nerves from foot to brain can produce pain "as if in the foot". The dropsy patient thirsty when drink will harm him: "a true error of nature" in the mind–body composite. Waking vs dreaming: we're awake when we can connect perceptions "with the whole of the rest of my life without a break".

**Objections and Replies**
- **Hobbes** (Third Objections): "I am thinking, therefore I am thought" or "I am using my intellect, hence I am an intellect" isn't valid; "I might as well say 'I am walking, therefore I am a walk'." Descartes: by "mind" he meant things endowed with thought, not faculties.
- **Gassendi** (Fifth): the wax: your "understanding" is just imagination; we "see" men when we only see hats and cloaks. Descartes: it's the mind that judges.
- Knowing that we think needs no prior definition of thought or existence.
- "I use the term '**mind**' rather than 'soul', since soul is ambiguous and often applied to something corporeal."
- **Infinite vs indefinite**: space, numbers, divisibility are "indefinite" (unlimited only in some respect); only God is infinite.
- **Caterus** (First): God as "from Himself" (*a se*): positively, as His own cause; negatively, "not from another". Descartes: **continuous creation**: "the separate divisions of time do not depend on each other", so a thing needs a power that "as it were recreates it continuously".
- Caterus's Thomist reply to the ontological argument: a horse thought of with wings doesn't thereby exist; "existence" isn't in a thing as perfections are. Descartes distinguishes possible from necessary existence.
- **Arnauld** (Fourth): conceivability isn't separability: abstraction "by an intellect conceiving inadequately" gives only a formal, not a real, distinction.
- Mersenne's theologians: God made things good because He **willed** them (Descartes's voluntarism about eternal truths).

## Barrett's *Death of the Soul* in More Detail

Corrected outline: **Part I, "Gathering of a New Age"** covers the 17th century (Galileo, Descartes, Leibniz) and the British empiricists (Locke, Berkeley, Hume: "The Puzzles of Sensation"); **Part II, "The Pivot"** is **Kant** (a map of the modern world; the power of mind; the finitude of mind; duty and beauty); **Part III, "Dispersion"** is the 19th–20th centuries (existentialism, deconstruction, Heidegger, analytic philosophy, Wittgenstein, the computer).

- Barrett's aim: to "lay hold of the fact of consciousness as a human reality that seems on the way to getting lost in the modern world".
- The **New Science** was as much **mathematical** as experimental: "thereby hangs a philosophical lesson". From the start, science is "dogged by its shadow, **scientism**".
- Our being here and not elsewhere is a brute fact: Heidegger's "thrownness". The star map and light-years: "I am even more poignantly aware of the brief finitude of my being."
- Descartes served as a volunteer soldier 1618–28. Today both phenomenology and analytic philosophy decry "Cartesianism".
- **Leibniz** saw the continuity of medieval with Greek thought; monads; "Why is there anything at all rather than nothing?"
- **Locke**'s "plain historical method"; association of ideas as a mechanical model of mind. The split between the scientific world and the human world "becomes a central and disturbing theme of modern thought". **Whitehead**: "the fallacy of misplaced concreteness" and "the **bifurcation of nature**" into primary (real) and secondary (subjective) qualities.
- **Berkeley**: Locke's "matter" "runs roughshod over the ordinary facts of perception". If empiricism means fidelity to experience, don't close the gates against the awareness of the **self**.
- **Kant**: "the first thinker who arrives at a really philosophical comprehension of what modern science is"; still preserves "the unity of a fundamentally theistic civilisation". Mathematics as the mind's active **synthesis** (counting marks on a wall: "a self that is only a heap of impressions could not count up to five"). **Science-technology** as one project: man "compels nature to answer his questions". The idea of a **Necessary Being** lacks definite content for proof, not meaning. The moral "ought" can't be defined by any "is"; "Thou shalt not lie!" and the guilt at a white lie.
- **Dispersion**: Hume's bundle self → existentialist and deconstructionist fragmentation. Deconstruction reading Frost's "Stopping by Woods" as anything but woods: "an absurd travesty".
- **Sartre**: "condemned to be free". **Heidegger**: the "I" as *Ichsein*, one mode of being among others.
- **Moore**'s open-question argument and the Bloomsbury "civilisation"; **Wittgenstein**'s positivist period (values in "the mystical") and later rejection of logicism.
- **Turing**: "Do machines think?" replaced by a behavioural test; Turing's own example interview about a sonnet ("would not 'a spring day' do as well or better?"). Barrett: doing problems "mechanically, without thinking" is exactly what minds do **not** essentially do.
