***On the Incarnation of the Word*** is a short treatise by **St. Athanasius of Alexandria** (c. 296–373), written as the second half of a two-part work with *Against the Heathen*. It answers one question: **why did the Word of God become man?**

### 1. Structure of the Work

| Chapters | Subject |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Creation and the Fall |
| 6–10 | The "divine dilemma" and its solution in the Incarnation |
| 11–18 | The second reason: restoring the knowledge of God |
| 19–25 | The death of Christ, and why the Cross |
| 26–32 | The Resurrection and its proofs |
| 33–40 | Refutation of the Jews, from the prophecies |
| 41–55 | Refutation of the Gentiles, from reason and from the results |
| 56–57 | Conclusion |

### 2. Creation and the Fall

- The world was made from nothing by the Word. The same Word who created is the one who comes to re-create.
- Man was made **in the image of God**, given a share in the Word's own reason, and so the possibility of incorruption, on condition of remaining in communion with God.
- By turning away from God, the source of being, man turned back toward **non-being**. The result was **corruption and death**, which Athanasius treats as a slide back into the nothing from which man was made, not merely a penalty imposed from outside.

### 3. The Divine Dilemma

Athanasius presents God as facing two things that both had to hold:

1. **God's truthfulness**: He had said that transgression would bring death (Genesis 2:17). He could not simply go back on His word.
2. **God's goodness**: it was unfitting that creatures made in His image should perish and His work come to nothing.

Could repentance alone have solved it? Athanasius answers no:

- Repentance can stop further sinning, but it cannot undo **corruption**, which had become part of man's condition.
- What was needed was not only pardon but **re-creation**, and only the Creator can re-create.

### 4. The Solution: the Word Takes a Body

- The Word, who is incorporeal and immortal, **took a human body** from a pure Virgin, so that He would have something that could die.
- He offered that body to death **on behalf of all**. Because it was the body of the Word, death could not hold it, and in Him death was spent and abolished.
- By union with Him, human nature is clothed with **incorruption** through the promise of the resurrection.
- Athanasius's image: when a great king enters a city and lives in one of its houses, the whole city is honoured and enemies no longer attack it. So the Word's dwelling in one human body affects the whole human race.

### 5. The Second Reason: Restoring the Knowledge of God

- Men had lost the knowledge of God and turned to idols, though they were made in His image and had creation, the Law and the prophets as witnesses.
- Athanasius's image: when a portrait on a panel is ruined by stains, the subject must come and sit again so that the likeness can be renewed on the same wood. So the **Image of the Father** came Himself to renew man made after the image.
- Because men were looking for God among sensible things, the Word became visible and worked among them, so that by His works they might recognise the Father.

### 6. Why the Cross, and Why Publicly

- **A public death**: so that the death was certain and witnessed, and the Resurrection could not be dismissed.
- **Not a death of His own choosing or by sickness**: He accepted the death His enemies devised, to show that He overcomes death in every form.
- **The Cross specifically**:
    - He bore the curse laid on us (Deuteronomy 21:23; Galatians 3:13).
    - With arms outstretched He draws both peoples, Jews and Gentiles, to Himself (John 12:32).
    - Lifted up in the air, He overthrows "the prince of the power of the air" (Ephesians 2:2).
- **The third day**: long enough to show He truly died, short enough that the body saw no corruption and the witnesses were still present.

### 7. The Resurrection and Its Proofs

Athanasius argues from present evidence, not only from past testimony:

- Christians, including women and children, **despise death** and go to martyrdom willingly. Death has lost its terror, which shows it has been defeated.
- **Idolatry and the oracles are fading**, and demons are driven out at the name of Christ and the sign of the Cross.
- A dead man does not do these things. Christ acts daily in the world, so He is alive.

### 8. The Famous Line

> "He was made man that we might be made God." (*On the Incarnation* 54)

- This is the doctrine of **theosis**: not that man becomes God by nature, but that he partakes of the divine life by grace (2 Peter 1:4).
- Salvation for Athanasius is therefore more than acquittal. It is the **healing and deification of human nature** through union with the incarnate Word.

### 9. Why It Matters

- **Against Arianism**: if the Son were a creature, He could not re-create or deify. Only one who is truly God can give a share in God's life. This is the logic Athanasius defended at and after Nicaea.
- **Against a purely legal view of salvation**: the problem is corruption and death, and the cure is the Incarnation, death and Resurrection taken together.
- **For Orthodox soteriology**: the work stands with St. Irenaeus's recapitulation as a foundation of the Eastern understanding of salvation.

### Summary

- Man, made from nothing in God's image, fell back toward corruption and death.
- God's truth and goodness both had to be upheld; repentance alone could not reverse corruption.
- The Word took a body, died in it for all, and rose, destroying death and restoring the image and the knowledge of God.
- The proof is visible in the defeat of the fear of death and the collapse of idolatry.
- The purpose of it all: that man might share in the life of God.
