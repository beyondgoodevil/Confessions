*On the Incarnation of the Word*, St. Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 296–373). Second half of a pair with *Against the Heathen*. Short, and written when he was young (probably before Arius was an issue, since Arius isn't mentioned). One question runs through it: **why did the Word become man?**

- In *Against the Gentiles* the Word is not the "seminal word" in creatures but the living Creator. Paradise is "figuratively called" paradise: contemplation of God isn't a place (Behr's introduction).
- Behr: "incarnation" for Athanasius is wider than the moment of conception. It covers the whole manifestation, death and resurrection, and continues in the Church (cf. Antony in the *Life*, emerging from his fortress in a natural state, "neither emaciated nor fat").
- Lewis's preface ("On the Reading of Old Books"): read one old book for every new one, since old books correct the blind spots of our own age. "Two heads are better than one... because they are unlikely to go wrong in the same direction." He found it a masterpiece "by a very simple test". Athanasius's epitaph: *Athanasius contra mundum*.

## 1. Layout

| Ch. | About |
|---|---|
| 1–5 | Creation and Fall |
| 6–10 | The "divine dilemma" and the Incarnation as solution |
| 11–18 | Second reason: restoring knowledge of God |
| 19–25 | The death of Christ, why the Cross |
| 26–32 | Resurrection and proofs |
| 33–40 | Against the Jews, from prophecy |
| 41–55 | Against the Gentiles, from reason and results |
| 56–57 | Conclusion |

## 2. Creation and Fall (1–5)

- World made from nothing through the Word. Same Word who made is the one who comes to re-make. He starts here on purpose.
    - **Ch. 2**: three wrong views of creation:
        - **Epicureans** (by chance): then everything would be the same, "the whole would have been a hand or eye or foot"
        - **Plato** (from pre-existent matter): makes God a craftsman, "weak", like a carpenter who needs wood
        - **Gnostics** (another creator): refuted from "He which made them at the beginning" (Matt 19:4)
    - **Ch. 4**: the reason for starting with creation: "our own transgression evoked the Word's love for human beings".
- Man made **in the image**, given a share in the Word's own rationality, so able to stay incorrupt *if* he stays in communion with God.
- Turning from God (source of being) = turning back toward **non-being**. Result: **corruption and death**.
    - **Ch. 5**: corruption grows past its natural power, with a Romans 1 catalogue of the world's state.
- Important: death here isn't mainly a sentence handed down. It's a slide back into the nothing we came from. That shapes the whole argument.

## 3. The Divine Dilemma (6–10)

Two things that both have to hold:

1. **God's truthfulness.** He said transgression brings death (Gen 2:17). He can't just take it back.
2. **God's goodness.** It's unfitting that creatures made in His image should perish and His work come to nothing.

Could repentance alone fix it? No:

- Repentance stops more sinning but doesn't undo **corruption**, which is now part of man's condition.
- What's needed is **re-creation**, not only pardon. Only the Creator can re-create.

(This is the step that separates Athanasius from a purely legal picture. If the problem were only guilt, forgiveness would do.)

## 4. The Word Takes a Body

- The Word is bodiless and immortal, so He **takes a body** from the Virgin in order to have something that can die.
- He offers it to death **for all**. Because it's the Word's body, death can't hold it. Death is used up in Him.
- United to Him, human nature is clothed in **incorruption**.
- His picture: a king enters a city and lives in one house. The whole city is honoured and enemies leave it alone. So the Word living in one human body changes things for the whole race.

## 5. Second Reason: Knowledge of God (11–18)

- People had lost the knowledge of God and gone after idols, despite the image in them, creation, the Law, the prophets.
    - **Ch. 12**: the backups God gave: the image itself, then creation, then the law and prophets, "a sacred school of the knowledge of God" for the whole world, not just the Jews.
- **Ch. 13**: why not men or angels to renew the image? Men are only "in the image"; angels aren't images at all. A human king sends letters and friends, and if need be comes himself.
- His picture: a portrait on wood gets ruined. The sitter has to come and sit again so it can be redone on the same panel. So the **Image of the Father** comes Himself to renew those made after the image.
- People were looking for God among visible things, so the Word became visible and did works they could see.
- **Ch. 17**: the Word in the body isn't **contained** by it. A soul can think of the sun but can't move it; the Word, while in a body, was still moving the universe and "at rest in the Father alone". Like the sun touching mud, He isn't defiled but purifies.
- **Ch. 18**: why Scripture says He ate, drank and was born: the body was really His, "that he might be shown possessing a real and not illusory body".

## 6. Why the Cross, Why in Public (19–25)

- **Public**: so the death is certain and witnessed, and the Resurrection can't be waved away.
- **Not from illness, not self-chosen**: He takes the death His enemies pick, to show He beats death in any form.
    - **Ch. 22**: He accepted the death that others gave because "it was not his own death that the Savior came to complete, but that of human beings".
    - **Ch. 24**: like a wrestler who lets the hostile spectators choose his opponent. He wasn't beheaded or sawn apart, so the body stayed whole and gave no excuse to divide the Church.
- **Why a cross**:
    - bears the curse (Deut 21:23; Gal 3:13)
    - arms stretched out, drawing Jews and Gentiles both (John 12:32)
    - lifted into the air, overthrows "the prince of the power of the air" (Eph 2:2)
- **Third day**: long enough to prove He really died, short enough that the body saw no corruption and the witnesses were still around.

## 7. Proofs of the Resurrection (26–32)

He argues from what can be seen *now*:

- Christians, women and children included, **despise death** and go to martyrdom. Death has lost its terror.
- **Idols and oracles are failing**. Demons are driven out by Christ's name and the sign of the Cross.
- A dead man doesn't do this. Christ is acting daily, so He's alive.

Interesting that this is an argument from effects, not from the empty tomb. Would it land the same way today?

## 8. Against the Jews and the Gentiles (33–55)

- **Ch. 33–40**: against the Jews from prophecy: Isa 7:14; Daniel's **seventy weeks** (Dan 9:24-25). Since Christ, "Jerusalem no longer stands, nor does a prophet arise": the "holy of holies" has come.
- **Ch. 41–43**: against the Greeks: their own philosophers call the cosmos a great body indwelt by the Word, so why is a human body absurd? Plato says the Maker took the helm when the world was sinking. Why not appear through the sun or stars? Because "the Lord came not to be put on display but to heal and to teach".
- **Ch. 44**: why not save by a mere nod? Creation was from nothing; healing is of something that exists and is corrupt from inside. Life had to be woven into the body: straw wrapped in asbestos.
- **Ch. 50–52**: barbarians who couldn't go an hour without a sword "turn to farming instead of war" (Isa 2:4).

## 9. The Line Everyone Quotes

> "He was made man that we might be made God." (ch. 54)

- **Theosis.** Not becoming God by nature. Partaking of the divine life by grace (2 Pet 1:4).
- So salvation is more than acquittal. It's the healing and deifying of human nature through union with the incarnate Word.
- **Ch. 54**: counting His achievements is like counting the waves of the sea.

## 10. Reading Scripture (56–57)

- **Ch. 57**: to understand Scripture you need "a good life and a pure soul". As to see the sun you must clean your eyes, so to understand the saints you must imitate their lives.

## 11. Why It Matters

- **Against Arius** (later, but the logic is already here): if the Son is a creature He can't re-create or deify. Only God gives a share in God's life.
- **Against a merely legal view**: the problem is corruption and death. The cure is Incarnation + death + Resurrection together.
- Stands next to St. Irenaeus on recapitulation as a base text for Eastern soteriology.
