## 1. The Old Testament

- Even **Gerhard May** (who argues *ex nihilo* was a 2nd-century invention) admits it "corresponds factually with the OT proclamation". Copan: the OT **demands** it, even if "out of nothing" is implicit.
- For Israel creation "did not need to be defended": "there was simply no alternative": it was the presupposition of their thought.

**Genesis 1:1-2**
- "**In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.** And the earth was **without form, and void**; and darkness was upon the face of the deep."
- Four readings: (1)–(3) various **dependent-clause** readings ("When God began to create... the earth was formless", as in the NEB 1970), where v.2 describes something already there; (4) v.1 a **main clause**, the absolute beginning and first act of creation, with v.2 describing the unformed state God made. Copan defends (4):
    - "in beginning" without the article doesn't require a construct ("in the beginning of"): many absolute time expressions lack it
    - Gen 2:1 ("Thus the heavens and the earth were finished") already summarises 1:2-31, so 2:4a begins a new section
    - Cassuto: the syntax of v.2 ("**And the earth** was...") "proves" it begins a new subject; v.1 is "an independent sentence... a formal introduction to the entire section"
    - the Septuagint reads it this way
- **"Heaven and earth"** is a **merism**: everything. Wenham: "In the beginning God created **everything**": it expresses **totality**, not order.
- Against Barbour's "creation of order from chaos".
- **"Double creation"**: first God creates the primordial substance from "total and absolute nothing", then forms it. James Barr thinks this makes God "sloppy"; Copan defends it. *Tohu* = an earth that can't (yet) be inhabited.

***Bara'*** ("create")
- In the OT, God alone is ever its subject, no material is ever mentioned, and it's tied to God's powerful **word**. "To create" fits better than "to make" or "to fashion". Not proof alone, but suggestive.
- Some scholars (and Jaki, who mocks exegetes for raising it "to a quasi-divine pedestal") use its etymology ("cut") to suggest shaping. But etymology isn't meaning: "nice" comes from Latin *nescius*, "ignorant".

**Elsewhere**
- Ps 33:6, 9: "**By the word of the LORD were the heavens made**... For he spake, and it was done."
- Ps 90:2: "Before the mountains were brought forth, or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world, even **from everlasting to everlasting, thou art God**." God before the world.
- Ps 146:6: God "made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them".
- Prov 8:22-26: Wisdom was there "**before** the earth was... when there were no depths", before "the first dust of the world".
- Isa 44:24: "I am the LORD that **maketh all things**; that stretcheth forth the heavens **alone**."
- Isa 45:7: "I form the light, and create darkness."

## 2. The New Testament

- John 1:3: "**All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.**" *Panta* without the article may mean "everything as a totality".
- Rom 4:17: "God, who quickeneth the dead, and **calleth those things which be not as though they were**." James Dunn calls it an undisputed "**theological axiom**": nothing preconditions God's activity: creation, resurrection, or a child to an elderly infertile couple (Abraham and Sarah).
- Heb 11:3: "the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that **things which are seen were not made of things which do appear**." *Mē ek phainomenōn*: William Lane: this "would seem to exclude any influence from Platonic cosmology".
- Col 1:16-17: "by him were **all things created**, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible... **he is before all things**, and by him all things consist."
- Rev 4:11: "thou hast created all things, and **for thy pleasure they are and were created**."
- 1 Cor 8:6: "one God, the Father, **of whom are all things**." Christ shares God's identity as **Creator**; the Resurrection is "the eighth day of creation", infusing creation with hope.
- *Ktisis* ("creation") means "the sum total of everything created" (BDAG). Moltmann: *ex nihilo* is "unquestionably an apt paraphrase" of what Scripture means by creation.

## 3. Extra-Biblical Witness

- Not a novel 2nd-century idea but a "continuous line of thought" in Jewish and Christian writers. Most held a **two-stage** creation (substrate first, then ordering); **all** denied that matter coexisted with God eternally.

**Jewish**
- **2 Macc 7:28**: "look upon the heaven and the earth... and consider that **God made them of things that were not**." The earliest clear statement (2nd c. BC); Jaki: "the first biblical appearance of the phrase".
- **Wisdom 11:17** ("out of formless matter") looks like pre-existing matter, but is about the second stage.
- **2 Enoch** (Jewish, Egypt): God created even "the void". **3 Maccabees**: God "first Father of all".
- **Philo**: a two-stage creation (first the intelligible world of Forms, then the sensible); "God is one" against polytheism; "eternity means deity", so nothing else is eternal. Christians adopted these principles, with the Logos/Wisdom as eternal with God.
- **Rashi**: Gen 1:1 doesn't give the order of creation.

**Christian**
- **Shepherd of Hermas**: God "made out of what did not exist the things that exist".
- **The Apologists**: God is "in need of nothing" (*adeētos*): no God–matter dualism, no intermediaries (against Middle Platonism). **Aristides**: imperishable Creator, corruptible creature; God first made the elements, then the cosmos.
- **Justin and Athenagoras**: Torchia: their teaching "clearly challenges any claim that these writers accepted a material substrate eternally coexisting with God".
- **Tatian**: May calls him "the first" to say matter was produced by God; Copan: continuity with his teacher Justin, not a break.
- **Theophilus of Antioch** and **Irenaeus**: explicitly against Plato's pre-existing matter. If matter were uncreated, it would be equal to God. Irenaeus: "God... created matter itself."
    - Theophilus: "out of things that are not He creates... whatever He pleases"; God is "without beginning because He is unbegotten". On the first day God made from nothing; on the other days He moulded what He'd made.
- **Basil** (*Hexaemeron* 2.4): "If matter were uncreated, then it would from the very first be of a rank equal to that of God and would deserve the same veneration."
- Against May's thesis (*ex nihilo* invented in the 2nd c. against Gnostics): Copan argues the 2nd c. made explicit what was already assumed. See [[The Forms and the Demiurge]].

## 4. What It Means

- Not that "nothing" is a stuff God used. It means God created **without any material cause**; everything other than God depends on Him for its existence.
- Includes a **temporal beginning** (the world isn't eternal) and **ongoing dependence** (conservation).
- Distinct from Aquinas, who held creation ex nihilo was compatible in principle with an eternal world (known by faith that it had a beginning); his second and third ways argue for a first cause even **assuming** an eternal world: creation as dependence. Craig argues for the beginning philosophically.
- **Creation vs conservation**: Philip Quinn treats conservation as continuous re-creation. Craig: there's an intuitive distinction, which Quinn obscures, and continual re-creation threatens identity over time.
- **Time**: on the **A-theory** (tensed facts are real), the universe's beginning is a real coming-into-being; McTaggart showed an A-theorist must be a **presentist**. See [[Presentism vs Eternalism]].
    - For B-theorists and Platonists, they offer a third kind of dependence, "*creatio stans*" (static creation), alongside *creatio originans* and *creatio continuans*.

## 5. Abstract Objects

- **Platonists** (realists) hold that abstract objects (numbers, sets, propositions, properties) really exist, necessarily and uncreated. Then not "all things" were made by God: a problem for John 1:3.
- If they exist, they seem **metaphysically necessary**: a challenge to aseity. And the realm is huge: the set-theoretic hierarchy (0 = ∅, 1 = {∅}, 2 = {∅,{∅}}... up to ω, the first infinite ordinal).
- Options:
    - **Absolute creationism**: God creates even abstract objects. **Leftow**: God is identical with His essence. Craig: that's question-begging.
    - **Divine conceptualism** (Menzel and Morris): they're God's thoughts; mathematical objects are "God's collectings and concomitant concepts".
    - **Anti-realism / nominalism**: they don't exist. **Fictionalism** (Hartry Field, Balaguer): mathematical sentences are literally false or truth-valueless, useful fictions. The theist attributes the fit between maths and the world ("homomorphic structuring") to God's design.
- **Quine's criterion** (ontological commitment read off the existential quantifier "∃") rejected: his ontology also treats past, present and future as equally real, incompatible with creation from nothing. Logicians distinguish objectual vs substitutional readings of "∃".
- Craig later wrote *God Over All* on this. See [[The Problem of Universals: Realism vs Nominalism]].

## 6. Philosophical Arguments (Craig)

Christians had to answer Aristotle's **arguments** for an eternal world, not just cite texts. The **kalam** arguments against an infinite past:

1. **An actual infinite can't exist**. An actual infinite = a collection with more definite, discrete members than any natural number; in set theory, a set equivalent to one of its proper subsets.
    - **Hilbert's Hotel**: full with infinitely many guests, it can take infinitely more without anyone leaving; check out all the odd-numbered guests and infinitely many remain; check out all but three and three remain. "Not according to the mathematicians, but just ask the housekeeping staff!" Subtracting identical quantities (ℵ0 − ℵ0) gives contradictory results.
    - So an infinite series of past events can't exist.
    - "Can't God be infinite?" Divine infinity means necessity, perfection, omnipotence etc., not an infinite **number** of things.
2. **An actual infinite can't be formed by successive addition**: you can't count to infinity; so the past, formed one event after another, can't be infinite. Otherwise today could never arrive.
    - **Tristram Shandy** (Russell): he writes his autobiography so slowly it takes a year per day; with infinite past time he'd have finished... yet he'd also be infinitely far behind. "It would have happened already."

So the universe began to exist; whatever begins to exist has a cause; so the universe has a cause. (A B-theorist would reject this, but also wouldn't hold *creatio ex nihilo* anyway.)

## 7. Scientific Evidence (Craig)

- **Big Bang cosmology**: the universe expanding from an initial singularity, about 13.8 billion years ago. Space and time themselves begin.
    - Wheeler: was there ever a greater prediction than the expansion of the universe?
    - **Friedman–Lemaître** standard model: a universe not eternal in the past. There's no "outside" to the expansion and no "before".
    - Hawking: "almost everyone now believes that the universe, and time itself, had a beginning at the big bang".
    - The real question: why the universe exists rather than nothing.
- **Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem** (2003): any universe that has on average been expanding has a past boundary, including inflationary and multiverse models. Vilenkin: "the cyclic universe is past-incomplete"; it extends to brane cosmology. An initial singularity isn't eliminated.
- **Second law of thermodynamics**: if the universe were infinitely old, it would already be at heat death (equilibrium). It isn't.
    - **Penrose / Weyl curvature**: the initial state was extraordinarily low-entropy (Bekenstein–Hawking entropy); without a singularity, the origin of the second law is unexplained.
    - Current composition: about 4% ordinary matter, 23% dark matter, **73% dark energy**: an accelerating universe that won't recollapse.

## 8. Naturalistic Alternatives

| Model | Problem |
|---|---|
| steady state | refuted by the cosmic microwave background |
| oscillating universe | entropy builds up each cycle; no known mechanism for a bounce. Zeldovich and Novikov: "an infinite future, but only a finite past". Joseph Silk: no more than about **100** previous oscillations, given current entropy |
| vacuum fluctuation | the vacuum is not nothing; and it should produce universes everywhere (Isham calls this "fairly lethal"). Avoiding it means the vacuum itself began to expand: back to an absolute origin |
| chaotic inflation | still needs a beginning (BGV) |
| quantum gravity (Hartle–Hawking) | removes a beginning only by using **imaginary time**, which hides the real difference; the real-time interpretation still has a beginning |
| time loop (Gott and Li-Xin) | "the laws of physics may allow the universe to be its own mother": Craig leaves it to physicists, but notes it's a strange explanation |

**Philosophical objections**
- **Grünbaum**: the inference to a first cause assumes "the spontaneity of nothingness". Craig: it rests on an **A-theory** of time, not on that.
- **Atkins**: "not something for nothing after all, but nothing for nothing": commits him to the absurdity that nothing exists, not even himself.
- **Virtual particles** don't come from nothing; they come from the quantum vacuum.
- **Post**: "by definition the universe contains everything there is", so it can't have a cause: begs the question.
- **"No definition of causality fits God causing the Big Bang"**: then either some undiscovered definition is right or cause is indefinable; neither shows God can't cause. Lewis's counterfactual analysis gives only that c and e are "causally related", not the direction.
- God "sans the universe" is timeless: time begins with the first event. The cause of the Big Bang is **God himself**, not an event of God's willing; His will is necessarily successful.
- It's more plausible to deny a premise of these objections than to affirm what Hume called "the absurd Proposition" that something might arise without a cause.
