**Natural theology** = reasoning about whether God exists and what God is like *without appealing to revelation or Scripture*. Contrast **revealed theology**, which starts from them. This is the frame for the individual proofs: [[The Unmoved Mover]] (Aristotelian), [[The Problem of the One and the Many]] sec. 6 (Neo-Platonic), [[Philosophy of Logic: What Grounds the Laws of Logic]] sec. 6 (Augustinian), [[The Leibnizian Cosmological Argument]], [[The Ontological Argument]].

Taliaferro's chapter is the introduction to the Blackwell volume. Read the objections carefully, the replies partly. Sections 5–7 are mine.

## 1. What Counts

- Plato and Aristotle did it (see [[The Good in Plato]], [[The Unmoved Mover]]). So did Anselm, Aquinas, Scotus, and Muslim philosophers, especially Avicenna on necessary and contingent being.
- Early modern philosophy is full of it: Descartes, Locke, Berkeley, Leibniz.
- **Hume's *Dialogues* and Kant's first *Critique* are natural theology too.** They argue about God without revelation. They just reach negative results.
- It doesn't have to be Christian or theist. Voltaire: God yes, revelation no. Spinoza: God = nature. Process theology. So natural theology by itself underdetermines *which* God.

## 2. Five Objections to the Whole Project

| # | Objection | From |
|---|---|---|
| I | **No logical space.** You can't have a theory of "everything" or of what's beyond the world. "Everything" isn't the name of one big thing | D. Z. Phillips |
| II | **Not a real explanation.** "God did it" gives no mechanism. Real explanations are scientific | Narveson, Bagger |
| III | **Anthropomorphism.** Why make human thought, "this little agitation of the brain", the model for the cause of the universe? And a bodiless agent that acts, hears, knows is incoherent | Hume; Rundle |
| IV | **Uniqueness.** We infer causes from repeated experience. We've seen one universe. So no inference to its cause | Hume |
| V | **Not enough.** Even if the arguments work they give an architect, not the omnipotent, good God of religion | Hume, Kant |

Kant on design: it gets you at most "a highest architect of the world", limited by his material, "not a creator".

V is Pruss's **Gap Problem**. Feser's Stage 2 arguments are aimed at it.

## 3. Taliaferro's Replies

**The strategy: start with mind.** I, II and III all assume physicalism: that "knowing", "intending", "acting" only make sense for bodies. So attack that first.

- Consciousness isn't something we postulate to explain data. It's what has the data. Chalmers against Dennett: experience is a thing to be explained, not a theory. It's "antecedent to, and a presupposition of, science and philosophy".
- If intentional explanation is real for *us* ("she raised her arm because she wanted to vote"), it's a legitimate kind of explanation, and it isn't a mechanism. Then explaining the cosmos by the intention of a non-physical agent is the same *kind* of explanation, not a non-explanation. Answers II.
- If mind isn't reducible to body in our case, a bodiless mind isn't obviously incoherent. Answers III (Rundle).
- This is why the Blackwell volume has chapters on consciousness and on reason. See [[Mind and Body: Dualism, Materialism, Emergence]].

**On I.** Naturalism is also a theory of everything ("everything is physical"). If such theories are impossible, naturalism goes too.

**On IV.** Cosmologists reason about the one universe all the time, comparing it with ways it might have been. Uniqueness doesn't block inference. (Hume's rule would also forbid the Big Bang.)

**On V.** True of any single argument. Hence:

**Cumulative case.** Several independent lines of argument can support each other.

- Oppy's warning: a pile of bad arguments is still bad, and if one is good you don't need the pile.
- Taliaferro: that's right for deductive proofs. But independent *evidence* does add up, as in a court case. The moral argument and the ontological argument, say, can each make the other's premises more plausible.

**Humility.** Kenny says agnosticism is the humble position: the theist claims knowledge, the agnostic only ignorance. Taliaferro: the agnostic also claims something (that everyone else should suspend judgement). Real intellectual humility is a "philosophical golden rule": treat other people's philosophies as you'd want yours treated, and consider the rival view at its strongest.

## 4. Objections from Inside Christianity

Taliaferro mostly argues with naturalists. But the sharper opposition to natural theology has been theological.

| Who | Objection |
|---|---|
| **Tertullian** | "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?" |
| **Pascal** | "God of Abraham, God of Isaac, God of Jacob, not of the philosophers and scholars." The proofs are remote and don't move anyone |
| **Luther** | reason is fallen; theology of glory vs theology of the cross |
| **Barth** | "Nein!" to Brunner, 1934. God is known only where He reveals Himself, in Christ. Natural theology is man making a God in his own image |
| **Kierkegaard** | proving God's existence to someone is an insult, like proving to a man that he exists |
| **Plantinga** ("Reformed epistemology") | belief in God is **properly basic**. It doesn't need arguments to be rational, any more than belief in other minds does. Arguments are optional |
| **Van Til** (presuppositionalism) | the proofs treat God as a hypothesis to be tested by a neutral reason. There's no neutral reason. Hence [[TAG]] |

On the other side: **Vatican I** (1870) defined that God "can be known with certainty from created things by the natural light of human reason". So for Roman Catholics natural theology isn't optional. That's part of why Feser writes as he does.

## 5. What Scripture Says

- **Romans 1:19-20**: "that which may be known of God is manifest in them... For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse."
- **Psalm 19:1**: "The heavens declare the glory of God."
- **Wisdom 13:1-9**: from the greatness and beauty of created things their maker is seen by analogy. And it blames those who didn't make the inference.
- **Acts 17:22-31**: Paul on the Areopagus starts from the altar to the unknown god and quotes Greek poets, *then* preaches the resurrection.
- **Acts 14:17**: God "left not himself without witness".
- But **1 Corinthians 1:21**: "the world by wisdom knew not God." And **1 Cor 2:14**: the natural man doesn't receive the things of the Spirit.

So: creation really does show God, people really are responsible for seeing it, and in practice unaided reason has not arrived. Both halves.

## 6. Where the Fathers Stand

*Mine. From memory, needs checking against texts.*

- **They use the arguments.** St. Athanasius, *Against the Heathen*: the order and harmony of opposites in the cosmos shows one Word ruling it. St. Gregory the Theologian, *Oration 28*: a lyre implies a lyre-maker. St. John of Damascus opens the *Exact Exposition* (I.3) with a proof from change and contingency, then one from the order of the world. St. Basil's *Hexaemeron* reads creation as a school.
- **But they limit what the arguments give.** The same *Oration 28*: reason shows *that* God is, not *what* He is. God's essence is beyond every concept. St. John of Damascus: "that God is, is clear; but what He is by essence and nature is entirely incomprehensible."
- **And knowledge of God isn't mainly inference.** It's communion: purification, illumination, theosis. The proofs are at best for the porch. See [[Nous]], [[The Jesus Prayer and Hesychasm]].
- **Barlaam and Palamas.** Barlaam said syllogisms can't demonstrate anything about God, so we know Him only indirectly. Palamas disagreed *both* ways: demonstration about God is possible, and beyond it there is direct knowledge in the uncreated light. So the Orthodox position isn't anti-rational, and isn't rationalist.
- **Natural contemplation** (*theoria physike*) in St. Maximus: seeing the *logoi* of things and through them the Logos. That's a kind of natural theology, but done by a purified *nous*, not by neutral argument. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].

So the patristic view has three levels:

1. creation testifies, and reason can see *that* God is (Rom 1)
2. what God is in Himself stays unknown (apophatic)
3. real knowledge of God is participation, by grace, in the Church

Natural theology in the modern sense lives entirely on level 1. The mistake is treating it as the whole, or as the foundation the rest is built on.

## 7. Classical Proofs and TAG

The existing note [[Why Is the Transcendental Argument Prior to the Teleological, Cosmological, and Other Arguments]] argues TAG comes first. Having now gone through several of the classical proofs, here's how I'd put the relation.

**The presuppositional complaint**

- Classical proofs begin from premises a naturalist is supposed to share (change, contingency, order). But there's no neutral ground: how you read those "facts" depends on your worldview. See [[Prior Assumptions and Presuppositions]].
- They conclude with *a* first cause, probably, not the Triune God, certainly (objection V).
- Each uses causality, logic, the PSR. TAG asks what grounds *those*.

**What I think is right in it**

- V is real. None of the five arguments I've looked at gets beyond a single, simple, intelligent necessary being.
- The premises do carry metaphysics. Feser's proof needs act/potency. Pruss's needs the PSR. Maydole's needs S5. An opponent can decline each.
- Feser's Stage 2 pushes toward absolute simplicity, and that's a theological cost, not a neutral result. See [[The Unmoved Mover]] sec. 7.

**What I think is overstated**

- "No neutral ground" can't mean "no shared ground", or TAG couldn't be argued either. The Fathers did argue from creation.
- The classical proofs aren't all "evidence for a hypothesis". Feser's and Pruss's are deductions from what any change or any contingent fact requires. That's already close to transcendental: "what must be the case for there to be change at all?"
- Several classical defences *are* transcendental arguments in miniature: Aristotle on non-contradiction, the PSR ("deny it and you can't trust reasoning"), the Augustinian proof. See [[Aristotle's Early Logic and the Thirteen Fallacies]] sec. 5.
- And TAG has its own gap: from "knowledge needs a ground" to "that ground is the Trinity as the Orthodox Church confesses it". See [[The Problem of the One and the Many]] sec. 8.

**Working conclusion**

- Not rivals. The classical proofs show that particular features of the world (change, composition, contingency, universals) lead to God. TAG shows that the tools used in any argument at all do.
- Neither replaces revelation. Both belong to level 1.
- Use: clearing away the claim that belief is irrational. Not producing faith. "No one can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost" (1 Cor 12:3).
