Leibniz's question: why is there something rather than nothing? His answer: because there's a necessary being that explains everything contingent. Pruss's chapter is 75 pages and very technical. I read the introduction, the core argument and the conclusion, and skimmed the long middle (his defences of the PSR and a section on theories of modality). So this is the skeleton.

## 1. Four Problems Every Cosmological Argument Faces

Pruss's own way of organising the topic, and the most useful thing in the chapter.

| Problem | What it is | Name from |
|---|---|---|
| **Glendower** | things "call for" an explanation, but is there one? | Shakespeare: "I can call spirits from the vasty deep." "But will they come when you do call for them?" |
| **Regress** | why not an infinite chain of causes, each explained by the one before? | Hume |
| **Taxicab** | you use the explanatory principle to reach God, then dismiss it. "What caused God?" | Schopenhauer: the PSR isn't a cab you can send away when you've arrived |
| **Gap** | granted a first cause, why think it's God? | (Richard Gale's term) |

- On the Gap: Aquinas's "and this all understand to be God" isn't papering over it. The next sections of the *Summa* argue for the attributes one by one.
- Pruss thinks the Gap is where the most work is left to do.

## 2. Three Families

Sorted by how they handle Glendower and Regress:

| | Principle | How it stops the regress |
|---|---|---|
| **Kalam** | whatever *begins* has a cause | argues the past is finite, so no infinite regress happened |
| **Thomistic** | every change / contingent thing has a cause | allows an infinite past, but argues a certain kind of causal series (intermediate causes depending on a non-intermediate one) must have a first member |
| **Leibnizian** | every contingent fact has an explanation (PSR) | an infinite chain doesn't explain why *the whole chain* is there |

- Feser's Aristotelian proof is the Thomistic kind. See [[The Unmoved Mover]] sec. 6.
- Only the kalam needs the universe to have a beginning. The other two work for an eternal world.

## 3. The Basic Argument

1. Every contingent fact has an explanation. (**PSR**, restricted to contingent facts.)
2. There is a contingent fact that includes all other contingent facts. (The **BCCF**, "big conjunctive contingent fact".)
3. So that fact has an explanation.
4. The explanation must involve a **necessary being**.
5. The necessary being is God.

Why 4:

- Call the BCCF *p* and its explanation *q*. Is *q* contingent or necessary?
- If contingent, *q* is part of *p*, so *q* would be explaining itself. Nothing contingent causes its own existence.
- Scientific explanation (laws + initial conditions) won't do: the laws and conditions are contingent, so they're inside *p*.
- Conceptual explanation ("the knife is hot because its molecules move fast") only re-describes one contingent fact by another.
- What's left: the causal activity of something that exists **necessarily**.

Why the regress doesn't help: suppose an infinite series of contingent things, each caused by the last. Every member is explained. But "why is there this series and not another, or none?" is a further contingent fact, and nothing in the series answers it. Leibniz's example was an eternal series of geometry books, each copied from the one before. You still haven't explained why there are geometry books.

## 4. The PSR

**For it**

- We assume it all the time. If a brick appears on the table we don't consider "no reason".
- Science assumes it: the alternative to finding an explanation is never "there isn't one".
- Without it, any event at all could be a brute fact, and then we couldn't trust inference to the best explanation, or even perception (my seeming to see a tree could be a brute fact).
- Pruss gives several more arguments, including from modality. (Skimmed.)

**Against it**

- **Modal collapse** (van Inwagen): if the BCCF has an explanation *q*, and *q* is necessary, and explanations entail what they explain, then the BCCF is necessary. So nothing is contingent. No free will, no chance.
    - Reply: deny that explaining = entailing. A free choice can explain an action without necessitating it. God's free decision explains the world. He could have done otherwise.
    - This is the pressure point for theology. See below.
- **Quantum mechanics**: uncaused events? Reply: indeterministic isn't unexplained. The laws and the prior state explain why decay *can* happen and with what probability.
- **Hume**: I can conceive of something beginning without a cause. Reply: conceivable isn't possible. See Feser on this in [[The Unmoved Mover]] sec. 6.
- **Brute fact**: "The universe is just there, and that's all" (Russell). Not an argument against the PSR, a refusal of it. But a coherent refusal.

**Weaker principles that still work.** If the full PSR is too strong, Pruss shows the argument runs on less:

- a causal principle only for "wholly contingent positive states of affairs" (Koons)
- a causal principle for chains as well as single things
- the *possibility* of explanation: every contingent fact **possibly** has an explanation (Gale and Pruss). With S5 this turns out to be enough.
- "if there's only one possible explanation, it's the true one" (Sherlock Holmes)

## 5. The Taxicab

- "Then what explains God?"
- The PSR was restricted to **contingent** facts. A necessary being's existence is explained, if "explained" is the word, by the necessity of its own nature.
- Not an ad hoc exemption: the whole point of reaching a necessary being is that it's a different kind of thing from what needed explaining.
- Same answer Feser gives: the premise was never "everything has a cause".

## 6. The Gap

Routes from "necessary first cause" to "God":

- **Inductive**: what sort of necessary being would produce a universe with order, minds, value? Brings in fine-tuning etc.
- **Deductive**: Aquinas's way. The first cause has no essence/existence composition, so is simple, so one, immaterial, perfect... And "the perfections of the effect must be found in the cause", so it has intellect.
- Pruss thinks this second route goes "through exciting metaphysical territory" and is under-explored.

## 7. Feser's Rationalist Proof

*Five Proofs*, ch. 5. I've only seen the chapter list and how he describes it, not read it. From that: same structure (PSR → contingent things need an explanation outside themselves → a necessary being), then he derives the attributes by showing the necessary being must be purely actual and simple, which hooks it into his other four proofs. So his five proofs share one Stage 2. Add detail when read.

## 8. From the Orthodox Side

*Mine.*

- **Creation from nothing fits.** That everything other than God is contingent and depends on Him for being is exactly the doctrine. St. John of Damascus opens the *Exact Exposition* with an argument of this type: all things are created or uncreated; what is created is changeable; the changeable was brought into being by something; you can't go on forever; so there's an uncreated maker. So the Fathers did use cosmological reasoning.
- **Modal collapse is the Orthodox objection to Thomism in another form.** If God is absolutely simple and His will = His essence = His act of creating, then creation looks as necessary as God. The Leibnizian needs God's creating to be *free*, which needs a real distinction between what God necessarily is and what He freely does. That's essence and energies. See [[Thomism Leads to Eternal Creation]], [[Essence-Energies Distinction]].
- So this argument, of all the classical ones, sits best with Orthodox theology, *provided* the necessary being's act of creating is not identified with its nature. Pruss's own reply to van Inwagen (explanation without entailment) is a philosopher asking for the same thing.
- **Leibniz himself got this wrong**, arguably: he held God *must* create the best possible world, which is collapse by another road.
- **Contingency of the logoi.** God necessarily exists, and freely wills this world and its *logoi*. See [[The Logoi of Creation in St. Maximus]].
- **Limits.** It gets to a necessary cause, and with work to one, intelligent, good. Not to the Trinity, not to the Incarnation. See [[Natural Theology: Is It Legitimate]].
- **Relation to TAG.** The PSR defence "without it you can't trust reasoning at all" is a transcendental argument sitting inside a classical one. See [[TAG]].
