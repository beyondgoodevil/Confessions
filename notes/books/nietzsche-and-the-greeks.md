Nietzsche was a professor of classical philology before he was a philosopher, and the Greeks stay at the centre of his thought.

## 1. Nietzsche the Philologist

- Philology, for Nietzsche, is the **art of reading well**: slowly, with caution, without forcing the text to say what one wants.
- He describes it as *ephexis* in interpretation, a holding back of judgement.
- This discipline is not left behind when he turns to philosophy. It becomes his standard of intellectual honesty, and his charge against theologians and metaphysicians is that they read badly.

## 2. Human and Humane

- Modern classicism praised the Greeks as serene and "humane".
- Nietzsche sees a people who knew cruelty, envy and strife, and who gave these a place (the contest, *agon*) instead of denying them.
- Their greatness was in what they made of human nature as it is, not in being gentle.

## 3. Homer

- Homer is Nietzsche's **counter-ideal** to Christian morality: "Plato versus Homer" is his name for the whole opposition.
- The Homeric gods are a way of affirming life. The Greeks used their gods to keep **bad conscience** at a distance: the gods took on the guilt, not only the punishment.
- Homeric values are noble: strength, honour, beauty, good fortune.

## 4. Heraclitus

- Nietzsche admires Heraclitus more than any other early philosopher, first of all for his **character**: solitary, proud, independent.
- He also admires the teaching: becoming, the unity of opposites, and above all the **law and measure** (*logos*) that governs change.
- Berry argues against the common view that Nietzsche took from Heraclitus a doctrine of radical flux in which nothing has any stable identity:
    - The river fragments, read carefully, speak of sameness through change.
    - The extreme flux doctrine belongs to later reports and to nineteenth-century handbooks (Zeller), not securely to Heraclitus.
    - What Nietzsche stresses is order within becoming.

## 5. Pyrrho and the Sceptics

Berry's main proposal is that the Greek school closest to Nietzsche's own practice is **Pyrrhonian scepticism**.

- **Suspension of judgement** (*epochē*): where arguments balance, withhold assent.
- **Anti-dogmatism**: the target is not this or that belief but the habit of claiming to know how things really are.
- **Therapeutic aim**: the sceptic seeks health of soul, a freedom from disturbance (*ataraxia*), not a new theory.
- Nietzsche's praise of the sceptics as the only honest type among philosophers, and his use of opposed perspectives, fit this model.

## 6. Not Imitation

- Nietzsche does not propose a return to Greece.
- The Greeks are a measure and a provocation: the task is to **surpass** them by action, not to copy them.

## Summary

- From philology Nietzsche keeps a rule of honest reading.
- From Homer he takes an ideal opposed to Christian morality; from Heraclitus, becoming under law.
- Berry's distinctive claim is that Pyrrhonian scepticism best explains his method.
