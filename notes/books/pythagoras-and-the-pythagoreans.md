Pythagoras (c. 570–495 BC) left no writings, and almost everything about him comes from much later lives by **Porphyry** and **Iamblichus** (3rd–4th c. AD), who made him a divine sage. So separating history from legend is hard. The *Sourcebook* collects those lives plus the fragments of later Pythagoreans (Philolaus, Archytas, the *Golden Verses*).

## 1. Life and Legend

From Iamblichus's *Life* (the first part of the *Sourcebook*):
- Born on **Samos**. Studied with Thales and Anaximander; travelled to **Egypt** (22 years in temples, it's said) and **Babylon**.
    - On the way to Egypt he sailed to Sidon (his "native country" in one tradition) (the *Lives*).
- Settled at **Croton** in southern Italy, founded a community, and had great political influence until a revolt burned the meeting house.
    - He taught in his fifties; the goal was discovering "the Divine within oneself" (Godwin's foreword).
    - The **end of the community**: the Cylonians set fire to Milo's house where the Pythagoreans had met.
- Legends: a golden thigh; seen in two cities at once; tamed a bear by speaking to it; remembered his past lives; heard the music of the spheres.
    - **Zalmoxis**, his Thracian slave, named from the bearskin (*zalmon*) he was born in.
- He called himself **philosophos**, "lover of wisdom", rather than *sophos*, "wise": only God is wise. The first use of the word, according to tradition. Life is like the Olympic games: some come to compete, some to trade, the best to **watch** (*theoria*).
- "Luxury is the first evil that glides into houses and cities, the second insolence, the third destruction."

## 2. The Community

- A shared life: common property among the inner circle, **silence** for five years for novices, memorisation, self-examination each evening (what did I do wrong today? what good did I do? what did I leave undone?).
    - **Morning memory exercise**: no Pythagorean got up before recalling the previous day in order: what he first said, heard, or ordered; the first person he met, the second... (an exercise of memory, alongside the evening self-examination).
- **Initiation tests**: he examined prospective disciples judiciously before admitting them, and graded teaching by level.
- Two groups: ***akousmatikoi*** (hearers, who kept the rules) and ***mathematikoi*** (learners, who studied the reasons).
    - Followers: *Pythagorikoi* (personal disciples) vs *Pythagoristai* (imitators).
- **Rules** (*akousmata*), some strange: abstain from beans; don't stir the fire with a knife; don't step over a crossbar; put on the right shoe first. Later read symbolically.
- **Vegetarianism**, at least for some: tied to kinship of all living things.
- Women admitted: **Theano**, said to be Pythagoras's wife, wrote letters on household virtue.
- *Mine*: the closest thing in Greek philosophy to a monastery. Hadot's point that ancient philosophy was a way of life fits it best of all. See [[Philosophy as a Way of Life]].

## 3. Number Is the Principle

- "All things are **number**" (Aristotle's summary). Things are made of, or imitate, numbers.
    - **Aristotle**'s report: the Pythagoreans "first developed the sciences" and thought the principles of mathematics were the principles of all things.
- **The One** isn't a number but the principle of number. Theon of Smyrna: "Unity is the principle of all things... all things emanate from it and it emanates from nothing." (Fideler)
    - **Iamblichus's titles** for the numbers: the One is called **Apollo** (*a-pollōn*, "not of many").
- Discovered (by legend, in a blacksmith's shop) that **musical intervals** are simple ratios of string lengths:
    - **The blacksmith's discovery**: hearing hammers ring in concord, he found the ratios by hanging weights on strings, then made a "string-stretcher" (*chordotonon*) (the *Lives*).

| Interval | Ratio |
|---|---|
| octave | 2:1 |
| fifth | 3:2 |
| fourth | 4:3 |

- If sound, which seems purely qualitative, is ratio, maybe everything is.
- **The scale**: built by dividing the octave (6:12) by the arithmetic and harmonic means (8 and 9); the whole tone is 8:9, and the leftover semitone 243:256 is the *leimma* ("left over") (Fideler).
    - **Archytas**: three means in music: **arithmetic**, **geometric**, and **harmonic** ("subcontrary"); distinguished physical and psychic time.
    - Overtones: the clarinet's timbre comes from emphasising the odd overtones.
- **The tetractys**: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10, arranged as a triangle. Contains all the musical ratios. Sworn by in the Pythagorean oath. 1 = point, 2 = line, 3 = surface, 4 = solid.
    - 4 = tetrahedron (the first solid) (Fideler).
    - **Theon of Smyrna**: there are **ten** tetraktys: numbers (1,2,3,4), multiplication (1,2,4,8 / 1,3,9,27), magnitudes (point, line, surface, solid), elements (fire, air, water, earth), figures, growth (seed...), societies (man, house, village, city), faculties (thought = 1, science = 2, opinion = 3, sensation = 4), seasons, ages of man.
- **Figurate numbers**: triangular, square, oblong numbers; odd and even.
- The **Pythagorean theorem** was known in Babylon earlier; the proof may be later.
    - **Heath** on Pythagorean maths: Euclid I.35–48 and Book II are Pythagorean.
- **Incommensurables**: the diagonal of a square isn't a ratio of whole numbers (√2). Legend says Hippasus was drowned for revealing it. A crisis for "all is number".
    - Heath: they proved the **irrationality of √2**, which wrecked their numerical theory of proportion for incommensurable magnitudes.

## 4. Limit and Unlimited (Philolaus)

- **Philolaus** (b. c. 474 BC) was the first Pythagorean to write the teachings down (Fideler).
    - His "five bodies" are probably the **Platonic solids** of the *Timaeus*.
- Everything is composed of **limiters** (*peras*) and **unlimiteds** (*apeiron*), fitted together by **harmony**.
- The **table of opposites** (Aristotle, *Metaphysics* A.5): limit/unlimited, odd/even, one/many, right/left, male/female, rest/motion, straight/curved, light/darkness, good/bad, square/oblong.
- Plato takes limit and unlimited into the *Philebus*. See [[The Good in Plato]].

## 5. Harmony of the Spheres

- The planets move at speeds in musical ratios, producing a **harmony** we don't hear because we've heard it from birth.
- Philolaus: the earth isn't the centre; it circles a **central fire** along with a "counter-earth". The first non-geocentric system.
- Pythagoras was the first to call the universe **kosmos** and the earth **round** (Theophrastus credits Parmenides) (the *Lives*).
- The **Nine Muses** = the sounds of the seven planets, the fixed stars and the counter-earth; **Mnemosyne** (Memory) their harmony.
- Plato's *Timaeus* builds the world-soul on Pythagorean ratios; Kepler (*Harmonices Mundi*) still looked for it.
- Music therefore heals: Pythagoras used melodies to calm passions. Music and medicine for the soul.
    - **Alcmaeon of Croton** first defined health as "the harmonious mixture of the qualities", a big influence on Hippocratic medicine (Fideler).

## 6. The Soul

- The soul is **immortal** and passes through other bodies, human and animal: **transmigration** (*metempsychosis*). Xenophanes mocks him for recognising a dead friend's voice in a beaten puppy.
- The body is a prison or tomb (*sōma sēma*) of the soul; life is **purification** to escape the cycle.
    - **Philolaus** opposed **suicide**: "not to lay down the burden but to help others carry theirs."
- Purification by **mathematics** (turning the mind from bodies to eternal ratios), **music**, **diet** and **silence**.
    - Philosophy as **care of the soul**: the first stage of ascent is ethical **purification** (*praxis*). Rather than escaping the world, the goal was to exist in the cosmos in "emotional repose and intellectual acuteness". Because each part is linked to the whole by *harmonia*, every action has repercussions (Fideler).
- **Man is a microcosm**: not because made of the four elements like other animals but because he "contains all the powers of the cosmos" (the *Lives*).

## 7. The Golden Verses

A short later poem (perhaps 4th c. BC – 1st c. AD) summing up Pythagorean ethics:
- honour the gods, then the heroes, then parents
- control appetite, sleep, lust, anger
- examine your day before sleeping (the three questions above)
- "if you leave the body and go to the free aether, you will be a god, immortal, no longer mortal"

Pseudo-Pythagorean treatises on ethics and politics (in the *Sourcebook*'s library) say more:
- virtue = "a mutual adaptation of the irrational part of the soul to the rational"
- the city has three classes (counsellors, auxiliaries, craftsmen); prefer first the beautiful, then the just, then the useful
- the **king** should tune the city "like a lyre", first establishing order in himself; he imitates the supreme God, who is called "father of gods and men" because he's mild to all under him (Sthenidas)
- marriage exists "for the communion of life"
- the world is called *kosmos* because of its order, arranged toward "the most excellent thing, God"

## 8. Influence

- **Plato**: the *Phaedo*, *Gorgias*, *Republic* (myth of Er), *Timaeus*. Plato's late "unwritten doctrines" (the One and the Indefinite Dyad) are very Pythagorean.
    - **Timaeus Locrus** (*On the Nature of the World*): probably a 1st-century Middle Platonist reading of the *Timaeus*.
- **Neopythagoreans** and **Neoplatonists** (Nicomachus, Iamblichus): Pythagoras as the source of all true philosophy. See [[Neoplatonism: Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus]].
    - **Moderatus** (Neopythagorean): three unities: the One above being (the Good), an active Logos (the intelligible), and Soul: a source for Neoplatonism.
- **Christian**: Clement of Alexandria and others saw Pythagoras as having learned from Moses. Augustine and Boethius kept the idea that number and music show the order of creation: "thou hast ordered all things in measure and number and weight" (Wis 11:20). The medieval **quadrivium** (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy) is Pythagorean.
- **Renaissance**: **Ficino** made him a link in the chain of "ancient theologians": Hermes, Orpheus, Aglaophamus, Pythagoras, Philolaus, Plato. See [[Christian Platonism]].
- **Godwin** (foreword): Western civilisation took "several wrong turnings" by being unfaithful to Pythagoras: applied mathematics gave the "conquest of Nature" while forgetting the precept to "honour first the Immortal Gods".

## 9. Notes

*Mine.*

- What the Fathers rejected: **transmigration** (Gregory of Nyssa's Macrina mocks it; see [[On the Soul and the Resurrection]]), the body as prison, and salvation as escape.
- What they kept: the world as ordered by measure and harmony, the evening self-examination (it appears in the desert monks almost word for word), and the idea of philosophy as a common life of purification.
- The idea that mathematics reveals the structure of reality is the root of modern physics (Galileo: the book of nature is written in mathematics).
