Written in Paris (French 1944, English 1957). Probably the most influential 20th-century statement of Orthodox theology in the West. Its main claim: doctrine and spiritual life aren't two things, and the dogmatic differences between East and West (Filioque, grace, the Church) produce different kinds of spirituality.

## 1. Introduction: Theology and Mysticism

- "Mystical theology" doesn't mean a special private experience. It means theology that is lived. Philaret of Moscow: no mysticism without theology, no theology without mysticism.
- Mysticism is the perfection of theology, and dogma is what keeps experience on track.
- So differences in dogma aren't abstract: they shape how people pray and what they expect to become.
- On reunion, quoting Barth: the union of the Churches is not made but discovered.

## 2. The Divine Darkness

- **Apophaticism is the fundamental character** of all Eastern theology, not one method among others. From Dionysius: God is known by unknowing. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]], [[Apophatic and Cataphatic Theology]].
- **Gregory of Nyssa**: every concept we form of God is an idol. The only fitting response is wonder. Moses enters the darkness on Sinai (Exod 20:21).
- **Gregory Nazianzen** corrects Plato: Plato said God is hard to know and impossible to express; Gregory says it's impossible to express and *more* impossible to know.
- This isn't **agnosticism**. It's an attitude of the mind that refuses concepts in order to meet God personally.
- Dogmas are **antinomies** (one nature, three persons): they don't resolve the mystery, they guard it.
- The apophatic way doesn't end in an impersonal abyss (as in Plotinus or Eastern religions). It ends in the **Trinity**.

## 3. God in Trinity

- Nazianzen: "No sooner do I conceive of the One than I am illumined by the splendour of the Three."
- *Ousia* (what is common) and *hypostasis* (what is particular). Person is not reducible to nature.
- The **monarchy of the Father**: the Father is the source of the Son and Spirit. Unity comes from the Person of the Father, not from an impersonal essence. See [[Monarchical Trinitarianism]].
- **The Filioque critique**: making the Spirit proceed from Father and Son puts the essence first and the Persons second, as relations within it. That's the root of the whole East–West divergence. See [[EO vs RC Trinity]], [[Filioque and the Franks]].
- Florensky: between the Trinity and hell there is no other choice.

## 4. Uncreated Energies

- 2 Peter 1:4, "partakers of the divine nature", isn't a metaphor.
- The **antinomy**: God is totally inaccessible in His essence, yet really communicates Himself.
- Palamas and the 14th-century councils: the **essence** is unknowable, the **energies** are God Himself acting and communicated. Uncreated, not a created grace. See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]], [[God's Uncreated Energies]].
- He criticises a "simplistic" idea of divine simplicity that can't allow this. See [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]].
- The energies aren't defined by creation: God would have them even if nothing were created. Creation is by **will** (Maximus), not by nature.

## 5. Created Being

- **Creation from nothing** (2 Macc 7:28) is an article of faith, not a philosophical conclusion.
- Philaret: all creatures are balanced on the creative word of God as on a bridge of diamond, above the abyss of nothingness.
- Creation is by **will**, not by necessity of nature. That's the difference from Neoplatonic emanation. See [[Neoplatonism: Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus]].
- The **logoi**: God's ideas or wills for each thing, which things are called to fulfil.
- Time and the *aeon* (the angels' mode of being).
- The cosmos has a vocation: to be united with God through man. The Church is that vocation being fulfilled.

## 6. Image and Likeness

- Augustine starts from the human mind and looks for the Trinity in it (the "psychological" analogy). Gregory of Nyssa goes the other way: starts from God and asks what man must be.
- The Fathers put the image in many places: the mind, freedom, dominion, even the body (Palamas). No single location.
- The key: **person vs nature**. Man as person isn't reducible to his nature. The image is the capacity for a personal relationship with God.
- **Basil**: man is a creature who has received the command to become god. Image is given, **likeness** is the task. See [[Image and Likeness]], [[Being Made in the Image of God]].
- After the Fall grace acts on man only from outside, until Pentecost.

## 7. The Economy of the Son

- **Cabasilas**: three barriers between man and God: **nature**, **sin** and **death**. Christ removes them by the Incarnation, the Cross and the Resurrection.
- **Maximus's five divisions** (created/uncreated, intelligible/sensible, heaven/earth, paradise/world, male/female): Adam was meant to unite them and failed; Christ does it.
- **Kenosis**, the **perichoresis** of natures, and Nazianzen's rule: what is not assumed is not healed. See [[Doctrine of Recapitulation]].
- Christ's work concerns **nature**: He restores and deifies human nature as such.

## 8. The Economy of the Holy Spirit

- **Two economies**, of the Son and of the Spirit. Not one subordinate to the other.
- Irenaeus: where the Church is, there is the Spirit.
- **Eternal procession** (from the Father alone, *ekporeuesthai*) vs **temporal mission** (sent through the Son, *proienai*, *procheesthai*). The Filioque confuses the two.
- **Pentecost**: the Spirit given to each person. Christ unites nature; the Spirit diversifies persons.
- The Spirit is distinct from the uncreated grace He gives. He "effaces Himself" as a Person: He has no image of His own in the economy. The saints will be His image.
- See [[The Holy Spirit in Orthodox Theology]].

## 9. Two Aspects of the Church

| Christological | Pneumatological |
|---|---|
| the Body of Christ | the fullness of the Spirit |
| nature, unity | persons, multiplicity |
| one | catholic |
| objective: sacraments, hierarchy | subjective: each person's sanctification |

- The canons are dogma applied.
- **Catholicity** isn't geographical: each local Church has the whole.
- The **Theotokos** as the boundary between created and uncreated (Palamas): already in the Kingdom.

## 10. The Way of Union

- **Seraphim of Sarov**: the true end of the Christian life is the acquiring of the Holy Spirit.
- No idea of **merit** in the Eastern tradition. Instead **synergy**: God's grace and human freedom working together (Gregory of Nyssa). See [[Synergy: Grace and Free Will]].
- The path: repentance, tears, prayer (Isaac the Syrian, Evagrius), knowledge (*gnosis*).
- Seraphim: what's lacking in us is a firm resolve.

## 11. The Divine Light

- Symeon the New Theologian: the experience of God as **light**. The light of Tabor is uncreated.
- The East has no "dark night of the soul" as a stage of the way, and no Gethsemane-centred spirituality like the Western focus on the Passion. Union is in light.
- **Motovilov's conversation with Seraphim**: Motovilov sees Seraphim's face shining like the sun.
- The body too will be transfigured at the resurrection.
- Who is in the Church after death is a mystery: its limits aren't for us to fix.

## 12. Conclusion: The Feast of the Kingdom

- Tradition is the **life of the Holy Spirit** in the Church, not a store of documents.
- East and West diverge on the doctrines tied to Pentecost: the Spirit, grace, the Church.
- With the Filioque the Spirit becomes a bond of unity between Father and Son, and in the Church a function of the unity of Christ's Body. In the East each person receives the fullness of the Spirit personally.
- The end: deification, the Kingdom as an eternal feast.

## 13. Notes

*Mine.*

- Systematic where Schmemann is liturgical. Read them together: [[Fr. Alexander Schmemann: For the Life of the World]].
- The "two economies" and "person vs nature" are the most distinctive Lossky ideas. Some Orthodox critics (e.g. Zizioulas, and later writers on the patristic sources) think he separates the Son and Spirit's work too sharply, or reads the Filioque into everything.
- His treatment of the Fathers is from the "neo-patristic" movement (with Florovsky): back to the Fathers, away from the Western-style textbooks.
