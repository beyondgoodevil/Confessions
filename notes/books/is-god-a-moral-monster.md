Written against Dawkins's line that the OT God is "arguably the most unpleasant character in all fiction: jealous and proud of it; a petty, unjust, unforgiving control-freak; a vindictive, bloodthirsty ethnic cleanser..." Copan takes the charges one by one.

## 1. The Big Picture

- The Mosaic Law is **not the ideal**. It's an **improvement** on the ancient Near East, given to a hard-hearted people where they were. Jesus says this Himself: "Moses because of the hardness of your hearts suffered you to put away your wives: but **from the beginning it was not so**" (Matt 19:8).
- Look at Genesis 1–2 for the ideal (every human in God's image, one-flesh marriage), and at the Law as a step toward it.
- Compare with other ANE law codes (Hammurabi, Lipit-Ishtar), not with modern Western law.
- The OT itself points beyond the Mosaic covenant: Jer 31:31-34 (a new covenant), Ezek 36:26.

## 2. Jealousy and Arrogance

- God's **jealousy** is like a husband's for his wife: a right exclusive claim in a covenant of love. Not envy. "I the LORD thy God am a jealous God" (Exod 20:5): in the context of idolatry, which is adultery.
- Is it arrogant for God to want praise? Not if He really is the greatest good: praising Him is for **our** good. (C. S. Lewis: praise completes enjoyment.)

## 3. Abraham and Isaac

- Gen 22: God tests Abraham. Copan: God never intended Isaac to die; Abraham expected God to raise him (Heb 11:19; "I and the lad will... come again to you", Gen 22:5).
- The scene ends **child sacrifice** in Israel, against the surrounding cultures (Molech).
- Typologically: the Father gives His only Son; the ram in the thicket. See [[Types of Christ in the OT]].

## 4. Purity and Kosher Laws

- Not arbitrary: they **set Israel apart**, teaching that God is holy and separation is needed. Clean/unclean taught order (creation categories) and life vs death.
- Temporary: abolished in Christ (Mark 7:19; Acts 10).

## 5. Harsh Punishments

- Death penalty for adultery, blasphemy etc. Copan: many may have been **maximum** sentences, with ransom possible (Num 35:31 forbids ransom only for murder, implying it was allowed for others).
- Compared to ANE codes, Israel's were milder: no mutilation for property crimes, no class distinctions in punishment ("one law" for native and stranger, Exod 12:49), **lex talionis** as a *limit* on revenge, not a demand.

## 6. Slavery

- Israelite "slavery" was mostly **debt-servitude**: voluntary, temporary (six years, Exod 21:2), with release and provisions (Deut 15:13-14).
- **Kidnapping** a person to sell was a capital crime (Exod 21:16), which would have outlawed the antebellum slave trade.
- **Runaway slaves** were not to be returned (Deut 23:15-16), unlike Hammurabi (death for helping one).
- Injury frees the slave (Exod 21:26-27).
- Harder texts: foreign slaves (Lev 25:44-46). Copan: still inferior to the ideal, tolerated as with divorce.

## 7. Women

- Women in Israel had far more standing than in surrounding cultures: the image of God in both (Gen 1:27); Deborah, Huldah; Proverbs 31.
- Hard texts (Deut 22:28-29, the rape law) are protective in context: the man must marry and support her and can never divorce her; her father can refuse (Exod 22:16-17).

## 8. The Canaanite Conquest

The hardest one: "thou shalt **utterly destroy** them" (Deut 7:2; 20:16-17).

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Hyperbole** | ANE war reports routinely exaggerate ("left none remaining") as stock language. Joshua says all were destroyed (Josh 10:40), but Judges 1 says many Canaanites were still there. Both are inspired; the first is rhetoric of victory |
| **"Drive out" more than "kill"** | the main command is to **drive out** or dispossess (Exod 23:28-30; Num 33:52): the Canaanites could flee |
| **Military targets** | Jericho and Ai were likely forts or garrisons, not cities of civilians; the "king" a military commander. "Women and children" may be stock phrase ("all in the city"). Rahab survives |
| **Judgment on sin** | God waited 400 years: "the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full" (Gen 15:16). Child sacrifice, cult prostitution (Lev 18:24-28). The same judgment fell on Israel later |
| **Unique and limited** | in one place, one time, for one purpose. Not a model for holy war; no command to repeat it |
| **Not ethnic** | foreigners could join (Rahab, Ruth, the Gibeonites); Israel was judged by the same standard |

## 9. The Fathers' Reading

*Mine.* Copan is a Protestant analytic philosopher and mostly reads the texts historically. The Fathers also read them **spiritually**:
- **Origen**: if read only literally, the wars are scandalous; they teach the war against the passions. The Canaanites are the sins to be destroyed in the soul.
- **Gregory of Nyssa**, *Life of Moses*: the death of Egypt's firstborn means killing evil at its first appearance, before it grows.
- **Ps 137:9** ("dash thy little ones against the stones"): the Fathers read it as dashing evil thoughts against the rock, which is Christ (1 Cor 10:4).
- This doesn't deny that history happened, but holds that the meaning of these texts is fully revealed in Christ, who rebukes calling down fire (Luke 9:54-56).

## Notes

*Mine.*

- The "improvement, not ideal" framework (Matt 19:8) is the key. It also answers slavery.
- Hyperbole is the strongest point for the conquest, and it's well attested in ANE sources.
- Still hard: the texts about infants. Copan's answers here are the least convincing. The patristic and Christ-centred reading helps more.
