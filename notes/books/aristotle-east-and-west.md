The best book on the philosophical background to the essence–energies distinction. Bradshaw's thesis: East and West took different paths from a single word, Aristotle's ***energeia***, and the divergence is a deep root of the schism, deeper than the Filioque or the papacy.

## 1. Aristotle

- ***Energeia*** was a word Aristotle more or less invented. Two meanings:
    - **activity**: the exercise of a capacity (seeing, thinking, building)
    - **actuality**: the state of being actual rather than potential (the act/potency contrast)
- ***Entelecheia***: being at the end, fully realised.
- The development goes from "activity" toward "actuality": being something *fully* is to be active in the way proper to it.
- **The Prime Mover** (*Metaphysics* Λ): pure *energeia*, "thought thinking itself". Its essence is activity. It moves the heavens as an object of desire. See [[The Unmoved Mover]].

## 2. Between Aristotle and Plotinus

- The Stoics, Philo and the Middle Platonists: *energeia* used for divine activity in the world. Philo distinguishes God's essence (unknowable) from His **powers** (*dynameis*), by which He is known and acts. An early version of the essence/energies idea.

## 3. Plotinus: Two Acts

- Every being has two acts:
    - the **act of the essence** (internal activity, what it is)
    - the **act from the essence** (an outward activity, like heat from fire)
- The One's outward act produces Intellect, and so on. Emanation is the "act from" of each level.
- This lets something be present and active beyond itself without being divided. See [[Neoplatonism: Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus]].

## 4. The Western Path

- **Augustine**, **Boethius**: the stress falls on God as **being** (*esse*), immutable and simple, and on the "act of the essence". God's activity toward the world is not something distinct in God.
- In the West, *energeia* fades, translated as *actus* or *operatio*, losing the sense of "activity" in which creatures can **participate**.
- So union with God is understood as **knowledge** (the beatific vision of the divine essence) and created grace, rather than participation in uncreated activity.
- Culminates in **Aquinas**: God as *ipsum esse subsistens*, pure act, with no real distinction between essence and activity. See [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]].

## 5. Theurgy

- Later Neoplatonists (Iamblichus, Proclus) used *energeia* for the gods' activity in rites: through ritual the soul shares in divine energy. Bradshaw shows a parallel with Christian sacramental participation, without the magic.

## 6. The Eastern Path

- **The Cappadocians** (against Eunomius, who claimed we know God's essence): God's essence is unknowable; we know Him from His **energies**: goodness, wisdom, power. The names of God name His energies, not His essence.
- **Dionysius**: the divine "processions" (*proodoi*) are God Himself going out; the creature participates in them. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]].
- **Maximus**: Christ has two natural *energeiai* (against monoenergism); the logoi of creation; deification as sharing in God's energy. See [[Action Is Not Proper to the Person]].
- **Palamas** (14th c.): the essence–energies distinction made explicit to defend hesychast experience of the uncreated light. See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]].
- Energies here keep both meanings: God's **activity** and the presence of God **in** it, real and participable.

## 7. Palamas and Aquinas

| | Aquinas | Palamas |
|---|---|---|
| God is | pure act, being itself | beyond being in essence; present in His energies |
| Distinction in God | essence and attributes identical; distinctions only in our concepts | real distinction (not separation) between essence and energies |
| Union with God | the vision of God's essence, by the light of glory (created) | participation in the uncreated energies; the essence remains beyond |
| Grace | created | uncreated |

Both are answering the same question: how creatures can know and share in God. Both use the Greek tradition; they inherited different halves of it.

## 8. Epilogue

- The West's loss of energeia as participable activity left God remote, known through created effects and concepts. That fed scholasticism's rationalism and, later, the secular world where God is absent from nature.
- The East kept a picture where the world is full of God's activity: "all things full of gods" in a Christian form.

## Chapter by Chapter

**Preface**
- Starts from Tertullian's "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?" To say they're two separate worlds puts in question both and the civilisation built on their union.
- The essence–energies distinction is "the most important philosophical tenet distinguishing eastern Christian thought from its western counterpart" (Lossky, Meyendorff); but "virtually everything else about this distinction is subject to dispute": its meaning, history, legitimacy.
- In the East there was never the division between philosophy and theology, or theology and mysticism, since those presuppose a Western concept of "natural reason".

**1. The Aristotelian beginnings**
- Early on, *dunamis* vs *energeia* = **possessing** a capacity vs **using** it: "the opposite of failing to possess sight is to possess it, the opposite of failing to use sight is to use it" (*Topics* I.15).
- *Eudemian Ethics*: a thing's proper **work** (*ergon*) is its **end** (*telos*). *Energeia* is "en-*ergon*": being at work.
- Then *energeia* extended to **motion** (defined as the actuality of the potential) and to the form–matter relation: "the proximate matter and the form are one and the same thing, the one potentially, the other actually" (*Met.* VIII.6).
- **Immanent** activities (seeing, thinking: complete at every moment) vs **transitive** ones (building: complete only at the end).
- "Actuality" and "potentiality" are Latin coinages (*actualitas*, *potentialitas*) by scholastic translators: our English is "Aristotelianised English". So we read the later meaning back into Aristotle.
- Actuality is **prior** to potency in definition, time and substance; and potency is "less definitively good" than the actuality it's named for.

**2. The Prime Mover**
- *Met.* Λ: the first heaven moves in a circle; "since that which is moved and moves is intermediate, there is something which moves without being moved".
- The Mover's life is **thought**, and "the act of contemplation is most pleasant and best": a life "such as the best which we enjoy, and enjoy for but a short time".
- Circular motion resembles divine thought: every point is beginning, middle and end; it can go on forever without changing state.
- Because intellect is identical with its object, the Prime Mover **is** the forms in eternal actuality.
- So in Aristotle the highest being is an *energeia* (an **activity**), not just a state: the starting point of the whole story.

**3. Between Aristotle and Plotinus**
- Aristotle's esoteric works went to Theophrastus and were largely lost until Andronicus's edition (1st c. BC). Meanwhile *energeia* had other careers:
- **Rhetoric**: "Homer is the only poet who wrote words possessing movement because of their vigour (*energeian*)" (Plutarch); Quintilian: *energeia* "derives its name from action" and keeps speech from being tame.
- **Medicine**: **Galen** distinguishes the *energeiai* of bodily faculties, which we can know, from their *ousiai*, which we can't: "they assign it a name on the basis of its capacity to do what it does." An early form of "known by energies, essence unknown".
- **Philo**: God known through His powers, not His essence.
- **Middle Platonism**: the "Second Epistle of Plato" (three "kings"), **Numenius**'s First, Second and Third gods: the First at rest, concerned with the intelligibles; the Second in motion, toward both intelligibles and sensibles.
- **Alexander of Aphrodisias** on the active intellect, using the first/second potentiality distinction.

**4. Plotinus and the theory of two acts**
- Why does the One produce? Plotinus appeals to the *Timaeus* 29e: the maker is good and free from envy.
- **Two acts**: the act **of** the essence (*energeia tēs ousias*: what a thing is, its own activity) and the act **from** the essence (*energeia ek tēs ousias*: an outflow, like heat from fire). Soul is "an expression and a kind of activity of Intellect, just as Intellect is of the One".
- The One "as it were makes himself... and is not as he chanced to be but **as he wills**": a self-willing One, with the constant hedge "*hoion*" ("as it were") to avoid duality.
- Aristotle's teacher–pupil analogy: the actuality of the teacher is in the pupil but "not cut off" from the teacher. Plotinus uses it: the outflow stays connected to its source.

**5. The Plotinian heritage in the West**
- Porphyry's (probable) *Parmenides* commentary: the One as **pure "to be"** (*einai*), with Being as its participated form. A crucial shift: the first principle is identified with **being itself**.
- The triad **Existence–Life–Thought**: in procession it is Life, in return, Thought.
- **Marius Victorinus** (who influenced Augustine) adopts this for the Trinity: *esse* "under two modes, one universal and originally original, from which comes esse for all others". The Father is "inactive action" (*actio inactuosa*), internal; the Son is God's action "external and manifest"; Christ and the Spirit are "one movement... act which acts".
- **Boethius**, *De Hebdomadibus*: *esse* vs *id quod est* (being vs that which is). God is pure *esse*. This is the line that leads to Aquinas.

**6. Gods, demons, and theurgy**
- In the NT, *energeia* is mostly **God's** working: "the effectual working (*energeian*) in the measure of every part" builds up the body (Eph 4:16). Each believer's activity, insofar as it builds up the Church, is God's activity too: **co-operation with the Spirit**.
- The Apologists: the Word "in idea" (*logos endiathetos*, within the Father) and "in operation" (*logos prophorikos*, uttered in creation) (Athenagoras).
- In early Christian texts *energeia* is often used of **demons**' activity (possession); in the **magical papyri** of divine power in charms.
- **Hermetic** writings: "the universe is God acting"; "all things are full of *energeiai*". Deification: the blessed "become powers and enter into God".
- **Iamblichus**: gods are good by essence, souls by participation. Three stages: participation, communion, **union**. We have an innate knowledge of the gods "coexistent with our very being". The highest form of union is exercising **our activity in common with God**.
- **Proclus**: "before the Word, the silence that supports the Word"; union through the "flower of the whole soul".

**7. The formation of the eastern tradition**
- **Eunomius**: God is simple, so "the unbegotten" must be His very essence; the Son, begotten, can't share it. He claimed God's essence can be known. He also said we needn't "unite the energeia to the essence".
- **Basil** replies: distinguish knowing **what** a thing is from knowing **how** it is (its properties, operations). We know God's energies, not His essence.
- **Gregory of Nyssa**: "the term 'godhead' is significant of **operation** (*energeia*) and not of nature." (Like "hydrogen": named from producing water, but meaning something else.)
- **Gregory Nazianzen**: God's majesty and glory are His "**back parts**", left behind as tokens, "like the shadows and reflections of the sun in the water".
- So the energies aren't merely God's effects but "must in some sense be God Himself": God as known to us, not as known to Himself (His "face").
- **Basil** on the Spirit: present to each "as though given to him alone", yet grace for all; enjoyed "according to the capacity, not of its power, but of their nature". The goal: "abiding in God, being made like to God, and, highest of all, being made God".
- **Dionysius**: to be "a co-worker of God" (1 Cor 3:9) and exhibit in oneself the divine *energeia*; the whole hierarchy "an image of the comeliness of God".
- Parallel with Iamblichus: union with divine activity as recovering one's true self. Paul: "it is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me" (Gal 2:20).

**8. The flowering of the eastern tradition**
- **Maximus** adopts the Cappadocian distinction between the divine *ousia* and the "**things around God**" (*ta peri theon*). God "is beyond being itself". The vision of God will be "a deification that is unceasingly active" and "experience by participation of the **goods** beyond nature" (plural: not seeing the essence).
- **Asceticism**: "pray without ceasing"; Paul "bearing about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus" (2 Cor 4:10): bodily communion with Christ's life.
- Sensation isn't separate from intellect: the soul "takes on the rational principles (*logoi*)" in things as "symbols of things unseen" and ascends to God.
- Deification **recapitulates the Incarnation**: "the human being is made God, and God is called and appears as human."
- **John of Damascus**, *De Fide Orthodoxa*: "God is said to be in a place where His energeia becomes manifest"; the energy is "indivisibly multiplied", an "irradiation" illuminating all creation. So the energy is "God Himself as He is participated by creatures".
- **The Spirit and the Son**: Basil: knowledge of God goes "from the one Spirit through the one Son to the one Father". The Spirit "accompanies the Word and manifests His energy" (Damascene). "We do not speak of the Spirit as **from** the Son, but yet we call Him the Spirit **of** the Son." This eternal manifestation is part of the Persons' identity, not only their mission.

**9. Palamas and Aquinas**
- **Augustine**: his ascent ends in "an epiphany of being": "in the thrust of a trembling glance my mind arrived at That Which Is" (*Conf.* VII.17). God is "absolutely simple essence", in whom to be is to be wise. "God is for the mind to understand, as body is for the eye to see." God is the "first Form" (*prima species*).
- **The hesychasts**: the monk recites the Jesus Prayer with his breathing "to lead the nous back to the heart". **Barlaam** (who knew mainly Nikephoros's method text) wanted instead to transcend the senses and body in "pure unknowing"; he denied bodily awareness of God and the uncreated light.
- **Palamas**: "You might as well claim that God is a creature as declare that His essential energies are created; for no one who has sense would say that the essential goodness and life are the superessential essence of God" (*Triads* III.1.23). He draws together the uncreated light, the "things around God", the divine names, and the indwelling of the Spirit under one concept: *energeia*.
- Missing in Palamas: the earlier teaching (Nyssa, Damascene, Gregory of Cyprus) on the **eternal manifestation** of the Spirit through the Son.
- **Aquinas**: in loving His essence God wills it to be "multiplied" through created likenesses. "God's action is His substance." Only God is His own *esse*; all else has *esse* by **participation** (Wippel: "a participated likeness of the divine *esse*, an intrinsic act of being efficiently caused in it by God").
- The **beatific vision**: God's essence, pure act, is "absolutely intelligible", seen by the created **light of glory**.
- **Rahner**'s "quasi-formal causality" tries to recover uncreated grace in the West.
- Thomists answer that the difference between God's necessary and free acts is a "logical distinction", not a real one.

**10. Epilogue**
- The Greek works were little known in the West; they inspired neither emulation nor fear, so there was no incentive to understand them.
- The Augustinian **elevation of the intellect** put "a kind of practical dualism" at the heart of Western spirituality. Missing: **synergy** as shared activity, and **energeia as the outward, manifest reality of personal being**.
- Bradshaw thinks **Aquinas failed** to resolve how God's free acts can be identical with His necessary essence (the objection: that would make God "subject to temporal succession... mutable"; Bradshaw's reply: not if the energies are real but distinct).
- He lists levels of divine energeiai: God's own being and life; the act of creating and "things around God" like providence and foreknowledge; and others (infinity, immortality, holiness, virtue).
- Voltaire: "a long dispute means that both parties are wrong." Bradshaw's history shows how differently such matters look once the shared roots are seen.
