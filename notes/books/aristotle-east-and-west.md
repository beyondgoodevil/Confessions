The best book on the philosophical background to the essence–energies distinction. Bradshaw's thesis: East and West took different paths from a single word, Aristotle's ***energeia***, and the divergence is a deep root of the schism, deeper than the Filioque or the papacy.

## 1. Aristotle

- ***Energeia*** was a word Aristotle more or less invented. Two meanings:
    - **activity**: the exercise of a capacity (seeing, thinking, building)
    - **actuality**: the state of being actual rather than potential (the act/potency contrast)
- ***Entelecheia***: being at the end, fully realised.
- The development goes from "activity" toward "actuality": being something *fully* is to be active in the way proper to it.
- **The Prime Mover** (*Metaphysics* Λ): pure *energeia*, "thought thinking itself". Its essence is activity. It moves the heavens as an object of desire. See [[The Unmoved Mover]].

## 2. Between Aristotle and Plotinus

- The Stoics, Philo and the Middle Platonists: *energeia* used for divine activity in the world. Philo distinguishes God's essence (unknowable) from His **powers** (*dynameis*), by which He is known and acts. An early version of the essence/energies idea.

## 3. Plotinus: Two Acts

- Every being has two acts:
    - the **act of the essence** (internal activity, what it is)
    - the **act from the essence** (an outward activity, like heat from fire)
- The One's outward act produces Intellect, and so on. Emanation is the "act from" of each level.
- This lets something be present and active beyond itself without being divided. See [[Neoplatonism: Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus]].

## 4. The Western Path

- **Augustine**, **Boethius**: the stress falls on God as **being** (*esse*), immutable and simple, and on the "act of the essence". God's activity toward the world is not something distinct in God.
- In the West, *energeia* fades, translated as *actus* or *operatio*, losing the sense of "activity" in which creatures can **participate**.
- So union with God is understood as **knowledge** (the beatific vision of the divine essence) and created grace, rather than participation in uncreated activity.
- Culminates in **Aquinas**: God as *ipsum esse subsistens*, pure act, with no real distinction between essence and activity. See [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]].

## 5. Theurgy

- Later Neoplatonists (Iamblichus, Proclus) used *energeia* for the gods' activity in rites: through ritual the soul shares in divine energy. Bradshaw shows a parallel with Christian sacramental participation, without the magic.

## 6. The Eastern Path

- **The Cappadocians** (against Eunomius, who claimed we know God's essence): God's essence is unknowable; we know Him from His **energies**: goodness, wisdom, power. The names of God name His energies, not His essence.
- **Dionysius**: the divine "processions" (*proodoi*) are God Himself going out; the creature participates in them. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]].
- **Maximus**: Christ has two natural *energeiai* (against monoenergism); the logoi of creation; deification as sharing in God's energy. See [[Action Is Not Proper to the Person]].
- **Palamas** (14th c.): the essence–energies distinction made explicit to defend hesychast experience of the uncreated light. See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]].
- Energies here keep both meanings: God's **activity** and the presence of God **in** it, real and participable.

## 7. Palamas and Aquinas

| | Aquinas | Palamas |
|---|---|---|
| God is | pure act, being itself | beyond being in essence; present in His energies |
| Distinction in God | essence and attributes identical; distinctions only in our concepts | real distinction (not separation) between essence and energies |
| Union with God | the vision of God's essence, by the light of glory (created) | participation in the uncreated energies; the essence remains beyond |
| Grace | created | uncreated |

Both are answering the same question: how creatures can know and share in God. Both use the Greek tradition; they inherited different halves of it.

## 8. Epilogue

- The West's loss of energeia as participable activity left God remote, known through created effects and concepts. That fed scholasticism's rationalism and, later, the secular world where God is absent from nature.
- The East kept a picture where the world is full of God's activity: "all things full of gods" in a Christian form.
