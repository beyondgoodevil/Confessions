A collection of essays, not a single argument, but one idea runs through all of them: **there is no true being without communion**. Nothing exists as an isolated individual, not even God. And the place where this is realised is the **Eucharist**, which makes the Church.

## 1. Personhood and Being

**The Greek problem**
- Greek philosophy thought of being as a closed, necessary cosmos. The One, or the world, is what's ultimate; particular things come and go. There's no room for the **person** as unique and free.
- The Greek *prosōpon* was a mask: a role on top of what you really are.
- Roman *persona*: a legal or social role.

**The patristic revolution**
- The Cappadocians identified ***hypostasis*** (concrete being) with ***prosōpon*** (person). So the person isn't a mask added to being. It **is** being in its concrete form.
- **The Father as cause**: God's being comes from a **person**, the Father, not from an impersonal substance. The Father freely begets the Son and brings forth the Spirit. So "God exists" because of a free, personal act of love. See [[Monarchical Trinitarianism]].
- So: **"being" is "communion"**. God is not first one substance and then three persons in relation. The communion of persons *is* the one God.
- Freedom and love are therefore ontological, not just moral.

**Two kinds of human existence**

| Hypostasis of biological existence | Hypostasis of ecclesial existence |
|---|---|
| born from *eros* and natural necessity | born from baptism, "from above" (John 3:3) |
| individualism: I exist as separate from others | person: I exist in relation |
| ends in death: nature wins over the person | eschatological: open to the Kingdom |
| the "tragic" human condition | a new way of being, given in Christ |

- Biological existence is caught between two failures: it wants personal communion but produces **individuals**, and it wants to survive but ends in **death**.
- Baptism and the Eucharist give a new hypostasis: you exist not because you were born, but because you are loved by God and in communion. That's salvation as **ontological**, not just moral.

## 2. Truth and Communion

Six patristic approaches to truth (the six sections):
1. **Logos** (Justin, Clement, Origen): truth as the rational structure of the world. Risk: Greek cosmology.
2. **Eucharistic** (Ignatius, Irenaeus): truth as life, given in communion.
3. **Trinitarian** (Athanasius, Cappadocians): truth is relational, because God is.
4. **Apophatic** (Dionysius): truth beyond concepts.
5. **Christological** (Maximus): truth as the person of Christ, uniting created and uncreated.
6. **Eikon**: truth as image, a reflection of the eschatological reality.

The point: truth is **"I am the truth"** (John 14:6), a person, met in communion, not a set of propositions.

## 3. Christ, the Spirit and the Church

- Christ is not an individual first and then a community. He is a **corporate personality**: "the one and the many".
- What makes Christ "the many" is the **Spirit**. The Spirit constitutes the Church, not just inspires it.
- So Christology has to be **conditioned by Pneumatology**. Zizioulas criticises the West (and Lossky) for separating the two economies. See [[The Holy Spirit in Orthodox Theology]].
- The Spirit also brings the **eschaton** into history: the Church is the Kingdom anticipated.

## 4. Eucharist and the Church

- **"The Eucharist makes the Church."** It's not one sacrament among others but the event in which the Church *is* the Church.
- **Catholicity** isn't geographical or numerical. Each local Eucharistic assembly, around one bishop, is the whole Church: the whole Christ is present in each.
- The **bishop** stands in the place of Christ at the Eucharist (Ignatius: "where the bishop is, there let the people be"). One city, one bishop, one Eucharist.
- **Apostolic succession**: not just a chain of hands from the past (historical) but also the community's being gathered around Christ as the apostles will be in the Kingdom (eschatological).
- **Laity** are an order too: everyone is ordained in baptism-chrismation. No one exists in the Church outside a relation.

## 5. Criticisms

- **Reading the Cappadocians** (Turcescu, Larchet, Behr): they didn't have a modern concept of "person", and they usually *don't* say the Father's person is the cause of the divine essence. Zizioulas is accused of reading personalist philosophy (Heidegger, Buber, Levinas) back into the Fathers.
- **Person over nature** goes too far: the Fathers make the essence and persons equally primary. Danger of making the essence secondary.
- **Eucharistic ecclesiology** (from Afanasiev) can make the Church look like a sum of local Churches, weakening primacy or universal structure. Zizioulas himself tried to balance this with a "primacy" at each level.
- **Lossky vs Zizioulas**: Lossky separates Christ (nature) and Spirit (persons); Zizioulas unites them. See [[The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church]].
