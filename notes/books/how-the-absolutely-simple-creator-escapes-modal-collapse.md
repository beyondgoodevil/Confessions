A follow-up to [[Divine Simplicity: Western and Orthodox Views]] sec. 3, where modal collapse came up as the hardest objection. Tomaszewski is a Thomist defending **absolute divine simplicity** (DDS).

**The worry**: if God has no real distinctions, His act of creating = His essence; His essence is necessary; so creation is necessary. God couldn't have done otherwise, and nothing could have been different.

## 1. The Simple Argument Is Invalid

The usual form (from Mullins):

- S1. Necessarily, God exists.
- S2. God is identical to God's act of creation.
- S3. So necessarily, God's act of creation exists.

**The mistake**: substituting a term that only **contingently** refers to something into the scope of "necessarily". Quine's example:

- C1. Necessarily, 8 > 7.
- C2. The number of the planets = 8.
- C3. So necessarily, the number of the planets > 7. ✗ (there could have been 5 planets)

"God's act of creation" is a description that picks out God **because** He creates; in a world where He doesn't create, it picks out nothing (or God under a different description). So S3 doesn't follow. See [[Modal Logic: Necessity and Possibility]].

**And it proves too much.** The same form gives:

- A1. Necessarily, God exists.
- A2. God is the Creator.
- A3. So necessarily, the Creator exists. → necessarily there is a creation.

Every theist (not just Thomists) accepts A1 and A2. If the simple argument worked, it'd refute all theism.

## 2. The Real Worry: Modal Indiscernibility

The stronger version:

- MI1. God is absolutely simple.
- MI2. Then He is **really, intrinsically the same** in every possible world (no accidents, nothing contingent in Him).
- MI3. Then His act is intrinsically the same in every world.
- MI4. If His act is intrinsically the same in every world, **its effects don't vary** across worlds.
- MI5. So the effects (creation) don't vary: modal collapse.

This one is valid.

## 3. Everyone Has This Problem

- Parallel argument for any theist who holds (a) God exists necessarily, (b) God has an essence, (c) the Principle of Sufficient Reason:
    - God's **essence** is the same in every world.
    - Whatever non-essential properties God has must come from His essence (or else they're unexplained, against the PSR).
    - Then they don't vary either... and so on to collapse.
- So the problem is really **getting the contingent from the necessary**. It hits everyone who says a necessary being explains a contingent world. Only Swinburne (who doesn't hold God's necessity in the usual sense) might escape. See [[The Leibnizian Cosmological Argument]].

## 4. The Solution: Reject MI4

**Creative non-determinism**
- MI4 assumes the relation between God's act and its effects is **deterministic**: same cause, same effect.
- Why assume that? There's no evidence for it, and it isn't required by the idea of causation. **Anscombe**: causation is the *derivation* of an effect from its cause, not necessitation. A cause can be the same and its effects differ.
- So God's act can be intrinsically the same in every world, while what comes from it differs.

**Hyperintensionality**
- Even granting determinism, God creates through **intellect and will**: His act follows on His knowledge of His ideas.
- Mental causation is **hyperintensional**: you can't swap even necessarily co-referring terms.
    - "Thinking I saw Jack the Ripper made me run" doesn't entail "thinking I saw Francis Tumblety made me run", even if they were necessarily the same man.
    - "God's idea of zebras caused zebras" is true; "God's idea of unicorns caused zebras" is false, though in God (simple) the ideas are **really** identical.
- The Thomist **virtual distinction**: God's ideas are really one but **virtually** distinct, distinct in what they can explain. That's enough for God's act to bear on this world rather than another, without composition in God.

**Conclusion**: the simple argument is invalid; the deeper worry hits everyone; and the Scholastic real/virtual distinction plus the role of God's intellect in creating shows how a simple God can freely create.

## 5. Notes

*Mine.*

- The point about Quine is clearly right: S1–S3 is invalid as stated. Most serious critics (e.g. Mullins) have moved to stronger versions.
- The "everyone has this problem" point is good, but non-classical theists answer that God can have **contingent properties** (He freely chose this), which the DDS can't allow. That's the real difference.
- The **Orthodox** answer avoids this by putting God's free will toward creation in the **energies**, really distinct from the essence. God's essence is necessary; His creative energy is free. Tomaszewski's "virtual distinction" is very close to Palamas's real distinction without separation, but says it's only in what it explains, not real. The Orthodox would say: then the freedom isn't real either. See [[Essence-Energies Distinction]], [[Implications of No Essence-Energies Distinction]].
