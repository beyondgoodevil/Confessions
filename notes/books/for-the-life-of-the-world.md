Originally written (1963) as a study guide for an American Christian student conference. Short, polemical, more an essay than a textbook. The title comes from John 6:51: "the bread that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world."

The thesis in one line: the Church's sacraments aren't religious extras added on top of ordinary life. They show what the world *is* and was meant to be.

## 1. The Life of the World

- Starts from Feuerbach: **"Man is what he eats."** Meant as materialism, but Schmemann says it's the most religious idea about man there is.
- Man is a **hungry being**, and the world is given to him as **food**. In Genesis food is God's gift and the means of communion with Him.
- So man is first of all a **priest**: he stands in the middle of the world, receives it from God and offers it back in thanksgiving. ***Homo adorans***, the worshipping being; man as **eucharistic**.
- **The Fall**: not first a moral failure, but man ceasing to see the world as God's gift. He loves the world *for itself*. The world becomes **"opaque"**, no longer transparent to God. Eating becomes just consumption, life becomes just survival.
- Two wrong answers in modern Christianity:
    - **"spiritual"**: religion is about the soul, and the world is left alone
    - **"activist"**: religion is about improving the world, and God is left out
    Both accept the split between "religious" and "secular".
- Christ came not to start a new religion but to restore the priestly life. Christianity is in a sense **the end of religion**: in the Kingdom there's no temple (Rev 21:22).

## 2. The Eucharist

The longest and most important chapter. The Liturgy is a **journey** or procession into the Kingdom, step by step.

| Stage | Schmemann's point |
|---|---|
| **Assembly** | the Liturgy begins when people leave home to *become* the Church. *Leitourgia*: an action by which a group becomes something corporately, more than the sum of its members |
| **Entrance** | the Church ascends; the altar is the throne of God and the Kingdom |
| **Word** | Scripture and sermon are a sacrament, not just teaching. The Word is heard *in* the Spirit |
| **Offering** | the bread and wine are our life and the whole world, offered. "To take the whole world into our hands as if it were an apple" and give it back to God |
| **Anaphora** | lifting up, thanksgiving. Thanksgiving is the true relation to God, restored |
| **Communion** | the Kingdom tasted. Christ is the perfect Eucharist: the one who gave thanks perfectly |
| **"Let us depart in peace"** | the return to the world, now as witnesses. The Liturgy ends in mission |

- **Joy** is the key word. Nietzsche's charge, that Christians don't look redeemed, sticks because we've lost the joy. "For behold, I bring you good tidings of great joy" (Luke 2:10).
- The Eucharist isn't one sacrament among seven. It's the sacrament of the Church, from which the others make sense.

## 3. The Time of Mission

- Christians have failed to **sanctify time**: either time is ignored ("spiritualisation") or treated as merely secular.
- The **feasts**: Passover and Pentecost were already in Israel; Christ fulfils them. A feast is the Kingdom breaking into ordinary time.
- **Sunday** is the **eighth day**: the first day of the new creation, beyond the week of this world.
- The **liturgy of the hours**: Vespers starts the liturgical day at evening (end, darkness, the coming night); Matins greets the light. Time itself becomes a sacrament.
- "Eternal Life, not eternal rest." The Kingdom is fullness of life, not sleep. "Behold, I make all things new" (Rev 21:5).

## 4. Of Water and the Spirit

- Baptism's meaning has shrunk to "validity" and juridical questions (is it valid? what's the minimum?).
- The full rite shows its cosmic meaning:
    - the **catechumenate** and **exorcisms**, the renunciation of Satan, turning to the East to unite with Christ
    - the **blessing of water**: matter restored to its proper function, as a means of communion with God
    - baptism as **death and resurrection** (Rom 6:3-4)
    - then **Chrismation** (personal Pentecost) and **Eucharist**. Originally one rite
- **Penance** is a return to baptism. *Metanoia*. Quoting Léon Bloy: "there is only one sadness, not to be a saint."

## 5. The Mystery of Love

- Marriage is not about "the family" as a natural institution, but about **love**, taken into the Kingdom.
- The **Theotokos** is the image of creation's response to God, its "yes".
- In the rite:
    - the **crowns** are crowns of martyrdom, and every marriage is a martyrdom, a witness through self-giving
    - the **cross** is at the centre of marriage
    - the **common cup** stands in for the Eucharist, which used to be the end of the rite
- The **priesthood** is also a sacrament of love: the priest is in a sense "married to the Church".

## 6. Trampling Down Death by Death

- Modern culture either **denies death** (the funeral industry, cosmetic corpses) or makes religion **death-centred** (Christianity as comfort about the afterlife).
- Christianity does neither. It proclaims death as **the enemy**, destroyed. "The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death" (1 Cor 15:26). See [[1 Corinthians 15:26: The Last Enemy That Will Be Destroyed Is Death]].
- Christ is **Life**, not just a teacher about life after death. The Paschal troparion: "trampling down death by death".
- The Orthodox funeral is a Paschal service; 1 Thess 4:16-17.

## 7. And Ye Are Witnesses of These Things

- **Mission**. The Church faces a double failure: it can't simply defeat the other religions, and it has been beaten by secularism.
- Christians who fight on the "religious values" front have already surrendered to secularism: they've accepted that religion is one department of life.
- The Church is the **sacrament of the Kingdom**: it doesn't exist for itself, but to make the Kingdom present in the world.
- The closing line, roughly: a Christian is someone who, wherever he looks, finds Christ and rejoices in Him.

## 8. Appendices

**Worship in a Secular Age**

- Secularism is not first atheism. It's the **negation of worship**: the denial of man as *homo adorans*. Baudrillard's "formal liturgy of the object" describes the replacement liturgy; see [[Enlightenment as Myth: Horkheimer, Adorno and Baudrillard]].
- *Lex orandi, lex credendi*: the Church's prayer is the source of its theology, not a decoration of it.

**Sacrament and Symbol**

- Orthodox school theology went through a **"Western captivity"** (Florovsky's word was "pseudomorphosis"): the textbooks took over scholastic categories.
- Scholastic sacramental theology asks about validity, matter and form, causality. The sign and what it signifies come apart.
- The patristic approach: a **symbol** is an *epiphany*, not a mere sign. It makes present what it reveals. The whole world was made to be "symbolic" in this sense, a means of communion with God.
