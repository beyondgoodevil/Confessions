Friendship (*philia*) was a central topic in ancient ethics, almost gone from modern ethics. Aristotle spends two of the ten books of the *Ethics* on it. The volume's essays run from Plato to Hölderlin; these notes follow the ancient and medieval part.

## 1. The Greek Words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ***storgē*** | natural affection, family love |
| ***philia*** | friendship; love between equals; also broader (fondness for wine, love of wisdom) |
| ***erōs*** | passionate, desiring love; in Plato, desire for beauty |
| ***agapē*** | rare in classical Greek; in the NT, God's self-giving love and the love Christians owe |

C. S. Lewis's *Four Loves* uses these four (affection, friendship, eros, charity).

## 2. Plato

**Lysis**: what is a friend? Like to like? Opposite to opposite? Ends in *aporia*. But hints: we love things for the sake of a "first friend" (*prōton philon*), the ultimate good, for whose sake we love all else.

**Symposium**: Diotima's **ladder of love**:
1. love of one beautiful body
2. of all beautiful bodies
3. of beautiful souls
4. of beautiful practices and laws
5. of beautiful knowledge
6. of **Beauty itself**, eternal and unchanging

Eros is **desire for the good to be one's own forever**: "giving birth in beauty". It's a *daimon* between mortal and divine. See [[The Good in Plato]], [[Beauty as a Way to God]].

**Phaedrus**: eros as a divine madness that reminds the soul of the Forms; the charioteer and two horses.

## 3. Aristotle (*Nicomachean Ethics* VIII–IX)

**Three kinds of friendship**, by what is loved:

| Kind | Loves the other for | Lasts |
|---|---|---|
| **utility** | what they're useful for | while useful: old people, business |
| **pleasure** | the pleasure they give | while pleasant: young people |
| **virtue** (complete) | **who they are**, their good character | as long as both are good: rare, needs time and "eating salt together" |

- Only virtue-friendship is friendship in the full sense; the others are friendships by resemblance.
- **"A friend is another self."** We wish our friend good **for his sake**, as we wish good for ourselves.
- Friendship needs **equality**, or proportion when unequal (parent and child, ruler and ruled). Between man and gods, the gap is too great for friendship.
- **Why does the happy man need friends** if he's self-sufficient? Because we contemplate our own good activity best in another; and living together is the human good.
- Self-love: the good man loves his own **best part** (reason) and so is the best friend to others.
- Friendship holds cities together; lawgivers care about it more than justice. See [[Aristotle's Politics]].

## 4. Hellenistic

- **Stoics**: only the wise can be friends, since only they are good. Love (*erōs*) as an effort to form a friendship because of beauty, in the wise.
- **Epicureans**: friendship the greatest good for happiness, beginning from utility but becoming loved for itself. See [[Epicurus and Lucretius]].
- **Cicero, *On Friendship (Laelius)***: friendship is "agreement on all things divine and human, with goodwill and affection". Only between good men. Don't ask a friend to do wrong. Hugely influential in the Latin West.

## 5. The Fathers

**St. Gregory Nazianzen and St. Basil**
- The great patristic friendship: students together at Athens. Gregory's funeral oration for Basil: "we seemed to have one soul in two bodies"; "we knew only two roads, the one to our church and its teachers, the other to our schools".
- Friendship grounded in a shared pursuit of virtue and God: Aristotle's virtue-friendship Christianised. Also strained when Basil made Gregory bishop of Sasima against his will.

**Augustine**
- *Confessions* IV: the death of a friend in his youth: "I wondered that other mortals lived, since he whom I loved as if he would never die was dead." He'd loved a mortal as if he were immortal.
- The lesson: "blessed is he who loves Thee, and his friend in Thee". Only friendships rooted in God are secure.
- **Friendship and virtue**: true friendship is possible only where there's charity "poured out by the Holy Spirit" (Rom 5:5). Pagan friendship can't be complete virtue-friendship without God. See [[Augustine's Confessions: Memory, Time and the Restless Heart]].

## 6. Aelred of Rievaulx, *Spiritual Friendship* (12th c.)

- A Cistercian abbot rewrites Cicero's *Laelius* as a Christian dialogue.
- "**God is friendship**" (adapting 1 John 4:16): "he who abides in friendship abides in God".
- Three kinds: **carnal** (vices), **worldly** (gain), **spiritual** (among the good, in Christ).
- Friendship is a step toward the love of God: "a friend cleaving to his friend in the spirit of Christ is made one heart and soul with him, and so mounting through degrees of love to friendship with Christ".

## 7. Aquinas: Charity as Friendship

- *ST* II-II q. 23: **charity is friendship** of man with God.
- Aristotle denied friendship between gods and men because of inequality. Aquinas: God **communicates** His happiness to us by grace, which makes a fellowship possible. "I call you not servants... but **friends**" (John 15:15).
- Charity extends to our neighbour **for God's sake**, even enemies, because they belong to God.
- So the Christian virtue of love is friendship transformed: Aristotle's structure, with the inequality bridged by grace.

## 8. Notes

*Mine.*

- **Eros and agape**: Anders Nygren (*Agape and Eros*, 1930s) said they're opposites: eros is self-seeking desire, agape is God's self-giving; Christianity should drop eros. Dionysius and the Orthodox disagree: Dionysius calls God's own love **eros**, ecstatic love going out of itself to creatures, and draws them back. Eros purified is not opposed to agape. See [[St. Dionysius the Areopagite]].
- Friendship with God is the Orthodox goal too: Abraham "the friend of God" (Jas 2:23); Moses spoke with God "as a man speaketh unto his friend" (Exod 33:11).
- The Orthodox Church has a rite of **adelphopoiesis** (brother-making) in old service books: a blessing of spiritual friendship. Its interpretation is disputed.
