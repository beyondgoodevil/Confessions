This is my plan for a **one-semester Calculus I course** on the usual pattern: **three 50-minute lectures a week (MWF) for 13 weeks**, which gives **39 lectures**, with **two evening midterms** and a final in reading period. The topic order follows a standard first-semester syllabus (limits → derivatives → applications → integrals → FTC), and every lecture is read out of **Spivak's *Calculus***, which supplies the actual definitions and proofs. Here's how it's laid out:

### 1. How the Course Is Set Up

- **Meeting pattern**: three lectures a week (MWF) over a 13-week term, plus a weekly problem section.
- **Topic order**: the standard first-semester order: limits and continuity first, then the derivative and the rules, then applications (related rates, max/min, MVT, curve sketching, l'Hôpital, optimization, Newton's method, antiderivatives), and finally areas, the definite integral, the FTC and substitution.
- **Exams**: both midterms are evening exams, so no lecture slot is lost to them.

### 2. How Spivak Fits a Standard Calc I Order

Spivak's own order is not a Calc I order. He does the real numbers, functions, limits and continuity, then least upper bounds, *then* derivatives, and only defines $\sin$, $\log$ and $\exp$ properly **after** the integral (Chapters 15 and 18). Spivak himself notes that Parts I and II need less time than their length suggests. For this course:

1. **Chapters 1–8** are compressed into Weeks 1–4: the axioms are covered quickly, while the ε–δ definition and the three "hard theorems" get full lectures.
2. **Trig, inverse trig, exp and log** are used *before* their rigorous construction (Lectures 17 and 20–22), the same way Spivak himself uses $\sin' = \cos$ in Chapter 10 "without proof". The honest definitions are flagged in those notes and paid off in Lecture 39.
3. **Newton's method and related rates** only appear in Spivak's problems (Ch. 22 and Ch. 10), so those lectures are built from the problems.

### 4. Exams

1. **Midterm I** (evening, end of Week 6): Lectures 1–17, i.e. limits, continuity and the differentiation rules up to trig.
2. **Midterm II** (evening, end of Week 11): Lectures 18–32, i.e. implicit differentiation through Newton's method.
3. **Final** (reading period): cumulative, with weight on Lectures 33–39 (antiderivatives, the integral and the FTC).

### 5. How Each Note Is Set Up

Every lecture note has the **definitions and theorems in callouts**, a **proof** whenever Spivak gives one that fits in a 50-minute lecture, **worked examples**, and a **Problems to Try** list from the end of the matching Spivak chapter. Problem numbers are Spivak's (3rd edition); "Problem 5-13" means Chapter 5, Problem 13.

### Conclusion

The course is a standard Calc I in **order** and **pace**, but **Spivak in content**: every rule is proved from the definitions, and the places where we borrow facts early (trig, exp, log) are marked and paid back once the integral is in hand.
