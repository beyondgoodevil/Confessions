Start with what a worldview is, then the argument itself, then the objections and applications.

## Worldviews and Presuppositions

The groundwork: everyone reasons from a worldview, and its starting points need justifying.

1. [[What Is a Worldview or Paradigm]] — A worldview as the set of presuppositions through which a person interprets reality, and why no one is without one.
2. [[Prior Assumptions and Presuppositions]] — The difference between prior assumptions and presuppositions, with examples.
3. [[Why Is It Necessary to Justify One’s Presuppositions]] — Why a worldview must justify its own presuppositions or collapse into arbitrariness.
4. [[To Give an Account for Something or to Justify the Existence of Something]] — What it means to justify something, using logic as the example, and why "we use our brain" is not enough.
5. [[It Is Impossible to Have a Coherent Worldview Without Objective Truths]] — What collapses without objective truth, with ten examples.
6. [[John 14:6: Jesus Is the Truth]] — John 14:6 and the Orthodox understanding that Truth is a Person.

## The Argument

From the short statement to the full version.

1. [[TAG for Slowbois]] — The Transcendental Argument in four plain steps, using a scientist's experiment.
2. [[TAG]] — The Transcendental Argument for God — knowledge requires transcendental categories, which must be grounded in God.
3. [[Step-by-Step Breakdown of the TAG Argument]] — The four steps of the Transcendental Argument and what transcendental categories are.
4. [[Transcendental Categories Are Required for Science and Knowledge]] — Why identity, causality, logic and the other transcendental categories are preconditions of science and knowledge.
5. [[Why Is the Transcendental Argument Prior to the Teleological, Cosmological, and Other Arguments]] — TAG examines the preconditions that the cosmological, teleological and moral arguments all assume.

## Objections

Common objections and their answers.

1. [[The Problem of Evil and Theodicy]] — Logical vs evidential problem of evil, the usual theodicies, where Orthodoxy puts the weight (privation, freedom, the Cross), and the presuppositional reply.
