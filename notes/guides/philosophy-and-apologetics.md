## Logical Fallacies

Each note defines one fallacy with examples.

1. [[A Circular Argument]] — The structure of a circular argument, three examples, and when circularity is not fallacious.
2. [[The Fallacy of Circular Reasoning]] — Circular reasoning (begging the question) — its structure and three examples.

### Attacking the Person or Changing the Subject

Replies that avoid the argument itself.

1. [[The Ad Hominem Fallacy]] — Attacking the person instead of the argument, with five types.
2. [[Tu Quoque Fallacy]] — Dismissing an argument by accusing the opponent of hypocrisy instead of addressing it.
3. [[The Appeal to Irrelevance Fallacy]] — The red herring — answering an argument with something unrelated to it.

### Shifting Standards

Changing the rules partway through.

1. [[Double Standard Fallacy]] — Applying different standards to comparable situations or people without justification.
2. [[Double Standard Fallacy 2]] — The structure of a double standard, with examples from morals, politics and religion.
3. [[Moving the Goalpost]] — The fallacy of changing the criteria of an argument after they have been met.

### Weak Generalisations

1. [[Hasty Generalization Fallacy]] — Drawing a universal conclusion from too small a sample.
2. [[Appeal to Generality]] — Making a claim too vague or sweeping to be challenged.
3. [[The Consensus Fallacy]] — The appeal to popularity — a belief is not true because many hold it.
