Start with person and nature, then the two wills, then the errors the councils rejected.

## Person and Nature

One divine Person in two natures.

1. [[The Hypostatic Union]] — One divine Person in two natures, the nature-person distinction, and the errors that follow from denying it.
2. [[Nature and Person Distinction in Christology]] — Nature and person defined, which of them acts, and why the distinction matters.
3. [[Usage of the Term Hypostasis in the NT]] — Four New Testament passages that use the Greek word hypostasis.
4. [[St. John of Damascus on Inhypostatisation of the Human Nature of Christ]] — Christ's human nature has no hypostasis of its own but subsists in the Person of the Logos.
5. [[Jesus Being Fully Divine and Fully Human Is Not a Contradiction]] — Why the excluded middle does not rule out two natures in one Person.
6. [[Why Christ's Two Natures Can Not Mix]] — Why a single mixed nature in Christ would produce a "Tertium Quid", neither truly God nor truly man.
7. [[Distinction in the Two Natures of Christ and Salvation]] — Five reasons a "Tertium Quid" Christ could not save.
8. [[Distinction in the Two Natures of Christ and Salvation 2]] — Five reasons a "Tertium Quid" Christ could not save.
9. [[Action Is Not Proper to the Person]] — Christ's actions belong to the nature through which they are performed, while attributed to the one Person of the Son.

## The Two Wills

Why two natures mean two wills.

1. [[Will Is Proper to Nature]] — Each of Christ's two natures has its own will, united in the one divine Person of the Son.
2. [[The Question of Christ's Two Wills]] — Dyothelitism — why two natures mean two wills, and why this does not divide Christ's person.
3. [[St. Maximus on the Two Wills in Christ]] — St. Maximus's defence of Christ's two wills against Monothelitism, ratified at the Sixth Council.
4. [[The Debate Between Maximus the Confessor and Pyrrhus]] — Maximus's defence of two distinct natures and wills in Christ against Pyrrhus.

## Nestorianism and Its Answer

The dispute settled at Ephesus.

1. [[Nestorian Key Beliefs]] — Three key Nestorian beliefs and their condemnation at Ephesus.
2. [[The Third Letter of Cyril to Nestorius]] — St. Cyril's teaching that the distinction of Christ's natures implies neither separation nor composition.
3. [[Council of Ephesus: Nestorianism]] — The controversy, proceedings, implications and aftermath of the Council of Ephesus (431 AD).
4. [[Key Teachings of the Council of Ephesus]] — Five teachings of Ephesus and their bearing on the Eucharist.
5. [[Augustine's Christology: Possible Heresies]] — Five emphases in Augustine's Christology and the heresy each could lead to if misread.

## The Incarnation

Why the Word became flesh.

1. [[St. Athanasius, On the Incarnation: Main Argument]] — Why the Word became flesh — the "divine dilemma", the defeat of death and the renewal of the image of God.
2. [[Church Fathers That Taught the Doctrine of the Deification of Human Nature Through the Incarnation]] — Seven Fathers, from Irenaeus to Photios, on deification through the Incarnation.
3. [[Christ's Ascension: NT and Psalm 110]] — Psalm 110:1 and five New Testament passages that cite it.
