Start with the doctrine, then its early witnesses, then the East-West dispute.

## The Doctrine

One God in three Persons.

1. [[The Trinity]] — One God in three Persons, with the Father as the eternal source of the Son and the Holy Spirit.
2. [[The Trinity Hinted in the Old Testament]] — Old Testament verses that speak of plurality in God, of the Word and Spirit, and of a divine Son, read in the light of the New Testament.
3. [[Monarchical Trinitarianism]] — A biblical and patristic defence of the Father as the sole source of divinity in the Trinity.
4. [[Hypostatic Origin vs Economia]] — How the Persons relate within the Trinity, versus how God acts in the world.
5. [[Distinction Does Not Necessitate Separation, Division, or Composition]] — Five everyday examples of things that are distinct without being separate.
6. [[St. Cyril of Alexandria on Perichoresis]] — Perichoresis — the mutual indwelling of the three Persons — as St. Cyril used it.
7. [[God the Father Created Through the Son and With the Holy Spirit]] — Creation as the work of the Trinity — from the Father, through the Son, with the Holy Spirit.
8. [[St. Maximus on the Holy Spirit]] — St. Maximus on the Spirit's omnipresence and His particular activity within the Church.

## The Divinity of the Son

Scripture on Christ sharing what belongs to God alone.

1. [[Jesus Christ Possesses the Same Powers as God the Father Part 1]] — Verses on Christ's omnipotence, omniscience and omnipresence.
2. [[Jesus Christ Possesses the Same Powers as God the Father Part 2]] — Verses on Christ's authority over life and judgment and His role as Creator.
3. [[Bible: John 17, Jesus Has the Father's Glory]] — John 17:1-5, where Christ asks to be glorified with the glory He had with the Father before the world was.
4. [[John 14:9: Jesus Explicitly States That He Is the Image of the Father]] — John 14:9 (KJV) — he that hath seen me hath seen the Father.
5. [[In John 5, Jesus Explains That No One Has Seen the Father Except the One Whom He Has Sent]] — John 5:37 (KJV) — no one has heard the Father's voice or seen His shape.

## Before and After Nicaea

The Trinity was taught before the council and defended after it.

1. [[Pre-Nicene Fathers Teach the Trinity 1]] — Ignatius of Antioch, the Didache and Justin Martyr on the Trinity before Nicaea.
2. [[Pre-Nicene Fathers Teach the Trinity 2]] — Irenaeus and Tertullian on the Trinity before Nicaea.
3. [[Pre-Nicene Fathers Teach the Trinity 3]] — Origen, Cyprian and Hippolytus on the Trinity, and the conclusion that it was taught before Nicaea.
4. [[Council of Nicea: Arianism]] — Seven key aspects of the First Council of Nicaea (325 AD).
5. [[St. Gregory of Nyssa's Writings Against Eunomius]] — Gregory's four points against Eunomius's claim that the Father and Son differ in essence.

## East and West

The Filioque and the two models of the Trinity.

1. [[EO vs RC Trinity]] — The Cappadocian and Thomist models of the Trinity compared.
2. [[The Distinction Between Relation of Origin and Relation of Opposition]] — The Cappadocian relation of origin compared with the medieval scholastic relation of opposition.
3. [[Filioque and the Franks]] — How the Frankish kingdom drove the addition of the Filioque to the Creed, and the Eastern response.
4. [[St. Photios and the Political Use of the Filioque by the Franks]] — St. Photios's argument that the Franks used the Filioque as a political tool against Byzantium.
5. [[Emanationism]] — Emanationism and how it distorts the doctrine of the Trinity.
