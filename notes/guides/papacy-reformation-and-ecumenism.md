Start with the papacy, then the Reformation, then ecumenism.

## The Papacy

From first among equals to supremacy.

1. [[Nicene Creed Canon 6: First Among Equals Explanation]] — Canon 6 of Nicaea and what its "likewise" implies about Rome and the other sees.
2. [[The Great Schism of 1054: Causes and Timeline]] — Why 1054 is the wrong date to fixate on. Doctrinal, liturgical and political causes, a timeline from 330 to 1965, what actually happened that July, and why 1204 mattered more.
3. [[The Gregorian Reforms]] — Seven changes of the 11th-century Gregorian Reforms, each with what came before.
4. [[Vatican I's View of Papal Infallibility]] — The three conditions under which Vatican I (1870) holds the Pope to be infallible.
5. [[Vatican 1 Papal Supremacy Goes Against the Canons of Nicea]] — Vatican I's declaration of papal supremacy contrasted with the earlier "first among equals" structure.
6. [[Popes Were Used as a Political Tool Throughout History 1]] — The papacy and secular rulers in the Carolingian era and the Investiture Controversy.
7. [[Popes Were Used as a Political Tool Throughout History 2]] — The papacy and secular rulers in the High Middle Ages and the Renaissance.
8. [[Popes Were Used as a Political Tool Throughout History 3]] — The papacy and secular rulers from the Counter-Reformation to the 20th century.

## The Reformation

What the Reformers taught.

1. [[Calvinism Views on Humans]] — Total Depravity in Calvinism, and why it excludes human infallibility.
2. [[Penal Substitution: Reformers That Believed in Penal Substitution]] — Ten Reformers and Reformed theologians who taught penal substitution.
3. [[Reformer That Believed That the Father Damned the Son]] — Five Reformers whose penal substitution teaching implies Christ bore the Father's wrath.
4. [[Sola Fide Still Requires Works: Mental Work]] — Believing is itself an act of the will, so sola fide does not remove works but moves them inward.

## Ecumenism

The branch theory and the movements built on it.

1. [[The Anglican Branch Theory]] — The theory that Rome, Orthodoxy and Anglicanism are three branches of one Church.
2. [[Branch Theory Refuted From the Bible]] — Gospel and Acts passages showing that acting in Christ's name is not the same as belonging to His one Church.
3. [[Mortalium Animos]] — Pius XI's 1928 encyclical condemning ecumenism and the Branch Theory.
4. [[Vatican II Ecumenism]] — Key points of Vatican II's ecumenical turn and its legacy, with the Orthodox objection.
5. [[Ecumenical Organizations]] — The World Council of Churches and nine other ecumenical bodies.
6. [[World Council of Churches and the Rockefeller Foundation]] — Rockefeller funding of the ecumenical movement that led to the World Council of Churches in 1948.
7. [[Swami Vivekananda and John D. Rockefeller Sr.]] — The reported 1890s meeting between Vivekananda and Rockefeller, and Vivekananda's role in interfaith dialogue.
