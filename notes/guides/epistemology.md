Begin with the vocabulary, then the main theories and their problems, then faith and reason.

## Basics

What the field asks and the terms it uses.

1. [[Epistemology]] — What epistemology studies and its key concepts, with the Orthodox grounding in revelation.
2. [[Epistemic Questions]] — Six types of epistemic question, with examples of each.
3. [[Epistemic Principle]] — What epistemic principles are, with four standard examples.
4. [[First-Order and Second-Order Questions]] — Questions about a subject versus questions about how we know the answers.
5. [[The Classic Laws of Logic]] — Identity, non-contradiction, excluded middle, and sufficient reason.
6. [[By Two or More Witnesses Something Is True]] — Four verses on a matter being established by two or three witnesses.

## Problems of Knowledge

Where the standard accounts run into trouble.

1. [[The Gettier Problem]] — Gettier's challenge to knowledge as justified true belief, with the classic coins example.
2. [[Criterion Problem]] — The problem of the criterion — what we know and how we know it depend on each other.
3. [[Foundationalism]] — Foundationalism, three example principles, and two critiques.
4. [[Skepticism]] — What skepticism is, its global and local forms, and a reductio against it.
5. [[Is Ought]] — Hume's is-ought distinction in four points.

## Reason and the Senses

Rationalism, empiricism and their limits.

1. [[Rationalism, A Priori Knowledge, and Non-Empirical Propositions]] — Key terms for propositions whose content is not found in sense data.
2. [[Critique of Empiricism]] — Seven reasons not all knowledge can come from the senses.
3. [[The Peripatetic Axiom]] — The Aristotelian axiom and four reasons it is criticised from an Orthodox perspective.
4. [[The Problem of Universals: Realism vs Nominalism]] — Whether shared natures such as "humanity" are real, the main answers from Plato to Ockham, and why the question matters for theology.

## Faith and Reason

How faith, evidence and spiritual experience relate.

1. [[Fideism]] — Fideism — faith rather than reason or evidence as the basis of religious knowledge.
2. [[Fideism (Secular)]] — Fideism outside a religious context — belief held without evidence or argument.
3. [[Fideism and Evidentialism]] — Fideism and evidentialism compared, with the Orthodox view that rejects both extremes.
4. [[Mysticism Does Not Contradict Evidentialism or Evidence]] — Eastern Orthodox mysticism transcends reason and evidence without rejecting them.
5. [[Nous]] — The nous as the eye of the soul in Orthodox Christianity.
